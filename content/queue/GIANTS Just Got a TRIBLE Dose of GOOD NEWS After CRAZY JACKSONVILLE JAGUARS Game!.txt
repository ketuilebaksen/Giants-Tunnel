And the Giants walked out of that building 6-3-1, still on top of the NFC East, with their first win of the season over a team with a winning record. That is good news number one. Good news number two is a defense that picked off Trevor Lawrence, stuffed a fourth down in the red zone, and watched Abdul Carter finally have the breakout game Harbaugh promised. And good news number three comes from the most unlikely place of all, the kicker and the special teams. Good news number one: the statement win the critics said they could not get.

Think about what people were saying all week. The Giants had not beaten a single team with a winning record. Their schedule so far ranked 31st in difficulty by ESPN's Football Power Index. The only winning team they had played was the Rams, and they lost that game by 22 points. One analyst said if Jacksonville went out and smacked the Giants, we would have to have a really tough conversation about what this team really is. Another said that if the Giants won this game, they would win the NFC East, and we could just simulate the rest of the season.

Well, the Giants won. And they did it against a team that came in scoring 29.9 points per game, fourth in the league, with a quarterback putting up a 112.8 passer rating. Jacksonville scored 18. The Jaguars fell to 7-3 and had their three-game win streak snapped. The critics' last argument, that this team had not beaten anybody, is gone.

Now, I am not going to tell you one win means the NFC East is clinched. That was an analyst's take, not a math fact. There is still a lot of football left. But this win matters for the standings, it matters for the playoff odds, and it matters for respect. Before the game, Pro Football Focus projected the Giants' playoff chances at about 76 percent with a win and around 49 percent with a loss. They got the win.

And next up is a road trip to Indianapolis. The Colts are 3-8. They just lost 26-3 to Houston on Thursday, and they waived Daniel Jones the next day, with Anthony Richardson set to start. That is a game this team has to win.

Good news number two: the defense was everything we hoped it would be.

Let's start with the guy everybody was talking about all week. Abdul Carter.

All week, the critics said Carter was a bust because he only had a couple of sacks. Harbaugh said the sacks would come. He said Carter is grading out really well on his sheet and on the coaches' sheet, that teams are accounting for him, and that he is a game wrecker. Before the game, the numbers backed Harbaugh up too. Carter had a 29 percent pass-rush win rate, and he had created 16 incompletions off his pass-rush wins, the most in the NFL. Pro Football Focus had him with an 89.3 overall grade, third among edge defenders.

And on Sunday, the sacks came. Carter had two sacks, a career high, and four quarterback hits. One report credited him with eight total pressures and two tackles for loss, and he drew several holding penalties too. Those two sacks doubled his season total in one afternoon. After the game, Carter called himself the "best in the world." You can like that or not, but after the week he had, with everybody calling him a bust, I understand why he said it. Harbaugh said the sacks would come. They came.

And it was not just Carter. DJ Reader got a sack, his second in three games. As a team, the Giants had three sacks, 11 quarterback hits, and 14 pass breakups. Trevor Lawrence threw it 47 times and completed 25 of them for 301 yards, with one touchdown and two interceptions. That is a lot of yards, I will not pretend otherwise. But a lot of incompletions, a lot of hits, and a lot of drives that ended without seven points.

Now the takeaways, because this is where the game was won.

Deonte Banks, the guy everybody was ripping all week for the pass interference against Washington, intercepted Lawrence in the first quarter and returned it 19 yards. That set up a Zvada field goal to tie the game. What a response from Banks. All week, the talk was about him watching the receiver instead of turning to find the football. On Sunday, he found the football.

Then in the fourth quarter, Paulson Adebo got his first interception of the season. Remember, Harbaugh told Adebo this week, in my mind, you're a starter in this league, and you're going to have to start again. Well, Greg Newsome II went down early with an ankle injury, Adebo came in, and he made a game-changing play. That is exactly why depth matters.

And then the two-point conversion. After Lawrence hit Brian Thomas Jr. for an 18-yard touchdown to make it 23-15 with 6:39 left, Jacksonville went for two. And Kayvon Thibodeaux intercepted it. The lead stayed at eight instead of six. In a game that ended 23-18, that was a massive play.

And do not forget the red zone stop. Before this game, I told you the Giants had been near the bottom of the league at preventing touchdowns in the red zone, and that Liam Coen is not afraid to go for it on fourth down. Well, Jacksonville went for it on fourth-and-one at the Giants' 11, and Dru Phillips broke up the pass. That play ended Jacksonville's streak of 14 straight red zone drives that ended in points. Fourteen straight. And the Giants ended it.

Before the game, one of the big concerns was play action. The analysis said the Giants' defense ranked 28th in EPA per play against play action, and Jacksonville led the NFL with 10.6 yards per attempt off play action, with six touchdowns and no interceptions. That was supposed to be the matchup that killed the Giants. But the way you beat play action is by wrecking the timing. Make the quarterback throw before the route develops. Carter's pressure, the quarterback hits, the pass breakups, that is how the Giants did it. Before the game, Arvell Reese had a 30 percent pass-rush win rate, fifth among players with at least 50 pass-rush snaps. He did not get a sack in this one, but the whole front made Lawrence uncomfortable all day.

Good news number three: Zvada and the special teams overhaul.

Let me tell you, this is the part that made me smile the most. All week, the story was the right hash. All six of Zvada's misses this season had come from the right hash. Harbaugh said he had a technique issue with his plant foot. He said Zvada was dead on in practice from, as he put it, the "amazing, scary right hash."

So what happened on Sunday? Zvada made a 53-yarder from the right hash. He made a 54-yarder from the right hash. He made a 43-yarder from the left hash. And yes, he missed a 32-yarder wide left, also from the right hash, on the first kick of the second half. So it was not perfect, and I am not going to hide that. But think about this. With those two long kicks, Zvada now has eight field goals of 50-plus yards this season. That ties Graham Gano's single-season franchise record from 2022. An undrafted rookie, in his first season, already tying the franchise record for 50-yard field goals. That is a big leg. Now he has to clean up the short ones.

And the rest of the special teams? Better. Not perfect, but better. Jordan Stout was a weapon, with four punts averaging over 60 yards gross, one downed inside the 20. Xavier Gipson, in his first game back, handled kick returns and a punt with no muffs. After Charlie Jones lost two fumbles on returns this season, a clean day from the return man was exactly what this team needed.

There was one ugly moment. Ameer Abdullah opened the game with a 47-yard kickoff return, the longest the Giants have allowed all season, and an illegal formation penalty on Elijah Campbell helped Jacksonville get a field goal. So the coverage units still need work. But overall, special teams did not lose this game. And that is progress.

Now let's be fair. The concerns.

First, the offense. McCarthy finished 18 of 38 for 173 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. He also ran four times for 38 yards, including a big 22-yard scramble on the touchdown drive. Protecting the ball against a defense that averages about 1.8 takeaways a game is a real win. But 18 of 38 is not good enough. One report called him a little too streaky, and I think that is fair. The touchdown came on an eight-yard pass to Malik Nabers with 6:04 left in the second quarter, his third touchdown of the season. Nabers had seven catches. And Jameis Winston punched in a one-yard quarterback sneak for the Giants' first lead, after a 37-yard pass interference penalty set up first-and-goal.

Second, the late-game offense, again. After building a big lead, the Giants did not get a first down on four straight possessions. Jacksonville cut it from 14 points to eight on the Thomas touchdown, and then later to five. That is a lot like the Washington game, when the Giants went three-and-out twice with a lead. One report pointed out the Giants barely ran the ball in that stretch until the final two minutes. This time, the Giants survived. Malachi Fields made a huge 19-yard catch for a first down with under three minutes left. Skattebo picked up another first down at the two-minute warning. And McCarthy took the knee. But the closing problem is still there, and Harbaugh knows it.

Third, Cam Skattebo. He ran 14 times for 34 yards and had an 11-yard catch-and-run. But he also fumbled in the third quarter, forced by Josh Hines-Allen. That was his fifth fumble of the season, which one report says leads the league. Jacksonville returned it 42 yards to the Giants' 31. The defense bailed him out, but that cannot keep happening. As a team, the Giants ran for just 77 yards on 25 carries, 3.1 per carry. Against a defense that allowed only 3.9 yards per rush coming in, that is not a shock, but it is something to fix.

And fourth, the injury to Greg Newsome II. He left early with an ankle injury. The Giants just extended him through 2029, so they need him healthy. Adebo was great in his place, but we will be watching that one closely.

Let me give some extra credit to a few guys who do not always get the headlines.

Isaiah Likely made history. He became the first Giants tight end with 600-plus receiving yards in the first 10 games of a season since Mark Bavaro in 1987. Mark Bavaro. That is the company he is keeping. I told you a few weeks ago that letting Wan'Dale Robinson walk and finding Likely was one of the best moves of the offseason. This is why.

Jalin Hyatt got his first catch of the season, a four-yard grab on third down to move the chains late in the third quarter. Nine different Giants caught a pass. That is the kind of spread-the-ball offense you need when the defense takes away your top option.

And Malachi Fields, the rookie, made the catch that basically sealed it. Nineteen yards, first down, under three minutes left. Remember when I said Fields has been nails? That is what nails looks like.

And I want to talk about two redemption stories from this game, because I think they say a lot about this locker room.

The first is Jameis Winston and the quarterback sneak. Against Washington, the sneak became a punchline. The "Who let the dogs out" cadence did not draw anybody offside, the play clock ran out, the Giants took a delay of game and punted, and Harbaugh said later he was kicking himself for not just going for it. People were blaming Skattebo for not pushing, until Harbaugh explained it was not a tush push. On Sunday, the Giants went right back to Winston on the goal line, and he punched in the one-yard sneak for the Giants' first lead of the game. Same package. Different result. That is a coaching staff that believed in its plan and a veteran quarterback who delivered.

The second is Deonte Banks. Less than two weeks ago, he was the most criticized player on the team. The 33-yard pass interference against Washington set up the game-winning drive. The talk all week was about his technique, about not turning his head to find the ball. He also hurt his shoulder in warmups against Washington and had to work his way back to a full practice this week. And on Sunday, he was the one who got the Giants' first takeaway, an interception of a quarterback with a 112.8 passer rating, with a 19-yard return. Football players remember the bad plays. Great football players answer them.

Now let's put it all together.

Before this game, people were debating whether the Giants should flip McCarthy for a first or second-round pick, or whether Jaxson Dart was the better quarterback. That was all speculation, not a reported offer. One analyst said the weakest link on this team was not the quarterback, it was the wide receivers. And I think the smartest takeaway from Sunday is this. McCarthy did not win this game by himself. He did not lose it either. He protected the football, made a few plays with his legs, threw a touchdown, and let the defense win. That is winning football, the way Harbaugh described it. But to beat the best teams in January, the passing game has to be more efficient. That is the next step.
