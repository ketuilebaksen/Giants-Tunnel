Ninety-three point two percent. That is the number that has Giants fans losing their minds this week. With 2:19 left in the fourth quarter on Thursday night, the Giants’ win probability against Washington was cited at 93.2 percent. And they lost. Since then, a wave of updates and hot takes has crashed over this team. One national analyst said the Giants showed none of the hallmarks of a serious team. Another said John Harbaugh specifically lost this game. Another said the Giants will only win the division by default. And one projection now has Dallas as the favorite in the NFC East. So today, I am going through every single one of these updates, and I am going to tell you which ones are fair, which ones are overreactions, and what this team has to do to shut everybody up.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I’m James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on the notifications, because it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now: after all the noise this week, are you still confident the Giants win the NFC East? Yes or no, in the comments.

Let me get the facts on the table first, so we are all working from the same game. Washington 24, Giants 21, at MetLife, in the rain. The Giants led 21-17 after J.J. McCarthy hit Isaiah Likely for a 66-yard touchdown with 8:13 left. Washington’s first three possessions of the fourth quarter ended in a turnover on downs, a Jevon Holland interception at the goal line, and an Ar’Darius Washington interception with about two minutes and ten seconds left. And then the Giants’ last three possessions went three-and-out, three-and-out, interception. Daniels hit Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds left, after a 33-yard pass interference on Deonte Banks, and Rasul Douglas picked off McCarthy to end it. Giants are 5-3-1, still alone in first.

Okay. Now the wave of updates.

Update number one: the 93.2 percent.

This is the number everybody is passing around. With 2:19 left in the fourth quarter, the Giants’ chance to win was at 93.2 percent. And yes, when you are up four, with the ball, after your defense just picked off the opposing quarterback for the second time, that number makes sense. All you need is a first down, maybe two, and the game is over.

But here is my take on win probability, and I want you to really think about this. A win probability of 93.2 percent does not mean you have won. It means that in a hundred similar situations, a team in your spot wins about 93 of them. It also means you lose about seven. That is not nothing. Every week in this league, somebody is on the wrong side of that seven. On Thursday, it was the Giants.

So is it embarrassing? Yes, it hurts, and the way it happened is ugly. But is it proof that this team is a fraud? No. Teams that win Super Bowls have games like this. The question is not whether you ever blow a 93 percent game. The question is whether you keep doing it. And that brings us to the next update.

Update number two: the “not a serious team” criticism.

One national analyst said, and I am going to paraphrase it fairly, that John Harbaugh is a good coach and a serious coach, a serious guy in a positive way, but that the Giants did not have any of the hallmarks of a serious team on Thursday, particularly from some of the young players and from some of the players who are supposed to be leaders. He said if he were a Giants fan, he would be beside himself about the missed opportunity and what he saw as the overall team immaturity.

So is that fair? Let me break it down.

The part I agree with: the finish was not mature football. Three-and-out, three-and-out, interception. A 12-yard punt from Jordan Stout when the Giants needed a real boot. A missed 33-yard field goal from Dominic Zvada. A pass interference in the final two minutes. A Malik Nabers fumble deep in Washington territory in the third quarter. A false start in a critical situation, which Harbaugh himself mentioned after the game. A couple of drops, including an early one from Likely. Those are the mistakes of a young team that is still learning how to close.

The part I do not agree with: “none of the hallmarks.” I think that is too far. This defense took the ball away three straight times in the fourth quarter. Holland got burned on the Antonio Williams touchdown and then came back and made the goal-line interception. That is a mature response. McCarthy played through a hip injury and threw for a career-high 293 yards, and after the game he did not make excuses. He said it absolutely sucks, gave Washington credit for making more plays, and said they have to learn from it and make sure it does not happen again in those critical situations. That is a young quarterback showing maturity. And Harbaugh walked into that press conference and said the team’s spirit is not one of fear, it is one of power and courage, and they are not going to curl up.

So my verdict on update two: the criticism is about half right. The finishing was immature. The team is not.

Update number three: “John Harbaugh lost this game.”

This is the loudest one. One critic said the Giants lost the game, and specifically, Harbaugh lost the game. The argument is that the Giants played scared and played not to lose down the stretch. The critic pointed at Skattebo and said, the man looks like he is ready to run through brick walls, and you have a short-yardage situation and you do not give him the ball. Another voice said the Giants should have gone for it on fourth-and-one and tried to get the one yard with Skattebo, and that they ran McCarthy on third down instead of throwing it.

Now, I want to be precise here, because a lot of people are mixing up plays. There were two different late possessions after the two interceptions. On one of them, the Giants were deep in their own end, around their own 11, facing fourth-and-one with Jameis Winston in the game. Harbaugh said after the game he was not seriously thinking about going for it there. He tried to use the cadence to draw Washington offside, Washington did not jump, the play clock ran out, they took the delay, and they punted. On the other possession, after the second interception, the Giants were on third down with a few yards to go, with nobody in the backfield, and they called a quarterback crack sweep for McCarthy. Sonny Styles blew it up for a loss. Harbaugh called it third-and-four after the game. Other people have called it third-and-three or even third-and-one. Whatever the exact distance, the point is the same. It was a short-yardage chance to put the game away, and the Giants’ hottest player was not on the field.

So did Harbaugh lose the game? My opinion, not a report: he did not lose it by himself, but that third-down call is on him and his staff. Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns. Harbaugh said after the game Cam ran really well, ran hard, and had an excellent game, and that the offensive line looked like it did in Houston. When you have that, you lean on it. The idea of a quarterback run with an extra blocker is fine on paper, but in that moment, you give the ball to the guy who has been running through people all night.

And the fourth-and-one at their own 11? I am with Harbaugh there. You do not go for it at your own 11 with a four-point lead. If you fail, you are basically handing Washington a touchdown. That is not playing scared. That is playing smart.

There is also a number going around from one of these shows, that Harbaugh has 47 fourth-quarter blown leads in his career over the last decade-plus. I have not been able to verify that number, and I am not going to treat it as fact on this channel. What I will say is this. Harbaugh coached 18 seasons in Baltimore, won a Super Bowl, and had a lot of close games. Any coach with that many games is going to have a long list of tough losses. One number thrown out on a show does not tell you the whole story.

Update number four: the Skattebo energy.

Can we talk about Cam Skattebo for a second? Because he was a house on fire on Thursday night. Two touchdowns. Flipping around. Staring guys down. And at one point on the sideline, he threw up at his feet and then covered it with a towel, and went right back to work. That is a football player. That is the kind of energy that wins you games in December.

And that is why the frustration about the late game is so loud. The offense had an identity on Thursday. Skattebo running hard, Likely catching everything after an early drop, McCarthy hitting big throws. And at the end, it felt like the offense went away from that identity. The critics are saying the late approach looked disconnected from the team’s strengths. On that one, I agree with them.

Update number five: the McCarthy final drive.

Here is the part of the game that is getting lost in all the noise. After Diggs scored, McCarthy got the ball back and moved the Giants into Washington territory, right around midfield. He was keeping the offense on schedule. He was finding Likely, taking the profit, not turning it over. And one analyst described some of his throws as grade-A, top-of-the-food-chain quarterback throws, including one to Likely over the middle where he looked like he was about to take a big hit. On that last drive, Washington started blitzing him, and I mean everybody. It felt like the whole city of D.C. was coming after him. McCarthy put up a couple of 50-50 balls, and all the Giants needed was another 10 to 15 yards to set up a field goal and force overtime. On the last one, he got hit as he threw, and Rasul Douglas picked it off.

So was that interception a bad decision? My opinion, not a report: it was a tough spot, and I understand why he gave his guy a chance. But this goes back to what we have been saying all week. Against six or seven rushers on Thursday, McCarthy was two for six for 11 yards and an interception. When teams send the house, he needs a hot read, a quick throw, something to punish the blitz. That is the next step for him.

Update number six: the special teams.

One analyst put it simply. The Giants woke up in New York on Friday and said we played not to lose, and special teams did us wrong. And here is the irony. John Harbaugh is a special teams guru. That is where he came from as a coach. And yet on Thursday, a 12-yard punt when they needed a big boot, a missed short field goal, and a kickoff that went out of bounds. In the first Washington game, the tie, they missed three field goals.

There is a really good point in all of this. Conservative football only works if the rest of your game is reliable. If you are going to play it safe, punt, and trust your defense, then your punter has to flip the field. If he does not, your caution becomes a bill your defense has to pay. That is exactly what happened on Thursday.

Harbaugh backed Zvada after the game, and I agree with that. He said they are not going to knee-jerk it with a rookie who has made a bunch of kicks, and that it is up to Zvada to do it. But the special teams have to be better, because this team is built to win close, low-scoring games.

Update number seven: the division.

This is the one that made me laugh and also made me think. One analyst said the division is awful, let’s be real, and that the Giants are going to win it by default. He said they are going to hold on for their lives, because Dallas will find a way to lose games. His line was that Dallas will win games you do not expect them to win, you get high on them, and then that is when they stink up the joint, especially around the holidays. He said he is still going with the Giants. He said he is not jumping on the Dallas train until Dallas gives him something to believe in, and he pointed out that Dallas has the 49ers coming up.

And then there is the number. One projection cited on one of these shows has Dallas at 51 percent to win the NFC East, and the Giants at 40 percent.

Now, let me push back on that, because I do not buy it. The Giants are 5-3-1. Dallas is 4-5. The Giants are a game and a half ahead of Dallas. The Giants beat Dallas 28-20 in week one. Washington is 3-5-1, two games back, but it has the head-to-head edge on the Giants now. Philadelphia is 2-7. I do not know what model that projection is using, and I am not going to pretend I can check it, but if it has Dallas as the favorite while the Giants have a game and a half lead and the head-to-head win, it must be leaning heavily on schedule strength or something else in its formula. That is fine. Projections are projections. The standings are the standings.

And here is my opinion on “winning by default.” There is no such thing. You win the division by winning more games than everybody else. Nobody gives you a banner because the other teams are bad. If the Giants win the NFC East, they will have earned it, and I do not care what anyone says about the division.

And think about what the remaining schedule means for the division race. The Giants still have the second games against Dallas and Philadelphia ahead of them. They already beat both of those teams once, including the 19-11 win in Philadelphia, the first win there since 2013. If the Giants take care of business in those two games, the projections will not matter, because the Giants will have settled it head-to-head on the field. That is the cleanest path, and it is right there for them.

Update number eight: the prediction that the Giants lose at least four of their next six.

One critic said that over the next six weeks, he expects the Giants to lose at least four more games. That is a big call. That would mean a stretch of 2-4 or worse. Let me tell you why I think that is an overreaction. This defense was fifth in the league in total defense heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. It went from one of the worst defenses in football in 2025 to a top-five unit. Arvell Reese is one of the best rookies in the league. The Giants have a ten-day break before hosting Jacksonville on November 22. Tracy is on IR and lined up to come back around week 13 against San Francisco. McCarthy just had a career night. This is not a team built to lose four out of six.

Could they hit a rough patch? Sure. Every team does. But four losses in six games, with this defense, would shock me.

So let me sum up the wave. The 93.2 percent: painful, but not proof of anything. The “not a serious team” take: half right about the finish, wrong about the team. “Harbaugh lost the game”: the third-down call is on him, but he did not lose it alone. The Skattebo energy: the best part of the night. McCarthy’s final drive: better than people think, but the blitz plan needs work. Special teams: has to be better. The division: the Giants are still on top, no default needed. And four losses in six weeks: I am not buying it.

Now, the receipts, because I always own them. I picked the Giants to win this game and get to 6-2-1. I was wrong. I said McCarthy would finish and play well, and he threw for a career high. I said Harbaugh was the right coach for big moments, and on Thursday, a couple of his late calls did not work. So I take the loss on my pick and I keep moving. And I am going to make a new prediction, and I want you to hold me to it. The Giants do not lose four of their next six. They beat Jacksonville on November 22 and get to 6-3-1.

And let me give the other side some credit, because it matters. Washington earned this. After two interceptions in a row, Dan Quinn was on the sideline telling his guys, let’s go get a stop and get back on the field. And then Daniels came out and manufactured a drive to win the game. That says a lot about Washington’s leadership and its quarterback. Calling the whole thing a Giants collapse erases what Washington did. They made the plays at the end, and the Giants did not.

Here is the bottom line. The Giants created enough chances on Thursday to win like a contender, and then failed the test that makes a contender real. That is the harsh truth. Talent creates control. Only execution keeps it. And now this team has ten days to fix the execution.

So I am going to say it again, and I believe it as much as I ever have. This team can go all the way. A top-five defense. A rookie pass rusher making history. A running back playing with fire. A young quarterback who can make the big throw. A coach who has been through this before. And a lead in the division with plenty of football left. The critics are loud this week. The best way to quiet them is to win, and keep winning.
