The New York Giants just made a decision that has a lot of people around the league shaking their heads. After a 24-21 loss to Washington, with a missed 33-yard field goal sitting right in the middle of a three-point game, John Harbaugh stood at the podium and said, flat out, we are not changing kickers. No panic. No tryouts. No knee-jerk move. Dominic Zvada is the guy. And that was not the only big decision Harbaugh explained that night. The zero blitz on the final drive. The empty backfield on third down. The fourth downs he passed on. Today, I am putting every one of those decisions on trial. I am going to grade them one by one, and I am going to tell you which ones I agree with and which ones I think cost this team a football game.

If you are new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on the notifications, because it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now: which decision from Thursday night bothered you the most? The zero blitz, the quarterback sweep, the punt, or keeping the kicker? Pick one and tell me in the comments.

Quick setup before we start grading. Washington 24, Giants 21, Thursday night at MetLife, in the rain. The Giants led 21-17 after a 66-yard touchdown from J.J. McCarthy to Isaiah Likely with 8:13 left. The defense got a fourth-down stop and then two interceptions of Jayden Daniels, one from Jevon Holland at the goal line and one from Ar’Darius Washington late. The offense went three-and-out after both picks. Then Daniels went 71 yards in three plays, with a 33-yard pass interference on Deonte Banks, and hit Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds left. McCarthy’s final deep ball was intercepted. The Giants are 5-3-1, still alone in first place in the NFC East.

Now, the trial. Five decisions. Five grades.

Decision number one: keeping Dominic Zvada.

This is the headline. A reporter brought up that there was another missed kick, and said that the confidence level in the kicker has been a topic for a couple of weeks now. And Harbaugh did not flinch. He said, we’re confident. He said, you can’t, as soon as a guy doesn’t make a play, start firing people left and right, especially your kicker who’s a rookie. He said, I’m confident he’s going to become a good kicker. And then he added something that I think a lot of people skipped over. He said, if at some point in time it doesn’t happen, it doesn’t happen. But we’re not going to panic halfway through the season with a guy who’s made a bunch of kicks. And then, with a little edge, if that’s what you’re inferring we should be doing. We’re just not going to knee-jerk it like that. He’s going to make kicks. He’s going to make a lot of kicks. I believe in him. I think he’s really good and really talented. We’re going to give him an opportunity, and it’s up to him to do it.

So let’s look at the case against this decision first, because it is real. In the first Washington game, the 27-27 tie in week five, the Giants missed three field goals. Three. On Thursday, Zvada missed a 33-yarder early, and the Giants lost by three. Think about that. In two games against the team that is now holding the head-to-head edge on you, the kicking game has cost you a win and then cost you at least a chance at overtime. If you are a fan who says, I cannot go into December with this, I hear you.

Now the case for the decision. Zvada went four for four in Philadelphia, including a 53-yarder, in the game that ended a 13-game losing streak in Philly. He has six field goals of 50 or more yards this season. That is a big leg. That is a rookie with real talent. And what is the alternative in mid-November? You go to the street. You bring in a veteran who has been sitting on his couch, who does not know your snapper and your holder, who has not kicked in your stadium in this weather. That guy is not automatically better. And you send a message to your whole locker room that one mistake gets you replaced.

But here is the part that matters most. Harbaugh did not say Zvada has a job forever. He said, if it doesn’t happen, it doesn’t happen. He said, it’s up to him. That is a coach putting the responsibility right on the player’s shoulders while still backing him in public. That is the line between loyalty and blindness, and Harbaugh walked it perfectly.

My grade: A-minus. Right decision for right now. But I will say this, and it is my opinion, not a report. If Zvada misses a makeable kick in a close game against Jacksonville, this conversation changes fast. The leash is not unlimited, and Harbaugh basically told you that himself.

Decision number two: the zero blitz on the final drive.

The very first question Harbaugh got was about the last two throws. And he gave a two-word answer that told you everything. Zero blitzes. He said, zero blitzes, and those are the routes that ended up getting run. Later on, he added, we get into a blitz and we get beat on an inside breaking route. You don’t really want that to happen, and it happened. That’s football.

Let me explain what that means if you are newer to football. A zero blitz means no deep safety. Everybody is either rushing the quarterback or locked in man coverage. If the quarterback gets the ball out before the pressure gets there, and the receiver wins his matchup, there is nobody behind him to make the tackle. That is exactly what happened on the Diggs touchdown. Diggs broke inside, broke a tackle, and there was nobody left. Thirty-two yards, touchdown.

And here is the stat that makes this decision even harder to defend. According to the breakdown after the game, all three of Daniels’ touchdown passes came against cover zero. Three for three. He was perfect on play action, six for six. So it was not like the zero blitz had been beating Daniels all night and then he got lucky once. Daniels had been reading it and beating it during the game.

Now, the defense of the decision. This is who the Giants are. Since Brian Burns went down in week three, this has been one of the most blitz-heavy defenses in football, right behind Brian Flores and the Vikings. That aggression is a big reason why this defense was fifth in the league heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game, after being one of the worst in the league in 2025. It produced seven sacks in Philadelphia. It forced three straight fourth-quarter stops on Thursday. You do not just turn off your identity in the final two minutes.

But my grade is a C. And here is why. Situation matters. You are up four, Washington needs a touchdown, there is under two minutes left. Your job is to keep everything in front of you and make Daniels go the long way. A field goal does not beat you. Only a touchdown does. When the only thing that can beat you is the big play, you do not want to be in the one coverage that gives up the big play. My opinion, not a report: I would have mixed in more coverage there, especially after the pass interference flag had already put Washington in Giants territory. Harbaugh owned it, and I respect that. But this one is going to be on the film-room screen for a long time.

Decision number three: the empty-backfield quarterback crack sweep on third down.

After the second interception, the Giants had the ball with a little over two minutes left, holding a four-point lead. Third-and-short. One first down and you are basically icing the game, because Washington would be burning timeouts and running out of time. And the Giants lined up empty, with nobody in the backfield, and called a quarterback crack sweep for McCarthy.

A reporter asked about it directly. He said, you’re empty, you have J.J. power there, there’s no one in the backfield, what was the thinking? And Harbaugh explained that it is a quarterback crack sweep, and the idea is you pick up an extra blocker with the quarterback running. He said, I thought we had a chance, but I have to see the tape to see how Styles got through there. You like to think he might get blocked, but I’m not exactly sure what the front was. We’ll have to watch the tape.

Sonny Styles, the Washington rookie linebacker, came through and made the stop for a loss. And Harbaugh said that tackle for loss was the key to the stop.

Here is my problem with this one. Cam Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns in this game. Harbaugh himself said after the game that Cam ran really well, Cam ran hard, it was an excellent game for him. He said the offensive line was much better, more like it was in Houston. So your running game is working. Your back is hot. Your line is moving people. And in the biggest third-down snap of the game, your best runner is not on the field, and you ask your quarterback, who is playing with a hip injury from the Philadelphia game, to run a sweep against a defense that had been loading up all night.

I understand the logic of the play. The extra blocker math is real. In theory, the quarterback run gives you a numbers advantage. But it also takes away the threat of the handoff, because there is no one to hand off to. When the defense knows there is no back, they can play the quarterback run with more freedom. And that is what Styles did.

My grade: D. My opinion, not a report: this was the decision that lost the game, more than the flag, more than the blitz. Put Skattebo in the backfield, hand him the ball behind a line that was winning, and take your chances. If you lose the game after that, you lose it with your best player doing what he was doing all night.

Decision number four: passing on fourth down.

There were two fourth-down decisions that came up at the podium, and they are related. First, after the sweep lost yards, a reporter asked how seriously Harbaugh thought about going for it. And Harbaugh said he thought about it, but if they had been at four yards or less, he thinks he would have gone for it. Losing the two yards on the sweep was disappointing. So the bad third-down play made the fourth-down decision for him. He punted.

The second one was deep in their own end. Fourth-and-one around their own 11, with Jameis Winston in the game. And Harbaugh said he was not seriously thinking about going for it there. The idea was to draw Washington offside. A reporter even asked about Jameis yelling “Who let the dogs out” at the line of scrimmage, and Harbaugh said, it’s part of the cadence. Teams do jump in those situations sometimes, so that was the idea. Washington did not jump. Harbaugh gave them credit and said they played with good discipline. The play clock ran out, the Giants took the delay, and they punted.

Let’s grade these together. Going for it on fourth-and-one at your own 11 with a lead in the fourth quarter is a huge gamble. If you fail, Washington is in the red zone and likely scoring. Using the hard count to try to steal a free first down, and then punting if it does not work, is the smart, conservative play. I am fine with it. And I will admit, the Winston cadence thing is fun. It is a veteran quarterback with a big voice doing his job.

The second one, after the sweep lost two yards, I am also fine with. Once you are at fourth-and-five or longer, you punt and trust your defense, which had just taken the ball away three straight times. The decision was right. It was the third-down call before it that put Harbaugh in that spot.

My grade: B. Smart, defensible decisions. The problem was the plays before them, not the fourth-down calls themselves.

Decision number five: sticking with McCarthy, and backing him publicly.

A reporter asked what Harbaugh thought of the way J.J. played. And Harbaugh did not hesitate. He said, I thought he played very well. There were some plays made. He made some throws, scrambled. I thought J.J. played very well. I thought everybody played well. We just didn’t play quite well enough.

Now, after this game, there are going to be people calling for Jameis Winston. Winston came in and saved the day in Philadelphia with a 14-play, 76-yard drive that ended with a touchdown to Likely. He was in for the sneak packages on Thursday. And McCarthy threw the final interception on a deep ball to Malik Nabers. So there is going to be noise.

But look at what McCarthy did on Thursday. He threw for 293 yards, a career high, on a wet ball, with a hip that was not fully healthy. He threw the 66-yard touchdown to Likely, his first touchdown pass as a Giant. The only blemish was the last throw, and that was third-and-10 with under a minute left, needing a field goal, with Washington sending pressure.

Now, there is a real issue that needs fixing, and I want to be fair. Against six or seven rushers on Thursday, McCarthy was two for six for 11 yards and an interception. Washington’s defensive staff comes from the Brian Flores tree, so the zero blitz was always coming. And it looked like the Giants did not have a great answer for it. That is on the quarterback and the coaching staff together. Hot reads, quick throws, sliding protection, screens, something to punish the blitz. That is the homework for the next ten days.

My grade: A. Backing your young quarterback publicly after a game like that is the right call. The blitz plan needs work. The quarterback decision does not.

So let’s add it up. Kicker: A-minus. Zero blitz: C. Empty quarterback sweep: D. Fourth downs: B. McCarthy: A. That is a decent report card, with one ugly grade that happened at the worst possible time.

Now let me talk about the most important thing Harbaugh said, because it was not about any single decision. It was about the team.

His opening statement was this. Tough loss, credit to the Commanders, they played a very good football game. We played a pretty good football game, too. Just not good enough to win. Then he said, our guys are tough, we have a good spirit. It’s not one of fear. It’s one of power and courage, and we will continue fighting.

And later he got even more specific. He said, it’s disappointing, but you can’t let it bring you down. Our spirit’s going to be fine. We’re not going to curl up. We’re going to come out fighting. He said the rest of the season is going to be a bunch of dog fights, and that he would love to say they are going to win all of them, but it’s probably not very likely, and you’re probably not going to put your money down on that. So you have to win enough. This was one that got away, and you have to get the next one, or the next one after that, and get as many as you need to win the division. Keep it in perspective.

He also gave a to-do list, which I love, because it tells you exactly what the next ten days are about. A false start in a critical situation. Not finishing some plays. Leaky yards in tackling. A couple of runs that went for more than they should have. And most importantly, letting Washington live in third-and-short. He explained that when the opponent is in third-and-one or third-and-two all night, you are not going to get much pressure. And on first down, it is mostly play action and the run game, so there is not much pressure unless you blitz. That is the real explanation for why the pass rush did not show up the way it did in Philadelphia. In Philly, the Eagles were 5 of 18 on third down and the Giants had seven sacks. On Thursday, Washington stayed on schedule. Fix early downs, and the pass rush comes back.

And one more decision I want to mention. A reporter asked about a quarterback sneak where it looked like Skattebo was staying in his stance instead of pushing, and whether the fullback was in the game. Harbaugh said he was not sure, they have about four sneaks they call, and you have to give him more than five minutes after the game to answer those questions. That might sound like a dodge, but I actually like it. He is not going to throw a player under the bus at the podium without seeing the tape. He is going to watch it, fix it, and handle it in the building.

Now let me hit the receipts, because I always own them. Before this game, I said the Giants would win and move to 6-2-1. I was wrong. I said McCarthy would finish and play well, and his coach said he played very well. I said Harbaugh was the right man to handle the big moments, and on Thursday night, a couple of his late-game calls did not work. So I own the miss on the game, and I will tell you that my trust in Harbaugh is still there, but he has to be better in those final minutes, and I think he knows it.

So where does this leave the Giants? Still 5-3-1. Still alone in first. Washington is 3-5-1, Dallas is 4-5, Philadelphia is 2-7. The Giants are two games up on Washington, a game and a half up on Dallas. Washington owns the edge head-to-head now, after the tie and this win, so the Giants do not want this to come down to a tiebreaker. But they still control their own path. They host Jacksonville on Sunday, November 22, after ten days off. And Harbaugh said it himself. A really good team is coming in, and it is a team the Giants can beat. Tracy is on IR and lined up to return around week 13 against San Francisco.

My prediction, and hold me to it. The Giants come out of this break with a cleaner late-game plan, Skattebo is on the field when it matters, Zvada makes his kicks, and the Giants beat Jacksonville to get to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I am going to say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A coach who backs his players and owns his calls. A young quarterback coming off a career night. A running back who carried the offense in the rain. A defense that took the ball away three straight times in the fourth quarter. And a division lead with plenty of football left. One bad call does not end a season. It sharpens one.
