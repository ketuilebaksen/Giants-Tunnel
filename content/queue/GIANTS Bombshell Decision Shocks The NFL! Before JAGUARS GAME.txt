The NFL just made a decision about this Giants game, and it tells you how big Sunday has become. The league moved Giants versus Jaguars to 4:25 Eastern, into the national spotlight. The Giants are first place in the NFC East, playing at home, and they are still about five-and-a-half-point underdogs. Everybody is calling this a nightmare matchup against a 7-2 Jacksonville team. But the more I dug into the numbers, the more I think people are missing something. There is a real weakness on this Jaguars team that the Giants can attack. And there is a real weakness on the Giants that could get them beat. Today, I am laying out both, and then I am giving you my score prediction.

First, let’s understand what is riding on this game. The Giants are 5-3-1. Jacksonville is 7-2. The Washington loss still stings, and now the Giants face a team that looks a lot tougher on paper. But one win could put this whole division race in a different light.

Here is the number that shows you how big this is. One playoff model gives the Giants about a 72 percent chance to make the playoffs right now, and roughly the same chance to win the NFC East. Beat Jacksonville, and those numbers jump to 85 percent for the playoffs and 84 percent for the division. Lose, and they drop to 67 and 66. Those are projections, not guarantees. But the message is clear. This is not just another game. It is the chance to get a sixth win, strengthen the division lead, and show everyone that this record means something.

And that matters, because the critics have one argument left. The Giants have not beaten a team with a winning record this season. Their schedule so far ranked 31st in difficulty by ESPN’s Football Power Index. Beat a 7-2 team on national television, and that argument is gone.

Now, I respect what Jacksonville has done. Seven wins do not happen by accident. They have beaten Cleveland, New England, Cincinnati, Philadelphia, Indianapolis, Baltimore and Tennessee. That is a strong list when you see it all together. But when I look closer, I do not see seven opponents who were all playing great football. Several of those teams have struggled. And Tennessee hung around with Jacksonville last week before the Jaguars finally pulled away 40-23. So when people talk like the Giants are facing an unbeatable machine, I think that goes too far. Jacksonville is very good. That does not mean there is no way to attack them.

So here is the weakness I have been looking at. The Jaguars’ running game.

Everybody talks about how Jacksonville can run the ball and control a game. But when you look at how efficiently they are actually running it, you see something different. They are averaging about four yards per carry, which ranks 22nd in the league. They have only 22 runs of 10 or more yards. Their rushing success rate sits around 17th. So for all the attention this offense gets, it is not consistently creating big gains on the ground.

And that matters a lot, because Jacksonville is a team that wants to run. They throw on only about 51 percent of their plays, which ranks near the bottom of the league in pass rate. They lean on the run and on play action. Their running backs, Bhayshul Tuten and Chris Rodriguez Jr., are physical. Tuten is a tough runner who can break tackles, and he is also a weapon as a receiver, with 18 catches for 206 yards and three receiving touchdowns. But if the Giants can stop the run on first down, Jacksonville’s whole approach gets thrown off. Second down becomes harder. Third downs get longer. And suddenly the Jaguars are not picking plays from a comfortable spot.

Now, I have to be fair. The Giants’ run defense is not perfect either. They are allowing about four and a half yards per carry, which ranks 26th. And Jacksonville likes to attack the edges in the run game. So this is not a weakness the Giants get for free. They have to earn it. They have to set the edge. They have to make clean tackles. If they force those outside runs back inside and keep Jacksonville behind the chains, this weakness becomes real. My opinion, not a report: this is where Arvell Reese has to be the star. A rookie inside linebacker who just had three sacks and 12 tackles in Philadelphia, and who made history as the youngest player ever with three sacks in a game. If he is flowing to the ball and tackling cleanly on first down, Jacksonville’s run game stalls.

And remember what Harbaugh said after the Washington loss. The biggest problem was letting Washington live in third-and-short. He said you are not going to get much pressure when the opponent is in third-and-one or third-and-two. Stop the run on early downs, and you get the third-and-longs where this pass rush can take over.

Now let’s talk about the Giants’ defense overall, because I do not think it gets enough credit. The Giants rank fifth in yards allowed, third in passing yards allowed, eighth in yards per play allowed, and 11th in points allowed. Yes, there have been frustrating moments, but this defense has kept the Giants in almost every game. And this is a unit that went from one of the worst in the league last season to top five.

But there is a huge reason this is not going to be easy. And his name is Trevor Lawrence.

Lawrence is playing at a level that deserves to be in the MVP conversation, even with Josh Allen and Joe Burrow getting a lot of attention. Jacksonville is scoring close to 30 points per game, fourth in the league. They are fourth in overall offensive EPA, second in passing EPA, and sixth in yards per play. And they have already created 33 completions of 20 yards or more. So even if the Giants slow down the run, they still have to deal with a quarterback who can punish one mistake immediately.

And here is what really concerns me. Jacksonville makes life easy for Lawrence with smart timing. He gets the ball out in about two-point-seven seconds, and the Jaguars have allowed only 14 sacks all season. They do not ask him to sit back there forever waiting for something to open. Those quick throws neutralize pressure. And they use play action a lot, which makes every fake handoff dangerous.

They also mix up their personnel to keep defenses guessing. They use three receivers with one back and one tight end on roughly 58 percent of their snaps. They use two tight ends about a quarter of the time. And they even go to three tight ends on about 12 percent of plays. That variety forces the defense to stay alert, and it puts a lot of pressure on the Giants’ linebackers to stay disciplined.

And look at the weapons around Lawrence. Tuten in the backfield. Rodriguez as the physical option. Parker Washington has become a real problem for defenses. Jakobi Meyers brings experience and reliable route running. Brenton Strange can hurt you at tight end. Brian Thomas Jr. has had limited targets, but he can still make a big play if the defense loses focus. And Travis Hunter has played less than 10 percent of the offensive snaps, but his talent demands attention every time he lines up there.

So how do the Giants slow all that down? Here is what I want to see.

Number one, make Lawrence uncomfortable without giving him easy openings. The Giants have 17 sacks on the season. And I would love to see Abdul Carter turn this into one of those games where the other offense feels him all afternoon. Harbaugh just called Carter a game wrecker and said the sacks are coming. But pressure does not always mean a sack. With a quarterback who gets the ball out in under three seconds, the goal is to make Lawrence move his feet, get hands into the throwing lanes, and force rushed decisions. And after what happened against Washington, with all three Jayden Daniels touchdowns coming against cover zero, I think the Giants have to be smart about when they blitz. Rush four, win up front, and save the big pressure for the right moments. My opinion, not a report.

Number two, take the ball away. The Giants are averaging about 1.3 takeaways per game, which is around the middle of the league. Against Washington, they got two interceptions in the fourth quarter, from Jevon Holland and Ar’Darius Washington. I believe the Giants need at least one extra possession to pull off this upset.

Number three, and this is the big one. Red zone defense.

Here is the problem the Giants cannot ignore. They have been the worst team in the league at preventing touchdowns once opponents get into the red zone. That is painful, because this defense has been so good between the 20s. Jacksonville’s red zone offense ranks 12th, and Liam Coen is not afraid to go for it on fourth down. We saw that aggressive approach against Philadelphia. So the Giants have to tackle cleanly, stay disciplined, and make Jacksonville settle for three instead of seven. A couple of red zone stops could decide this game.

Now let’s talk about the hardest part of this matchup. The Giants’ offense against Jacksonville’s defense.

Even if the Giants’ defense delivers, the offense has a mountain to climb. Jacksonville allows the second-fewest points per game in the league. They rank ninth in yards allowed, and fourth in overall defensive EPA. They have been strong against both the pass and the run. They have given up only 23 completions of 20 yards or more, and only 16 runs of 10 yards or more. And they allow just 3.9 yards per rush. Steady gains are hard to find.

And they are getting help. Jacksonville traded for defensive tackle Jordan Davis and safety Jessie Bates III. Neither played against Tennessee last week, so both could debut against the Giants. Davis adds size and strength inside, and Bates brings experience to the back end. Harbaugh said this week that it does not change much structurally about how Jacksonville plays defense, and that the Giants’ offensive line has already faced both of those players this season, so they have a sense of them. But it is still a lot of added talent.

Here is the other thing that worries me. Jacksonville does not need to blitz constantly to get pressure. Their blitz rate is around 20th in the league, but they generate pressure on roughly 37.5 percent of opposing dropbacks. They average about 1.8 takeaways per game, fourth in the league. They are sixth on third-down defense and fifth at stopping red zone touchdowns. So the Giants’ offensive line cannot afford communication mistakes, and the ball has to be protected. One careless throw, or one drive killed by a penalty, could erase all the work the defense puts in.

But here is the encouraging part. I know the Giants’ offense has been closer to the middle of the league in a lot of major categories. But there are two numbers I really like. The Giants rank inside the top 12 in third-down conversion rate, and inside the top 11 in red zone touchdown rate. That is exactly where this matchup could be won. The Giants do not need a long touchdown every possession. They need manageable third downs, clean decisions, and touchdowns when the chances come. Think back to how the Giants moved the ball against Houston. That kind of patience and execution has to show up again.

And it ties right into what Harbaugh said about J.J. McCarthy this week. He wants McCarthy confident, aggressive and smart. He wants him to complete passes and handle all the blitz checks. Against a defense that gets pressure without blitzing much, the blitz checks are a little less important, but the quick decisions are even more important. McCarthy has to get the ball out on time, protect it, and lean on Cam Skattebo and Isaiah Likely. Skattebo just ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns against Washington, and Likely went for 127 yards and a 66-yard touchdown. Those two have to be the engine on Sunday.

Let me give you four individual matchups I will be watching closely on Sunday. These are my picks, not anything the team has said.

Matchup one: Arvell Reese against Bhayshul Tuten. Tuten is a tackle-breaker who also catches the ball out of the backfield. Reese is a rookie who can cover, blitz and tackle in space. If Reese wins this matchup, Jacksonville loses both its run game and its check-down option.

Matchup two: the Giants’ interior offensive line against Jordan Davis. Davis is a massive body in the middle. If he collapses the pocket, McCarthy has nowhere to step up, and the run game inside dies. The Giants have to double him when they can and get the ball out quickly when they cannot.

Matchup three: Isaiah Likely against Jessie Bates III. Bates is a smart, experienced safety who reads quarterbacks well. Likely has been McCarthy’s favorite target over the middle the last two weeks. If Bates takes Likely away, McCarthy has to find Nabers, Mooney and Malachi Fields on the outside. If Likely still finds space, the Giants’ offense keeps its rhythm.

Matchup four: the Giants’ secondary against Parker Washington and Brian Thomas Jr. on play action. Jacksonville’s explosive plays come off the fake handoff. The safeties cannot bite on the run fake and leave a receiver behind them. After the Washington game, where the Giants gave up a 31-yard touchdown and a 32-yard touchdown, the deep ball is the one thing this defense cannot allow.

Win three of those four, and I like the Giants’ chances a lot.

So when I put the whole matchup together, the winning formula looks simple, even if it will be hard to do. Make Jacksonville play your kind of game. On defense, stop the run on first down, keep Lawrence uncomfortable, and win in the red zone. On offense, protect the quarterback, keep the chains moving, and finish drives with touchdowns. This game is going to be about little moments adding up.

And there is one more advantage I really like for the Giants. Because they played on Thursday night against Washington, they have had a long break to recover, study this matchup and get ready. And they are playing at MetLife, in a national window, where one big defensive stop can wake the whole building up. The Giants have also made special teams moves this week, bringing in Xavier Gipson to return kicks and Tavierre Thomas to help on coverage. Harbaugh said Zvada was dead on in practice from the right hash. Those small details matter in a close game.

Now let me talk about Dominic Zvada, because I keep coming back to him. He missed three field goals in the Washington tie. He went four for four in Philadelphia. Then he missed a 33-yarder against Washington last week. All six of his misses this season have come from the right hash. And now imagine this game coming down to one final drive, with the Giants giving him a chance to win it. I can see it. The whole season comes down to one kick.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. I picked the Giants to beat Washington, and I was wrong. I said Harbaugh would get more aggressive after that game, and he is promising an aggressive game plan. And I said the critics would keep bringing up the soft schedule until the Giants beat a winning team. Well, here is the chance.

So here is my prediction, and you can hold me to it. The Giants hold Jacksonville’s run game under four yards a carry. Reese and Carter combine for at least two sacks. The defense gets one big red zone stop that forces a field goal. McCarthy plays a clean game with no turnovers and leans on Skattebo and Likely. And it comes down to the end. Giants 23, Jaguars 20, with Zvada hitting the game-winning field goal in the final minute. Yes, from the right hash. That is a bold pick, and I know how hard it will be. But a sixth win against this opponent would send a serious message about the NFC East race, and about this team. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time, because I believe it. This team can go all the way. A top-five defense that has kept them in every game. A young quarterback playing winning football. A running back playing with fire. A coach who has promised to be aggressive. And a two-game lead in the division. The Giants are underdogs at home. Good. Let them be underdogs. This team has already shown it can win tough games when people doubt them. I am not counting them out.
