On the final press conference before Philadelphia, a reporter asked about an open roster spot, and Harbaugh did not dodge it. He said, yeah, there’s something to it, there’s a reason for it. He said he was not making any announcements at that press conference, but that the Giants have certain roster moves they need to make with their various situations. Folks, with the trade deadline on Tuesday, and Cam Skattebo’s hamstring still a question mark, that is not a small comment. And on Skattebo, Harbaugh said the Giants are keeping the door open. Let’s keep it open, he said. Let’s see what happens. Malik Nabers is a full go. Charlie Jones is managing a thumb injury. And Harbaugh revealed that starting safety Jevon Holland is the backup punt returner. Today, I am going to break down everything Harbaugh said, what those roster moves could mean, and why his message about discipline might be the most important thing he said all week. Let me start with a fun moment, because Harbaugh opened the press conference in a great mood. The UFC heavyweight champion visited Giants practice, and a bunch of the players were sparring with him a little bit. And Harbaugh said the champ told him he always dreamed of being a tight end. So Harbaugh joked that he had to check out his hands, to see how he looked. You never know, he said. That could be the big story. The Giants’ newest free agent. Everybody laughed. But it tells you something about the mood around this team. Loose, confident, and having fun, even in the biggest week of the season.

Now, let’s get to business, starting with the roster moves. A reporter pointed out that the Giants had opened up a roster spot, and asked if there was anything to it. Harbaugh said yes. There is something to it. There is a reason for it. He said he was not making any announcements at that press conference, but the Giants have certain roster moves they need to make with their various situations. And when another reporter asked if he meant internal roster moves, he did not go any further.

So what could those moves be? I want to be clear, this is my read, not a report. The most obvious one is the backfield. With Skattebo dealing with a hamstring and Tyrone Tracy Jr. on injured reserve, the Giants have Devin Singletary and rookie Gregory Desrosiers on the active roster. Eric Gray and Phil Mafah are on the practice squad. An open roster spot could mean the Giants want to sign one of those backs to the active roster instead of just elevating him for one game. Remember, a practice squad player can only be elevated a limited number of times in a season. If the Giants think they will need extra running back depth for both Philadelphia and Washington on Thursday night, signing a back to the 53 makes a lot of sense.

And think about who the most logical candidate would be. Eric Gray was a fifth-round pick by the Giants in 2023, and he played 34 games for this franchise before being released at the end of August. He knows the offense, he knows the building, and he can help on special teams. Phil Mafah is younger, but he has less experience in this system. If I had to guess, Gray is the first back the Giants would turn to. He has been through NFL game days with this team, and on a short week, familiarity matters a lot.

And the other possibility is the trade deadline. An open roster spot is exactly what a team needs if it is planning to add a player. The Giants have made an offer for Jonathan Taylor, according to Jordan Raanan. Albert Breer called them a serious suitor. And Raanan said he thinks the Giants are more likely to trade for a defensive tackle than a running back. The deadline is Tuesday. If a deal is coming, having a spot ready is smart planning. But I want to be careful. An open roster spot can mean a lot of things, and Harbaugh specifically talked about internal moves. So I would not read this as a trade announcement. I would read it as a team getting ready for whatever comes.

Now, Skattebo. A reporter asked if the Giants are going to keep the door open for him, and Harbaugh said yes. Let’s keep it open. Let’s see what happens. Then he was asked if Skattebo was able to do anything in practice that day, and Harbaugh said no, he didn’t do anything today. The Giants listed him as doubtful on the official injury report.

So let me be realistic. Doubtful usually means a player is not going to play. And a running back with a hamstring who did not practice fully all week is unlikely to suit up in a physical division game on the road. And here is a receipt. A few videos ago, I predicted Skattebo would play on a managed workload. That does not look like it is happening. But I also think resting him is the right call. The Giants play Washington on Thursday night. If Skattebo can be ready for that game, sitting him on Sunday is a smart trade-off. That is my opinion, not a report.

And here is why keeping the door open matters, even if he does not play. By not ruling Skattebo out, the Giants force the Eagles to prepare for him. Philadelphia’s coaches have to game plan for the possibility that Skattebo is in the lineup. That is a small edge, but in a division game, every small edge counts. It is a classic coaching move, and Harbaugh has been doing it for years.

Now, Malik Nabers, and this was the best news of the press conference. A reporter asked about Nabers not practicing on Thursday. Harbaugh said it was just an in-season type thing, nothing serious. Nabers practiced fully on Friday. It is not an issue, Harbaugh said. He’ll be full go for the game. And then Harbaugh had some fun with it. He pointed out that Nabers had already made the announcement himself, after saying on Thursday that he was good for Sunday. Harbaugh joked, I think he said I practiced or something like that. That is what I said yesterday.

That is a relief for every Giants fan. Nabers tore his ACL early last season, so any knee issue gets attention. But Harbaugh made it clear this was nothing. And with the Eagles missing DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, the Giants will have the best receiver on the field on Sunday. One analyst said Nabers has been on a month-long heater. And a film breakdown this week showed McCarthy hitting him for 42 yards against Houston after looking off the safety. If McCarthy has time, Nabers is going to get his chances.

Now, Charlie Jones. He is the Giants’ returner, and he is listed as questionable with what Harbaugh called a thumb injury. Harbaugh said Jones was out there catching passes, just to make sure he could catch, but that he had not talked to him yet to see how he felt. So that one is truly up in the air.

And that led to one of the most interesting questions of the press conference. A reporter pointed out that Jevon Holland is listed as the backup punt returner, and asked how comfortable Harbaugh is using an every-down starter in that role. Harbaugh said it is not ideal. If the Giants have to do it, they will. But it is not ideal. And then he praised Holland. He said Holland is really good, and that he has no worries or qualms about him at all. He said, knowing Holland, he would be thrilled to get the opportunity, the more he can do, and that he would be very capable of doing it very well. But it would not be the ideal.

Let me explain why that matters. A starting safety plays almost every defensive snap. Returning punts adds more hits, more running, and more risk of injury. It also puts the ball in the hands of a player who does not do it every week, in a loud, hostile stadium, where one muffed punt could change the whole game. That is why Harbaugh called it not ideal. But it also tells you how much he trusts Holland. And remember, Holland had a great reputation as a playmaker earlier in his career. If he gets the chance, I would not be surprised to see him make something happen.

And there is a bigger point about special teams in this game. The Eagles ruled out receiver and returner Britain Covey, so they have their own return questions. Field position could be a hidden factor. In a close division game on the road, a long punt return or a pinned punt inside the ten can swing momentum. Harbaugh came up as a special teams coach, and he spent years as the Eagles’ special teams coordinator. He knows how much this phase of the game matters. I expect the Giants to be ready. And remember, the Giants’ kicking game has been shaky, with Dominic Zvada missing two field goals in Houston. In a game that could be decided by a few points, every special teams detail, from the returner to the kicker, has to be clean.

And this connects to the roster moves too. If Charlie Jones cannot go, the Giants might want a returner they trust more than an every-down safety. That could be part of what Harbaugh meant when he talked about various situations. Again, my read, not a report.

Now, let’s get to the part of the press conference that I think was the most important. Discipline. A reporter pointed out that this is a hostile environment and a heated division rivalry, and asked if Harbaugh tells his players to be careful and keep their emotions in check. Harbaugh said yes, that is definitely part of the message. He said there are different ways to try to make sure the players understand that being smart is really important, and playing with discipline is really important. That is a big part of winning football.

And this is huge in Philadelphia. That crowd is loud. The players on the other side are frustrated after six straight losses. There will be pushing after the whistle. There will be trash talk. And the worst thing a road team can do in that building is lose its cool. A personal foul on third down. A taunting penalty after a big play. A late hit on Joe Milton. Those are the kinds of mistakes that give a struggling team life. The Giants have to be the calmer team.

And the numbers back that up. The Giants’ point differential is only plus-2 on the season, 163 points scored and 161 allowed. This team has played a lot of close games. When the margin is that small, penalties matter even more. A 15-yard flag can turn a field goal into a punt, or a stop into a first down. The Giants beat Houston by protecting the football and avoiding big mistakes. They lost to New Orleans after four turnovers. The formula is clear. Clean football wins for this team. Sloppy football loses.

And think about who this applies to. Cam Skattebo plays with what Harbaugh called a fierce joy, and he drew an unnecessary roughness flag in Houston for lowering his helmet. Abdul Carter and Arvell Reese play with a lot of emotion off the edge. Those are the players who make this team special. But in Philadelphia, that emotion has to be under control. Play hard, play physical, and walk away after the whistle.

Then a reporter asked about Harbaugh’s history with the Eagles. Harbaugh spent nearly a decade on the Eagles’ staff, most of it under Andy Reid, before he became the head coach in Baltimore. Does this game mean more to him? Harbaugh said he had not really thought about it. He said it is something kind of cool, but beyond that, no, because it is just a really important game for the team. What overwhelms his thinking, he said, is the opportunity the Giants have against a big rival. He said the team knows what kind of environment it is going to be, and what type of game it is going to be. Everything else is just kind of interesting. Pulling up on the bus, walking into the stadium. He said that the few times he has played there since he left, it was always interesting, because he had been there before, but so long ago. But this is way, way, way more about what is at stake for the Giants.

And then a reporter asked why he keeps calling this such a big game. And Harbaugh’s answer was simple. Division game, man. We’re trying to win the division first. That’s our first goal. He said this game would go a long way toward that. Division games are big. Division games on the road are really big. Those are big wins if you can get them, and they are tough to get. He called it a daunting challenge, and said the team is excited about the opportunity.

And here is why he is right. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. A win today makes the Giants 5 and 2 and 1, unbeaten in three division games, and five losses ahead of Philadelphia in the loss column. And then Washington comes to MetLife on Thursday night. The Giants have not won the NFC East since 2011. This is how you end that drought. One division game at a time.

Then came the most thoughtful question of the press conference. A reporter talked about compartmentalizing at this time of year. Two games in five days. The trade deadline coming. And he asked whether handling all of that is about maturity, experience, or just being a successful group. Harbaugh said it was a great point, and a really interesting proposition. He said he does not know the answer for sure. But he said he feels very confident in his players. He knows they can handle whatever is thrown at them.

And then he explained why. He said each player and each coach knows they have a specific job to do, and that is what they are going to be concerned about. Anything else is not your business. It is nothing you can worry about. You can do what you can do. He said the players can handle the pressure, the distractions, and all the different things being thrown around. And then he said this. Now, how well we do it, that’s up to us. That’s what’s going to determine whether we win or not.

I love that answer. Because it is exactly the right way to handle trade rumors. The players do not control whether the Giants trade for Jonathan Taylor. They do not control what the Colts want. They do not control the injury report. They control how they play on Sunday. And Harbaugh is telling them to focus only on that.

And then the reporter asked if that applies to Harbaugh too, after all his years in the league. And Harbaugh laughed and said, I don’t know. That’s too much for me to think about. Fair enough. That is a coach who is not getting caught up in his own story. He is thinking about Philadelphia, and nothing else.

And it fits everything Harbaugh has preached since the bye. The one-and-oh mindset. The opportunity is not the division, it is this game, and inside this game, it is the next play. A team that can live in the moment like that is a team that can handle a hostile road environment, a short week, and a trade deadline all at once. That is not easy. But this group has handled a lot already this season. A season-ending injury to the starting quarterback in week two. A blowout loss to the Rams. A tie in Washington. A painful loss to the Saints. And every time, they came back.

So let me put it all together. Harbaugh hinted at roster moves, without saying what they are. Skattebo is doubtful, but the door is open. Nabers is a full go. Charlie Jones is questionable with a thumb injury, and Jevon Holland could return punts if needed. And the message to the team is clear. Be smart. Play with discipline. Do your job. And understand what is at stake.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, what roster moves the Giants announce before kickoff, and whether a running back gets elevated or signed. Number two, whether Skattebo is active or inactive. Number three, who returns punts if Charlie Jones cannot go. Number four, the penalty column, because discipline will decide this game. And number five, how the Giants respond to that crowd early.

And here is my prediction, and I am not changing it. The Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. McCarthy throws his first touchdown pass as a Giant. Singletary has a big day. And the Giants win the penalty battle, because Harbaugh has been preaching discipline all week. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, with roster moves coming, a full-go Nabers, and a head coach who is completely locked in on one thing. Winning the division. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
