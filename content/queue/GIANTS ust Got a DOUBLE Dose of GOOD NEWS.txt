The New York Giants just got a double dose of good news, and it came from the last place you would expect it: a postgame podium after a 24-21 loss to Washington. John Harbaugh walked into that room, looked at a group of reporters who wanted panic, and gave them the opposite. He said J.J. McCarthy played very well. He said Cam Skattebo played an excellent game and that the offensive line looked like it did in Houston. He refused to throw his rookie kicker under the bus. And he told the whole building that this team’s spirit is not one of fear, it is one of power and courage. That is the good news. And today I am going to break down every important answer from that press conference, what it tells us about this team, and why I walked away from it more confident, not less.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I’m James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on the notifications, because it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now: after hearing Harbaugh’s answers, do you trust this coaching staff to fix the late-game problems, yes or no? Tell me in the comments.

Let me set the scene quickly for anybody who missed it. Thursday night at MetLife, in the rain, Washington beat the Giants 24-21. The Giants led 21-17 late. Jevon Holland picked off Jayden Daniels at the goal line with 5:27 left. The offense could not close it out. And then Washington went 71 yards in three plays, and Daniels found Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds left. McCarthy’s last deep shot was intercepted. The Giants are 5-3-1, still alone in first place in the NFC East, two games ahead of Washington at 3-5-1 and a game and a half ahead of Dallas at 4-5.

Now, Harbaugh’s opening statement. He said tough loss, credit to the Commanders, they played a very good football game. And then he said this: we played a pretty good football game, too, just not good enough to win. He said the guys are tough, there is a good spirit, it is not one of fear, it is one of power and courage, and we will continue fighting.

Think about the tone there. This is a coach who has been in a lot of rooms like this one. Eighteen seasons in Baltimore, a Super Bowl ring, playoff wins, playoff losses, gut punches in division games. He knows that the first thing a team hears after a loss like this is the head coach’s voice. If he walks in angry, the locker room tightens. If he walks in panicked, the locker room panics. He walked in steady. That is a choice. And it is the right one.

So let’s get into the two doses.

Dose number one: Harbaugh is all in on J.J. McCarthy, and the run game is back.

A reporter asked him directly what he thought of the way McCarthy played. And Harbaugh did not hedge. He said, I thought he played very well. He said there were plays made, he made some throws, he scrambled. And then he said it again. I thought J.J. played very well.

Now, why does that matter so much? Because all week, the conversation around this team has been about the quarterback. Before the game, the stat was that McCarthy was the only quarterback of the 31 with multiple starts since week seven without a turnover, but also the only one without a passing or rushing touchdown. Then Thursday happened. He threw for 293 yards, a career high. He threw his first Giants touchdown pass, a 66-yarder to Isaiah Likely. And then he threw the interception on the last drive. So the outside world wanted to turn the story into whether McCarthy is the guy. And the head coach, in the first press conference after that loss, shut that down. He played very well. Period.

That is a coach protecting his quarterback. And I love it, because it is not blind protection. Harbaugh also listed what went wrong for the team, and I will get to that. But when it came to McCarthy, he made sure the message was clear. This is our quarterback, and he played well.

And then there was the run game. A reporter asked Harbaugh about Skattebo’s two touchdowns, and Harbaugh lit up. He said, I thought it was good, Cam ran really well, Cam ran hard, he played really well, I thought it was an excellent game for him. And then he added the part I want every Giants fan to hear. He said the offensive line was much better, more like it was at Houston. It was good to see.

That is huge. Remember Philadelphia. The Giants ran 31 times for 80 yards in that game. Singletary carried it 21 times for 31 yards. McCarthy was sacked five times before halftime. That offensive line had a rough day against the Eagles front. And on Thursday night, Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns, caught passes for another 44, and McCarthy had time to throw for a career high. Harbaugh himself is telling you the line is trending back toward where it was in Houston. If that is the line you get for the rest of the season, this offense becomes a very different problem for defenses.

And let me add one more thing about Skattebo. Before the game, the worry was ball security in the rain. He had four fumbles on the season coming in, and the one he lost was in the first Washington game. On Thursday, in a downpour, he scored twice and did not put the ball on the ground. Harbaugh called it an excellent game. That is a back who answered the critics.

Dose number two: Harbaugh is not panicking, and that includes his rookie kicker.

This was my favorite exchange of the whole press conference. A reporter brought up another missed kick from Dominic Zvada. He missed a 33-yarder early in this game. And the reporter asked about the confidence level in the kicker, because it has been a topic for a couple of weeks now.

Harbaugh’s answer was perfect. He said, yeah, we’re confident. He said, you can’t, as soon as a guy doesn’t make a play, start firing people left and right, especially your kicker who’s a rookie. He said, I’m confident that he’s going to become a good kicker. And then he got a little edge in his voice. He said, we’re not going to panic halfway through the season with a guy who’s made a bunch of kicks, if that’s what you’re inferring we should be doing. We’re just not going to knee-jerk it like that. He said, he’s going to make kicks, he’s going to make a lot of kicks. I believe in him. I think he’s really good and really talented, and we’re going to give him an opportunity, and it’s up to him to do it.

Here is why I love that. Zvada has been a weapon for this team. He went four for four in Philadelphia, including a 53-yarder, in the game that snapped a 13-game losing streak in Philly. He has six field goals of 50 or more yards this season. That is not a kicker you cut after one miss in a rainstorm. And Harbaugh made it clear, he is the guy. But he also said, it is up to him to do it. That is accountability without panic. That is exactly how you handle a young player.

And the panic theme went beyond the kicker. A reporter asked how difficult it was to miss a chance to separate in the division after the big win in Philadelphia. Harbaugh said the best word was disappointing. He said, we worked hard for this, we put a hard week in, we were prepared, the guys came out with tremendous energy, they played very hard. Plays were made, plays were missed, but it wasn’t good enough to win. And then he said, it’s disappointing, but you can’t let it bring you down. Our spirit’s going to be fine. We’re not going to curl up. We’re going to come out fighting.

And then he gave the most realistic, most honest view of the rest of this season I have heard from any coach this year. He said, it’s going to be dog fights, every one of them. He said, I’d like to say we’re going to win all of them, but it’s probably not very likely, you’re probably not going to put your money down on that. So we got to win enough. He said, this was one that got away, and we got to find a way to get the next one, or the next one after that, and get as many as we need to win the division. And he finished with this: just try to keep it in perspective, that’s very important.

That is the voice of a coach who knows how to win a division. He is not telling his team they have to be perfect. He is telling them they have to be good enough, often enough. And with a two-game lead over Washington and a game-and-a-half lead over Dallas, the Giants do not need perfect. They need to keep stacking wins.

So those are the two doses. Harbaugh is backing McCarthy and loving the run game. And Harbaugh is refusing to panic, about the kicker, about the division, about anything.

Now, I promised you a full breakdown, and that includes the hard answers, because Harbaugh did not dodge them.

The first question he got was about the last two throws, the touchdowns that beat them. And his answer was short. Zero blitzes. Zero blitzes, and those were the routes that ended up getting run. Later in the press conference, he went into a little more detail. He said, we get into a blitz and we get beat on an inside breaking route. You don’t really want that to happen, and it happened. That’s football. It happens.

So now we know. On that final drive, the Giants sent the house. Zero blitz means no deep safety help, everybody coming or everybody in man coverage. And Washington had the right call. We have talked on this channel about how blitz-heavy this defense has become since Brian Burns went down in week three. Since the week four Cardinals game, the Giants have been right near the top of the league in blitz rate. Most of the time it has worked. It created a defense that was fifth in the league in total defense heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. But the cost of a zero blitz is exactly what happened. If they pick it up, somebody is one-on-one with nobody behind him.

Was it the right call? My opinion, not a report: I understand it, but I would have liked to see a mix in that final drive. With a four-point lead and under two minutes, you can make Daniels go the long way. But I also understand Harbaugh’s thinking. This defense picked Daniels off twice in the fourth quarter by being aggressive. You live with the aggression, and sometimes it bites you. The good news is, Harbaugh owned it. He did not blame the players. He said that’s football, it happens, and you keep chasing improvement.

The second set of questions was about not closing the game out on offense after the interceptions. And this is the part of the press conference that tells you the most about the details.

Harbaugh said the key was a run play on third-and-four that got stopped for a loss. He said, it was a TFL, that was the key to the stop. And when asked about a specific McCarthy run, he explained it was a quarterback crack sweep, a play where the quarterback running gives you an extra blocker. He said he thought they had a chance, but that Washington’s rookie linebacker Sonny Styles made a great play and ran through, and he would have to see the tape to see how Styles got through there.

And then there was the fourth-down decision. A reporter asked how seriously he thought about going for it. Harbaugh said he thought about it, but if they had been at four yards or less, he probably would have gone for it. He said losing the two yards on that play was disappointing. So the tackle for loss by Styles changed the math on fourth down. Instead of a manageable fourth-and-short, it was a longer distance, and Harbaugh decided not to roll the dice.

There was also a fourth-down situation deep in their own end, and this is where it got fun. A reporter asked about Jameis Winston being out there and yelling “Who let the dogs out?” at the line of scrimmage. Harbaugh laughed it off a little and said, it’s part of the cadence. He said, teams do jump in those situations sometimes, so that was the idea. He said he was not seriously thinking about going for it there, deep in their own territory. The idea was to draw Washington offside and steal a first down. Washington did not bite. Harbaugh gave them credit and said they played with good discipline.

I love that, by the way. That is a smart coach using every tool. Bring in the veteran quarterback with the big voice, use a hard count, try to steal a first down without risking a turnover deep in your own end. It did not work, but it was the right idea.

And there was one more question about a quarterback sneak with Jameis, where it looked like Skattebo stayed in his stance instead of pushing the pile. Harbaugh said he would have to see the tape. When a reporter asked if the fullback was in the game, Harbaugh said he was not sure, they have about four sneaks they call, and you have got to give him more than five minutes after the game to answer these kinds of questions. I appreciated that. He is not going to make something up at the podium. He is going to watch the film and fix it.

Then he listed what went wrong for the whole team, and I think this is the most important part of the press conference, because it is the to-do list for the next ten days. He said they had a false start in a critical situation. They didn’t finish some plays. They didn’t always tackle. There were some leaky yards in tackling, although there were good tackles, too. A couple of runs got more than they needed to. And they let Washington get into third-and-short too often instead of third-and-long.

And that connects to another question. A reporter asked why the Giants could not get consistent pressure on Daniels. Harbaugh said part of it is Daniels moves around pretty good. And then he went back to the down-and-distance problem. He said you get them in third-and-one or third-and-two, there’s probably not going to be much pressure. On first down especially, it’s mostly play action, and they’re running the ball, so there’s not a lot of pressure on play action unless you blitz.

That is a really good point and I want you to think about it. This pass rush, Arvell Reese and company, is built to feast on third-and-long. In Philadelphia, the Eagles were 5 of 18 on third down, and the Giants had seven sacks. On Thursday, Washington stayed ahead of the chains. They ran the ball, they used play action on early downs, and they lived in third-and-short. That takes the pass rush out of the game. So the fix is not just about the rush. It is about early-down defense, tackling, and stopping the run on first and second down so the pass rush gets its chance on third-and-long. Harbaugh is telling you exactly what he is going to work on.

Now a reporter asked Harbaugh what the response would be from the locker room, because this team has won most of these close games this season. And Harbaugh said, they’re good, they’re down, they’re disappointed, and then he said, I think they felt right to the end that they were going to find a way to win the game. He mentioned Holland’s interception and said, we think we’ve got a shot, and it would have been great to close it out on offense. They were not able to do it, and that is disappointing. And then he said there were a lot of plays that Washington did not make either, that the Giants made. You keep chasing improvement, all the little details, and try to improve as the season goes along.

And here is the receipts part of this video, because I always own them. Before the game, I told you the Giants would win and move to 6-2-1. I was wrong. I also told you McCarthy would finish the game and play well, and the head coach just told you he played very well. So one wrong, one right. I own the miss, and we keep moving.

Now let me put my own spin on all of this, and I will label it clearly. My opinion, not a report: this press conference was the most important thing that happened on Thursday night, more than the final score. Here is why. Every good team in the NFL loses a game like this at some point. What separates the teams that win in January from the teams that fade in December is how the building responds. And the tone at the top sets that response. Harbaugh came in calm, honest about the mistakes, protective of his young players, and clear about the goal. He backed his quarterback. He backed his kicker. He praised his running back and his line. He owned the blitz call. He laid out the to-do list. And he told his team, we’re not going to curl up, we’re going to come out fighting.

That is a coach who has been here before. And that is why I am not worried about the Giants falling apart after this loss.

Let’s talk about what is next. Harbaugh mentioned it himself at the podium. He said they have a really good team coming in next Sunday, a team they can beat, and they just have to get back to work. That is Jacksonville, at MetLife, on Sunday, November 22. That is ten days after the Thursday night game. Ten days to heal up McCarthy’s hip, which he played through after the Zack Baun hit in Philadelphia. Ten days for Skattebo to build on an excellent game. Ten days for Zvada to get his confidence back on the practice field. Ten days for the defense to clean up the tackling and the early-down run defense. And later in the season, Tracy is on IR and lined up to come back around week 13 against San Francisco.

And here is the thing about the division. The Giants still lead the NFC East. They beat Dallas 28-20 in week one. They won in Philadelphia 19-11, the first win there since 2013. They tied Washington and lost to Washington, and yes, Washington now has the edge head-to-head, so the Giants do not want this race to get tight. But they are two games clear of Washington, a game and a half clear of Dallas, and the Eagles are 2-7. The division is still in the Giants’ hands.

So here is my prediction, and you can hold me to it. The Giants come out of this long break and beat Jacksonville at MetLife to get to 6-3-1. The offensive line plays like Houston again. Skattebo has another big day. Zvada makes his kicks. And Harbaugh’s message about power and courage shows up on the field. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it, like I always do.

And I am going to say it one more time, because that press conference made me believe it even more. This team can go all the way. A coach who stays steady in the storm. A young quarterback his coach believes in. A running back who ran hard in the rain. An offensive line trending up. A defense that is top five in the league. And a division lead with plenty of football left. That is a contender. A contender that lost a dog fight, and is about to get back in the next one.
