A-plus. That is the grade Matt Nagy just gave J.J. McCarthy for how fast he is learning the Giants offense. Not a B. Not a solid start. An A-plus, for a quarterback who has been in the building for less than two weeks. And on the same day, the Giants published a depth chart with McCarthy officially listed as the number two quarterback behind Jameis Winston heading into Washington. Folks, the McCarthy story just got a lot more real. But here is the twist, and it is the part I want to spend most of this video on. The most interesting thing about McCarthy right now might not be his arm at all. It might be his legs. Because this 3 and 1 Giants team has a problem hiding behind the wins, and it is the running game. Against Arizona, Cam Skattebo and Najee Harris combined for 88 yards on 27 carries. John Harbaugh admitted it himself, the run game needs to get better. So today, I am going to break down what the A-plus really means and what it does not mean, why a mobile quarterback could unlock this ground game, the play action fix that works no matter who is under center, how the Giants should defend Jayden Daniels now that he is fully practicing, and the injury report news that just turned in the right direction. Let’s start with the grade, because I want to be careful with it. Matt Nagy gave McCarthy an A-plus for picking up the offense. That is encouraging, folks, and considering how recently he arrived, it is a great place to start. But let’s be honest about what it measures. Learning what a play is supposed to look like on the whiteboard and executing it with a 270-pound pass rusher bearing down on you are two very different things. An A-plus in the classroom means McCarthy knows the terminology, he knows the protections, he knows where his answers are when the defense brings pressure. And that is the foundation. You cannot evaluate what a quarterback brings on the field until he knows exactly what he is being asked to do. So the grade does not mean he is ready to take over. It means the first step is done, and done quickly.

And he has a lot of help with that process. Brian Callahan, the former Titans head coach, is now the Giants’ passing game coordinator and quarterbacks coach. Folks, that is a serious resume to have working with a young quarterback every day. Callahan has spent years building quarterback rooms, and between him and Nagy, McCarthy has two experienced offensive minds helping him learn the language, the protections, and the progressions. That is how you speed up a learning curve.

Now, the depth chart. McCarthy is officially listed as the backup behind Winston. Remember when Harbaugh said earlier this week that it was on the table? Well, it is now in writing. Jake Haener, who was promoted to the active roster on Wednesday, slides into the third spot. So check that box on our McCarthy signals list. Signal one, named the backup. Done. Signal two, practice reps and progress. The A-plus grade says that one is moving fast. Signal three, a package of plays. We learned this week that the Giants had one ready against Arizona and never got close to using it. All three signals are flashing, folks. That does not mean a quarterback change is coming Sunday. It means the McCarthy era is no longer a question of if, but when, and Jameis Winston controls the when.

Because let me be very clear about this. I am not ready to push Jameis Winston out of the picture, and neither is this coaching staff. The Giants have won the last two games he started. That matters. Has it all looked great? Absolutely not. There have been turnovers, missed opportunities, and throws he would like to have back. But against Arizona, down 14 to 3, he helped bring this team back with three touchdown passes. You can want more from the quarterback position while still giving him credit for what he has done. Winston is the starter. There has been no announcement that this is now McCarthy’s offense. And I am not going to take a positive update about learning a playbook and turn it into a quarterback change that has not happened.

And about Minnesota, let me say this, because I see a lot of people looking at McCarthy’s numbers there and deciding the story is already written. In Kevin O’Connell’s offense, the quarterback had to recognize the coverage and get the ball out on time, and McCarthy still has room to grow there. That is fair. But young quarterbacks have things to work through. He lost his entire rookie season to a knee injury. He was learning on the fly. And he still won his last four starts last season, including a 16 to 3 win over Green Bay in the finale. A new team brings another adjustment, and none of it guarantees he figures it out here. But I want to see what this staff can do with him. Could a different approach help? Sure. Could Nagy and Callahan make some of his decisions easier while using his movement more? I think so. But he still has to execute. You can change the playbook, move the pocket, and give him cleaner reads, and he still has to make the throw. A change of scenery does not fix everything by itself.

And folks, be careful with quarterback comparisons built on one advanced stat. You will see people throwing around expected points added or completion percentage over expected to prove McCarthy is better than Winston, or the opposite. Those numbers can help, but you have to ask the right questions. Which games? How many throws? Against which defenses? A handful of games does not tell you the whole story. What earns McCarthy more responsibility in this building is consistent good decisions in practice and, eventually, in games. That is the real test.

Now here is where it gets really interesting, and this is the heart of today’s video. McCarthy’s skill set could connect directly with the biggest problem on this offense. The running game. Look at the numbers from Arizona, folks. Cam Skattebo, 18 carries for 58 yards. Najee Harris, 9 carries for 30. That is 27 carries for 88 yards, barely over three yards a pop. A lot of work for not a lot of room. And here is the trend that worries me. In week one against Dallas, this team ran for 165 yards and bullied the Cowboys for sixty minutes. Against Tennessee in the nor’easter, 139 yards on 33 carries, grinding out a win. And now against Arizona, the ground game stalled. The Giants won, but I do not want the result to make us overlook how hard it was to move the ball on the ground.

And I love how Skattebo runs, folks. He fights through contact, he keeps his legs churning, and he gets you something when there is nothing there. But you cannot ask a running back to do that on every single carry and call it a functioning run game. At some point, you have to give him a crease. Let him get moving before somebody meets him in the backfield. Same thing for Harris. These guys need help getting through the line. Part of that is blocking, absolutely. Everybody has an assignment, and those blocks have to get made. But part of it is about the defenders you are blocking. Right now, defenses are crashing hard on the running back, because they do not have to worry about anything else.

And that is where a mobile quarterback changes the math. Winston can move around a little, sure, but defenses do not look at Winston and McCarthy the same way when it comes to keeping the ball and getting outside. If McCarthy gives you a believable threat on the edge, that changes what the defensive end has to account for. Picture it, folks. Zone read. The defensive end sees Skattebo getting the handoff and crashes down hard. McCarthy pulls it and there is open grass outside. Maybe that happens once. But the next time, that defensive end hesitates for half a second, worried about the keeper. And that half a second is a crease for Skattebo. That is the idea. You are making the defense account for everybody instead of letting it attack the same gap without hesitation. And remember, McCarthy ran for 181 yards and four touchdowns last season in Minnesota, and he played in a Michigan offense under Jim Harbaugh that leaned heavily on the run game. That gives the Giants something to build with.

Now, this is the NFL, not Ann Arbor. Defenders will change the look, close space fast, and force him to make decisions. So yes, use the movement, use the bootlegs, give him manageable reads. But he also has to show he can punish a defense through the air when it starts leaning too hard toward the run. A quarterback who can only hurt you with his legs gets figured out. A quarterback who can hurt you both ways is a problem.

And while we are talking about adding juice to the run game, I want to see more of Tyrone Tracy Jr. We have talked about him all season, the fourth running back, the trade candidate, the guy with a career-high in special teams snaps. But give Tracy an opening and he has the speed to turn it into something explosive. Now, that does not mean you ignore ball security or pass protection just to get him touches. He still has to handle those responsibilities. But if the Giants are looking for another way to threaten a defense, and with Najee Harris missing Wednesday’s practice for a personal reason, giving Tracy some real opportunities is worth considering. Skattebo’s toughness, Tracy’s speed, and eventually a quarterback keeper in the mix. That is a run game defenses have to think about.

But here is the fix I like most, because it works no matter who is taking the snaps. Play action. Harbaugh has acknowledged the run game needs to improve, and I agree. But the answer is not just handing it off again and hoping the next carry looks different. If defenders are stepping forward aggressively to stop the run, make them pay for it. Make them think about what is happening behind them. That is where Malik Nabers comes in. Fake the handoff, get a linebacker to hesitate, and suddenly there is room over the middle for an on-time throw. Or if the protection holds and the matchup is there, take a shot down the field. We saw Winston hit deep balls of 40 and 38 yards against Arizona. Imagine those coming off play action with linebackers biting on the fake. Play action gives the defense a reason to be careful instead of letting everybody come downhill without a second thought. And better balance helps everyone. It helps the running backs, it helps the protection, and it makes the passing game cleaner. And if Washington brings extra pressure, the Giants need an answer ready. The quarterback has to know where the ball is going before the snap, not hold it while a good opportunity disappears. That is what I want to see on Sunday. More plays where the offense gives itself a reasonable chance before somebody has to do something special.

Now let’s flip to the other side of the ball, because Washington has its own quarterback update, and it directly affects what the Giants defense has to prepare for. Jayden Daniels is expected back. He injured his left elbow against Dallas in week two and missed the next two games. The key detail, folks, is that it is his non-throwing arm, and he was a full participant in practice on both Wednesday and Thursday. He is expected to wear a brace. So barring a setback, that is the quarterback the Giants are preparing to face. And I would not build a game plan around hoping he is limited. Prepare for everything he can do and adjust if something looks different. The Commanders are 1 and 3, the Giants are 3 and 1, but do not look at those records and assume this is going to be comfortable. It is a division game, on the road, against a desperate team that just got its franchise quarterback back.

And here is what Daniels does that makes defensive coordinators lose sleep. He can turn a perfectly good defensive play into a disaster. You cover the first read, you get some pressure, everything looks fine, and then he slips outside, and suddenly a defender has to decide whether to come forward or stay with his receiver. Now you have a quarterback on the move, receivers adjusting their routes, and defenders trying to make decisions in space. That is where big plays happen. You do not want to give him those opportunities over and over.

So for me, it starts with rush discipline. Get after him, absolutely, but do not run so far past him that you open the door. Make him work from the pocket. Make him go through his reads. And if he takes off, take a good angle and finish the tackle. You do not need anything reckless. You need somebody in the right spot who can actually get him on the ground. And folks, this is exactly why the Jadeveon Clowney signing makes so much sense this week. Clowney has spent his whole career setting the edge and keeping contain, and he knows Dennard Wilson’s system from their time together in Baltimore. Put Clowney on one side, Abdul Carter on the other, and Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne collapsing the middle, and Daniels has nowhere easy to escape. And with Arvell Reese, the fastest linebacker on the field, available as a spy, the Giants have the athletes to mirror him.

Now, we saw the Giants increase their blitzing against Arizona, and I liked it. Dennard Wilson got aggressive, and the Cardinals offense was dealing with chaos. But against Daniels, how you blitz matters. If you send an extra rusher and nobody accounts for the escape route, you have brought more people and somehow made it easier for him to run. That is the concern. I want pressure that makes him uncomfortable without giving him a simple way out. Disguise it, keep contain, and make him beat you from the pocket with a brace on his elbow. That is the recipe.

Now, the injury report, because there is good news here. Malik Nabers and Andrew Thomas both missed Wednesday’s practice, and that had a lot of Giants fans nervous. On Thursday, both returned as limited participants. That is a step in the right direction, folks. Remember what I said on Wednesday, the first report of the week is the least reliable, and Thursday tells you more. Seeing them back on the field is encouraging. But limited practice is still limited practice, so I want to see their final game statuses on Friday before assuming everything is good to go. With Thomas especially, we have learned that Friday is the day that matters.

Chauncey Golston missed practice again with his neck, so there is more uncertainty there. Isaiah Likely and Cam Skattebo were limited again. Being limited does not automatically mean you are sitting out, but it does not guarantee you are playing either. We will see how the week finishes. And Deonte Banks was a full participant again, which is great news after the impact he made last week. You want the reigning NFC Defensive Player of the Week carrying that confidence into another big game.

And folks, all of these updates connect. Everything we talked about today, improving the run game, protecting the quarterback, creating space for the receivers, depends on who is available. Thomas protects the blind side and anchors the run blocking. Nabers is the target that makes play action dangerous. Likely is the tight end who stretches the middle. Skattebo is the engine of the ground game. Having all four on the field would make every single fix we discussed easier. If one or two are limited, the depth gets tested again, and this team has shown it can handle that. But a healthy Giants offense going into Washington would be a scary thought for the Commanders.

So let’s put it all together. McCarthy is learning at an A-plus pace and is officially the backup. Winston is still the starter, and he has earned that by winning. The running game needs answers, and the solutions are on the table, play action, more of Tracy’s speed, and down the road, a quarterback who can threaten the edge. The defense has a plan for Daniels, rush with discipline, keep contain, and let Clowney, Carter, Lawrence, Payne, and Reese close the escape routes. And the injury report is trending in the right direction. Folks, this is a 3 and 1 team that still has room to grow, and I love that. The best teams in January are the ones that keep getting better in October. And this team has more ways to get better than almost anyone in the league. That is why I keep saying it, and I will keep saying it. This team can go all the way.
