Malik Nabers did not practice on Wednesday. Neither did Andrew Thomas. Neither did Najee Harris. And on the same afternoon the first injury report of the week dropped, the Giants put Braxton Berrios on injured reserve, promoted a quarterback off the practice squad, and signed a new receiver. Folks, I thought this was going to be a quiet week. The Giants are 3 and 1, they are sitting on top of the NFC East, and I figured nobody in that building was going to touch anything before a divisional trip to Washington. I was wrong. Wednesday brought real decisions and a few names on that injury report that made me take a long, deep breath. So today, I am going to walk you through every move the Giants made, why I think they made it, what the injury report really means and what it does not mean, and then we are going to celebrate some very good news, because Deonte Banks just made history and picked up a league award. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the move that tells us the most, and that is the quarterback room. On Wednesday, the Giants signed Jake Haener to the active roster from the practice squad. Now, to understand why that matters, remember how we got here. Jackson Dart went down in Los Angeles. The Giants traded with Minnesota for J.J. McCarthy about a week and a half ago. Jameis Winston is leading the offense. And against Arizona, the Giants dressed three quarterbacks, Winston, McCarthy, and Haener, who was elevated from the practice squad for that game. But a practice squad elevation is a temporary thing. You elevate a guy for a game, and he goes back down. Teams only get a limited number of those elevations per player. Now the Giants have made it permanent. And folks, I think that tells you something important. This team is not comfortable being thin at quarterback. They already lost one quarterback to a serious injury this season, and they do not want to be scrambling again on a Sunday in a hostile divisional stadium.

And it fits perfectly with what John Harbaugh said on Monday. He said there is a possibility that J.J. McCarthy will be the backup in week five at Washington, that they will see how it goes during the week, but that it is definitely on the table. So now think about the depth chart. If McCarthy becomes the number two, then Haener becomes the third quarterback, safely on the active roster, no elevation needed. And that is smart roster management. The coaching staff wants to see what it has in McCarthy as fast as possible, because you do not trade for a former top-ten pick to leave him buried. But you also do not want to rush him into a role he is not ready for. Having Haener locked onto the active roster gives them a safety net. If McCarthy looks comfortable by Friday, he is the backup, and Haener is the emergency guy. If McCarthy still needs time, Haener can slide into the number two role without any roster gymnastics. Either way, the Giants are covered. And credit to Jake Haener, folks. This man has been elevated, waived, brought back, elevated again, and every single time, he has shown up ready. A spot on the active roster is a reward he has earned.

Now let’s talk about the receiver room, because this one is more disappointing. The Giants placed Braxton Berrios on injured reserve because of the foot injury he suffered against Arizona. And the timeline here is interesting. On Monday afternoon, Harbaugh said Berrios was not on IR at that point and that they would probably know more by the end of the day or the next day. He described it as an ankle-foot issue, Berrios got twisted up on one of the punt returns. So on Monday, they were still hoping. By Wednesday, the decision was made. And when a team puts a player on injured reserve, it is telling you it does not expect him back anytime soon. Under the current rules, he has to miss at least four games. That is a real loss, folks, because Berrios was part of what they were doing, both as a receiver and as a return man. He was one of three Giants who left Sunday’s game and did not return, and he is the one who ended up on IR.

To fill that gap, the Giants signed Dante Fleming to the practice squad. Now, I will be honest with you, this is the name most people are going to scroll right past. On its own, it does not move the needle. But when you lose a receiver, you want a body in the building. You want depth. You want somebody who has been learning the playbook and is ready to step in if something else goes wrong. And as you are about to see, there is a very good reason the Giants might want that extra receiver around this week. And on the return side, keep an eye on Charlie Jones, the return specialist the Giants signed earlier this season, a guy who took a kick back for a touchdown in each of his first three NFL seasons. With Berrios out, this could be Jones’ moment on the best special teams unit in football.

Now, the injury report. The Giants released their first one of the week on Wednesday, and I am going to go through every name, because I want you to have the full picture and not just a scary headline. Five players did not participate. Defensive lineman Chauncey Golston, with a neck injury. Running back Najee Harris, listed as not injury related, personal. Malik Nabers, with a knee. Safety Jason Pinnock, with a concussion. And left tackle Andrew Thomas, with a groin.

Folks, when I saw Malik Nabers and Andrew Thomas on that same list, I took a long breath. One is your number one receiver, the man who just had 112 yards and a touchdown against Arizona, his first 100-yard game since before his ACL injury. The other is your left tackle, the guy who protects Jameis Winston’s blind side. Two of the most important pieces on this offense, and neither one of them practiced.

But now let me give you the calm voice, because this is exactly where fan bases overreact. It is Wednesday. The first injury report of the week is the least reliable one. A player can be held out on Wednesday and still play on Sunday, and it happens all the time, especially with veterans and especially with soreness after a physical game. Let’s take them one at a time. Andrew Thomas has been managed carefully all season. We talked about this before the Arizona game, when Thomas had a pattern of veteran rest days early in the week, and the lesson was simple, Friday is the only day that matters with Thomas. A groin listing on a Wednesday for a veteran left tackle is something to watch, not something to panic about. If he is limited on Thursday and full on Friday, he is playing.

Nabers is the one I am going to watch most closely, and I will be honest about why. A knee listing for a player coming back from a torn ACL will always make Giants fans nervous. And he took some hard hits against Arizona, including that contested catch where he went down hard and stayed down for a moment before returning. But here is what we do not know. We do not know if it is the same knee. We do not know if it is soreness or something structural. And I am not going to guess, because guessing about a player’s knee is how rumors start. What I will say is this. If Nabers is limited on Thursday, that is a good sign. If he misses Thursday too, then we start to have a real conversation. Until then, we wait.

And Najee Harris being out for a personal reason is just that, personal. We wish him and his family the best, and we do not speculate. The football side of it is simple. If Harris misses time, the Giants have the deepest running back room in the league. Remember when people were asking why the Giants were carrying four running backs? This is why. Tyrone Tracy Jr. and Devin Singletary are both experienced, both capable, and both ready. Tracy has been a trade candidate all fall, and a week like this is a reminder of why you do not give away depth in October.

Now, when you pair the Nabers listing with Berrios going on IR, my attention goes straight to the receiver room. If Nabers is dealing with a knee and Berrios is out, then suddenly the depth there is a real question. That is why the Dante Fleming signing does not feel random to me. It feels like the Giants protecting themselves. And it puts a spotlight on Malachi Fields, the rookie third-round pick who caught his first career touchdown against Arizona. If Nabers is limited on Sunday, Fields could be asked to do a lot more, and so could the tight ends.

Which brings me to the limited group, where you can find some hope and some concern at the same time. Isaiah Likely was limited with a knee and groin. Tight end Chris Manhertz was limited with a shoulder. And Cam Skattebo was limited with a shoulder. Limited means they were getting work in, and that is better than sitting out. But folks, that is a lot of names, and look at who they are. Tight ends and a running back. The guys who help this offense run the way it wants to run. Manhertz is a blocking tight end in the heavy personnel packages this team loves. Likely is the receiving tight end who caught two touchdowns in the opener. And Skattebo is the heartbeat of the run game. If even one of them is less than full strength, it changes the game plan. If Harris is out and Skattebo is limited, the Giants might lean on Tracy more than we have seen all year. If Likely is limited, Theo Johnson might get more snaps. This is where depth wins games.

And do not forget the defensive side of that report. Chauncey Golston missing practice with a neck injury thins out the defensive line rotation, but this is a spot where the Giants have options. Darius Alexander has been creating pressure on the inside, Jihad Ward brings veteran snaps off the edge, and with Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne anchoring the middle, the rotation can absorb one missing piece. Jason Pinnock’s concussion matters more for the back end, because he had earned real safety snaps this season. Concussions follow the league protocol, so there is no rushing that one, and the Giants will lean on Jevon Holland and Tyler Nubin, with the rest of the secondary ready to rotate. And if Jayden Daniels is back and scrambling, having fresh, disciplined safeties is going to be important.

On the positive side, Deonte Banks was a full participant with his calf, and so was Brian Hudson with his ankle. I would rather see a guy full than limited, and limited than out, and those two are full. Good news.

And let’s talk about Hudson, because the center position is one of the most intriguing situations on this roster. Before the Arizona game, the same five offensive linemen had played every single snap together this season. Then Hudson made his first career start at center in place of John Michael Schmitz. Schmitz had missed practice because his wife had their baby, so congratulations again to that family, and he did not have a lot of rest. The staff felt Hudson had been doing so well, had the reps, and had made the calls, so the timing made sense. Harbaugh said it paid off and that it is not a long-term thing, they just have two centers they really like. When asked what it means for this week, he said they had not had the personnel meeting yet, that Hudson played really well and maybe it creates some options, but Schmitz was playing really well too. So the door is open both ways. And here is a thought, folks. If Andrew Thomas cannot go on Sunday, the coaches might want the most experienced line possible, and that could tip the scale toward Schmitz returning at center to keep the communication steady. On the other hand, if Hudson is playing well, why change? Either way, it is a decision worth watching. A line that had not changed all season finally getting a shakeup is a notable moment.

Now let’s get to the good news, because there is plenty of it. Deonte Banks has been named NFC Defensive Player of the Week. And folks, he earned every single bit of it. On fourth and goal, with the Giants clinging to a six-point lead, he intercepted Jacoby Brissett and returned it 98 yards for a touchdown as time expired. That is how the Giants beat Arizona 36 to 24.

And now the history. That 98-yard return is the second-longest touchdown with no time left on the clock since the 1970 merger. The only one longer was a 100-yard interception return by Brock Marion, but his team was already up by 20 points at the time. Banks did it with the game on the line. That makes it a completely different kind of play. And the stat sheet backs it up beyond the pick six. Banks had four passes defensed in that game, the most of any player in week four, and tied for the most by anyone in a single game this season. He is also the first player since Ja’Quan McMillian in 2024 to have at least four passes defensed and an interception returned for a touchdown in the same game.

This is Banks’ first time winning one of the league’s weekly awards. And he is the first Giant to win NFC Defensive Player of the Week since Bobby Okereke in the 2025 season finale, and before that, you have to go back to Kayvon Thibodeaux in week fifteen of 2022. And remember, folks, Banks had missed the previous two games with a calf injury. He came back, got flagged for pass interference in the final minute, and then turned around and made the defining play of his career on the very same drive. That is not just a great play. That is a statement about who this young man is becoming.

And let me say something about Banks’ journey, because I think it is one of the best stories on this team. There was a stretch where Banks looked like a player who had lost his confidence. The penalties, the coverage busts, the criticism. People wondered if he would ever live up to being a first-round pick. And then a new coaching staff came in, Dennard Wilson, John Harbaugh, and something changed. They rebuilt his belief. They put him in positions to succeed. And now, with Joey Porter Jr. joining this secondary and Jevon Holland patrolling the back end, Banks gets to play with even more confidence. A Defensive Player of the Week award and a spot in the NFL record books, folks, I think it is fair to say Deonte Banks is back.

And let’s give some love to Arvell Reese too, because he keeps looking the part. He was nominated again for Pepsi NFL Rookie of the Week, after nine solo tackles, three passes defensed, a tackle for loss, and his first career interception against Arizona. He leads the entire league, not just rookies, with 26 solo tackles through four weeks. He is just the fifth Giants rookie with at least three passes defensed and an interception in a game, and the only one since 1994 to add nine tackles to that. His competition includes Jacob Rodriguez of Miami and Carnell Tate of Tennessee, his former Ohio State teammate. Fans can vote on Instagram or X through Thursday at 11 a.m. Eastern. Now let’s look at Sunday. The Giants are 3 and 1 and sitting on top of the NFC East. The Eagles are 2 and 2 after losing 24 to 20 to the Rams. The Cowboys, the team the Giants beat in the opener, are 2 and 2 after beating the Texans 34 to 30. So the Giants have a little cushion. And they face a Washington team that is 1 and 3 after a rough loss to the Colts in London, but I would not take them lightly. Dan Quinn said the goal is for Jayden Daniels to have a full week of practice and start against the Giants after missing two games with a left elbow injury, and he will be wearing a brace on that elbow. Terry McLaurin was held out last week with a hamstring. And Marcus Mariota, who was starting in place of Daniels, hurt his knee in London and was replaced by a rookie. So Washington has its own problems. But if Daniels is back, that changes how the Giants have to approach this game.

So here is where I land, folks. This was a busy Wednesday. The Giants promoted Jake Haener to protect the quarterback room. They put Braxton Berrios on IR and signed Dante Fleming for depth. They are deciding between Brian Hudson and John Michael Schmitz at center. And they have a handful of important names on the injury report, with Nabers, Thomas, and Harris not practicing. But here is the thing I want you to remember. This team has spent the entire season proving it can win with players missing. They won without Dart. They won without Burns. They won with a rookie kicker scoring every point. The next man up mentality is not a slogan in this building, it is the identity. So whether Nabers and Thomas are good to go on Sunday or not, I trust this roster, I trust this depth, and I trust this coaching staff to have a plan. Thursday and Friday will tell us a lot more.
