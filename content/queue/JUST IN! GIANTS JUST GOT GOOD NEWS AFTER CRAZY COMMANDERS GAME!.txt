The Giants just got good news after that crazy game in Washington, and it comes from three directions at once. First, a former Pro Bowl pass rusher with 71 and a half career sacks, a man who already played for John Harbaugh in Baltimore, is coming in for a workout. Yannick Ngakoue. Second, while the Giants were surviving the wildest eight seconds of their season, the Philadelphia Eagles were losing theirs, and now the Giants sit alone in first place in the NFC East with two fewer losses than anybody else in the division. And third, the film from Washington revealed something about this offense that might unlock the rest of the season, if the coaching staff is willing to listen. Folks, this was a tie that felt like a loss for most of the afternoon, then felt like a win, then felt like a loss again, and ended as the strangest result I have ever seen. My hair is a lot grayer than it was before kickoff. But when the dust settled, the news coming out of it is good. Let’s get into it.

Let me be honest with you about how that afternoon felt, because I think a lot of you went through the same thing. Somewhere in the middle of the second half, I had this video completely written in my head, and it was a video about moving on from Jameis Winston. He had just fumbled in a nasty, wet game where one turnover could decide everything, and Washington had a short field. I remember thinking, okay, this is it, it is time for J.J. McCarthy. The Giants were trailing by a lot heading into the fourth quarter. It felt like they were going to get run off the field. And then Winston finished with 326 yards, three touchdowns, and no interceptions, and I had to throw the whole thing out. Then it turned and it felt like the Giants had it in the bag. Then Cam Skattebo fumbled in overtime, and suddenly I was back to expecting a loss. And then somehow, we got the craziest tie I have ever seen. That is the kind of afternoon it was.

And I owe you a quick correction, because this channel always keeps the receipts honest. In my last video, I said Winston protected the football for the entire game. That was not fully accurate. Zero interceptions, yes, and that is still a huge step for him. But he did lose a fumble in the second half that gave Washington a short field. The Giants lost two fumbles in that game, one from Winston and one from Skattebo in overtime. So the turnover story is better than it was against Arizona, but it is not finished. Ball security, for everybody, is the number one fix this week.

So let’s start with the first piece of good news, because it might be the biggest one long term. The Giants are bringing in Yannick Ngakoue for a workout. A former Pro Bowl defensive end with 71 and a half career sacks. And here is the connection you need to know, folks. He spent time with John Harbaugh in Baltimore. He knows the system. He knows the expectations. Just like Jadeveon Clowney and Jihad Ward before him, he is a player Harbaugh already trusts. And with Brian Burns out for the year, the Giants need every edge rusher they can find.

Now, his story is interesting, and I want to tell it honestly. Ngakoue broke his ankle in 2023 and, by his own account, tried to play in 2024 before he was fully recovered. That season, he had just one and a half sacks combined for the Ravens and the Patriots, and he was cut by both. So he took an entire year off to heal properly. And now he says he is finally healthy for the first time since 2023. Look at what he did before the injury. Ten sacks in 2021. Nine and a half in 2022. Even in 2023, with the injury, he had four for a Bears team that needed help on the edge. He is 31 years old, and he is not an every-down player at this stage of his career. But think about the role, folks. A third-down pass rusher, coming off the edge while Abdul Carter is lined up on the other side drawing all the attention. That is a matchup Ngakoue could win a lot. If he is healthy, this could be a very smart pickup. It is a workout for now, not a signing, so we will see if the Giants follow through. But the fact that they are looking tells you this front office is not done.

And folks, notice the pattern here, because it tells you exactly how this front office thinks. Patrick Ricard, the fullback, came from Baltimore. Isaiah Likely came from Baltimore on a three-year deal. Jadeveon Clowney played for Harbaugh in Baltimore. Jihad Ward has ties to Harbaugh. And now Ngakoue, another former Raven, is walking through the door. Some people might call that a Baltimore pipeline, and they would be right. But there is a reason for it. When a first-year head coach is installing a new culture, the fastest way to spread it is through players who already speak the language. They know what Harbaugh expects in meetings, in practice, and on game day. They help teach the young guys. Likely just made franchise history in his fifth game as a Giant. Clowney gives the edge a veteran voice. If Ngakoue signs, he joins a room that already has people who know exactly who he is and what he can do. That makes the transition a lot faster, and in the middle of a division race, fast matters.

And that leads me to the second piece of good news, the one that makes the Ngakoue workout even more interesting. Abdul Carter is becoming a star. He is now doing it for multiple weeks in a row without Brian Burns next to him. The week before Washington, against Arizona, he was living in the backfield, with pressure after pressure coming in under two and a half seconds. Folks, that number is almost absurd, because the average time for a quarterback to get the ball out is longer than that. Pressures that fast mean he is beating his man off the snap. And against Washington, he followed it up with a sack and a forced fumble. The sack was a thing of beauty. He worked straight through the left tackle, and then when Jayden Daniels took off, which most edge rushers in the league cannot handle because Daniels is so fast, Carter caught him from behind and brought him down.

And honestly, he should be doing even better. There was one play where you can see him coming off the edge, and the tackle has him in what looks like a headlock, a straight-up hold around the neck, and nothing gets called. It feels like that happens two or three times a game. But even with that, Carter looks exactly like the player the Giants drafted him to be. And here is why that matters so much. A defense built around two dominant rushers is in trouble the moment one of them gets hurt, and that is exactly what happened when Burns went down. Carter has responded the way you hope a young star would, by making the extra attention look manageable. Now opposing coaches have to decide. Do you chip him with a back? Slide the protection to him? Or accept a one-on-one with a player who keeps winning them? Whatever they choose, somebody else gets a better matchup. And that is exactly why the Ngakoue workout matters. Carter can carry a lot, but he cannot rush from both sides at once.

And I want to give some credit to Kayvon Thibodeaux, because a lot of people will look at the box score, see one tackle, and say he had a quiet game. He did not. He had three pressures. And the biggest play he made might have been on that wild final sequence from Washington. Go back and watch it. Daniels is looking to throw to the tight end, and Thibodeaux completely takes that away. Then Daniels decides he might be able to run for it, and Thibodeaux has the awareness to know the zone behind him. He knows he has help on the tight end, so he stays on Daniels. That forces Daniels back up the middle, where he ends up sacked, and the game is over. Honestly, I am still stunned Daniels did not just throw it out of bounds. But Thibodeaux’s presence took away the sideline too. That is a veteran play, folks. It shows the versatility of this whole group. Thibodeaux, Carter, and Burns when he was healthy, all three can rush the passer, and all three are good enough athletes to drop into a zone.

Now, the third piece of good news, and it is a big one. The standings. Here is something wild. With eight seconds left in both games, it looked like Washington was going to beat the Giants and the Eagles were going to win their game. And then the two craziest eight-second stretches of the entire NFL Sunday happened. The Eagles took a loss, and the Giants got a tie. So now, the Giants are 3 and 1 and 1. The Eagles are 2 and 3. The Cowboys are 2 and 3. Washington is 1 and 3 and 1. The Giants have two fewer losses than anybody else in the division. They are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. A tie that easily could have been a defeat feels like a gift. And folks, when your division rivals are losing in the final seconds while you are surviving in the final seconds, that is the kind of thing that happens to teams that end up winning the division.

Now let me put the good news on pause, because there is a bigger conversation this game started, and it is about the offense. Dan Schneider tweeted something that summed up what I have been feeling for weeks. His point was that Winston operates this offense ten times better from the shotgun and in tempo, and that if Winston is the guy moving forward, the Giants should become a shotgun team. Forget what they wanted this offense to look like with Jackson Dart. The line cannot run block consistently right now anyway. So lean into the spread, and let Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely win their matchups, which they do most of the time.

And I completely agree. Here is why. When Winston is under center running the bootlegs, the play action, and the run-pass option concepts the staff built for Jackson Dart, it does not look natural. He does not have the athleticism to make that style hum. But every time the offense breaks down and the Giants basically say, look, we just have to score, and Winston gets to sit back in the shotgun, find his rhythm, and run the quick no-huddle stuff, it has been close to unstoppable. You saw it again in Washington. That is not a bad defense, it is a solid unit, and Winston torched it once the offense spread out.

And there is a ripple effect people are missing. When the Giants run that slow, power offense from under center, the safeties creep up and stack the box, and the Giants cannot run the football. In the first half in Washington, the running backs had 21 yards on eleven carries. But once Winston was allowed to throw from the shotgun, those safeties had to back up, and Skattebo started getting real running lanes in the fourth quarter and overtime. The passing game was feeding the run game instead of fighting it.

Now, let me give you the other side, because I want to be fair. There is a real argument that the best thing for this team, with the defense it has, is to run a slow, pro-style offense. Burn the clock, keep opposing offenses off the field, keep your pass rushers fresh, build a lead, and then let Carter and company pin their ears back in the fourth quarter. I can see that being the Giants’ best route. But it only works if the quarterback can run that offense effectively. And Winston, from what I have seen, cannot.

So that is the decision on the coaching staff’s desk this week. John Harbaugh and Matt Nagy have to answer one honest question. Do you truly believe J.J. McCarthy can do what Jackson Dart was supposed to do in that system? If the answer is yes, make the switch when he is ready and run the offense the way it was designed. But if you think McCarthy will be pretty good, maybe a little better than Winston in that system but still not great, then stick with Winston and change the offense to fit him. There is no sense in making a quarterback run something he does not have the athleticism for. Great coaches adjust to the personnel in front of them. And folks, Harbaugh has done that his whole career. He won a Super Bowl with Joe Flacco and then rebuilt his entire offense around Lamar Jackson. He knows how to adapt.

Think about how that looks inside the building. A coaching staff falls in love with a plan in the offseason. Plans like that take months to build. Walking away from it in October feels like admitting something. But the evidence is sitting right there on film. Every time the offense goes to tempo and shotgun, it moves. Every time it goes back under center, it labors. If I am Harbaugh, I am not asking which offense I want. I am asking which one gives my defense the best chance to win games. And the honest answer might be the one nobody planned on. That is also where Winston gains leverage. He does not need to be a star. He needs to be the right fit for the version of the offense that works. And Washington showed he can be.

Let’s talk about the people who made that game worth watching on offense. Malik Nabers had 11 catches, 121 yards, and a touchdown. Isaiah Likely had 11 catches for 94 yards and two touchdowns. Those two together look like the one-two punch this franchise has wanted for years, and I think they are going to be a problem for defenses the rest of this season and well beyond it. And I loved the smaller contributions too. Malachi Fields made a big play here and there. Tyrone Tracy Jr. made a nice play in the passing game. And Theo Johnson, knock on wood because I do not want to jinx it, made a few important catches for the second straight game. When the ball spreads around like that, defenses cannot just double one guy.

Now, the kicker. Dominic Zvada missed three field goals in a game that went to overtime, and I know that hurts. It is the first thing people point to. But I am giving him a pass, and here is why. This was a nasty, wet, sloppy game. By the end, the natural grass was torn up with muddy spots everywhere. Go outside in the rain and the mud and try to plant your foot for a powerful kick, and see how that goes. If a kicker cannot plant, he has nothing. And there were reports before the game that he had struggled in rain, mud, and wind during the preseason. If that is accurate, then sending him out for those 50-plus-yard attempts in that weather put him in a bad position. This is the same undrafted rookie who scored every Giants point against Tennessee and became the first Giants rookie with three 50-yard field goals in a season. One sloppy afternoon in the mud does not change who he is. Judge the kicker in the dry. Judge the coaching in the mud. Maybe the right call in those conditions is punting, or going for it on fourth down, instead of 50-yard tries on a chewed-up field. That is a conversation for the film room.

And the same fairness goes for Skattebo. Yes, he has a fumble issue, and yes, he has to clean it up. If he does not fumble in overtime, the Giants probably win without needing any luck. But when the big bad play comes at the very end, it is easy to pin everything on one person, and I think that is unfair. If Winston does not fumble and hand Washington a short field, the Giants probably win. If Tyler Nubin does not miss that tackle on Washington’s opening touchdown run, the Giants probably win. A lot of different people cost the Giants points in that game. It was not one man. And the good news is that every one of those problems is fixable.

So here is why all of this matters beyond one game. The Giants came out of Sunday still alone in first place with two fewer losses than anybody in the NFC East. The division race is still wide open, and a tie that could have been a defeat feels like a gift. But gifts only get you so far. The Giants have to be honest about what makes them dangerous. Their receivers are elite. Their young pass rusher is turning into a star. Their quarterback can light up a good defense when the offense is built for him. And their biggest problems, the fumbles, the kicking in bad conditions, the missed tackles in the secondary, are all fixable. The scheme is the real question, and it is sitting right on the coaching staff’s desk.

And with the New Orleans Saints coming to MetLife, where the Giants have not lost all season, this is the perfect week to make the right decision. Spread it out. Let Winston sling it from the shotgun. Feed Nabers and Likely. Let Carter hunt. Maybe add Ngakoue to the rotation. Folks, if this staff adapts the way great staffs do, I still believe what I have been saying for weeks. This team can go all the way this season.
