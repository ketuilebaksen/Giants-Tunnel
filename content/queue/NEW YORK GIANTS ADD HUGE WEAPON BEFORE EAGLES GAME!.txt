The Giants just found the weapon they have been missing all season, and they did not have to make a single trade to get it. In Houston, rookie receiver Malachi Fields caught four passes for 83 yards, including a 47-yard catch and run, and Pro Football Focus gave him a game grade of 88.9. Folks, for weeks this channel has been saying the same thing. The Giants’ passing game was too concentrated on Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely, and against good secondaries, somebody else had to step up. On Sunday, against one of the better secondaries in the league, somebody did. And Fields is not the only smart bet that paid off. A quarterback who cost a fifth-round pick. A right tackle on a mid-level contract who shut down the NFL’s sack leader. A tight end who is putting up numbers next to the best in football. Today, I am going to break down all four of those bets, what they say about how this roster was built, and what it means for the Eagles game after the bye. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with a receipt, because this one feels good. A couple of weeks ago, I told you that Malachi Fields had exactly two catches in each of his first five games, and that his role had not grown the way it needed to. I told you the Giants’ passing game was dangerously concentrated, with Nabers and Likely combining for 63.5 percent of the targets against Washington. And I told you that against teams with strong secondaries, like Houston and Philadelphia, a third option would stop being a luxury and become a necessity. I asked whether Fields needed more opportunities or needed to do more to earn them, and I said both could be true. Well, in Houston, he got the opportunity, and he earned it.

Four catches. Eighty-three yards. A 47-yard catch and run that flipped the field. And an 88.9 grade from PFF, which tells you the impact went beyond the catches. His blocking deserves real attention too. Receivers who block well help the offense on plays where they never touch the ball, and the Giants clearly benefited from better blocking on the perimeter in this game. Think about those two runs at the end, Skattebo’s 42-yarder in overtime and Singletary’s 30-yard game-winning touchdown. Big runs like that do not happen without receivers sealing the edge downfield. That is how a young receiver earns a bigger role. Not just with highlights, but with the dirty work.

And a quick update on those two runs, because in my last video I told you the final two plays were both runs of 30-plus yards. Now we have the exact numbers. Skattebo’s run in overtime went for 42 yards. Singletary’s game-winning touchdown went for 30. So, the Giants went from zero runs of 30-plus yards in 461 plays, to back-to-back runs of 42 and 30 yards to end a road game in overtime. That is the kind of finish that changes how a team sees itself.

Now, I want to keep expectations where they belong. Fields was a third-round pick, and he is still learning what works against NFL defenders. I am not expecting a finished product. I am looking for steady progress that gives his quarterback reasons to keep coming back to him. And what I liked most in Houston was the connection between Fields and J.J. McCarthy. Timing takes repetition. A young receiver has to prove to his quarterback that he will be exactly where the play needs him to be. One big afternoon gives them something to build on, and now they get two full weeks of practice during the bye to build on it.

And Giants fans, we have seen this story before. Victor Cruz went undrafted. Mario Manningham was a third-round pick. Both of them became huge parts of a Super Bowl team. And here is a fun one. Cruz’s breakout game came in 2011, in Philadelphia, against the Eagles. Three catches, 110 yards, two touchdowns. That is where a legend started. I am not saying Fields is Victor Cruz. But his next game is in Philadelphia, too. Wouldn’t that be something?

Here is why Fields matters so much right now. He does not need to replace what Nabers brings. He needs to become someone defenses cannot ignore while they focus on the bigger name on the other side. Because the Eagles will have studied the Houston tape. They will see Fields winning downfield. They will see Likely as a featured target. They will see McCarthy’s willingness to push the ball. And they will adjust. Some defenses will bracket Nabers and dare the young receivers to beat them. Others will sit on the tight end. How Fields responds to that attention will tell us whether Houston was a breakout or a one-week spike.

Now, the second bet, and it is the one that got everybody’s attention. J.J. McCarthy. The Giants gave Minnesota a 2027 fifth-round pick, and in return, they got a quarterback who went tenth overall in 2024. When you say that out loud, it sounds like a typo. And in Houston, in his first start for his new team, McCarthy went 20 of 28 for 252 yards. He pushed the ball downfield. He gave his receivers chances to make plays. He looked comfortable in the pocket. Throwing downfield with confidence, on the road, in your first start for a new team, against a good defense, is not something every young quarterback can do.

And I want to remember why the Giants made that move. Jackson Dart suffered a season-ending knee injury. New York needed help, and it got it without giving up premium draft capital. That does not mean Dart has disappeared from the plans. He is still the quarterback this franchise traded back into the first round to draft. But McCarthy gave the Giants a real option for this season, at a price that did not hurt the future.

There is a comparison that keeps coming up, and it is Bo Nix. Both were first-round picks in 2024. Both can move and threaten a defense outside the pocket. I would give McCarthy the edge in arm strength. But having that ability and using it consistently are two different things. Nix has a much bigger body of NFL work, and he has Sean Payton building around him. We have seen how much the structure around a quarterback shapes his development. Protection, play calling, and receivers all matter. So, I would not put them on equal footing based on one game. The questions for McCarthy are the ones that come next. How does he respond when defenses adjust? Can he stay patient when the deep shot is not there? Can he keep an ordinary drive alive instead of forcing something? Those answers will tell us far more than one afternoon in Houston.

But I will say this. The way McCarthy used Fields told me something. That 47-yard catch and run did not come from a quarterback checking the ball down out of fear. It came from a quarterback who trusted a rookie receiver in a big moment, on the road, and gave him a chance to make a play. Young quarterbacks often lock onto their best receiver and ignore everyone else. McCarthy spread the ball around. That is a sign of a quarterback who is reading the field, not just the first option.

And from Minnesota’s side, the question is whether the Vikings eventually wish they had waited. I am not calling it a disaster for them today. But if McCarthy develops into a reliable starter, a fifth-round pick is going to look like a very small return. For the Giants, the logic was simple. They bought a real chance at quarterback upside for a manageable price.

Now, the third bet, and this is the one that does not get enough credit. Jermaine Eluemunor. Quarterbacks get the attention, but a dependable right tackle changes how comfortable an entire offense feels. You do not need every lineman to be a star. You need someone who can handle hard assignments without the coaching staff constantly rearranging the protection to help him. And against Houston, Eluemunor came back after missing the previous game with a calf injury, and his assignment could not have been harder. Will Anderson Jr. came into that game leading the NFL with 7.5 sacks. And he finished the game without a sack and without a single quarterback hit. Folks, that is huge. It does not mean every snap was perfect. It means a game-wrecking opponent did not wreck the game.

And look at Eluemunor’s contract in that context. His deal averages about 13 million dollars a year. Compare that to the top of the right tackle market, where a player like Chicago’s Darnell Wright is on an extension averaging around 29 million a year. I am not saying Eluemunor is as good as the best right tackles in football. I am saying the Giants do not have to pay top-of-the-market prices to get solid, competitive play at that spot. That is smart roster building. Every dollar you do not spend at right tackle is a dollar you can use somewhere else, whether that is re-signing your own young players or adding a key piece, like a veteran safety at the trade deadline.

The one caveat is health. Eluemunor was in a walking boot after the game because of that calf injury, the same one that had already cost him a game. So, the bye week matters a lot for him. A healthy Eluemunor is a real asset. A tackle playing through a lingering calf problem against elite edge rushers is a risk. And with the Eagles next, the Giants need him back at full strength.

And think about the whole line heading into Philadelphia. Andrew Thomas has been working through a groin injury all season. Eluemunor has the calf. But the depth is better than it was a month ago. Rookie J.C. Davis already showed against the Saints that he can step in at tackle and hold up, with no pressures allowed in 25 snaps. If the bye gets Thomas and Eluemunor healthy, the Giants could walk into Philadelphia with their best line of the season protecting a young quarterback. That is a big if, but it is a real possibility.

And then there is the fourth bet. Isaiah Likely. Coming into week seven, PlayerProfiler had Likely first among all tight ends in receiving yards and target share, second in receptions and routes run, and sixth in expected points added, which measures how much a player’s plays increase his team’s chances of scoring. That puts him in conversations with names like Brock Bowers, Trey McBride, and Tucker Kraft. You do not get into that group with one highlight. You need consistent involvement throughout an offense, and Likely has had it.

Houston was a quieter game for him as a receiver, and I want to say that honestly. But that is also how a healthy offense works. Not every player has to dominate every week. When defenses focus on Likely, somebody else has to make them pay. Against the Texans, that somebody was Fields. That is exactly the balance this offense needs.

And Likely’s contract makes the bet look even better. He signed a three-year, 40 million dollar deal, which works out to about 13.3 million per year. That is a meaningful investment. But if he keeps producing at this level, it is going to look like good value. And his blocking matters too. When a tight end can contribute as a receiver and stay involved in the run game, the offense gets more flexibility without changing personnel. That is especially valuable for a coach like John Harbaugh, who wants an offense that can win in multiple ways. Remember, Harbaugh coached Likely in Baltimore, where he was stuck behind Mark Andrews. Harbaugh said earlier this season that Likely did not have as many opportunities there, for good reasons. Now he has them, and he is making the most of them.

So, step back and look at all four of these bets together. McCarthy cost a late pick. Eluemunor and Likely came at mid-level prices. Fields was a third-round pick. None of these moves required the Giants to bet the future. If any one of them does not work out, the damage is limited. But when several of them hit at the same time, like they did in Houston, the upside is significant. That is a smart way to build a roster, especially for a franchise that has been through as many lean years as the Giants have.

And it is not just the bargains. Successful roster building needs both. Premium picks who justify the investment, like Malik Nabers at sixth overall and Abdul Carter at third overall. And lower-cost additions who become meaningful contributors, like Fields, Tyrone Tracy Jr., rookie tackle J.C. Davis, and now McCarthy. That is where the real financial benefit shows up. Productive players on manageable contracts give the Giants options. They can add depth, address a weakness, or keep a player they have developed. When every answer does not require the most expensive player available, the roster becomes easier to strengthen year after year.

There is also a longer-term question hiding inside the McCarthy trade. What happens next year when Dart is healthy? If McCarthy keeps playing like he did in Houston, the Giants could end up with two young quarterbacks who have both shown they can win games. That is a rare position for any team. It could create a tough decision, but it also creates leverage. A quarterback who has played well is a valuable trade asset, or he is a valuable insurance policy behind a young starter coming off a major knee injury. Either way, the Giants turned a fifth-round pick into flexibility at the most important position in the sport.

And from Harbaugh’s point of view, these are exactly the kind of players coaches love. Hungry players. Players with something to prove. A quarterback who got moved by the team that drafted him. A tight end who spent years waiting for a bigger role. A rookie receiver from the third round. A veteran right tackle who has bounced around the league. Those players tend to play with an edge, and you could see that edge in Houston.

Now, the division, because it just moved again. On Monday night, the Dallas Cowboys beat the Philadelphia Eagles 38 to 21. So, Dallas snapped its losing streak, and the Eagles, already without Jalen Hurts, took another loss heading into their game against the Giants. The Giants still sit on top of the NFC East at 4 and 2 and 1, unbeaten in the division, and they were the first team in the division to win a game since week four. But here is the thing. The Giants cannot rely on their rivals struggling. Dallas just reminded everybody that this division can turn in one night. The Giants need consistent football from their own roster.

And here is the good news in that result. The Giants already beat Dallas, 28 to 20 in week one. So, if the Cowboys get hot and the race tightens, the Giants hold the head-to-head tiebreaker. That week one win, which felt like a long time ago, might matter a lot in December. Every division game counts twice, once in the standings and once in the tiebreakers, and the Giants have not lost one yet.

And think about what that means for the trip to Philadelphia. The Eagles just got embarrassed on Monday night. They are going to come out of that loss angry, and they are going to treat the Giants game like a must-win. A desperate team at home in a division game is dangerous, no matter who is playing quarterback. That is why the bye week matters so much. The Giants need Eluemunor’s calf to heal, Skattebo’s hamstring to check out, and the secondary to get healthy, including players like Greg Newsome II, who is worth watching once he is back to full strength.

So, did the Giants outsmart the league? I would say it is too early for that kind of claim. One win does not prove a roster philosophy. Fields has one big game. McCarthy has one start. Eluemunor has a calf that needs to heal. Likely had a quiet afternoon in Houston. But I do think the Giants deserve credit for something important. They identified undervalued players, paid reasonable prices, and put them in roles where they could succeed. That is not luck. That is process. And a good process tends to produce good results over time, even if not every individual bet pays off.

Here is what I will be watching during the bye. Number one, Eluemunor’s calf and whether he is out of the walking boot. Number two, how much the coaches expand Fields’ role, because he just earned more targets. Number three, the McCarthy and Nabers connection, with two full weeks of practice. And number four, the Eagles. How do they respond after that Monday night loss to Dallas?

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. They found a new weapon in Houston without making a trade. They have a quarterback who cost a fifth-round pick and won his first start, a right tackle who shut down the league’s sack leader, and a tight end putting up numbers next to the best in football. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
