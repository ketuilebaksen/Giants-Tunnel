J.J. McCarthy could be one snap away from the field this Sunday in Washington. On Monday afternoon, John Harbaugh was asked about the backup quarterback spot behind Jameis Winston, and he did not dodge it. He said there is a possibility that J.J. McCarthy is the number two quarterback in week five, that they will see how it goes during the week, but that it is definitely on the table. And on the very same day, down in Washington, Dan Quinn stepped to a podium and said the goal is for Jayden Daniels, the former number two overall pick and the 2024 Offensive Rookie of the Year, to get a full week of practice and start on Sunday against the Giants. So here is where we are, folks. A first-place division showdown with quarterback questions on both sidelines. The Giants are deciding who stands one injury away from the huddle, and Washington is deciding whether its franchise player is back. Today I am going to break down both stories, what each one really means, the injury picture on both sides, the center decision that turned into a good problem, a rookie linebacker who now leads the entire NFL in a major category, and the one homecoming storyline nobody is talking about enough. Let's start with McCarthy, because that is the headline that is going to dominate the week. First, let's be careful, because this channel does not turn possibilities into announcements. Harbaugh did not say McCarthy is the backup. He said it is a possibility, that it is definitely on the table, and that they will see how it goes during the week. That is not a done deal. But folks, when a head coach brings a name up himself and calls it a real option, you take it seriously. And think about how fast this has moved. McCarthy was acquired from Minnesota in the trade frenzy that followed Jackson Dart's injury, and he has been in this building for about a week. New system, new teammates, new terminology, new everything. And his name is already in the conversation for a game day role in a divisional road game. That tells you how quickly this staff wants to find out what it has in him.

Now, the phrase that matters most in Harbaugh's answer is "we'll see how it goes during the week." Here is how I read it. The coaches want to watch how comfortable McCarthy looks in practice before they commit. Can he run the huddle without stumbling over the play calls? Does he know the protections? Does he know where to go with the ball when the first read is covered? Because the backup quarterback on a road trip is not a ceremonial job. If Winston goes down in the second quarter in a hostile stadium, that man has to walk into the huddle cold and run the offense. You cannot have a backup who looks lost on the sideline. If McCarthy shows he is ready by Friday, great. If he is not, you roll with what you have.

And here is the interesting wrinkle. Against Arizona, the Giants actually dressed three quarterbacks. Jameis Winston, J.J. McCarthy, and Jake Haener, who was elevated from the practice squad for the game. Folks, notice who was in uniform and who was not. The staff chose to dress McCarthy in his first week in the building, which tells you they wanted him on that sideline, absorbing the game, feeling the speed, hearing the calls through the headset. Now the question becomes, what happens to Jake Haener if McCarthy becomes the official number two? And let me be clear, Haener has not done anything wrong. He has bounced on and off this roster all season, elevated, waived, brought back to the practice squad, and every time the team called, he showed up ready. He was elevated for a game the Giants won. But if you are a coach with a former top-ten pick who has now had a full week to settle in, I think you want to see him in that role. Haener could still be the emergency third, or the staff could keep things flexible. It is early in the process.

Now let's connect this to the bigger McCarthy story we have been tracking on this channel since the day he arrived. Remember the checklist we built? Three signals that would tell us McCarthy was moving toward the field. Signal one, being named the clear backup. Signal two, getting meaningful practice reps with the starters. Signal three, a special package, a handful of designed plays that get him on the field in specific situations. Folks, being the official number two in a road division game would be the strongest version of signal one we have seen yet. And remember what the insiders were saying before Arizona, that McCarthy could play as soon as he was comfortable, possibly as early as Washington, if Winston struggled against the Cardinals. Well, Winston did not struggle. He threw three touchdowns, found Malik Nabers for 112 yards, and led the Giants to 36 points. So the McCarthy timeline did not speed up because of a crisis. It sped up because McCarthy is learning fast. And that is the healthiest possible way for this to unfold, folks. The starter is winning, the young quarterback is developing behind him, and the team is not forced into a panic move. That is exactly what you want from a quarterback room.

And here is why this matters so much. Jameis Winston is steering the ship right now, and the Giants are 3 and 1 because of it. But Winston is also a quarterback who plays with a gunslinger's mentality, he stands in the pocket, he takes hits, he extends plays. If he is the guy you are trusting every single week, you better know exactly who is next in line, and you better trust that guy too. Having a former top-ten pick with starting experience as your number two is a luxury most teams in this league would kill for. McCarthy won his last four starts in Minnesota last season, including a 16 to 3 win over Green Bay in the finale. He is not a stranger to winning NFL games. If the worst happens on Sunday, the Giants would not be handing the ball to a scared kid. They would be handing it to a guy who has been there before.

Now let's flip to the other sideline, because Washington has its own quarterback mystery, and it is a much bigger one. Dan Quinn said on Monday that the goal is for Jayden Daniels to get a full week of practice and start on Sunday against the Giants. Daniels has missed the previous two games with a left elbow injury. And I want to be just as careful here as I was with McCarthy, because the key word is goal. Quinn did not say Daniels is starting, period. He said the goal is a full week and then a start. That means it is still tied to how Daniels looks in practice. If he gets through the week clean, I think he is out there on Sunday. If there is any setback with that elbow, it changes. And anybody who tells you they know for sure right now is guessing.

Here is why this is such a big deal for the Giants. Washington has been in chaos at quarterback. Marcus Mariota started in place of Daniels for two straight weeks, and in that lopsided loss to the Colts in London, Mariota suffered a knee injury and had to be replaced by a rookie. So Washington has a franchise quarterback it desperately wants back, a veteran backup who just got hurt, and a rookie who had to finish a game in another country. That is a lot of uncertainty at the most important position in football. And it creates two completely different game plans for Dennard Wilson's defense. If Daniels plays, the Giants are facing one of the most dangerous dual-threat quarterbacks in the league, a guy who can beat you with his arm, with his legs, with scrambles that turn broken plays into thirty-yard gains. If he does not play, the Giants could be facing a rookie who is still finding his feet, depending on what happens with Mariota's knee. You prepare very differently for those two quarterbacks.

So let's talk about how the Giants would defend Daniels if he does play, because this is where it gets fun. The first rule against a quarterback like Daniels is contain. You cannot let him escape the pocket and run free, because that is where he does his most damage. And folks, think about the weapon the Giants have for exactly this assignment. Arvell Reese. A rookie linebacker with the speed of a safety and the instincts of a ten-year veteran. If I am Dennard Wilson, I am thinking seriously about using Reese as a spy, mirroring Daniels on passing downs, ready to explode the moment he takes off. Reese has the range to cover sideline to sideline, and he has already shown he can rush the passer and drop into coverage. A spy role would let him do both. Then you need your edge rushers, Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux, Jihad Ward, to rush with discipline, staying in their lanes and not getting too far upfield, because Daniels lives in the gaps that undisciplined rushers leave behind. And in the middle, you need Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne to collapse the pocket so Daniels cannot step up and escape. Pressure from the inside is the best way to beat a mobile quarterback, because he can run around edge pressure, but he cannot run through a wall.

And that brings me to the storyline I think is being underrated this week. Daron Payne is going home. Payne spent his entire NFL career in Washington before the Giants brought him to New York, and now, just weeks later, he walks back into that stadium wearing the enemy's colors in a division game. Folks, you know he has this one circled. And here is the part that should worry Washington. Payne has practiced against Jayden Daniels. He knows his tendencies, his escape routes, the way he likes to spin out of pressure. He knows that offensive line, he knows their calls, he knows their weaknesses. That knowledge is priceless in a game like this. I would not be surprised at all if Payne plays with an extra edge on Sunday, and with Lawrence next to him, that interior wall could be the reason Washington's offense never gets comfortable.

And Washington's problems do not stop at quarterback. Terry McLaurin, their top receiver, was held out of the Colts game with a hamstring injury. Dan Quinn said they are still evaluating him and will have a better update on Wednesday once they see him working off to the side with the trainers. He called it a difficult call, but the right call. Folks, you do not hold out your best receiver unless you are being careful, and the fact that he is working off to the side, not in full practice, means I am not going to assume he is back. If McLaurin plays, he is a matchup the Giants have to respect, a veteran route runner who has tormented this division for years. And if he does play, this could be the first real test for Joey Porter Jr. in a Giants uniform, a big, physical corner lined up across from Washington's most dangerous receiver. That is a matchup I want to see. If McLaurin does not play, Washington's passing game gets even thinner, and the Giants secondary, which has been ending games with interceptions, could feast.

So that is the Washington picture. A quarterback they hope is back, a receiver who is questionable, and a backup situation that got complicated. But the Giants are not walking into this game completely clean either. Three Giants left the Arizona game with injuries and did not return. Braxton Berrios had a foot injury, safety Jason Pinnock had a concussion, and defensive lineman Chauncey Golston had a neck injury. The one I am watching most closely is Berrios. Harbaugh said he is not on injured reserve at this point, which is a good sign, but that they would probably know more by Monday or Tuesday. He explained that Berrios got twisted up with an ankle and foot issue on one of the punt returns. So we are in wait-and-see mode. Not the worst-case news, but not a he-is-fine update either. If Berrios cannot go, keep an eye on Charlie Jones, the return specialist the Giants signed earlier this season, a guy who scored a return touchdown in each of his first three NFL seasons. On a special teams unit that is already the best in football, Jones stepping into a bigger role could be a real weapon. With Pinnock and Golston, I do not have more details yet, and I am not going to pretend I do. But Pinnock's concussion matters for the safety depth, and with a dual-threat quarterback possibly on the other side, every healthy defensive back counts.

Now let's talk about the center position, because it turned into what I would call a good problem. Before the Arizona game, the same five offensive linemen had played every single snap together this season. Then Brian Hudson, the backup center, made his first career start in place of John Michael Schmitz. Harbaugh explained that Schmitz had missed a couple of days of practice because his wife had a baby, so congratulations again to the Schmitz family, and he did not get much rest. Meanwhile, Hudson, who the coaches call Huey, had been doing so well in practice that the staff felt he had the reps and was making the calls correctly. They had already been thinking about working him in here and there, so it made sense to give him the start. And Harbaugh said it paid off, that Hudson played really well. A guy gets his first career start, the team scores 36 points, and the coach praises him afterward. That is a great day for a young lineman.

But Harbaugh also made it clear this is not a long-term thing, because the team has two centers it really likes. When he was asked on Monday what it means for week five, he said they had not had the personnel meeting yet. He said Hudson played really well, which could create some options, but Schmitz was playing really well too. So there is no clear answer yet. If Schmitz is rested and ready, there is a good argument he goes back in, because he has been the starter and the staff values that consistency, especially on the road in a division game, where crowd noise makes the center's communication even more critical. But you can also argue, why change what just worked? Honestly, folks, I do not think there is a wrong answer here. Having two centers you trust makes your offensive line deeper, and over a seventeen-game season, depth along the line is the kind of thing that wins you games in December.

Now let's get to the young man who is quietly becoming one of the best stories in the entire NFL. Arvell Reese has been nominated for the Pepsi NFL Rookie of the Week award again, the second time in just four games. And he earned it. Against Arizona, he led the Giants with nine solo tackles, three passes defensed, one tackle for loss, and his first career interception. That is a full stat sheet, folks. But here is where the numbers get really special. Through four weeks, Arvell Reese leads the entire NFL, not just rookies, the entire league, with 26 solo tackles. The number five overall pick, four games into his career, leading every linebacker in football in solo stops. He also became just the fifth Giants rookie with at least three passes defensed and an interception in a single game.
