First, John Harbaugh says the up-tempo offense that saved the Washington game is not a one-time emergency button anymore. He said it is good to see, and that it probably needs to be part of what this team does going forward. Second, Harbaugh made a decision at center. Brian Hudson, who has started the last two games in place of John Michael Schmitz, will start again against New Orleans. Folks, put those two together and you can see the plan. A faster offense, with the center who has been running it, built around a quarterback who just did something no Giant has done since 1950. And they get to unleash it on a 1 and 4 Saints team that just lost its third straight home game. Today I am going to break down both pieces of news, why tempo fits this team so well, the injury report that has me watching closely, and why I think Sunday is the perfect setup for the Giants to protect their lead in the NFC East. Let’s start with the first piece of news, because I cannot stop thinking about what happened in the second half in Washington. The Giants were down 14 in the fourth quarter. And with how the offense had looked for most of that afternoon, I honestly figured that was it. Then they scored two touchdowns in the final eight minutes of regulation and walked away with a 27 to 27 tie. And the day after the game, John Harbaugh pointed to one specific thing that he believes opened the offense up. The word he kept coming back to was tempo.

And here is what I love about how he said it. Harbaugh told reporters they had been working on tempo, that it has been part of the system from day one. And then he admitted something honest. Maybe they got to it because they had to. Maybe the game flow forced it. Folks, he could have pretended it was some grand plan all along. Instead, he basically said the situation pushed them there, and once they got there, it opened up the offense. That is a coach who is willing to learn from what is working instead of protecting his ego. And then came the part that really matters. Harbaugh said it was good to see, and that it probably needs to be part of what they are doing going forward. That is a clear signal. This is not a coach saying we will pull it out again if we fall behind. This is a coach saying, we found something that works, and we are going to use it.

And folks, that is exactly what I wanted to hear. Because if the Giants only go fast when they are desperate, they will always be chasing games. Bring that tempo in earlier, and now they are setting the terms instead of reacting. When I say formula, I am not talking about a trick play or a gadget. I am talking about something simple. Playing faster. Not letting the defense substitute. Not letting the defense communicate. Not letting the defense breathe.

Now think about what that comeback really looked like. A 27 to 27 tie in Washington is a strange result, and nobody leaves happy. But the offense stopped sputtering and scored two touchdowns in the last eight minutes. That is not luck, folks. That is an offense that found something. When a team has no choice, it plays the most aggressive version of itself. And that version looked a lot better than anything we saw before it.

And the biggest reason it worked was Jameis Winston. Making his third straight start in place of the injured Jackson Dart, Winston became the first Giant since 1950 to account for at least 300 passing yards, three touchdown passes, and 25 rushing yards in the same game. Since 1950, folks. Think about all the quarterbacks who have come through this franchise in that time. Y.A. Tittle, Fran Tarkenton, Phil Simms, Eli Manning. None of them put that exact line together. That stat tells you Winston was not just throwing the ball. He was moving, making plays with his legs, driving the offense. And remember, Harbaugh just told us that Jameis said in a team meeting, I have always been a scrambler. Well, the numbers back him up. And a fast tempo fits a quarterback like that. When the pace goes up, you want a guy who can process quickly, get the ball out, and keep the defense guessing. Washington proved the formula can work with him running it. And let’s not forget the bigger picture of Winston’s run. Three straight starts since Dart went down, and the Giants are 2 and 0 and 1 in them. Six touchdown passes over the last two games. Back-to-back three-touchdown games, the first Giant to do that since Eli Manning in 2016. And zero interceptions in Washington, in the rain, on the road. The backup who was supposed to be a placeholder is now putting his name next to franchise legends in the record book. Folks, whatever happens with J.J. McCarthy down the road, Jameis Winston has earned the right to run this offense his way, and the coaching staff is finally building it around him.

And let me explain why tempo is such a powerful weapon in general, because I think a lot of fans hear the word and do not realize how much it changes a football game. When an offense goes no-huddle, the defense cannot substitute. If they have their base personnel on the field and the offense spreads out with four receivers, the defense is stuck with linebackers trying to cover receivers. Defensive linemen cannot rotate, so by the third or fourth play of a drive, the pass rush is gassed. And the defensive play caller has less time to get the call in, which means simpler coverages and fewer disguises. Basically, tempo takes away the defense’s chess pieces and makes them play checkers.

And there is history behind it, too, including a little Giants history. In the early nineties, the Buffalo Bills ran the famous K-Gun offense with Jim Kelly, a no-huddle attack that wore defenses down and took them to four straight Super Bowls. And the first of those Super Bowls was against the Giants, in Super Bowl XXV, where Bill Parcells and Bill Belichick beat that no-huddle by controlling the clock and keeping Kelly on the sideline. So the Giants know both sides of this story. Now, with this roster, the Giants get to be the team that pushes the pace. And with a defense built around blitzing and a running game that can grind when it needs to, they can still slow it down and play keep-away when the situation calls for it. That flexibility is the real weapon.

Now, let me be fair about the risks, because tempo is not free. When a fast offense stalls with a quick three-and-out, the defense ends up right back on the field without much rest. And this defense is banged up right now, with Abdul Carter dealing with a calf, Tyler Nubin with a neck injury, and Colton Hood in the concussion protocol. If the Giants go fast and keep going three-and-out, they could wear their own defense down. That is why the smart version of tempo is selective. Use it after big plays, when the defense is tired and scrambling. Use it on opening drives to set the tone. Use it in the two-minute drill. And mix in huddle drives with Breece Hall and Cam Skattebo running the ball when you need to rest your defense and control the clock. The best tempo teams are not fast every snap. They are fast when it hurts the defense the most.

And picture what a smart tempo game plan against the Saints might look like. Script the opening drive at full speed, with quick throws to Nabers and Likely to get Winston into rhythm early. After a big gain, line up immediately and snap it before New Orleans can substitute. When the Giants get a lead, slow down, huddle up, and let the run game and play action burn clock. And sprinkle in the McCarthy package Harbaugh hinted at, because nothing changes the tempo of a game like a completely different quarterback with a completely different skill set jogging onto the field. Folks, that is a game plan that keeps a struggling defense guessing all afternoon.

But tempo does not mean anything without playmakers who can take advantage of it, and the Giants have two of them. Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely each had eleven catches, at least 90 receiving yards, and a receiving touchdown in the same game. They are just the sixth pair of teammates in NFL history to do that, and only the second receiver and tight end duo. The only other pair was Justin Jefferson and T.J. Hockenson in Minnesota. That says a lot about what happens when this offense gets moving.

And here is how it all connects. Nabers gets better when the offense is not stuck waiting around. Get him the ball in rhythm, with the defense not set, and he does real damage. Then add Likely, a tight end who caught eleven balls and found the end zone twice. That is a matchup nightmare for a defense trying to line up and communicate while the clock is running. How do you handle both of those guys when you cannot even get your personnel settled? And now add Breece Hall, the explosive running back who just arrived from across the hallway at MetLife, catching passes out of the backfield. Folks, a tempo offense with Nabers, Likely, and Hall is going to be a nightmare to defend. That is why I believe the faster approach is not a one-game thing.

Now, the second piece of news, the decision at center. Brian Hudson made his second straight start against Washington, the first two starts of his career, in place of John Michael Schmitz. And Harbaugh already announced that Hudson will be the starter again against New Orleans. His explanation was simple. Hudson played well the week before, he played well in this past game, and while there are things he can do better, there are things he did really well. That is what they based the decision on.

And I like that a lot, folks, especially with tempo becoming a bigger part of the offense. The center is the player who makes the calls and gets everybody on the same page. In a no-huddle offense, there is no time for confusion. You need a center who is locked in, who knows the protections, and who has chemistry with the quarterback and the guards. Hudson has been doing that. Now, Harbaugh made sure to say that John Michael Schmitz is still in the plans, and I believe that. Schmitz was playing well before his wife had their baby and he missed time. But for this week, the job belongs to Hudson. You reward the player who has been performing, and you do not mess with chemistry up front while the offense is building momentum. That is smart coaching.

Now, the part that has me a little concerned. The injury picture. Right tackle Jermaine Eluemunor with a calf injury, rookie cornerback Colton Hood with a concussion, and safety Tyler Nubin with a neck injury all left Sunday’s game. Harbaugh said they were day-to-day with a chance to play. But on Wednesday’s first injury report, none of those three participated. On top of that, left tackle Andrew Thomas did not practice with his groin, and Abdul Carter did not practice with a calf issue. Folks, I do not love seeing that many names, especially up front.

And here is why the offensive line part matters so much for this tempo plan. If Eluemunor cannot go and Thomas is dealing with that groin, you are asking a lot from the line while also trying to play faster. Tempo works best when protection is solid. When the offense speeds up, the linemen have less time to rest between snaps, and the quarterback needs a clean pocket to get the ball out quickly. So the health of that group is going to decide how much of the formula the Giants can actually use. But it is still early in the week. A Wednesday report is never the final word. We have seen Thomas miss Wednesdays before and play on Sunday. So I am not panicking. I am watching.

And Carter is the defensive name to watch. Harbaugh told us earlier this week that Carter is doing very well, and that the status would come out on the injury report. Now we know it is a calf. If he is limited, Dennard Wilson’s aggressive blitz plan becomes even more important, and guys like Kayvon Thibodeaux, Jadeveon Clowney, and the blitzing defensive backs will have to step up.

Speaking of blitzing defensive backs, let’s talk about Andru Phillips. He had two sacks against Washington, making him just the fifth Giants defensive back since 1982 to have two sacks in a game. The others are Bill Currier, Jason Sehorn, Tito Wooten, and Jason Pinnock. Folks, Phillips is becoming a real weapon in this blitz-heavy scheme, coming off the edge from the nickel spot when quarterbacks are focused on Carter and Reese. And speaking of Arvell Reese, he is the only Giant in the last 30 seasons to reach 40 or more tackles in the first five games of his career. The defense is doing its part. If the offense can give it a lead to protect, that changes the whole feel of a game.

Now, why are the Saints such a good opponent for this formula? New Orleans is 1 and 4. They just lost to the undefeated Minnesota Vikings, 23 to 13. That was their third straight loss, all at home, and their only win came back in week two at Baltimore. So you have a team that is struggling to find answers. And that is exactly the kind of team you want a faster offense to go up against. When a defense is already searching for confidence, the last thing it wants is an offense that does not let it breathe.

Now, I want to be fair, because Harbaugh was earlier this week. He called the Saints a very hungry team, a very talented team, and a very well-coached team. And he pointed out that young quarterback Tyler Shough and his top receiver have been building a real connection. A 1 and 4 team with nothing to lose can be dangerous. So the Giants cannot take this one lightly. But this is a perfect chance for Harbaugh to get ahead of the game. The Giants do not have to wait until they are down 14 to speed things up. They can come out in tempo from the first drive, get Winston into rhythm, get the ball to Nabers and Likely, mix in Hall, and see if the Saints can keep up.

And here is a fun storyline for Sunday. Drew Brees, who was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame this year, will be the color analyst on the FOX broadcast. So the Saints legend will be calling the game from the booth. And think about the connection, folks. Jameis Winston spent time in that New Orleans quarterback room with Brees back in 2020. Now Brees gets to watch his former teammate lead a first-place Giants team against his old franchise. You cannot write it better.

Let me also mention the roster moves, because they show this team is paying attention to depth. The Giants signed linebacker Malik Harrison to the active roster. He is a special teams player who played 56 snaps and recovered a fumble on the opening kickoff against Tennessee. They added defensive end Dawuane Smoot to the practice squad. They released C.J. Ravenell. And defensive lineman Chauncey Golston went to injured reserve with his neck injury. None of these are headline moves, but they show a team trying to stay deep for a long season. And Golston going to IR is a real loss for the defensive line rotation, so somebody like Darius Alexander, who has been improving since coming back from being a healthy scratch, will need to step up.

And then there is the bigger picture. The Giants are 3 and 1 and 1, sitting on top of the NFC East. And they would have stayed there no matter what happened in Washington, because the two-time defending division champion Eagles lost to Jacksonville 19 to 17 in London. The Eagles and the Cowboys are both 2 and 3. The Commanders are 1 and 3 and 1. The division is wide open, and the Giants are the ones in front. A win over a 1 and 4 Saints team gives them a real cushion before the trip to Houston and the bye week. You want to go into that stretch with as much momentum as possible.

So here is where I land, folks. I think John Harbaugh found something real in Washington. Faster tempo, Jameis Winston running it, Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely getting the ball, Breece Hall adding speed, and Brian Hudson holding down the middle. And the Saints are the perfect team to see it again. If the injuries stay manageable, especially on the offensive line and with Abdul Carter, I like the Giants to win this game and keep their division lead. And you know what I have been saying all season. This team can go all the way.
