Let’s start with why this conversation is happening right now, because the timing is everything. The Giants just beat the Texans 27 to 21 in overtime. They are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East, unbeaten in the division, and heading into their bye week. J.J. McCarthy went 20 of 28 for 252 yards in his first start. Cam Skattebo ran for 124 yards on 17 carries. Devin Singletary scored the winner in overtime. A couple of weeks ago, people were talking about trading for a running back like Jonathan Taylor. After Sunday, that urgency is gone. The running back room went from a question mark to a pile of good news.

So, the shopping list has moved to the other side of the ball. And when you look at that side, the list is long, because this defense has been bleeding bodies all season. Brian Burns went on injured reserve back in September. Chauncey Golston is on injured reserve. Dexter Lawrence is gone. In the secondary, Jevon Holland, who is tied for the league lead with three interceptions, missed the Houston game while working through the concussion protocol. Greg Newsome II and Colton Hood have both been dealing with concussions. Tyler Nubin has been dealing with a neck injury. Paulson Adebo just worked his way back from injured reserve. And Tremaine Edmunds has had a shoulder injury. That is a defense that has been held together by depth players and effort for weeks. It held Houston to 21 on Sunday, and that is a credit to everybody in that room. But if you are a contender, you do not want to keep asking depth players to carry you in November and December.

Now, let’s talk about Arizona, because the Cardinals’ situation is the other half of this story. Remember, the Cardinals just traded Marvin Harrison Jr. to Houston. When a team trades a young star receiver in the middle of the season, it is telling you something about where it is headed. The Cardinals look like a team in rebuild mode, and teams in rebuild mode want draft picks, not 30-year-old veterans. Their home results have been rough too, by every account. And the Giants know that team well. They beat Arizona 36 to 24 earlier this season.

So, if you are the Cardinals, a veteran safety, no matter how good, is not your priority. He is a chip you would rather turn into a draft pick. And I want to be fair to Arizona, because a rebuild is not a joke. It is a group of people trying to build something. Baker is not someone they are dumping. He has been a leader in that locker room for years. That is exactly why the Giants would have to pay a real price. The number being floated is a third-round pick. I have no confirmation of that price, so treat it as part of the speculation. But it is a reasonable starting point for a player like Baker.

So, who is Budda Baker, for those of you who only know the name? He came into the league as a second-round pick out of Washington in 2017, and he has been one of the most versatile defensive backs in football ever since. He has made multiple Pro Bowls. He has been an All-Pro. He has piled up more than 100 tackles in a season more than once, which is almost unheard of for a defensive back, because it means he is constantly around the football, whether it is in coverage, against the run, or coming off the edge on a blitz. And what makes him so valuable is not one skill. It is three jobs in one body. He can cover the slot. He can blitz from the box. And he leads a locker room. In football terms, that is a player who lets a defensive coordinator draw up a defense with fewer compromises.

And think about what each of those jobs would do for this specific Giants defense.

First, the slot coverage. The Giants have been patching the secondary every week. A safety who can line up over the slot receiver lets the cornerbacks, Deonte Banks, Paulson Adebo, and Joey Porter Jr., stay outside where they are best. It also gives Dennard Wilson a big nickel option, where Baker plays like a slot corner but with a safety’s toughness against the run. That matters a lot against offenses that love to spread the field with three receivers.

Second, the blitzing. All season, this channel has talked about Dennard Wilson’s blitz-heavy approach. We saw Andru Phillips get two sacks off the slot in Washington. We saw Arvell Reese come on delayed blitzes. Baker has been one of the best blitzing safeties in the league for years. With Brian Burns out, a defense that can create pressure from the secondary does not need to find another edge rusher to get home. Picture it. Carter on one edge, Thibodeaux on the other, Reese walking up in the A-gap, and Baker screaming off the slot. Quarterbacks would not know where it is coming from.

Third, the leadership. This is the one that does not show up on a stat sheet, but every coach in the league says he wants it. Harbaugh has been talking all season about accountability and effort. After the Saints game, Dennard Wilson called out players for not running to the ball. A veteran like Baker, who plays every snap like it is his last, is exactly the kind of player who raises the standard in a meeting room without saying a word.

And here is the contract detail that makes this so interesting. If the reported number is right, and Baker’s dead money in 2027 would be only about 3 million dollars, then the risk is small. In plain language, if the Giants trade for him and decide to move on after this season, the cost of walking away is tiny. That means this could be a move for right now, a rental for the stretch run, with the option to keep him if he fits. For a team in first place, that is exactly the kind of move you want to make.

Let me quickly explain dead money, because it confuses a lot of people. When a team signs a player, part of his contract is usually paid as a signing bonus, and that bonus gets spread across the years of the deal for salary cap purposes. If the team cuts or trades the player later, the part of that bonus that has not been counted yet hits the cap all at once. That is dead money, money you are charged for a player who is no longer on your team. So, when a player’s dead money in a future year is small, like the 3 million reported for Baker in 2027, it means the team that holds his contract has a cheap exit. The Giants would not be locked in. They could keep him if he plays well, or move on without wrecking their cap. That flexibility is a big reason why a player like Baker is so attractive at the deadline.

Now, let me give you a Harbaugh parallel, because he has done this exact kind of move before. In 2022, in the middle of the season, the Ravens traded for linebacker Roquan Smith, sending the Bears a second-round pick and more. A lot of people questioned the price. And Roquan transformed that defense almost overnight. He brought speed, communication, and leadership, and the Ravens’ defense took off. Harbaugh saw firsthand what one veteran leader on defense can do in the middle of a season. Baker is a different position and a different player, but the idea is the same. One move, at the right time, can change the identity of a defense.

And there is a Giants parallel too. Back in 2019, the Giants traded a third-round pick and a fifth-round pick to the Jets for Leonard Williams in the middle of the season. People hated it at the time, because the Giants were not contending. But Williams became one of the best players on that defense for years. The difference now is that the Giants are contending. They are in first place. A third-round pick for a player who can help you win the division this year is a very different calculation than a trade made during a losing season.

And there is something else that makes this interesting. In his first seasons as general manager, Joe Schoen’s biggest deadline moves were sales. In 2022, he traded Kadarius Toney to Kansas City. In 2023, he sent Leonard Williams to Seattle. Those were moves made by a front office that was building for the future. A trade for Budda Baker would be a very different kind of move. It would be a front office saying, we are in this race right now, and we are going to add to it. Fans notice that. Players notice that too. When a front office buys at the deadline, the locker room hears the message. We believe in you. Go win it.

And if the Giants cannot get Baker, the Cardinals are not the only rebuilding team out there. Remember, the Colts just fired their general manager in the middle of the season, and we talked about names like DeForest Buckner on the defensive line. With Dexter Lawrence gone and D.J. Reader carrying so much of the run defense, an interior lineman could help just as much as a safety. The point is, the Giants have options. Baker is the most natural fit in the secondary, but he is not the only way to strengthen this defense before the deadline.

Now, I owe you a skeptic, because a story with no doubters is not a story. So, here is the other side.

First, age. Baker turns 31 early next year. Safeties who rely on speed and physicality can decline quickly, and the Giants would be betting on what he has left, not what he did three years ago. Second, the price. A third-round pick might not sound like much, but this front office has been finding value in the middle and late rounds. Tyrone Tracy Jr. was a fifth-round pick. J.C. Davis was a day-three pick. McCarthy cost a fifth-round pick. Every pick Schoen trades away is one fewer chance to find the next one of those. Third, the cap. A veteran with a big contract has to fit somewhere, and the Giants already have money tied up across the roster. And fourth, other teams. If Baker really is on the market, the Giants will not be the only team calling. The Bills were mentioned. Other contenders will be too. A bidding war could push the price beyond what makes sense.

And there is one more thing I want to say clearly. Right now, this is speculation. Trade lists are not trade reports. A player being a natural fit is not the same as a deal being close. I have seen too many deadline rumors fizzle out to treat this as done. So, until there is an official announcement, I am treating this as an idea that makes a lot of sense, not a move that is happening.

But let me tell you why I think the Giants should at least make the call. The NFC East is wide open. The Eagles are expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the rest of the season. The Cowboys have been struggling. Washington has not won since before that tie. And the Giants just became the first NFC East team to win a game since week four. That is a window. Windows do not stay open forever. And the Giants have not won the NFC East since 2011. When you have a chance to end a drought like that, you push your chips in, as long as the price is reasonable. A third-round pick for a proven, versatile safety with a small 2027 dead money hit is the definition of a reasonable price.

And think about the bye week timing. If the Giants make a move like this before the deadline, Baker would have time to learn Dennard Wilson’s defense while the team is healthy and rested. He would walk into the Philadelphia game with a full week of practice behind him. That is a much better situation than trading for a player and throwing him into a game three days later.

And think about what Baker would see in Philadelphia. With Jalen Hurts expected to miss the rest of the season, the Eagles will likely lean even harder on their run game and quick passes to their receivers. That is exactly where a safety like Baker earns his money. He can come down into the box and fill against the run like a linebacker. He can jump a quick throw to the slot. And he can make a backup quarterback’s life miserable with a well-timed blitz. That is not a hypothetical fit. That is the exact game the Giants have right after the bye.

And then, the week after Philadelphia, Washington comes to MetLife with Jayden Daniels. Remember, Harbaugh said the biggest weakness of this defense has been explosive plays on quarterback scrambles. Tyler Shough ran all over the Giants in the Saints game. Daniels is one of the most dangerous running quarterbacks in the league. A safety with Baker’s speed and instincts could be used as a spy, shadowing Daniels and taking away the scramble lanes, while the rest of the defense plays its normal coverage. That is a role very few safeties in the league can play well. Baker is one of them. Two division games, two different problems, and one player who could help solve both.

And do not forget the injury history in this secondary. Three different defensive backs have dealt with concussions in the last few weeks, and concussions are unpredictable. A player can feel fine on Wednesday and have a setback on Friday. When you are in a playoff race, you cannot build your secondary on hoping everybody clears the protocol on time. Adding a proven veteran gives the Giants insurance, so that one bad week of injuries does not turn into a lost game in a division race.

So, how would Baker fit next to the safeties the Giants already have? Holland is the ball hawk, the guy who gets interceptions. Ar’Darius Washington has been playing like he got shot out of a cannon, with an 81.4 grade against the Saints. Jason Pinnock is a veteran who just forced his first fumble of the season. Tyler Nubin is working back from his neck injury. Baker would not replace any of them. He would sit on top of that group as the hybrid piece, the player who can move from deep safety to the slot to the box depending on the call. That kind of flexibility makes everyone around him better, because it lets the coaches put each player where he is strongest.

And let me answer one more question you are going to ask in the comments. What about the offense? Should the Giants be adding there instead? I talked last week about Quenton Nelson and the offensive line, and I still think the interior line needs help. But after Sunday, after 124 yards from Skattebo and two runs of 30-plus yards in overtime, the urgency there has dropped a little. And honestly, the Giants can make more than one move. A safety and a lineman are not mutually exclusive, as long as the price on each one makes sense.

And number four, the rest of the trade market, because if the Giants do not get Baker, there could be other veteran defenders available from rebuilding teams.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. They are in first place. They have a quarterback who just won his first start and a running game that just found its identity. The one thing that could hold them back is a defense that has been stretched thin by injuries. Budda Baker would address that directly.
