And here is one more reason I am feeling good. Look at how this team has responded to adversity this season. Remember when Jackson Dart went down against Los Angeles? The Giants came back the next week, in awful weather, and beat Tennessee 12-7. And after that frustrating New Orleans game, the Giants answered with one of their most impressive performances of the season in Houston. This team has a pattern. When people start doubting them, they respond. And now they come into Jacksonville at home, with ten days of rest, after a loss that has everybody doubting them again. I want to see that same response.

Okay. Now the bad news, because the Giants are making this harder than it needs to be.

Bad news number one: the self-inflicted wounds.

Let’s start with the kicker. Dominic Zvada has missed six field goals this season, and every one of those misses has come from the right hash. That is not a tiny detail. Harbaugh confirmed this week that there is a technique issue with his plant foot that is showing up on the right hash. And here is why it matters so much. When games are being decided by a handful of points, and you do not fully trust your kicker, it changes your third-down and fourth-down decisions. Instead of settling for a manageable kick, you feel pressure to make something spectacular happen. That is no way for a run-first team to operate.

Some people are saying it is time to bring in competition, and one name being floated is Ben Sauls, who finished 2025 kicking well for the Giants. He is not on a roster right now, and there was an injury settlement involved when he left, so his readiness would have to be checked before any move. I understand the argument. Have a backup plan. Do not spend December worrying about field-goal angles. One field goal can decide this division.

Here is my take. Harbaugh has made it clear he has confidence in Zvada, and he is not going to knee-jerk it with a rookie who has made a lot of big kicks, including four for four in Philadelphia and six from 50-plus this season. I respect that. But I also think there is nothing wrong with bringing a veteran in for a workout, just to know your options. My opinion, not a report: if the right-hash problem shows up again in a close game against Jacksonville, I think the Giants at least look at another kicker.

Next, Deonte Banks and that pass interference.

Let’s look at it fairly. On that play, Treylon Burks is pulling on Banks too. I understand why Giants fans are upset about the call. But I am still not letting Banks completely off the hook. The issue is technique. He keeps putting himself in tough spots because he is watching the receiver instead of turning his head and locating the football. When a corner never looks for the ball, any contact looks worse to an official. Look at how good corners handle deep balls. They make that late head turn, find the football, and suddenly the same contact looks like a fair fight for the ball. That is what Banks has to learn. You do not need an interception on every deep ball. You need to give yourself the best chance to avoid a flag. Costly interference penalties at the worst possible moment are killing this team. Stop giving away free yards.

And then the offensive identity, which might bother me even more. Why are the Giants getting cute in short yardage? I keep going back to that third-down play against Washington, with McCarthy running the quarterback crack sweep outside and losing two yards, instead of leaning into what this team does best. Cam Skattebo was moving the pile all night. Two touchdowns. Seventy-eight rushing yards. Harbaugh said the offensive line looked like it did in Houston. If you need three yards, give it to the guy who is fighting for every inch. Run straight ahead. Do it twice if you are in four-down territory. I do not need tricks when you have an offensive line and a running back who can put a real physical problem in front of a defense.

Now, to be fair, the loss was not all coaching. Players have to catch the ball, hold on to it, make their blocks and execute. But coaching matters when the plan starts drifting away from the team’s strengths. And if you are worried about your kicker, you cannot make up for it by asking McCarthy to force tough throws into traffic. Get the manageable yards. Protect the football. Make the opponent deal with Skattebo. That is the identity I want back.

Bad news number two: the schedule is no joke, starting Sunday.

Jacksonville is first, and I think this is one of the hardest tests the Giants have had all season. The Jaguars are 7-2 with a plus-125 point differential after beating Tennessee 40-23. You can argue their schedule has helped them, and there are questions about their quarterback play, but I am not going to pretend they are an ordinary opponent. The Giants opened as home underdogs for this game. That tells you how the betting market sees it after Thursday night.

And think about what a win would mean. Beat a 7-2 team as a home underdog, ten days after a gut-punch loss, and the whole national conversation flips. The “not a serious team” talk goes away. The “Harbaugh lost the game” talk goes away. The Dallas-is-the-favorite talk is already fading. A win on Sunday would turn this week from a week of doubt into a statement. And a loss would not end anything, because the two-game cushion is still there. That is the beauty of what Dallas just did for the Giants. It gave them room to breathe, and room to swing big.

Still, I like the Giants’ chance to compete. And here is how I want them to play. Do not try to prove something by throwing the ball all over the field. Play your game. Run the football. Make smart decisions. Do not give Jacksonville easy chances. Jacksonville has handled teams it should beat. The Giants tend to play up or down to their opponent, and this week they need the version of themselves that showed up in Houston.

After Jacksonville and Indianapolis, San Francisco comes to MetLife. Brock Purdy is incredibly accurate, and Christian McCaffrey makes defenders pay for the smallest mistake. That offense rarely wastes chances. Short completions turn into big gains. But I also saw a San Francisco defense that Dallas was able to attack at times. Even with names like Nick Bosa and Fred Warner, I did not see an unbeatable unit. The Giants can compete with San Francisco if they stop making the mistakes they made against Washington.

Then Seattle on the road, which is a tough, physical opponent in a hard place to play. Cleveland at home, which is not a free win. Detroit on the road, another tough trip, although there is some uncertainty around Aidan Hutchinson after his knee injury. Then Dallas, with the division possibly on the line. And Philadelphia to close. I refuse to count any of those games as wins before they are played. They all have to be earned.

And let’s not forget the good news on the injury side. Tracy is on IR and is lined up to come back later in this stretch. McCarthy’s hip has had ten days to heal. Skattebo is fresh. This defense was fifth in the league in total defense heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. Arvell Reese is one of the best rookies in football. The Giants have the pieces to handle this schedule. They just cannot keep handing games away.

One more thing on the defense, because it ties all of this together. If Dallas is going to be undone by its defense, the Giants have to make sure theirs stays the strength it has been. That means tackling better on early downs, so opponents are not living in third-and-short like Washington was. Harbaugh said that himself. Get teams into third-and-long, and Reese and this pass rush take over.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Last week, I told you the Dallas projection at 51 percent was too high, and that the Cowboys had not given anyone a reason to believe in them. After a 46-point, 584-yard beatdown at home, I feel pretty good about that one. I also told you the Giants would beat Washington. Wrong. And I told you the late-game identity had to change. That one is still on the to-do list.

Here is my prediction for Jacksonville, and you can hold me to it. The Giants lean on Skattebo early and often. The offensive line plays like it did in Houston. McCarthy makes smart throws and protects the football. Banks turns his head and plays the ball. Zvada makes his kicks. And the Giants pull the upset at home, as underdogs, to get to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time, because I believe it. This team can go all the way. The Cowboys just opened the door. Washington is two games back. The Giants are alone in first with eight games to play. The talent is there. The coach is there. The defense is there. What they need now is clean, physical, dependable football when games get tight. Give me a strong running game, fewer flags, smarter calls, and a team that fights for every yard. Do that, and the Giants walk right through the door Dallas just opened.
