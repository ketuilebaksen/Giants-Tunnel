The Giants just made a series of roster moves during the bye week, and one of them hurts. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is going on injured reserve with the hamstring injury he suffered in Houston, and he will miss at least the next four games, including the Eagles game. To replace him, the Giants signed an undrafted rookie running back off the Los Angeles Chargers’ practice squad, a team coached by John Harbaugh’s brother, Jim. They finally signed veteran defensive tackle Dean Lowry, a deal that was reported a month ago and never happened until now. And Joe Schoen stepped to the podium and said something about the trade deadline that has Giants fans buzzing. Meanwhile, the Colts just traded away Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner, two names this channel has been talking about for weeks. Which leaves one big name still sitting in Indianapolis. Jonathan Taylor. Folks, the Giants play the Eagles on November 8th, and the trade deadline is November 10th, just two days later. Today, I am going to break down every move, what Schoen said, and how aggressive I think this front office is really going to be. Let’s get into it.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I’m James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now. With Tracy out, would you trade for Jonathan Taylor?

Let’s start with the worst piece of news, because it forces everything else. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is going on injured reserve, and he will miss at least four games. He left the Houston game in the first half with a hamstring injury, but here is what stuck with me. He stayed on the sideline the whole game. So, a lot of us thought, okay, maybe this is a short-term thing, maybe he avoids injured reserve and comes back right after the bye. That is not the case. And with the bye week already giving him extra time, a four-game minimum on top of that tells me this hamstring is more serious than anybody wanted to admit.

So, here is the schedule he will miss. Week nine, at the Eagles. Week ten, against Washington. Week eleven, against Jacksonville. And week twelve, against the Colts. The earliest he could come back is week thirteen, against the 49ers. That is a big chunk of the season.

Now, Joe Schoen said at the podium that he does not expect this to be a long-term injury. And I want to believe him. But I am going to wait and see, and here is why. Earlier this season, there was hope that Jackson Dart’s injury was not major, and it ended his season. There was hope for Brian Burns, and he tore his ACL. And it took until the day before kickoff in Houston to rule Tremaine Edmunds out for the year. So, when the front office tells me something is not serious, I am going to wait for the proof. I would love for this to be a four-game thing. But this team keeps getting snakebitten by injuries this season.

And the timing is what makes it so frustrating. Think about Tracy’s season. He spent a good chunk of it in the doghouse, for fumbling and for missing blocks that got his quarterback hit. And then he turned it around. All three of his touchdowns this season came in the last two weeks, two against the Saints and one in Houston. He graded as the best pass protector on the entire team against New Orleans. Harbaugh said he had earned more carries, praising his assignments, his physical running, his acceleration, and his speed. The run game finally had real success against the Texans, 188 yards on the ground. And now the Giants have to keep it going without number 29.

And it is not just the running that the Giants lose. Think about McCarthy. A young quarterback in his second start, on the road, in a hostile division game, needs a running back who can pick up the blitz. Tracy was that guy. The Eagles are going to look at the tape, see a young quarterback, and send pressure. Now somebody else has to stand in there and protect him. Singletary has a reputation as a reliable pass protector, which is one reason he has stuck in this league as long as he has. But this is a real test, and it is something I will be watching closely in Philadelphia.

Now, the move to replace him. With Tracy on injured reserve, the Giants were down to only two active running backs, Cam Skattebo and Devin Singletary. So, they went out and signed an undrafted rookie running back who spent the preseason with the Chargers and was on their practice squad. He is 5 foot 11, around 200 pounds, and he played in three preseason games for Los Angeles. And honestly, I like the numbers. Twenty-three carries for 166 yards, that is 7.2 yards per carry, with a long of 34 and a touchdown. And in his final preseason game, against the Rams, he ran for more than 118 yards on just nine carries and scored. Now, that is preseason, and I am not trying to crown anybody. But it tells me he can make something happen when he gets the ball in his hands. He is a quick back with some burst, which is a different style from Skattebo.

And you know I have to say it. I bet John Harbaugh got a little inside scoop from his brother, Jim, who coaches the Chargers. When your brother has been watching a young back in practice every day, that is a pretty good scouting report.

So, here is how the running back room looks now. Cam Skattebo is your lead back. Devin Singletary is the backup and the guy who just won the Houston game in overtime. The new rookie is the third back. And Patrick Ricard is the fullback, and Giants fans should know that name, because Ricard spent years blocking for Harbaugh in Baltimore. That is a coach who knows exactly what he has in his fullback.

But here is the concern. Skattebo is now the clear number one, and he said he felt his hamstring grab on his biggest run in Houston, that 42-yarder in overtime. He also fumbled five times in his first 13 career games, according to ESPN. He ran with a fierce joy in Houston, 17 carries for 124 yards, but now he is going to carry a bigger load, and the Giants cannot afford for him to go down too. Singletary has to be ready for a bigger role, and the rookie has to be ready to contribute sooner than anyone expected.

Now, the second move, on the defensive side. The Giants signed veteran defensive tackle Dean Lowry to the practice squad. And you might remember, it was reported about a month ago that he was expected to sign, and then it just did not happen. Well, now it finally did. This is a player with a lot of football behind him. He was a fourth-round pick by the Packers in 2016 and spent his first seven seasons in Green Bay. Then he spent time with the Vikings and the Steelers. He was with Pittsburgh in 2025 too, but he never played, because he tore his ACL in training camp in August of 2025. So, this is a comeback story.

What I like is the experience. We are talking about 132 games played and 85 starts in the NFL. He had a really good season in 2020, and he put up 43 tackles in 2022. And he is joining a position of real need. Since Dexter Lawrence left, this defensive tackle group has not been as strong as I would like. D.J. Reader has been really good this year, but outside of him, there has not been much. Shelby Harris has made some plays. Darius Alexander has struggled against the run. Remember, the Giants worked out nose tackles a couple of weeks ago, and this is the result. Lowry might not be a star at this stage of his career, but a veteran who knows how to hold his gap could help a run defense that gave up 175 yards to the Saints.

And the practice squad is actually a smart way to bring him in. Coming off a torn ACL, he has not played a real snap in more than a year. The practice squad lets the Giants get him back into football shape without using an active roster spot. And when they need him, they can elevate him for game days, the same way they did with Trace Ford before Ford earned his full roster spot. If Lowry looks like himself in practice, he could be on the field in a few weeks. If he does not, the Giants did not give anything up. Low risk, real upside. That is the kind of move this front office has been making all season.

Now, the part that really got my attention. The trade deadline. Schoen was asked whether the long-term absences of Brian Burns and Jackson Dart change how the Giants approach the deadline. And his answer was basically that it does not affect them, that they are where they are. But then he said more. He said the Giants are going to look at all the options out there. He said certain teams have already started making trades, that the Giants have been in contact with those teams, and that they have a pretty good idea who may or may not be available. And he made it clear that any move has to make sense for both the short term and the long term.

And Ryan Dunleavy, who covers the Giants closely, said the team is going to be aggressive at the deadline. Now, we will see if that turns out to be true. But people who are close to this team believe the Giants could make some moves. And after hearing Schoen, I lean that way too. This is not a front office that is going to panic and throw assets at the problem because of the injuries. But it is a front office that has done its homework and is ready to pull the trigger if the right deal is there.

And speaking of trades, look at what happened in Indianapolis. The Colts sent Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. And then DeForest Buckner was traded back to the 49ers, for what is expected to be a 2027 fourth-round pick.

And I owe you a receipt here. A couple of weeks ago, I told you Quenton Nelson should be the Giants’ first call if the Colts started selling. I said I could see the Giants opening with a second-round pick. Well, Seattle got him for a third and a fifth, which is less than I thought it would cost. Do I wish the Giants had been the team to make that call? Honestly, yes. A top guard for a third-round pick would have been a great deal for a run game that was struggling. But to be fair, the run game just put up 188 yards in Houston, so maybe the Giants felt the line was trending in the right direction. And we do not know what conversations happened behind the scenes. Buckner going back to San Francisco for a fourth also tells you the price of veterans at this deadline is not sky-high.

So, that leaves one big name still hanging over Indianapolis. Jonathan Taylor. And now the dots are connecting. The Giants are expected to be aggressive. Tracy is out for at least four games. And Jordan Raanan, who covers the Giants, seems to think the Colts are the connection. Now, I am not going to sit here and tell you it is happening. But you can see why people are putting it together.

So, do the Giants go all-in for Taylor? Here is the problem. Look at their draft picks. For the 2027 draft, the Giants do not have a guaranteed fourth-round pick, and they do not have a fifth, since that went to Minnesota for McCarthy. The compensatory pick formula projects them to get a fourth, but nobody knows that for sure. What they have for sure is a first, a second, a third, and two sixth-round picks. So, it is a real question whether they would even want to move one of those day-two picks, when they would only have two picks left on day two and two on day three.

And let me be consistent with what I have said on this channel. A couple of weeks ago, I said I would not give up a first-round pick for a running back while the offensive line was a big concern. I still feel that way about a first. But the situation has changed. Tracy is out. Skattebo is carrying the load with a hamstring that grabbed on him. And the Giants are in first place. If the Colts are willing to take something like a third-round pick, the way they did for Nelson, I would think about it seriously. Taylor would give this offense a true lead back for the stretch run. But his contract is big, and the Giants have to think about the long term, exactly like Schoen said.

And do not forget the other name we talked about last week. Budda Baker. With Edmunds and Burns gone for the season, the defense might be an even bigger need than the backfield. If the Giants only make one move, I still think it should be on defense. But Schoen sounded like a general manager who might make more than one.

Now, the timing. The trade deadline is November 10th, which is two days after the Giants play the Eagles in week nine. And I think that game could matter here. If the Giants win in Philadelphia, they are 5 and 2 and 1, with a real cushion in the division, and maybe that makes them a little more aggressive. If they lose, it could change the temperature in the building. Either way, the next two weeks are going to be wild.

And here is one more thing about the Colts selling off veterans. The Giants play Indianapolis in week twelve. If the Colts keep moving players, the team the Giants face later in November could look very different from the one that started the season. Without Nelson on the line and Buckner in the middle of the defense, Indianapolis just got weaker in the trenches. That does not mean it will be an easy game. But a team in full sell mode, with a front office in transition, is usually not a team at its best in late November. That is a small piece of good news hiding inside all of this trade chatter.

Now, let me get to what Schoen said about the players already on this roster, because some of it really matters.

First, Deonte Banks. Schoen said he is really proud of Banks for how he put his head down, worked, and overcame adversity. And I completely agree. Banks is the feel-good story of this team. People called him a bust. All offseason, at every checkpoint, veteran workouts, OTAs, minicamp, training camp, he was a winner. Then he had his 98-yard pick six earlier this season, and he put together another great game against the Texans. He is tied for second in the league in passes defended. And Schoen brought up possible extensions during the bye week. That is a name I would keep an eye on. Banks looks like a player in line for a real payday.

Then, the quarterback room. Schoen called it really good, and you could see him light up a little when he talked about it. He was proud of how McCarthy played in Houston. He said McCarthy is their starter right now, at least for this season. And he said it would be a good problem to have if they had to decide between McCarthy and Jackson Dart, or even to have three quarterbacks they believe in. That is a confident front office.

And here is what I found really interesting. Schoen talked about the work the Giants did on McCarthy before the 2024 draft. He said that kind of research may not pay off right away, maybe not even in that year’s draft. But down the road, it can let you make a move like the one they made. Basically, the homework paid off, and it made them confident enough to trade a fifth-round pick for McCarthy. And I think that is the same mindset they will bring to this deadline. Do the work early, and act when the moment comes.

On Jackson Dart, he is still on crutches. But Schoen shared that Dart believes that if the Giants make the playoffs, he will be able to come back. That is just the kid’s mindset. And I love that attitude. I would not count on it, because a knee injury like that takes time. But if the Giants make a deep run, imagine Dart coming back in January. You have to respect a player who is attacking his rehab like that.

And then there is Abdul Carter. Schoen said Carter has been phenomenal since the day he got here. He did not get a sack in Houston, but he had six pressures, he drew holding penalties, and Harbaugh said he took command of the locker room at halftime. In his second season, Carter is turning into the leader this defense needs, especially with Burns out for the year. And next to him, rookie Arvell Reese is putting together a season that should have him in the Defensive Rookie of the Year conversation.

So, the bye week is not a quiet week. Schoen is going to sit down with Harbaugh, go over the whole roster, talk about extensions, and look at trade candidates. The Giants lost Tyrone Tracy Jr. They added a rookie back with some burst and a veteran defensive tackle with 85 career starts. And they are sitting at 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, with a quarterback their general manager believes in and a deadline coming up in eleven days.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, Skattebo’s hamstring, because he is now the lead back. Number two, whether the new rookie gets a role in Philadelphia. Number three, any real reporting on Jonathan Taylor or Budda Baker, not lists or guesses, actual reports. And number four, the Eagles game itself, because the result could decide how aggressive this front office gets on November 10th.

I have said it all season, through every injury and every setback, and this week is no different. This team can go all the way.
