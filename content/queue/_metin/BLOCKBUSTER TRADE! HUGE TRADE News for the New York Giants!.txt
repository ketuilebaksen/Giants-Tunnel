The Jonathan Taylor trade is not dead. In fact, ESPN’s latest reporting says it is very much alive, and the Giants are right in the middle of it. Here is where it stands. The Giants made an offer. The Colts did not accept it. And a lot of fans took that as the door closing. But according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, executives around the league believe Taylor is worth about a second-round pick plus an additional pick. That is roughly where the Giants’ offer reportedly is. The problem is that the Colts may be holding out for the equivalent of a first-round pick. And one AFC executive summed it up perfectly. They want a bunch, he said, but this one still has a decent chance to happen. Indianapolis just is not giving him away. So this is not a no. It is a not yet, and not for that price. And ESPN’s Dan Graziano went even further. He said he expects Taylor to be traded before the November 10th deadline, and he would not be surprised at all if it was the Giants. Today, I am going to break down the gap between what the market says and what the Colts want, why the Colts are not lowering their price, what it means for the Giants’ 2028 picks, and the defensive tackles ESPN just connected to New York. Let’s get into it.

If the Colts’ price is a first-round pick, do the Giants pay it or walk away?

Let me start by clearing something up, because I have seen a lot of confusion this week. Jordan Raanan reported that the Giants made an offer for Taylor. He did not report that the Colts flatly rejected it and ended talks. What he said was that the Colts reportedly want a first-round pick, they might accept a second plus more, and the Giants have not closed that gap. Separately, Tom Pelissero reported on Halloween that the Colts turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick, from a team he did not name. Those could be connected. They might not be. Either way, the Giants are in the conversation. They are just not at the Colts’ number yet.

And I actually think the Giants may have made more than one offer, or at least had more than one conversation. Remember, Joe Schoen said during the bye that the Giants had already been in contact with the teams that had started selling. Indianapolis was the most active seller in the league. So it makes sense that the Giants felt out where the Colts were, made an offer to gauge the temperature, and are now deciding whether to adjust. That is how smart teams negotiate. You do not drop your best number on the first call. That is my read, not a report.

Now, here is the gap that this entire negotiation lives in. On one side, you have the market. Fowler said executives he has talked to believe Taylor is worth a second-round pick plus an additional pick. On the other side, you have the Colts. Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter have both reported that the Colts are looking for something like the Christian McCaffrey package, the second, third, fourth, and fifth-round picks San Francisco sent Carolina in 2022. A lot of people consider that roughly equal to a first-round pick. So the market says a second plus something. The Colts say a first or the equivalent. That is the gap.

And why are the Colts holding firm? Because they can. Graziano wrote that he gets the sense enough teams are interested in Taylor that it is too soon for the Colts to even think about lowering their demands. And Schefter said the Colts have offers on the table from several teams, and they are waiting to see if any of those teams sweeten the pot. That is a bidding war. The Colts are not upset about the Giants’ offer. They are sitting with a stack of offers and asking, who is going to come back with more?

And remember who else is in this. ESPN listed the Broncos, Packers, Vikings, Seahawks, and Giants as teams with at least some interest. The Athletic’s Daniel Popper added Cincinnati as a possible landing spot. Some of those teams have issues. Minnesota has cap problems, according to Jonathan Jones. Seattle got two running backs back from injury. But even if the field is smaller than it looks, it only takes one team to say yes to the Colts’ price.

And here is the timing piece that I find fascinating. Schefter believes the Colts will ultimately move Taylor, but he said it could be today, tomorrow, this week, or next week. Think about that. Nobody knows exactly when this happens, including the people reporting on it. And the deadline is Tuesday. That means it could happen before the Eagles game on Sunday, or it could happen on Monday or Tuesday, after the Eagles game, right before the Giants play Washington on Thursday night.

Now, I owe you a receipt here, and I want to be upfront about it. A few videos ago, I predicted that Taylor would not be a Giant by November 10th. I said I thought the Colts’ price would stay too high for this front office. ESPN’s insiders are now leaning the other way. Graziano expects Taylor to be traded and would not be surprised if it was the Giants. Schefter thinks the Colts will move him. I am not changing my prediction yet. I still think the Giants stay disciplined. But I want you to know that some of the best-connected people in football disagree with me. If they are right and I am wrong, I will own it.

So let’s talk about the pick problem, because this is where it gets complicated for the Giants. The McCaffrey package was built on mid-round picks. A second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth. And the Giants do not have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks. The fourth went in the move up for Malachi Fields, and the fifth went to Minnesota in the J.J. McCarthy trade. So the exact pieces of a McCaffrey-style deal, the Giants cannot hand over this year.

What they do have is basically all of their 2028 picks. And remember what John Harbaugh joked about last week. He said 2028 is always easier to put on the credit card. But here is the real question. Will the Colts take 2028 picks? A rebuilding team usually wants picks as soon as possible, so it can start building right away. If the Colts want 2027 picks and the Giants want to pay with 2028 picks, that is a real hurdle. A 2028 pick is worth a little less to most teams, because it is further away. So the Giants might have to pay a little more in 2028 to match what the Colts would get in 2027.

And this is where a conditional pick could be the answer. Conditional picks are common at the deadline. They let both sides bridge a gap without either side giving in completely. For example, the Giants could offer a 2028 third-round pick that becomes a second-round pick if certain things happen. Maybe if the Giants win the division. Maybe if Taylor hits a certain number of yards or plays a certain percentage of snaps. That gives the Colts the chance at a higher pick, and it gives the Giants protection if things do not work out. Art Stapleton’s trade idea included a 2028 second that could become a first. That kind of structure could be how the Giants and Colts meet in the middle. I want to be clear, nobody has reported that the Giants have offered a conditional pick. That is just how I would try to close the gap.

And think about the Colts’ side of the timeline. Taylor said this week that the rumors have been dragging, that at this point it just becomes noise, and that clarity should come by Tuesday. He has not asked for a trade. He told the owner he loves the organization. But the owner did not promise him he would stay. And according to The Athletic, trading him would leave the Colts with about 10 million dollars in dead money in 2027. So the Colts are weighing a lot of things. They can keep a happy star who is signed through 2028. Or they can take the best offer and start the rebuild with more picks. Every hour that passes without an offer at their price, they have to ask themselves whether that price is realistic. As the deadline gets closer, the leverage starts to shift toward the buyer.

So what would I do? My ceiling has not changed. A 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 third-round pick. That is it. That is already more than the offer Pelissero said was turned down, and it shows real interest. And I want to explain why I would even go that far. Because this is not a one-year move. Taylor is signed through 2028. Jaxson Dart comes back next year. Brian Burns comes back next year. Tremaine Edmunds comes back next year. The Giants will have their first-round pick, they will have money to spend in free agency, and they will have Taylor as the lead back for two more seasons. You are not trading for ten games. You are trading for three playoff pushes.

But if the Colts need more than that, a first-round pick, or two seconds, or a McCaffrey-style package, I am out. And I would feel fine about it. The Giants ran for 188 yards in Houston. Devin Singletary just scored a walk-off touchdown. Cam Skattebo just had a 124-yard game. Tyrone Tracy Jr. will be back around week 13. The running back room is thin right now, but it is not broken.

And here is the funny thing about being disciplined. If the Giants do end up paying two seconds or a first for Taylor, a lot of people, including me, would say they overpaid. And then the first time he breaks a 70-yard touchdown run in December, everybody would forget about the price. That is how trades work. Fans judge the deal by how it looks on paper on Tuesday. Then they judge it by how it looks on the field in January. Schoen and Harbaugh have to think about both.

And remember the price the Bears paid for Jeffery Simmons. Two first-round picks, a fourth, and they got a fifth back. That is what an All-Pro defensive tackle costs in the middle of a season. The Colts are watching those deals too. When they see a team pay two firsts for a defensive tackle, a first-round equivalent for the best running back on the market does not look crazy to them. But running back value is different. Most front offices do not pay premium prices for running backs anymore, because they have seen how quickly backs can decline. That is why the executives Fowler talked to put Taylor at a second plus something. The Colts are betting that one team will break from that thinking. The Giants have to decide whether they want to be that team.

And there is one more thing Harbaugh said last week that matters here. He said the Giants would think about how any trade affects the locker room, and that they might even talk to some of the players before making a move. If Taylor comes, the first conversation should be with Skattebo and Singletary. Skattebo just had the best game of his career. Singletary just scored a walk-off touchdown. They need to know they still have roles. A great locker room can handle a star arriving. But it has to be done the right way.

Now, running back is not the only place the Giants are looking. And ESPN just connected them to two defensive tackles. First, Vita Vea from Tampa Bay. Fowler reported that Vea is on the Giants’ radar, but the expectation is that the Buccaneers will keep him. And that makes sense. Vea is one of the best run-stuffing defensive tackles in football. He is a massive presence in the middle, and he has been the anchor of Tampa’s defense for years. Teams that are competing do not usually trade players like that.

But imagine Vea next to DJ Reader. That would be two big bodies in the middle, and it would be a nightmare for any team trying to run the ball. With Dexter Lawrence gone, that is exactly the kind of interior presence the Giants have been missing. Based on the contract breakdowns I have seen, Vea’s cap hit this year would not be huge for a team acquiring him midseason, but it would be a lot bigger next year. And the Buccaneers would want real value back. With the Giants missing their 2027 fourth and fifth, it would probably take a future pick or more. I do not expect it to happen, because Tampa is not expected to sell. But I love that the Giants are asking.

The second name is Grover Stewart from the Colts, and Fowler called him a more realistic option. Indianapolis has been in sell mode. They traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle. They traded DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. They fired general manager Chris Ballard. And Stewart, who is 33 years old, looks like he could be the next veteran out the door. He is a big, physical run stopper, exactly the kind of player who helps on third and short.

And here is my price on Stewart. A sixth-round pick. The Giants have two sixths in 2027, and I would offer one of them for a 33-year-old defensive tackle who can help the run defense for the rest of this season. I would not go higher than that. At his age, you are buying help for ten games and a possible playoff run, not a long-term piece. And Harbaugh said any deal should not be a one-and-done. Stewart is closer to a one-and-done, so the price has to be small.

And this is where it gets interesting. Stewart and Taylor are on the same team. If the Giants are already deep in talks with the Colts about Taylor, adding Stewart to the conversation could change the math. Maybe the Giants pay a little more for Taylor if Stewart comes with him. Maybe the Colts like getting two veterans off their books in one deal. Art Stapleton floated that kind of idea a couple of weeks ago. It was his idea, not a report. But the more you look at it, the more it makes sense for both sides.

And remember how hard the Giants have chased a defensive tackle. They called the Titans about Jeffery Simmons, and Harbaugh called the owner. They were interested in Daron Payne before he broke his foot. Raanan said he thinks the Giants are more likely to trade for a defensive tackle than a running back. I predicted a couple of videos ago that the Giants will make a defensive tackle trade before the deadline. I am sticking with that one.

Now, none of this matters if the Giants do not take care of business on Sunday. Philadelphia is a division game, and I think it is the biggest game of the season so far. We will probably say that every week from here on out, but right now it is true. If the Giants beat the Eagles, it is a major step toward winning the NFC East for the first time since 2011.

So let’s look at the injury report. Abdul Carter missed Monday and Wednesday with an illness, but he was a full participant on Thursday. He should be good to go. Cam Skattebo did not practice on Wednesday or Thursday with his hamstring, and the outlook on him does not look great. Two missed practices is noteworthy. Friday will tell us a lot.

Malik Nabers did not practice on Thursday because of a knee issue. But here is some good news. Nabers said he is good for Sunday. I will take him at his word, but I will still be watching Friday’s report closely. Andrew Thomas has been limited with a groin. Jermaine Eluemunor has been limited with a calf. Charlie Jones, the Giants’ returner, has a hand injury. I expect most of those players to play, but we have seen guys get limited on Wednesday and Thursday, sit out Friday, and get ruled out. So keep your eyes on it.

And there is good news on the tight end side. Theo Johnson was a full participant all week after missing time with a neck injury. That gives McCarthy another big target, especially if Nabers is limited. Malik Harrison and Colton Hood were also full participants by Thursday.

And on the other side, Philadelphia is dealing with its own problems. Joe Milton is making his first start for the Eagles in place of Tanner McKee. Saquon Barkley missed practice on Wednesday with hamstring and back issues, although he was upgraded to limited later in the week. DeVonta Smith missed practice too. That is a lot of uncertainty around a first-time starter.

So here is how I see the next five days. Sunday, the Giants play in Philadelphia. Tuesday, the trade deadline. Thursday, Washington at MetLife on a short week. That is the most important stretch of the season. A win in Philadelphia could make the Giants more comfortable walking away from Taylor’s price. A loss, or more injuries, could push them to pay more. And either way, I think they add a defensive tackle.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, Friday’s injury designations for Skattebo and Nabers. Number two, whether the Colts lower their price on Taylor as the deadline gets closer. Number three, whether the Giants come back with a better offer, maybe using 2028 picks. Number four, whether Grover Stewart ends up in a deal, with or without Taylor. And number five, the result in Philadelphia, because that could decide what the Giants do on Tuesday.

And here is my prediction. I still do not think Taylor ends up a Giant, although ESPN’s insiders think I might be wrong. I think the Giants add a defensive tackle. And I still think the Giants break the streak in Philadelphia on Sunday. You can hold me to all three.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, with an offer on the table for one of the best running backs in football, two defensive tackles on their radar, and a division game on Sunday. It is a not yet, not a no. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
