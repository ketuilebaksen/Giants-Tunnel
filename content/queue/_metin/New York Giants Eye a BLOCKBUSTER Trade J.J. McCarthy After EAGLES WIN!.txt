The streak is over. The New York Giants went into Philadelphia and won, 19 to 11, for their first win in that building since 2013. Thirteen straight losses, gone. And now, with the trade deadline on Tuesday, the Giants are in a position nobody would have believed a few weeks ago. They are 5 and 2 and 1. They are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. Dallas is 4 and 5. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 7, with seven straight losses. Before the game, one analyst said that if the Giants beat the Eagles, it would just add fuel to the fire and make it even more likely that they make an impact move before the deadline. Well, they beat the Eagles. But it was not clean. J.J. McCarthy got hurt. Jameis Winston came off the bench and threw the go-ahead touchdown. Arvell Reese had three sacks. And the Giants had to survive a dropped would-be 80-yard touchdown and a Hail Mary attempt on the final play. Today, I am going to break down the win, what it means for McCarthy, and why I think it changes what the Giants do on Tuesday. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with the receipts, because I made a bunch of predictions for this game, and you deserve to know how they turned out. Prediction number one, the Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. Got it. Prediction number two, the pass rush forces Joe Milton into at least one big mistake. Got that too. Arvell Reese forced a fumble that Micah McFadden recovered, and the defense sacked Milton on the final play. But prediction number three, McCarthy throws his first touchdown pass as a Giant. Wrong. Jameis Winston threw the only touchdown. Prediction number four, Singletary has a big day. Wrong. The Giants had just 16 rushing yards in the first half. And prediction number five, the Giants win the penalty battle. Very wrong. They had seven penalties before halftime. So two out of five. I will take the win, and I will own the misses.

Now, let’s go through how this game played out, because it was a grind. The Giants opened the scoring with two field goals from rookie kicker Dominic Zvada, from 53 and 49 yards. Then Saquon Barkley punched in a three-yard touchdown run for the Eagles. And after a Giants penalty on the extra point attempt, Philadelphia went for two, and Joe Milton ran it in. So the Eagles led 8 to 6.

And then the game turned into a slugfest. The Eagles’ punter, Braden Mann, boomed an 86-yard punt at the end of the first half, the longest punt in the NFL since 2001. The Giants struggled to run the ball. McCarthy was under pressure all half, and he was sacked five times before halftime. Charlie Jones, who was questionable with a thumb injury, fumbled a punt and gave Philadelphia the ball at the Giants’ 47-yard line. Deonte Banks was flagged for a 30-yard pass interference. And Malik Nabers dropped a pass that could have been a big play, and he was frustrated enough to throw his helmet onto the bench late in the second quarter.

That is a lot of things going wrong. And this is exactly the kind of game the old Giants lost in Philadelphia. A sloppy first half, a loud crowd, a couple of special teams mistakes, and the Eagles hanging around. But this team did not fold.

Then came the scary moment. In the third quarter, with the Giants trailing 8 to 6, McCarthy ran the ball and took a hit from Zack Baun. He hurt his hip and had to come out. And in came Jameis Winston. Remember, Winston started four games earlier this season while Dart was out. He went 2 and 1 and 1 as the starter. And he did not miss a beat. He came in for the final two plays of a drive and one more possession. He hit Malachi Fields for 14 yards to the Eagles’ three-yard line. Then he threw a five-yard touchdown pass to Isaiah Likely. Giants 13, Eagles 8.

And think about that. A pregame analyst called his shot this week, predicting a touchdown to Isaiah Likely. He was right about the receiver. He just had the wrong quarterback. Winston finished 6 of 8 for 71 yards with the touchdown, plus a 10-yard scramble. That is what a great backup does. He comes in cold, in the most hostile building in the division, and he gives you the lead.

And then McCarthy came back. He returned for the final play of the third quarter and finished the game. He went 12 of 19 for 127 yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions. It was not a pretty day for him. But he protected the football again, and he stayed in the game after taking a shot. That says a lot about his toughness.

Now, the fourth quarter belonged to Zvada. He hit field goals from 38 and 49 yards. That made him four for four on the day, including the 53-yarder. And I have to say, that is a huge story. In Houston, Zvada missed two field goals, and I questioned whether the Giants should bring in competition at kicker. I said that in one of my videos. He answered it. Four for four on the road, in a division game, with the wind and the noise. That is a rookie kicker growing up in front of our eyes. Credit to him.

And think about how much those kicks mattered. The Giants scored 19 points, and 12 of them came from Zvada’s leg. In a game where the offense struggled to finish drives, settling for field goals is not ideal. But making all of them is the difference between winning and losing. If Zvada misses even one of those four, this game is tied or worse at the end. The Giants have a plus-10 point differential now, after sitting at plus-2 coming into this game. Close games are this team’s identity, and close games are won by kickers.

The Eagles added a 45-yard field goal from Jake Elliott, and it was 19 to 11. But the Eagles had one more chance. And this is where the game almost slipped away. Milton threw deep to receiver Darius Cooper for what could have been an 80-yard touchdown. Cooper dropped it, as Ar’Darius Washington went by. That is a huge break for the Giants. And then, on the final play, Dennard Wilson sent a blitz, and the defense sacked Milton to stop a Hail Mary attempt at the buzzer. Game over. Streak over.

Now, let’s talk about the star of the game. Arvell Reese. The rookie linebacker had two sacks and nine tackles by halftime. He forced a fumble that McFadden recovered. And he finished with three sacks of Joe Milton. Three sacks, in Philadelphia, in his rookie season. Remember what Joe Schoen said about him during the bye. As big as he is, as fast as he is, as productive as he has been, the kid just turned 21 a couple of months ago. It’s insane. The sky is the limit. On Sunday, the whole league saw why.

And it shows you how creative Dennard Wilson has been with this defense. Without Brian Burns and Tremaine Edmunds, Wilson has been moving Reese all over the field. Linebacker, edge rusher, blitzer. And on Sunday, Reese was the best player on the field. Milton went 18 of 34 for 169 yards. He did run in the two-point conversion. But outside of the Barkley touchdown and the one deep shot that got dropped, the Giants’ defense controlled this game.

Now, let’s talk about McCarthy’s hip, because that is the biggest question coming out of this game. We do not have an official update yet on how serious it is. He was able to return and finish the game, which is a good sign. But the Giants play Washington on Thursday night. That is a short week. If McCarthy is limited, Winston is ready, and he just proved it again. But this is something to watch closely in the practice reports this week.

And this is where the analysts’ comments before the game get really interesting. One of them said he did not expect McCarthy to play the A-plus game he played in Houston. He said McCarthy made no mistakes, threw the ball down the field, was accurate, was physical, and was in and out of the huddle with no problems with play calling or clock management, which is what you would expect from a guy who had only been there three weeks. He said if McCarthy could play 75 to 85 percent of that level for the rest of the season, the Giants are going to have a good problem at quarterback going into next year’s draft.

Another analyst said McCarthy used to suffer from what he called fastball syndrome. Everything was a fastball. A throw to the flat at 52 miles an hour that bounces off the running back’s head. But in Houston, he said McCarthy was layering passes, using his legs to step up and climb the pocket, and when he had to uncork one, the arm talent was always there.

And John Harbaugh talked about this too, before the Eagles game. He said you really get to know a player by being around him in person all the time. You think you know a player from tape, but you don’t until you see him in person. And he said the chess match is on. Defensive coordinators were going to study what the Giants did against Houston and game plan against it. And that is exactly what Philadelphia did. They took away the run in the first half, they got pressure on McCarthy, and they made the Giants earn everything. McCarthy did not have a great day. But the Giants still won. That is what good teams do.

And there is one more thing to take from this game about McCarthy. He was sacked five times before halftime, he got hit hard enough to leave the game, and he came back. A young quarterback who is still learning a new offense could have been rattled by that. Instead, he returned for the final play of the third quarter and closed out the game without a turnover. In his two starts as a Giant, he has not thrown a single interception. That is the trait Harbaugh values most. Protect the football, and give your defense a chance. On Sunday, the defense did the rest.

Now, let’s get to the trade deadline, because Tuesday is tomorrow. Before the game, one analyst said the Giants seem hellbent on making a move and upgrading their roster before the deadline. He said that if they beat the Eagles, that just adds fuel to the fire. He said the Giants see an opportunity to win the NFC East, and they are aware that there has not been a home playoff game at MetLife Stadium in 15 years. They want to do something.

And I think he is right. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. Dallas is two games behind in the standings, and three losses behind in the loss column. Washington and Philadelphia are buried. One analyst said the Giants’ magic number is five. Find five more wins, and you win the division. With nine games left, that is very doable. This is the window. And the Giants know it.

But what kind of move? This is where the debate gets interesting. One analyst said that if the Giants are going to be aggressive, which Joe Schoen has said they will be, he would encourage them to go get a player who is going to be part of the defensive core for the future. He said he still thinks running backs are a luxury. A 20 million dollar running back, he said, you better feel like you are going to the Super Bowl. He would rather firm up the defense.

And I lean that way too, especially after Sunday. Look at what won this game. Arvell Reese and the defense. Three sacks from a rookie, a forced fumble, a stop on the final play. The defense is the strength of this team. And with Dexter Lawrence gone, Jeffery Simmons in Chicago, and Daron Payne on injured reserve, the interior of the defensive line is still the biggest need. Jordan Raanan said he thinks the Giants are more likely to trade for a defensive tackle than a running back. Names like Grover Stewart and Christian Barmore have come up. I predicted a few videos ago that the Giants will add a defensive tackle before the deadline. I am sticking with it.

But here is the counterpoint. The Giants had 16 rushing yards in the first half on Sunday. Cam Skattebo was out with his hamstring. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve. The running back room is thin. And that is why the Jonathan Taylor conversation is still alive. The Giants made an offer, according to Raanan. Albert Breer called them a serious suitor. The Colts want more. But after a game where the Giants could not run the ball, the urge to add a back has to be stronger.

And then there is the timing problem. One of the analysts made a great point before the game. The Giants play Washington on Thursday night. If they do a trade on Tuesday, can a new player be ready to play on Thursday? Probably not, in a new system. Another analyst pushed back and said teams have told him running backs are easier to just plug in. But even then, two practices before a Thursday night game is not much. Ideally, a deal would have been done before the Eagles game. Now, any player the Giants add is more about the rest of the season than about Thursday.

So here is my take, and this is my opinion, not a report. If the Colts lower their price on Taylor to a second plus a mid-round pick, the Giants should do it, because he is signed through 2028 and he would help for three playoff pushes. If not, they should walk away and add a defensive tackle instead, and trust Skattebo to come back. And after Sunday, I would rather see the defensive tackle than a running back at a premium price.

And let me talk about Harbaugh’s culture, because one analyst brought up something I loved. He said Harbaugh sacrificed the New Orleans game by not giving Cam Skattebo the ball after his overtime fumble in Washington. He made a point about that player. And maybe he lost a game because of it. But he ended up saving the season, because Skattebo came out in Houston running like a man possessed. The analyst said he would not have wanted to tackle Skattebo that day. He said what Harbaugh is doing is brilliant.

And another analyst said Harbaugh has changed the dynamics for the Giants. He called him a stud and a culture guy, and said the Giants have needed that. You saw it on Sunday. A sloppy first half. A quarterback injury. A muffed punt. Seven penalties before halftime. And this team still found a way to win in a building where it had lost 13 straight times. That is culture.

And now, one of those analysts was asked, who is going to win the NFC East, the Giants or the Cowboys? And he said the Giants, assuming they do not lose any other significant players. He pointed out that the Giants already beat Dallas once. And he thinks they might add a player or two, because they need some refortification in certain areas.

But I want to be careful, because this team still has problems to fix. The penalties have to stop. Seven in one half is not going to work against better teams. The running game has to come back. The special teams mistakes, like the muffed punt, cannot happen in December. And McCarthy’s protection has to be better. Five sacks before halftime is way too many. The Giants won this game with defense and a kicker. They will need more than that to win in January.

And the next test is right around the corner. Washington comes to MetLife on Thursday night. The Giants tied them 27 to 27 earlier this season. Washington just lost to the Rams, and it is 2 and 5 and 1. But it will be a short week for both teams, and the Giants will be dealing with McCarthy’s hip and Skattebo’s hamstring. Harbaugh called the Eagles and Washington the two most important games of the season. The Giants won the first one. Now they need the second.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, McCarthy’s hip and his practice status this week. Number two, whether the Giants make a trade before the deadline on Tuesday, and whether it is a running back or a defensive tackle. Number three, whether Skattebo is back for Thursday. Number four, the penalty count, because it has to come down. And number five, Arvell Reese, because he is turning into a star.

And here is my prediction for the deadline. I still think the Giants add a defensive tackle. I still do not think Jonathan Taylor ends up a Giant. You can hold me to both, and I will own the receipts either way.

The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. They are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. They just ended 13 years of pain in Philadelphia. They have a rookie linebacker who looks like a future star, a backup quarterback who keeps delivering, a kicker who just went four for four, and a head coach who has completely changed this culture. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
