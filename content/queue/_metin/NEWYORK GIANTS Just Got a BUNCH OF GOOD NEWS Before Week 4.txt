Alright friends, you know the situation the Giants were carrying into week four. Jackson Dart done for the year. Brian Burns down against the Titans. Andrew Thomas missing practice after practice, and nobody feeling great about how Sunday was going to look. Then Friday happened, and honestly, I had to sit back and say it out loud, the Giants just got the best injury news of their entire season, because the final report came out and there is nobody on it. Nobody, folks. And that is only half the story, because the J.J. McCarthy timeline just accelerated in a way I did not see coming, straight from one of the biggest insiders in football, and if Sunday goes sideways for Jameis Winston, the next era might start sooner than any of us planned. Clean health, a quarterback clock ticking louder, a league award with championship history attached to it, and a rookie making records at 21 years old. This is the final update before kickoff, and it is a loaded one. Let's get into all of it.

If you are new here, do not forget to hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing but it helps this channel a lot, and on a weekend like this one, you want every update the moment it drops.

Let's start with the injury report, because that is what set this whole week up, and the arc of it was an emotional rollercoaster. Wednesday, Andrew Thomas does not practice because of the groin. Thursday, same thing, and now the nerves are real, because we have all watched this movie, your franchise left tackle missing back-to-back days while you are trying to protect Jameis Winston is not a recipe for sleep. Tyrone Tracy Jr. was sitting out with a knee on top of it, and Harbaugh's "he should be fine" is, let's be honest, exactly what a coach says whether it is true or not. We learned that the hard way this season. And then Friday arrives, I pull up the final injury report, and folks, I had to refresh the page. There is nobody on it. Nobody doubtful. Nobody questionable. Nobody out. A completely clean injury report, the first one of this entire season, in the same month this team lost its quarterback and its defensive captain in back-to-back weeks. Andrew Thomas was back at practice as a limited participant, which following a rest week is exactly the pattern you want. Tracy's knee had already been upgraded to limited on Thursday and turned into nothing. For a team that has spent a month getting punched by the injury gods, this is as good as a Friday can possibly go.

And let me make the Andrew Thomas pattern explicit, because I want this fan base to recalibrate how it reads these reports. The Giants are clearly running a veteran maintenance program with the big man, early-week rest, Friday ramp, Sunday go. So when you see Thomas missing a Wednesday or a Thursday from here on out, do not panic, Friday is the only day that matters. And here is the honest caution that comes with that same lesson, cutting the other way. Brian Burns missed every single practice two weeks ago, Wednesday through Friday, and still played. So missed practices do not mean a player is out, and full participation does not make a player bulletproof. The reports are clues, not verdicts. Friday's verdict, though, was beautiful, and nowhere more than in the secondary. Deonte Banks, who has missed the last two games with that calf, is off the report completely. Back. I would assume he steps right back in at corner for a healthy chunk of snaps. Tyler Nubin, who missed week three with a calf of his own, also clean, also back. Folks, think about what that means for one single Sunday. A defense that just lost its leader up front gets two defensive backs returning at once, to a unit that was surviving on duct tape, in the same stretch where Joey Porter Jr. is walking through the door. Two weeks ago the secondary was the thinnest room in the building. By Sunday afternoon it might be the deepest. One more housekeeping note, John Michael Schmitz missed Friday for a personal matter, but he is expected to play, so the interior line is intact, and Micah McFadden is not on the report either. Clean across the board. Whatever happens against Arizona, nobody gets to blame the medical tent.

Okay, that takes care of the health side, and it makes the quarterback conversation even spicier, because the McCarthy timeline just moved, and it moved publicly. Ian Rapoport went on the Pat McAfee Show and was asked directly when J.J. McCarthy could play, and the way I took his answer, if Jameis Winston looks okay, or less than okay, you are probably going to see J.J. McCarthy as soon as he is comfortable in the offense. Sit with that phrasing, folks. Not when Winston collapses. Not after a losing streak. As soon as McCarthy is comfortable. Now, I will be honest with you about my own expectations, because this tunnel keeps its receipts. I figured it would take two or three genuinely bad games from Winston before the staff even considered the switch. That is not how it sounds anymore. The way the reporting reads now, if Jameis Winston does not play well against the Cardinals this week, we could realistically see J.J. McCarthy as early as next week against Washington. I am not telling you that is guaranteed, but I am telling you it is no longer out of the cards, and that changes the entire stakes of Sunday. This is not just a winnable home game anymore, folks. For Jameis Winston, this is a job interview with the door open behind him. And the logic underneath it is the same logic we have preached since the trade. You do not spend a 2027 fifth round pick on a former tenth overall selection, a man who went 6 and 4 as a starter, to have him hold a clipboard for fifteen weeks. That is a team with a plan, and I fully believe McCarthy is under center at some point this season. The only variable is the date, and Winston holds the pen on that calendar every single Sunday.

Harbaugh, of course, played it more carefully, and the details are worth parsing. Winston is the starter, full stop, and he took all the first team reps this week. As for McCarthy, Harbaugh said whether he dresses as the number two or the number three, they will see on game day, but he will be ready for whatever he has got to do. That phrasing makes sense with Jake Haener re-signed and sitting in the picture, but folks, you can read between those lines from a mile away. The staff is keeping every option open on purpose. And the strongest evidence that they are serious about compressing this timeline came from the offensive staff's own description of the onboarding, because what they are doing with McCarthy this week is remarkable. They are trying to squeeze months of offensive installation into a matter of days. Extra hours on terminology. Extra hours on protections. Extra hours on concepts, and the actual game plan on top of it, all while he also handles his scout team duties. And here is the detail I genuinely love, the clever little hack inside it, McCarthy is running the scout team offense this week, but the Giants are deliberately using concepts and terminology similar to their own system whenever they can, so that even his Arizona-imitation reps double as Giants classroom time. Remember my gripe from earlier this week about wasting him on scout work? Consider it half-withdrawn, folks, because the staff found a way to make even those reps teach the system. Nobody is putting an artificial timeline on the kid, but every single structural choice says the same thing, get him ready as fast as humanly possible. And I appreciate the honesty in the evaluation too. The staff praised the national championship, the first round pedigree, the exact attributes they were looking for, and then said plainly that there are aspects of his game that need correcting, that he is a capable player and they will keep working. No sugar, no poison, just a real evaluation. That is how this regime talks about everybody, and it is why I trust them.

And then McCarthy himself spoke to the New York media for the first time as a Giant, and the kid won the room. He admitted the trade call was a bit of a shock, then immediately reframed it, at the end of the day it is an opportunity, and he is grateful to be here. He told the story that gave me chills, folks, about loving his visit with the Giants during the 2024 draft process, back when New York was rumored to be taking him sixth overall before the board broke differently. His words, this is a full circle moment. Two years later, the franchise that almost drafted him finally got its man, for a fifth round pick instead of a top ten selection. He called what John Harbaugh built in Baltimore super inspiring. He revealed he has not even spoken to Jim Harbaugh, his college coach, since the trade, which tells you how fast this all moved. And on the playbook, his confidence was simple, he feels like he is picking it up quickly because, in his words, an offense is an offense, and a lot of the concepts are the same. Easy to say, harder to prove, but I will tell you what I would love to see Sunday, and I know I am dreaming, give me one play. One package. A zone read look, a bootleg, something to get the man's cleats wet in front of the home crowd. I do not expect it, folks. But after the week of signals we just read, I will not be shocked by anything anymore.

Alright, the roster ledger, because the front office kept churning all week and the moves tell their own story. The heaviest one first, Brian Burns officially went on injured reserve with the torn ACL from the fourth quarter against Tennessee, and Harbaugh's words about him deserve a moment, he called the man a rock, basically the leader of this defense. And here is the detail that hurts the most, the streak is over. Thirty-seven consecutive appearances since the day Burns arrived in New York, never missed a game in blue, and the number stops at 37 through no fault of his will. Heal up, captain. The building will be waiting. In his place came the reunion we covered this week, Jihad Ward, scooped off Minnesota's practice squad, and the fuller resume makes the move look even smarter. The man spent 2022 and 2023 right here in this building, 34 games, 20 starts, eight sacks in that stretch, so he knows the stadium, the city, the standard. He is 32 now, a former second round pick from way back in 2016, he played for Harbaugh in Baltimore, and just last season he set a career high with 22 quarterback hits while playing all 17 games for the Titans. All seventeen, folks, at age 31, on a front covered by Dennard Wilson's coaching tree. Nobody replaces Brian Burns, let's not pretend otherwise, but as depth moves go, a durable, versatile veteran who requires zero onboarding is exactly the right patch, and Harbaugh said the quiet part out loud anyway, Thibodeaux and Carter are going to have to carry a much bigger workload. The rest of the week's churn, rapid fire. Braxton Berrios and Charlie Jones signed up from the practice squad. Odell Beckham Jr. released, we covered that chapter in full. P.J. Mustipher added to the practice squad for interior beef, Josh Tupou brought back for the same, Jake Haener re-signed to keep the quarterback pipeline stocked, and on Friday, Kendrick Green added to the practice squad with Darian Kinnard released to make the room. A dozen little moves, one clear message, this front office patches holes the day they appear, not the month after.

Now let's get to the happiest part of the week, because the Jevon Holland story keeps growing new chapters. We celebrated the NFC Defensive Player of the Month award when it dropped, but the historical context that came out since is even wilder than we knew. Since 1970, folks, Holland is one of exactly two NFL players, two, with at least three interceptions, two forced fumbles, and a fumble recovery in the first three games of a season. The other was Domingo Bryant, in 1987. That is a list of two names across more than half a century of football, and one of them plays safety for your New York Giants right now. He sealed the month with that end zone interception of Cam Ward with 17 seconds left to lock up the 12 to 7 win, and get this, three games into the season he has already set a career high for interceptions in a season. Harbaugh put it simply, this is the kind of player he has always been, and it is wonderful to see him flourishing. And the franchise history attached to this award reads like a prophecy, folks. Holland is the first Giant to win a player of the month honor since Landon Collins in November of 2016. The only other Giants defensive back to ever win it was Jason Sehorn, back in December 1997. And before Collins, the most recent Giant winner was Jason Pierre-Paul in December of 2011. You remember what happened at the end of that 2011 season, right? A Lombardi Trophy came home to New York. I am not saying an October award wins you a Super Bowl, folks. I am saying the last time a Giants defender was playing this kind of award-collecting football in a winning season, confetti followed. Let the omen breathe.

And the awards table has a second nameplate this week, because Arvell Reese was nominated for Rookie of the Week after the Titans game, and the deeper you look at his film, the better it gets. The 12 tackles that tied the franchise rookie record since tracking began in 1994, you knew about. But the kid is 21 years old, folks. Twenty-one. Harbaugh handed him a game ball in the locker room and said he sprang onto the scene, and the advanced numbers say the tackles were the least of it. By Pro Football Focus charting, Reese allowed just three receptions for 14 yards in coverage, a passer rating against of 79.2, which for a rookie linebacker is outstanding, and he even made an open-field special teams tackle at the end of Jordan Stout's 74 yard punt, because apparently covering sixty yards of grass to clean up a return is just something he does between defensive series. Through three games, he leads all rookies and sits tied for second in the entire NFL with 17 solo tackles, with only one player in the league ahead of him. That is the fifth overall pick earning the slot, week by week, and next to Tremaine Edmunds' veteran radar, that linebacker room has quietly become a strength nobody saw coming in August. And while we are handing out the flowers, two more quick ones, Jordan Stout, the first Giant ever with multiple 70 plus yard punts in one season, and Dominic Zvada, the undrafted rookie who has scored literally every Giants point for two weeks running. The kids are alright, folks.

One last storyline before kickoff, and it is the best kind of problem, the safety room. With Tyler Nubin healthy again and Jevon Holland playing the best football of his life, the two starting spots write themselves. But here is the wrinkle, Jason Pinnock went and earned himself a role while Nubin was out, and Harbaugh gave him real flowers at the podium, saying the man might have earned more playing time. So now Dennard Wilson has a decision that coaches dream about, three safeties who all deserve the field. And knowing how this coordinator thinks, do not be surprised if the answer is all of the above, big nickel packages, three-safety looks on passing downs, Holland roaming free while Nubin and Pinnock split the structural work. Depth stopped being a weakness on this defense about two trades ago, folks. Now it is becoming a weapon.

And one quick word on what a clean report actually means tactically, because it is more than good vibes. With Banks and Nubin back, Dennard Wilson gets his full menu for the first time in weeks, real man coverage options outside, disguised safety rotations, the aggressive pressure looks we saw flash against Tennessee. Against a Jacoby Brissett offense built on rhythm and the quick game, with Trey McBride hunting the middle, having your actual starters on the back end is the difference between contesting those throws and chasing them. Health is a scheme unlock, folks, not just a mood.

So add it all up, Big Blue, and look at the table that has been set for Sunday. A spotless injury report for the first time all season. Two defensive backs returning at once, with a shutdown corner settling into the building behind them. A rookie class collecting nominations and game balls. A safety making half-century history. A veteran quarterback playing for his job with the most accomplished bench in football warming up behind him, and a clock on that depth chart that just got publicly wound by the league's top insiders. The Cardinals are coming to MetLife at 1 and 2, the Giants are 2 and 1 with first place air in their lungs and the legacy blues on their backs, and for once, absolutely everything broke right in the final 48 hours. No excuses left, folks. Just football.
