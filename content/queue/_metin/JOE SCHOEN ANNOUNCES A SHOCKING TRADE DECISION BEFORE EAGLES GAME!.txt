Joe Schoen has made his trade deadline decision, and it is not the one a lot of fans wanted to hear. Losing Tyrone Tracy Jr. to injured reserve has not pushed the Giants into a panic move at running back. Schoen said it directly. Tracy’s injury is not like the season-ending ones this team has dealt with, and it will not be long term, so it does not change how the Giants approach the position. And then he gave us the rule that will decide every move this team makes before the deadline. It has got to make sense short term and long term. But do not mistake that for sitting still. Schoen also revealed that the Giants have already been in contact with the teams that have started selling, and that they have a pretty good idea who is really available. So the Giants are shopping. They are just not going to overpay. And Schoen explained exactly why, with a lesson he learned the hard way in 2022. Today, I am going to break down Schoen’s entire bye-week press conference. The trade decision, the roster moves, the J.J. McCarthy story that nobody knew about, the Deonte Banks turnaround, and the warning Schoen just gave to everyone getting carried away with first place. Let’s get into it.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I’m James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now. Do you trust Joe Schoen to make the right call at the deadline, or do you want him to go all in?

Let’s start with the roster moves, because Schoen opened his press conference with what he called quick housekeeping. Four moves. First, the Giants added veteran defensive tackle Dean Lowry to the practice squad. Second, they moved on from receiver Jason Brownlee, who was on the practice squad. Third, Tyrone Tracy Jr. went to injured reserve. And fourth, the Giants signed a rookie running back out of Memphis from the Los Angeles Chargers’ practice squad straight to their active roster.

That last move is the one people asked about first. With Tracy out, what does the running back room look like? Schoen did not dodge it. That is why we are poaching the running back from the Chargers, he said. He also mentioned a practice squad running back the team likes. And he said the Giants have been rolling with three backs, which is typically the number you want, depending on special teams. Then he said the line that matters most. The Giants still have a lot of confidence in Scat and Motor. That is Cam Skattebo and Devin Singletary. And he said he is excited to see what the rookie can do.

So, let me be clear about what that means. When a reporter asked if Tracy’s injury changes the approach to running back with the deadline coming up, Schoen said no. Tracy is not going to be out long term. This is not like the season-ending injuries the Giants have dealt with. That is a very important answer, because a lot of people, including me in my last video, were talking about whether the Giants should go get a veteran back. Schoen’s answer tells you the Giants view the running back position as a short-term problem, not a long-term hole. They are not going to spend real draft capital to fix a problem that fixes itself in four weeks.

Let’s do the math on that. Tracy has to miss at least four games. That is Philadelphia, Washington, Jacksonville, and Indianapolis. The earliest he can come back is week 13 against San Francisco. So, for four games, the backfield is Skattebo, Singletary, the rookie, and Patrick Ricard at fullback. That is thin, but it is workable. And remember what Skattebo and Singletary just did in Houston. Skattebo ran for 124 yards and a touchdown on 17 carries. Singletary scored the 30-yard walk-off touchdown in overtime. Those two backs combined to beat one of the best run defenses in football on the road. So, I understand why Schoen trusts them.

But here is the concern I still have, and I want to be fair about it. Skattebo felt his hamstring grab on that 42-yard run in overtime. If that hamstring becomes a real problem, the room gets very thin, very fast. That is the one scenario that could change Schoen’s thinking. So, if you see the Giants add a back before November 10th, I would bet it is because of Skattebo’s hamstring, not because of Tracy.

Now, let’s get to the bigger trade picture. A reporter brought up 2022. That year, the Giants were also off to a good start under a new head coach, and at the deadline, they did not make a big buying move. The question was whether this situation is different. And Schoen gave one of the most detailed answers I have heard from him. He said there are a lot of factors. Where the Giants are pick-wise. They do not have a fourth or a fifth-round pick right now. In some situations, that may rule some players out. Maybe a player’s value is a third, and a third is too rich, but a sixth is not enough. Then you start looking at future picks, contract status, and where you are on the depth chart.

And then he said this. It is easy to say go sign X, Y, and Z position. But if a team is not really ready to get rid of somebody, or it is asking too much, and it does not match up with where you see the value, then it does not make sense to do it. And he added that certain teams have already started making trades, the Giants have been in contact with those teams, and they have a pretty good idea who may or may not be available.

Think about which teams he could be talking about. The Colts have already traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. That is a team that has clearly started selling. I want to be careful here, because Schoen did not name any team. But when a general manager says he has been in contact with teams that have started making trades, the Colts are an obvious team to think about. And yes, that means the Jonathan Taylor conversation is not going away. But based on everything Schoen said, I would be surprised if the Giants paid the kind of price Indianapolis would want for Taylor. Schoen’s whole answer was about value and not overpaying.

And there is a timeline element that I find really interesting. Schoen said this year is a little different, because the Giants had 14 days until the trade deadline when he spoke. He said the staff would go over the whole roster with coach Harbaugh. Who are the free agents? Who are the trade candidates? And then a big question. Do the Giants use the bye week to approach some of their own players about contract extensions, or do they allocate draft resources and financial resources to somebody outside the building? That is the real choice. Every dollar and every pick you spend on an outside player is a dollar or a pick you cannot spend on keeping your own guys.

I think that is a really smart way to frame it. A lot of fans see the trade deadline as only about adding. But for a general manager, it is also about protecting what you already have. The Giants have young players who are playing well, and some of them are going to want to get paid. If Schoen spends big at the deadline, that could make those conversations harder. So when he says it has got to make sense short term and long term, that is what he means.

Another reporter asked Schoen how much losing the starting quarterback and the team’s top defensive player factor into trade decisions. And Schoen said, not much. He said the Giants were 1 and 0 with Jaxson Dart, and then Dart got hurt seven plays into the next game. Then the Giants went out and made a move, and J.J. McCarthy is the starter now. That is past tense. The Giants are where they are. Every move they make now will be judged short term and long term, not as a reaction to what happened in September.

Now, let me get to my favorite part of this press conference, because it is a story nobody knew about. Art Stapleton asked Schoen about the McCarthy trade. The Giants had a lot of history with McCarthy going back to the 2024 draft. So how does what he is seeing now compare to what the Giants thought two years ago?

And Schoen told a great story. He said the Giants’ pre-draft process is so thorough that sometimes the work is not for that draft. It might be for down the road, when a player becomes a free agent or becomes available in a trade. The Giants did extensive work on McCarthy in 2024. They had him in the building. And then Schoen revealed this. The Giants worked McCarthy out on Easter morning at 8 a.m. And eight of his teammates showed up. The head coach was there. The video guy was there. On Easter morning, at 8 a.m., all those people came out to help McCarthy with his workout. Schoen said that told the Giants something about the kid.

Folks, I love that story. Think about what it takes for eight college teammates to show up on Easter morning for somebody else’s workout. That is not something you can fake. That is respect. That is leadership. And it is exactly the kind of thing that does not show up on a stat sheet but shows up in a locker room. And it explains why the Giants felt comfortable trading for him in September. They were not trading for a stranger. They had done the homework two years earlier.

And then Schoen added the other piece. Coach Harbaugh’s brother coached McCarthy. Jim Harbaugh coached him at Michigan, and they won a national championship together. So the Giants had inside information on who McCarthy is as a person and as a competitor. Schoen said there are some players you rule out because they do not fit, and maybe you would never trade for them. But because of the work the Giants did in the pre-draft process, they felt comfortable with who McCarthy was. And they liked the film they saw from Minnesota.

Now, let me be fair about something. McCarthy’s time in Minnesota was not a smooth ride. He threw 11 touchdowns and 12 interceptions in 10 starts there, and he had fallen to third string behind Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz before the trade. So the Giants were not buying a finished product. They were buying the person and the potential, and they paid a 2027 fifth-round pick to do it. Two games into his Giants career, that bet looks like a steal. The Saints game, 243 yards in the fourth quarter in relief. Then Houston, 20 of 28 for 252 yards, zero touchdowns, zero turnovers, and a road win in overtime. And here is a receipt I have to own again. I was the guy who told you Jameis Winston would start in Houston. I was wrong. The Giants believed in McCarthy before I did.

Next, let’s talk about Deonte Banks, because Schoen’s answer on him was emotional. A reporter pointed out that players drafted by one regime usually do not last when a new regime comes in. Banks was a first-round pick under the previous staff. So why has it worked for him?

Schoen said he is really proud of Banks for putting his head down, working, and overcoming adversity. It has not always been perfect for him here. He had a really good rookie year, to the point where he was shadowing the other team’s number one receiver. Then, for whatever reason, he did not perform to what he was capable of over the next couple of years. But when Harbaugh’s staff came in, they gave everybody a blank slate. What you did in the past does not matter. We are going to look at you for what you are and how you execute in this program. And Banks took advantage of every opportunity. He won the job coming out of training camp and has done a really good job this season.

And then Schoen said something that tells you a lot about Banks as a person. He never came up to my office and asked to be traded. He never complained. He just put his head down, kept going to work, and always had a positive attitude. Think about how rare that is. A former first-round pick goes through a couple of rough years, watches the coaching staff that drafted him get replaced, and instead of asking out, he just works. That is a player who deserves the success he is having.

And the reporter followed up with the natural question. Are there extension talks with Banks this week? Schoen did not commit. He said the staff would go over the whole roster with Harbaugh once the dust settled from Houston, look at the free agents and trade candidates, and start having those conversations. So, there is no announcement yet. But the fact that the question is even being asked tells you how much Banks has turned his career around. A year ago, nobody would have asked about extending him.

Schoen also shouted out another player who embodies this team. Micah McFadden. Schoen reminded everyone that McFadden got hurt in the first game last year. He re-signed with the Giants. Then the Giants drafted Arvell Reese, which could have pushed McFadden down the depth chart. And instead, when he got another opportunity to start, he went out and led the team in tackles. He had 10 solo tackles in Houston alone. That is exactly the kind of next-man-up story that has defined this season.

And when Schoen was asked about the state of the team at the bye, his answer was simple. He is happy where they are. He really likes the team. He likes the makeup of the players and the grit. And then he listed the moments. The last play of the game in Tennessee. The last play in Arizona. Even the way Washington played out. And then the walk-off run by Motor on Sunday in Houston. Over and over again, this team has found a way at the end of games.

He also gave credit to a group that almost never gets credit. The strength and conditioning staff and the training staff. He said they did a phenomenal job through camp and through the season, making sure the players hit their numbers at practice. And because of that, when the Giants get into the fourth quarter and overtime, they still have their stamina. That is why they could finish Houston with two big runs at the end, Skattebo’s 42-yarder and Singletary’s 30-yard touchdown. Think about how true that is. The Giants ran 34 times for 188 yards and four touchdowns in Houston, and they were stronger in overtime than the Texans. That is conditioning.

Then Schoen talked about Harbaugh. A reporter asked if the team is taking on the personality of its head coach, and Schoen said absolutely. It started on day one in the spring. The details, the messaging, the leadership. Harbaugh’s record speaks for itself. The players were eager to buy in. And Harbaugh and his staff laid out the plan. This is what it looks like. This is how you practice. This is how you approach OTAs, the offseason program, training camp, and every week in season.

And then Schoen mentioned the Saints game, and I appreciated his honesty. He said the Giants turned the ball over four times and lost at home. They made it close at the end, but he wished they had not needed to. Then the team went on the road, into a hostile environment, down five starters on defense, and performed the way it did in Houston. That bounce-back, he said, is the resilience coach Harbaugh and the entire coaching staff have instilled in this team.

Down five starters on defense. Let that sink in. Tremaine Edmunds is gone for the season. Brian Burns is on IR with a torn ACL. And the Giants were missing more starters on top of that in Houston. And they still held the Texans to 237 total yards. This defense is not built on one or two stars anymore. It is built on a system and on guys who keep stepping up.

Now, let’s get to the part of this press conference I think is the most important. Paul Schwartz asked Schoen about 2022. That year, the Giants had a new coach, a winning season, and a playoff win. And the question was whether people in the building had overjudged that team, and how Schoen guards against doing that again now.

And here is why that question matters so much. After 2022, the Giants made big decisions based on that season. And the next two years did not go the way anyone hoped. The 2023 team won six games. The 2024 team won three. So, when Schoen is asked about overjudging a good season, he knows exactly what the reporter is talking about.

And Schoen’s answer was really thoughtful. He said that would be a postseason lesson learned, something he has learned and has seen happen at other places too. His approach at the trade deadline is short term, long term, and big picture. And then, after the season, he wants to go back and look at it clearly, whatever the record ends up being. Not emotional after a win or a loss. Not the sky is falling. Not we are the best team in the league. He wants to understand why the record was what it was. Was it turnovers? Was the team not good enough? Did it not match up in the division? And he said the key is not to get caught up in the success or the failure of a season, and to look at it with as clear a vision as you can, so you do not repeat those mistakes.

So, let me put all of this together, because I think this is the real headline. Schoen’s trade decision is not a single player. It is a philosophy. He is not going to let a first-place record trick him into overpaying for a short-term fix. He is not going to let a four-game injury to Tracy push him into a move he does not believe in. And he is not going to judge this team on emotion. That is a general manager who learned from 2022. And I would rather have that general manager than one who chases every headline.

But let me give you the other side too, because I think it is fair. There is a risk in being too careful. The Giants have not won the NFC East since 2011. They are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, and unbeaten in the division. The Eagles are without Jalen Hurts and just lost 38 to 21 to Dallas. Windows like this do not open every year. If the Giants stand still and the division slips away by one game, people will ask whether one more piece would have made the difference. So, I do not want Schoen to be reckless. But I do want him to be aggressive where the value is right. And Ryan Dunleavy has said he expects the Giants to be aggressive. So, the door is open.

What would I want? My opinion, not a report. I would look at a defensive player before I look at a running back. With Edmunds and Burns gone for the season, the defense is running on effort and depth. A veteran pass rusher or a linebacker on an expiring contract, someone a selling team does not need, at the price of a future sixth or a conditional pick, would fit exactly what Schoen described. Short-term help, without hurting the long term. That is the kind of move I would expect.

And then there is the calendar. The Giants play in Philadelphia on November 8th. The deadline is November 10th. Then Washington comes to town on a short week. Schoen will be making his final decisions with one division game in the books and another one days away. If the Giants win in Philadelphia, I think they buy something. If they lose, I think they get even more careful. That is my read.

The last question we saw in this press conference was about something every Giants fan has been waiting for. Meaningful games in November for the first time in three years. Schoen said the team has earned the right to be in first place in the NFC East, and that it has meaningful games ahead that he is looking forward to. And so am I.

So, here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Giants add anyone before November 10th, and at what price. Number two, Skattebo’s hamstring, because that is the one thing that could change the running back plan. Number three, whether the Giants open extension talks with Deonte Banks or anyone else during the bye. Number four, the rookie running back from Memphis, and whether he gets real snaps in Philadelphia. And number five, Dean Lowry, and whether the Giants elevate him for the Eagles game.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. They have a general manager who is disciplined, a head coach who has the whole building bought in, a young quarterback whose teammates showed up for him on Easter morning, and a roster full of players like Banks and McFadden who keep proving people wrong. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
