Let me start with the trade deadline, because Harbaugh finally explained his thinking in a way that I think every Giants fan should hear. A reporter asked him about the idea that making a move at the deadline shows your team you believe in them. That when you add players at this time of year, you are telling the locker room, I think we can do something special. And Harbaugh flipped it around. He said you could just as well make the case that you are showing belief in your guys by keeping all the guys you have. He said the Giants love their guys, and they feel like they have everything they need to accomplish everything they want to accomplish. Now, he said, we just have to go do it.

I love that answer. Because think about the message it sends. The Giants made a real offer for Jonathan Taylor, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth, and the Colts said no. They chased defensive tackles like Jeffery Simmons and Daron Payne. They did not land anyone. A lot of coaches would have come out and said they were disappointed. Harbaugh came out and said, we believe in the guys we have. That tells the locker room something. It tells Cam Skattebo and Devin Singletary that the Giants trust them to carry the running game. It tells DJ Reader and the interior defensive line that the Giants trust them to hold up against the run. That matters.

And look at what those players have done. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. They are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. They just won in Philadelphia for the first time in 13 years. They did it without Brian Burns, without Tremaine Edmunds, without Jaxson Dart, and without Skattebo. If that is not a roster worth believing in, I do not know what is.

And there is a practical side to this too. A new player traded on Tuesday would have had just two practices before a Thursday night game. That is not enough time to learn a new playbook, new calls, and new teammates. Harbaugh said before the deadline that you would rather make a trade as early as possible so the player has time to prepare, but you do not always have that luxury. By standing pat, the Giants kept a locker room that already knows the system and already trusts each other. On a short week, that familiarity matters.

And the Giants did get some players back this week, without a trade. Cam Skattebo was a full participant on Tuesday after missing the Eagles game with his hamstring. Darnell Mooney was full with his chest. Charlie Jones was full with his hand. J.J. McCarthy was full with his hip. In a way, getting Skattebo back is like adding a player at the deadline. He ran for 124 yards in Houston. He is the spark this offense was missing in Philadelphia. And Tyrone Tracy Jr. is still on track to return from injured reserve around week 13.

Now, dose number two. Harbaugh always talks about the one-and-oh mindset. Every week is its own game. But this week, a reporter pointed out that one of the players had called it more like a two-and-oh week, because of what it means in the division. And Harbaugh agreed. He joked that the reporter must be a math guy. And then he explained it. He said he mentioned it to the team. If you won the first game against an opponent, then the second game either puts you a game ahead or makes you even. If you lost the first game, the second game either makes you even or puts you a game behind. But the Giants and Washington tied. So this is for all the marbles. It is a tie right now, and tonight is the tiebreaker.

And Harbaugh used a great comparison. He said it is like going into the second half of a game tied. The Giants have a chance to win right now and be one up on Washington for the rest of the season. That matters for tiebreakers in the division race, and it matters for the Giants’ grip on first place. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 4 and 5. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 7. A win tonight puts the Giants at 6 and 2 and 1, and 3 and 0 and 1 in the division. That would be a massive cushion with eight games to go.

And one analyst called this a prime opportunity to create separation from Dallas. The Cowboys almost lost to the Colts last weekend, a team that is selling and rebuilding. Dallas found a way to win, but it was not pretty. The Giants have a two-game lead in the standings over Dallas right now. A win tonight would make it even harder for the Cowboys to catch up.

Now, dose number three. The Giants’ pass defense. One reporter asked a member of the Giants’ secondary about the numbers, and they are wild. The Eagles threw for 169 yards against the Giants last week. The week before, the Texans threw for 153. And this week, that same Texans team threw for around 350 yards against someone else. Through nine weeks, only one team has thrown for more than 200 yards against the Giants all season.

So why? The defensive back said it starts with the mindset going into games, and how close the group is. He said the players want to succeed for each other. And then he gave credit to the pass rush. He said when you have guys rushing the passer like the Giants have, it makes the secondary’s job a lot easier. He said he has seen Abdul Carter get to the quarterback in around two seconds. When you have rushers that fast, the defensive backs can play great complementary football.

That is exactly how a great defense works. The pass rush and the coverage feed off each other. When Carter, Arvell Reese, and Kayvon Thibodeaux are getting to the quarterback in two seconds, the cornerbacks do not have to cover for very long. They can sit on routes, because they know the quarterback is going to have to get rid of the ball. And when the coverage is tight, the pass rushers get that extra half second they need to get home. That is the cycle. And right now, the Giants are winning it every week.

And think about who the Giants have done this against. C.J. Stroud and the Texans offense in Houston, held to 153 passing yards and 237 total yards. Joe Milton and the Eagles in Philadelphia, held to 169 passing yards and 220 total yards. That is two straight games where the defense has kept the opponent under 240 total yards. And the Giants did it without Brian Burns and Tremaine Edmunds, and with an interior defensive line that everybody said needed help. Greg Newsome, who just signed a three-year, 46 million dollar extension, and Deonte Banks, who held Nico Collins to one catch on four targets in Houston, have been a big part of that. This is a secondary that is playing with confidence.

And the perfect example was the last play in Philadelphia. The Eagles needed a miracle. They lined up for a Hail Mary. And Dennard Wilson did something almost nobody does. He sent six rushers. One analyst said he could not believe it. He said, hey, let’s throw a Hail Mary and take a shot, and the Giants said, hell no, we’re blitzing you. And it worked. The defense sacked Joe Milton before the receivers could even get down the field. Game over.

And the analyst said it was brilliant. Think about it. On a Hail Mary, the receivers are all running deep. They are not breaking off hot routes. So if you bring six rushers, the quarterback does not have time to wait for his receivers to get to the end zone. And if he throws it early, nobody is there to catch it. The analyst asked why other defensive coordinators do not do that when teams line up for a Hail Mary. Just bring the house. The simple things are genius. That is Dennard Wilson.

And one analyst summed it up in a way I loved. He said this was another game where, if any of the previous Giants coaches were in charge, the Giants lose. He named Joe Judge, Pat Shurmur, and Brian Daboll. He said the difference is that now the Giants have a head coach who knows what he is doing. It was not pretty, and it was not easy. But the Giants won.

Now, let’s get to the quarterback debate, because one analyst said something that is going to split the fan base. He said the most interesting thing about the Eagles game was Jameis Winston. When McCarthy went down with his hip, Winston came in and clicked. On third and 15, he dimed one to Malachi Fields for 16 yards and a first down. Then he converted a fourth and one himself. And then he found a wide-open Isaiah Likely in the end zone for the touchdown. It was a 14-play, 76-yard drive, and it put the Giants up 13 to 8.

And the analyst said he was not the only one who thought, I don’t care if J.J. is feeling better, I’m sticking with Jameis. He made it clear it was not a long-term declaration. He said it was in the moment. He was trying to win a football game, and in that moment, he felt like the Giants had something with Winston.

And another analyst agreed that McCarthy was not playing well in Philadelphia before he got hurt. He said McCarthy was struggling, and it did not look like the same quarterback who beat the Texans. He said Winston came in and did a Jameis thing. He looked terrible on one throw, and then threw a touchdown pass. That is what he does. But he also said McCarthy needs to pick it up, and that this game is a perfect opportunity for him to do that.

And Harbaugh has already made his call on this. After the Eagles game, he said the coaches did talk about leaving Winston in for the final drive. But the trainers and the doctor said McCarthy was good, and McCarthy insisted on going back in. Harbaugh chose his starter. And this week, McCarthy went from limited on Monday to a full participant on Tuesday. The decision is made. McCarthy is the quarterback. The only question is how he plays.

So let me give you my take. I understand the feeling. When your backup comes in and leads the only touchdown drive of the game, it is natural to want to ride him. But I am with Harbaugh on this. McCarthy is the starter. He practiced fully on Tuesday after the hip pointer. He has not thrown an interception in his two starts as a Giant. And remember, before he got hurt in Philadelphia, he was sacked five times. A lot of his struggles were about the protection and the lack of a running game, not just about him. With Skattebo back, I expect McCarthy to look a lot more like the quarterback who beat Houston. That is my opinion, not a report.

And here is a fun stat. One analyst pointed out that McCarthy has been good at covering the spread. In his last six starts, going back to Minnesota, his teams are 5 and 1 against the spread. The Giants are favored tonight. And with the Giants at home, on a short week, the analysts on that show liked the Giants.

Now, let’s talk about Jayden Daniels, because he is the biggest threat the Giants face tonight. Daniels can beat you with his arm and his legs. But one of the analysts raised an interesting point about him. He said he does not always understand what Daniels is doing at the end of games. With a couple of plays left, he does not throw the ball into the end zone. The analyst asked whether Daniels is coached that way, or whether he is just doing it himself. Another analyst said if Daniels is doing it on his own, that is worse, because it means the instincts are not there. That is a criticism I have not heard about Daniels often. But if late-game decisions are a weakness for Washington, that is something the Giants’ defense can attack.

And the Giants know how to finish games. They held on against Tennessee. They held on against Arizona. They won in overtime in Houston. They stopped the Hail Mary in Philadelphia. This team has been in close games all year, and it keeps finding a way. That experience matters in a game like tonight.

And Washington is banged up too. Center Lucas Patrick did not practice on Tuesday because of a concussion. Receiver Treylon Burks was limited with a foot injury. And Daron Payne, Washington’s best interior defensive lineman, is out for the season with a broken foot. Daniels was a full participant with a left elbow issue, so he will play. But a backup center against this Giants pass rush, on a short week, is a matchup the Giants should be able to win.

But I also want to be careful. One analyst said something that stuck with me. He said there is a fear he has about this team, because he wants to love them so much. He said the Giants cannot keep playing to the level of their opponent. With two and a half minutes left in the third quarter in Philadelphia, the Giants were losing 8 to 6 to a 2 and 6 team. That could have been a disastrous loss. The Giants found a way, but they need to be sharper. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1, but it is a division rival with a talented quarterback, and it will play like its season is on the line. Because it is.

And remember the first game against Washington. It was a 27 to 27 tie, with Jameis Winston throwing for 326 yards. And it was the game where Cam Skattebo fumbled in overtime, which led to Harbaugh benching him for the Saints game. Skattebo answered that benching with the best game of his career in Houston. Tonight, he gets a chance to finish what he started against Washington. That is a storyline I am going to be watching closely.

And here is the other thing. Arvell Reese is now reportedly the betting favorite to win Defensive Rookie of the Year, neck and neck with David Bailey of the Jets. After three sacks in Philadelphia, he has earned it. And tonight, against Jayden Daniels, he has a chance to make his case on national television. If Reese can run down Daniels the way he ran down Joe Milton, the whole country will be talking about him.

And the Giants have a decision to make with Reese. Do they use him as a pass rusher, where he just had three sacks? Or do they use him as a spy, to keep Daniels from running wild? On the last play in Philadelphia, the Giants sent the house. Against Daniels, that is a risky move, because if he escapes, there is nobody left to chase him. Dennard Wilson has been brilliant all season. Tonight is another test. My guess is we see a mix. Reese rushing on obvious passing downs, and Reese spying on third and medium, when Daniels is most likely to take off. The key is disguise. If Daniels cannot tell before the snap whether Reese is coming or waiting, that hesitation alone can win the down.

Here is what I will be watching tonight. Number one, McCarthy’s first few series. If he starts fast, the quarterback debate goes away. Number two, Skattebo’s workload in his first game back. Number three, how the Giants handle Jayden Daniels, especially on third down. Number four, Abdul Carter, who was limited this week with knee and back issues. And number five, the penalties, because the Giants had seven in the first half in Philadelphia, and they cannot do that again.
