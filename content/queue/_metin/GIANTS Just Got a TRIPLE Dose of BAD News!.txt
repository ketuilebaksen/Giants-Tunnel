A national skeptic just picked the New York Giants to win the NFC East for the first time all year. And in the very same breath, he said he doubts they can win even one playoff game. That is where this team is right now, Big Blue. The respect is finally showing up, and the doubts are showing up right next to it. After the win over Jacksonville, the critics came with three pieces of bad news. A quarterback who completed 47 percent of his passes. An asterisk on the win because of a banged-up Jaguars offensive line and a replay call that went New York's way. And an offense that could not use one of its favorite weapons. I am not going to hide from any of it. Today I am going to go through all three, tell you which ones are real, which ones are overblown, and what has to change before the Giants walk into Indianapolis on Sunday.

Before we get to the bad news, let me give you the context, because it matters.

The Giants beat Jacksonville 23-18. They are 6-3-1 and on top of the NFC East. And one national voice said this was the most stunning result of the entire weekend. More stunning than San Francisco blowing out Minnesota. He said he did not even know if he could call the Giants paper tigers, because he was not sure he thought they were tigers at all. He thought they had beaten bad teams, that they were well coached with a good defensive front, and that McCarthy had given them stability. But he expected them to get thoroughly exposed once they went up a weight class or two. And then he said the opposite happened. The Giants dominated, and the game was not as close as the score.

Another analyst said that for the first time all year, he expects the Giants to win the division. He said he did not believe that before this week. He said the defensive line is crescendoing, and he thinks the Cowboys might just run out of time, especially with the Giants playing this well. He said the Giants have a lot of young guys who could be stars, and they are all starting to come together. And he said he cannot say enough about the job Harbaugh has done instituting a hard-nosed, tough, professional culture.

That is a big shift. A month ago, the national conversation about this team was a shrug. Now the question is not if the Giants are good. It is how good. And that is exactly where the bad news comes in.

Bad news number one: the quarterback and the playoff ceiling.

Here is the knock, and it is a fair one. J.J. McCarthy went 18 of 38 against Jacksonville. That is about 47 percent. The same analyst who picked the Giants to win the division said this: he does not think they are a threat in the playoffs because of McCarthy. He said McCarthy has been somewhat of a revelation, but mostly because nobody had any expectations for him. He said McCarthy missed a lot of throws, including some open guys. He said Jacksonville has a top-five type of defense, but you still should not complete 47 percent of your passes. And then he said he doubts the Giants win one playoff game, and he certainly does not think they could win two.

Another voice was a little kinder. He said the Giants can win a playoff game, but he does not see them as a threat to win the NFC.

So, is this bad news real? Yes. I am not going to sit here and tell you 47 percent is fine. It is not. Against a good defense, McCarthy left plays on the field. And when the schedule gets harder, and when January comes, you cannot count on the defense holding a top offense to 18 points every single week. At some point, the quarterback has to win you a game, not just avoid losing it.

But here is the other side of it, and I think it matters. McCarthy threw no interceptions. He ran four times for 38 yards, including a big 22-yard scramble on the touchdown drive. And he made the best throw of the day on the touchdown to Malik Nabers. Matt Nagy moved Nabers inside to the slot to get him one-on-one. Nabers made it look like he was running across the field, then slipped back outside and ran to the back corner, and McCarthy put it right on him. One analyst called it a terrific throw. That is a real NFL throw against a real NFL defense.

And there is a bigger picture here. A low completion rate against a top defense, with a game plan that kept help in to block, is not the same as a quarterback who cannot play. It means the margin is thin. My opinion, not a report: McCarthy is good enough to win this division with this defense. Whether he is good enough to win two playoff games is the question that will define this season. And I think it is fair to be skeptical. I would rather hear that now and have the team answer it than pretend it is not there.

And of course, that brings up the other quarterback. Jaxson Dart is off the crutches, but there is no clear timeline, and nobody should make one up. If Dart gets healthy and plays at a high level down the stretch, this whole conversation changes. If not, McCarthy has to be better than 47 percent. Either way, the quarterback position is the number one thing standing between this team and a real January run.

Bad news number two: the asterisk on the win.

The critics are pointing at two things to say this win was not as impressive as it looked.

First, the Jacksonville offensive line was banged up. Their starting center, Robert Hainsey, was out. Their left tackle, Anton Harrison, was out. And then the backup, Walker Little, went out early in the fourth quarter. One analyst said the game was decided in the trenches, and that Jacksonville went into this game with a banged-up offensive line.

Second, the Parker Washington play. One analyst broke it down frame by frame. He said when Washington's elbow hits the ground, his hand opens, but the ball does not slide. He said even if a receiver has the ball in one hand and it hits the ground with control, it is his. His words: that is a catch in every bar, in every dorm room, in every house, and it should be a catch in any stadium. He said the NFL got this one wrong. He said it may not be the reason Jacksonville lost, but it is a big reason they trailed by so many points after the field goal.

So let me deal with both of these, one at a time.

The offensive line injuries are real. Missing your starting center and your starting left tackle is a big deal against any pass rush, and it is a really big deal against Abdul Carter. I will give the critics that. But the same analyst who pointed it out also said this: that matters, but it does not excuse an offense that never found a reliable answer. And you know what else? This is the NFL. The Giants have played most of the season without their starting quarterback. They just lost a starting cornerback, Greg Newsome, to injured reserve, and he is headed for tightrope surgery on that high ankle sprain. Nobody gives the Giants an asterisk for that. Injuries are part of the game. You play who is on the field.

And the Washington call? I said it in my last video, and I will say it again. The Giants got a break. I watched it again after hearing that breakdown, and I think the case for a catch is strong. The elbow hits, the hand opens, but the ball does not look like it moves much. If that is ruled a touchdown, Jacksonville gets four more points on the board in a game it was trying to climb back into, and the whole fourth quarter feels different. So yes, Jacksonville has a legitimate gripe. But here is the line from that breakdown that I want every Giants fan to remember: the officials did not spend the afternoon collapsing Jacksonville's pocket. Abdul Carter did.

So the asterisk is fair to talk about. But I do not think it changes what we saw. The Giants were the better team for most of the afternoon. One call and a couple of injuries do not erase 14 pass breakups, two interceptions, a fourth-down stop and a pass rush that never let Trevor Lawrence get comfortable.

Bad news number three: the offense lost its safety net.

This one is less talked about, but I think it might be the most important for the rest of the season. One analyst pointed out that the game plan did not fit what McCarthy has been successful doing. Because Jacksonville has a really good pass rush, the Giants had to keep their tight ends in to block. And Isaiah Likely, the guy who has been McCarthy's bread and butter, his comfort zone, his safety net, had only three targets in the whole game. The logic was simple. If you let Likely run routes, that is one more rusher who can come and hit your quarterback.

Think about what that means. When McCarthy is under pressure and his first read is covered, Likely is the guy he looks for. Take that away, and you get a quarterback holding the ball, missing throws and completing 47 percent. So the completion percentage and the Likely problem are connected. That is not just a quarterback problem. It is a protection problem and a game plan problem.

And there is one more piece here. The fumble. A couple of videos ago, I said McCarthy fumbled on that play when Antonio Johnson picked it up. I need to correct that. The fumble was by Cam Skattebo, with the Giants up 20-6. Johnson scooped it up, and it was McCarthy who chased him down and made the tackle. I got that wrong, and I want to own it. And it makes McCarthy's play even better, because he was cleaning up somebody else's mistake. But it also shines a light on a real problem: that was Skattebo's fifth fumble of the season, which one report says leads the league. If the Giants want to be taken seriously in January, ball security has to be fixed now.

So here is how I see the third piece of bad news. The offense has a protection issue against good pass rushes. When it has to keep tight ends in, it loses its best safety valve. And the run game has a ball security problem. Those are fixable things. But they have to get fixed, because the teams the Giants will see in the playoffs all have good pass rushes.

Now, let me give you the good news hiding inside all this bad news. Because there is a lot of it.

First, Abdul Carter. One analyst credited him with 12 pressures and two sacks against Jacksonville. Twelve. Another analyst called him unblockable and said he looked like a young Micah Parsons. There was one snap early in the game where three Jacksonville blockers were trying to deal with him, and they still could not keep him from wrecking the play. And the reason is preparation. Carter was watching Trevor Lawrence walk to the line, peeking at the play clock, and reading the center's head. The moment the center lifted his head, Carter was gone. One analyst said it looks like a guy who picked up a tell, not a guy who got lucky guessing. That is a second-year player studying film like a veteran. That is not going away.

Second, the coaching. Matt Nagy moved Nabers around to create a matchup and got a touchdown out of it. One former head coach, Rex Ryan, said he knows Nagy well, that Nagy has been around the Chiefs, and that he knows how to move players around. And on the other sideline, Rex ripped Liam Coen for kicking the field goal down eight from the eight-yard line. Rex said analytics should be a tool, not everything. He even talked about the Jacksonville two-point try after their touchdown. When you go for two and miss, he said, all the momentum goes to the other team's defense. Suddenly that defense feels great about itself. That is exactly what happened. The Giants' defense got the stop, then got the decision it wanted, and Jacksonville never got the ball back.

Third, the hustle. One analyst said the McCarthy tackle is a play that will be underrated. A quarterback putting his body on the line to stop a fumble return. And then Dru Phillips broke up the pass on fourth-and-one. Ask yourself how different that game looks if McCarthy just watches that return instead of ending it.

And fourth, the respect. A skeptic who thought this team would get exposed now picks it to win the division. That does not happen by accident.

So now let's look ahead, because the Giants have a chance to answer at least one of those pieces of bad news on Sunday.

The Giants go to Indianapolis. The Colts are 3-8, coming off a 26-3 loss to Houston, and Anthony Richardson is set to make his first start since Week 16 of the 2024 season. The Colts will have had 10 days to prepare. Richardson has a big arm and can run, and Jonathan Taylor is still in that backfield. This is a game the Giants should win, but it is a classic letdown spot after an emotional win.

And here is why this game matters for the bad news. Indianapolis is not Jacksonville. If the Giants can protect McCarthy without keeping everybody in to block, Likely should be back in the game plan. That means more easy completions, more rhythm, and a better completion percentage. This is McCarthy's chance to show the 47 percent was about the matchup, not about him.

Here are my keys for Sunday.

One: get Likely involved early. Three targets cannot happen again. Let him run routes, and let McCarthy find his safety net. That one change can fix a lot.

Two: McCarthy over 60 percent. I want to see rhythm. Quick throws, play action, high-percentage looks early, then take a shot to Nabers when the matchup is there.

Three: Skattebo has to hold onto the ball. Two hands in traffic. No more free gifts for the other team.

Four: contain Richardson and Taylor. Do not let the Colts' quarterback escape the pocket. Keep everything in front, and make him throw from the pocket under pressure.

Five: Paulson Adebo has to back it up. With Newsome out, Indianapolis is going to test him. He came up big against Jacksonville with an interception and a career-high four passes defended. Do it again.

And before you buy into the idea that this team cannot win in January, let me remind you of a little Giants history. In the 2007 season, the Giants were a 10-6 wild card team. Almost nobody gave them a chance. They won three road playoff games, and then they beat an undefeated New England team in the Super Bowl. How did they do it? A pass rush that would not let Tom Brady breathe. In the 2011 season, the Giants went 9-7. Again, nobody thought they were a real threat. And again, they got hot in January, the pass rush took over, and they beat New England in the Super Bowl one more time.

Now, I am not saying this team is the 2007 Giants. Those teams had Eli Manning, a quarterback who made big throws in big moments. That is exactly the piece the critics say this team is missing. But here is the point. In the playoffs, a pass rush that can take over a game is the great equalizer. It turns elite quarterbacks into average ones. It turns close games into wins. And right now, the Giants have a young pass rush that is getting better every single week. If the quarterback can just give this defense a little more help, the history of this franchise tells you what can happen.

So when somebody says the Giants cannot win two playoff games, my answer is simple. Maybe. But this franchise has heard that before. And it has two Lombardi Trophies from seasons when people said the same thing.
