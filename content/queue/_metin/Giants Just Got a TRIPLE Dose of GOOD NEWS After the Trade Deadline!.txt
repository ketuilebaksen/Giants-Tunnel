That is three doses of good news wrapped inside one painful night, and I am going to walk you through every single one of them.

I told you this was the tiebreaker game, the one John Harbaugh himself called the game for all the marbles, and I told you the Giants would take it. They did not. Now let me set the table for how this game went, because if you only saw the final score, you missed a lot of football.

The first quarter and most of the second were a grind. The weather was nasty. There was severe weather around MetLife before kickoff, fans were moved out of their seats for a stretch, and the rain came and went all night. Dominic Zvada, who had been automatic, who went four for four in Philadelphia including a 53-yarder, missed a 33-yard field goal early. That is the kind of night it was. Washington got on the board first with a 46-yard field goal from Drew Stevens early in the second quarter to make it 3-0.

Then the Giants answered with the kind of drive that tells you what this offense wants to be. Sixteen plays. Seventy-two yards. McCarthy managing it, the line leaning on people, and Cam Skattebo, back from the injury that kept him out of the Eagles game, finishing it with a one-yard touchdown run. Giants up 7-3 with 2:35 left in the half. And this is where the first warning sign showed up, because Washington went 67 yards in the final two minutes, and Daniels hit Ben Sinnott for a one-yard touchdown with five seconds left in the half. 10-7 Washington at the break. That is a back-breaker type of drive, and it would not be the last one.

Coming out of the half, the defense did its job. Shelby Harris got to Daniels for a sack and forced a punt. And then Skattebo went to work again, a 13-yard toss play, outside, untouched for long stretches, into the end zone. Giants up 14-10 with 10:22 left in the third. At that point, you are feeling it. The crowd is feeling it. Skattebo is running hard, the Giants are in control of the line of scrimmage, and you are thinking this is the night they bury Washington and take a stranglehold on the division.

But here comes the swing. Malik Nabers caught a ball deep in Washington territory in the third quarter, and it got knocked loose. Fumble. Washington ball. That is points off the board, at minimum a field goal, maybe a touchdown, and in a three-point game, you do not need me to tell you how much that hurt. Washington then put together a six-play, 77-yard drive, and Daniels found Antonio Williams for a 31-yard touchdown, beating Jevon Holland in coverage. 17-14 Washington.

Then the fourth quarter. And this is the stretch that should have won the Giants this football game.

The defense got a huge fourth-and-one stop at its own 30 with 9:04 to go. Huge. And on the very next sequence, McCarthy dropped back and hit Isaiah Likely for a 66-yard touchdown. Sixty-six yards. Giants up 21-17 with 8:13 left. Likely finished with nine catches for 127 yards, a career high, on a night when he set his previous career high in catches just one week earlier in Philadelphia. And then Jevon Holland, the same guy who got beat on the Williams touchdown, came back and picked off Daniels at the goal line with 5:27 left. That is a redemption moment. That is a game-sealing type of play. Daniels threw two interceptions in the fourth quarter. Two. And the Giants still lost.

Here is how. With 1:46 to go, Washington got the ball at its own 29. Three plays. Seventy-one yards. A 33-yard pass interference call on Deonte Banks against Treylon Burks put them in business, and then Daniels hit Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds left. 24-21 Washington. The Giants got one last chance, needing only a field goal to force overtime, and McCarthy’s deep shot for Nabers was intercepted. Ballgame.

So that is the game. A Giants team that had two Daniels interceptions in the fourth quarter, a 66-yard touchdown, a fourth-down stop, and a 21-17 lead with under two minutes to go, found a way to lose. And yes, that hurts. That one stings. That is the kind of loss that keeps you up at night.

But here is why this video is called a triple dose of good news, and I mean every word of it.

Dose number one: the standings did not move the way you think they did.

The Giants are 5-3-1. They are still alone in first place in the NFC East. Washington is now 3-5-1. Dallas is 4-5. Philadelphia is 2-7. Let me do the math out loud so nobody has to guess. Against Washington, the Giants are two games up. Against Dallas, they are a game and a half up. Against Philadelphia, it is not even close. Now, Washington owns the head-to-head piece of this, because they won this game after the 27-27 tie in week five, so if it ever comes down to a tiebreaker between these two, that matters. I am not going to hide that from you. Harbaugh called this game the tiebreaker game for a reason, and Washington took it. But a tiebreaker only matters if you are tied. The Giants are two games clear of Washington with eight games to go. They control their own path. They lost a game they should have won, and they are still the team everyone else in this division is chasing.

And here is the thing that people forget. Before this season started, how many of you would have signed up for 5-3-1 through nine games, first place in the division, with a win in Philadelphia for the first time since 2013? Every one of you. Every one of you would have taken that in a heartbeat. The loss is real. The standing is also real. Both things are true at the same time, and the second one is the one that decides January.

Dose number two: the trade deadline decision looks smarter today, not dumber.

I know the first reaction for some people after a loss like this is to go straight back to the deadline. Why did Schoen stand pat? Why didn’t they get Jonathan Taylor? Let me remind you what that conversation looked like. The Giants put a real offer on the table for Taylor, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth, according to Jay Glazer. Indianapolis did not take it, and eventually the offer was pulled. The Colts kept Taylor, and he is under contract through 2028. Indy moved other pieces, but not him. And Harbaugh said it himself, there was no short-term fix at an exorbitant cost, and standing pat was about showing belief in your guys by keeping all the guys you got.

Now look at Thursday night. Was the problem the running game? Cam Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns and caught passes for another 44 yards. That is 122 yards from scrimmage and two scores from a guy who missed the previous game. The run game was not why the Giants lost. Was the problem the passing game? McCarthy threw for 293 yards, a career high. Likely went for 127. The offense scored 21 points in the rain against a division rival, and it moved the ball when it had to. Where did the game get lost? A fumble in the red area, a missed short field goal, a 12-yard shank on a punt from Jordan Stout, a couple of drops, and a pass interference call on the final drive. Those are execution problems. Jonathan Taylor does not fix a muffed opportunity in the red zone. Jonathan Taylor does not cover Treylon Burks on a deep ball.

So when I say the deadline decision looks smarter, here is what I mean. The Giants kept their draft capital, they kept their locker room intact, and the guys they kept just put up 21 points and nearly 300 passing yards in a game played in a monsoon. My opinion, not a report: if Schoen had paid a second-rounder for a back and the Giants still lost this game on a defensive breakdown, people would be screaming about wasted picks right now. Instead, the Giants still have every one of those picks, and the young core is still the core.

And let’s talk about that core, because it was not just about who they did not trade for. It was about who they did not trade away. Kayvon Thibodeaux was not moved. And the contract that tells you everything is Newsome’s, because the Giants extended their corner, three years, 46 million dollars, 32 million guaranteed, through 2029. That is a franchise telling you, these are our guys, this is the group we are going to war with. Deonte Banks did not take an extension, and after Thursday night, that conversation gets more interesting, because the pass interference on the final drive is the kind of play that sticks to you. But the point stands. The Giants made a decision at the deadline to build around what they have, and one loss does not change the math on that decision.

Dose number three: the calendar is on the Giants’ side.

This is the part people are not talking about enough. Because the Giants played on Thursday night, they now get an extended break. They do not play again until Sunday, November 22, when they host Jacksonville at MetLife. That is ten days. Ten days for McCarthy’s hip to keep getting better. Ten days for Skattebo, who just came back and took a full workload, to get his legs fully under him. Ten days for the secondary, the special teams, and the ball security issues to get cleaned up on the practice field. And you know who else is on the way. Tracy is on IR and is lined up to come back around week 13, which is the San Francisco game. So the Giants get a long rest now, a home game against Jacksonville, and then a reinforcement coming back for the stretch run.

Think about how big that is mentally, too. A loss like this, if you have to turn around and play four days later, it can snowball. You do not have time to process it. You do not have time to fix it. Here, Harbaugh gets a full week and a half to grab his team by the collar, show them the tape, show them exactly where the game slipped away, and get them ready. And if you know anything about John Harbaugh, you know that is exactly what he is built for. That is a coach who has been through this in Baltimore over and over, the gut punch in a division game, the long week, the response. This is a test of his program, and I would bet on him passing it.

So those are your three doses. Still in first place. A deadline decision that holds up. And a calendar that gives this team time to heal and regroup.

Now, I promised you on this channel that I would never just spin you. So let me be the critic for a few minutes, because there are real things to worry about after Thursday.

Worry number one: finishing. This team led 21-17 with under two minutes to go and lost. This team picked off the opposing quarterback twice in the fourth quarter and lost. You cannot do that in December. Good teams close. And the Giants have now had two division games against Washington this season, a 27-27 tie and a 24-21 loss, where they had chances to win in the final minutes. That is not bad luck twice. That is a pattern, and Harbaugh has to break it.

Worry number two: the final defensive series. Seventy-one yards in three plays. That is not a defense bending. That is a defense breaking. The coverage on Burks led to the pass interference, and then Diggs got loose for 32 yards. Remember all season, I have been telling you this defense was elite at keeping the ball in front of it. Coming into this game, only one opponent all season had passed for 200 yards against them. The Eagles managed 220 total yards in Philly. This defense was built to stop explosive plays. On Thursday night, it gave up a 31-yard touchdown and a 32-yard touchdown. That has to be addressed on tape this week.

Worry number three: ball security, and I want to be fair about this. I warned you before the game that rain was in the forecast and that ball security would decide this. The Nabers fumble in the third quarter was the turning point of the game. Nabers is the NFC Offensive Player of the Month, he is the best player on this offense, and he finished with five catches for 44 yards and a lost fumble. That is not his standard. After the week he had in Philly, with the dropped pass and the helmet throw, I think this is a moment where Nabers needs a big response game. And I think he gets one. My opinion, not a report: the Jacksonville game is a Nabers game. Write it down. That is a receipt we will come back to.

Worry number four: the little things. A missed 33-yard field goal from a kicker who has been brilliant. A 12-yard punt from a punter who averaged over 52 yards a punt in Philadelphia. Drops from Likely and from Theo Johnson on a night when Likely also had a career game. Those are not things you can blame on the scheme. Those are things you have to clean up. And here is the strange part. The fact that the Giants lost by three points while making all of those mistakes tells you how good this team can be when it plays clean. That is not spin. That is the tape.

Now let’s go to the young quarterback, because this is the storyline that matters the most going forward.

J.J. McCarthy came into this game with a hip pointer from the Zack Baun hit in Philadelphia. He was limited Monday, full Tuesday, and there was real conversation about whether Jameis Winston would have to step in at some point. Instead, McCarthy played the whole game and threw for 293 yards. That is a career high. In the rain. Against a division rival. With a hip that was not 100 percent a week ago. Was the last throw a good decision? It was a deep shot with the clock running down and only a field goal needed, and it got picked. Some people will say you have to take a shorter throw there. Some people will say you take the shot when your best receiver is one-on-one. I lean toward the second group, but I understand the first. What I will not do is let one throw erase a night where he was the reason the Giants had a lead in the fourth quarter. The 66-yard touchdown to Likely was a big-time throw at a big-time moment.

And let me say this about the quarterback room overall, because we spent weeks on this channel talking about McCarthy versus Winston. Winston bailed this team out in Philadelphia. McCarthy carried the passing game on Thursday. The Giants have two quarterbacks who have now both made winning plays in big division games in back-to-back weeks. That is a luxury. That is depth. That is something most teams in this league would kill for. The quarterback position is not the reason this team lost on Thursday, and it is not going to be the reason this team falls apart. If anything, McCarthy’s line on Thursday is part of the good news, not the bad.

Now let’s talk about Skattebo for a second, because I think his night is getting buried under the result. Before the game, I told you about the fumbles. Four on the season, and the one he lost came in the first Washington game. So what did he do? He ran with the ball high and tight in the rain, he scored twice, he caught passes, he did not put the ball on the ground. That is a player who took the criticism and answered it. And now he gets ten days to rest before Jacksonville. If you are looking for a reason to feel good about the second half of this season, a healthy Skattebo running behind this line is right near the top of the list. I would also love to see Desrosiers get a few more touches, like he did in his debut against the Eagles, just to keep Skattebo fresh for December. That is my opinion, not something the team has said, but it makes a lot of sense to me.

And Isaiah Likely. Can we give this man his flowers? Five catches for 53 yards and a touchdown in Philadelphia. Nine catches for 127 yards and a 66-yard touchdown on Thursday night. He has become a weapon. Yes, he had a drop. But when the Giants needed a big play in the fourth quarter, he was the one who made it. Between Likely, Theo Johnson’s blocking, Nabers, Mooney, and Fields filling that old Slayton-type role on the outside, this receiving group has more ways to hurt you than it did a month ago. Let’s also talk about the defense in a fair way, because they did a lot right before that last drive. A fourth-and-one stop with nine minutes to go. Two interceptions of Jayden Daniels in the fourth quarter, including Holland’s at the goal line. A Shelby Harris sack out of halftime that forced a punt. This is still one of the best defenses in football. They got beat on two big throws, and one of those came on a drive that started with 1:46 left, aided by a 33-yard flag. Arvell Reese, the rookie who had three sacks and twelve tackles in Philly, who is right there in the Defensive Rookie of the Year race, did not get home on the final drive, and Washington did a good job keeping the pocket clean. That is something to watch. Before the game, I talked about how Myles Garrett got three sacks on Daniels earlier this year. The blueprint is there. The Giants just did not finish it when it mattered.

And I want to be careful here, because I know some people want to put this all on Deonte Banks. Yes, the pass interference was a killer. Yes, he declined an extension, and yes, this will be part of that story going forward. But football is a team game, and that drive had three plays. The pass rush did not get there, the coverage behind Banks did not stop Diggs, and the offense before that could not put the game away with a first down or two when they had the lead. The responsibility is shared. Harbaugh will say the same thing, I would bet on it.

So where does this leave us? Let me lay out the path.

The Giants are 5-3-1, two games up on Washington, a game and a half up on Dallas. They have ten days off. Then they host Jacksonville on November 22. Then the schedule keeps rolling, with Tracy coming back around the San Francisco game in week 13. They have played Dallas once and beaten them. They have played Philadelphia once and beaten them on the road. They still have the second games against both of those teams ahead of them. In other words, this division is still in the Giants’ hands. Washington took a piece of the tiebreaker on Thursday, but the Giants still have the lead, and they still have a win over Dallas and a win in Philadelphia in their pocket.

Here is my prediction, and you can hold me to it, because I always own the receipts. The Giants beat Jacksonville on November 22 and get to 6-3-1. McCarthy is healthier and sharper after the break. Nabers has his response game. And this defense goes back to what it has done for most of this season, which is suffocate people, take away the big play, and make you earn every yard. And let me say this one more time, because I believe it in my bones. This team can go all the way. I said it early in the season. I said it when they won in Philly. And I am saying it today, after a loss that hurt as much as any loss this season. Good teams lose games like this. Great teams learn from them. The Giants had a lead in the fourth quarter against a division rival, with two picks of the other team’s quarterback, and lost on a 57-seconds-left touchdown. That is not a team that is bad. That is a team that is close. That is a team that needs to close. And with ten days to get right, a healthy roster, and a coach who has been here before, I think this team learns that lesson in a hurry.
