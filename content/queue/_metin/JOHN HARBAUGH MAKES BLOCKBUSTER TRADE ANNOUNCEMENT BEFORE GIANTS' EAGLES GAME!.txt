John Harbaugh just made his trade deadline announcement, and he did it in the most Harbaugh way possible. On his first day back from the bye, he confirmed it. Yes, he called the Tennessee Titans’ owner about Jeffery Simmons. And then he told the entire league that the Giants are open for business. We’re open for any call, he said. Anybody wants to call us, call. He even turned it into a song, and the room landed on Call Me Maybe. But do not let the jokes fool you. Underneath them was a very serious message. Harbaugh said the Giants are turning over every stone, making every call, and they are not apologizing for it at any point, ever. Now, I want to be clear before we go any further. As of right now, the Giants have not made a new trade. There is no blockbuster deal on the table that I can confirm. But the head coach just told you, on camera, that the Giants want to add significant players before November 10th. And he also told you exactly what kind of deal they will and will not make. Today, I am breaking down the whole press conference. The trade talk, the injury updates on Abdul Carter and Cam Skattebo, the short-week plan, and the Philadelphia streak Harbaugh says he refuses to inherit. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with the phone call, because I owe you another small receipt on it. In my last video, I told you that, according to Jonathan Jones, Harbaugh called Titans owner Amy Adams Strunk and got the same answer the Giants got from general manager Mike Borgonzi. Harbaugh’s own version is a little different. He said, I did, I left a message. And when a reporter asked if he actually spoke with her, he said no, he did not. So the call was made, but the conversation never happened. Either way, the result was the same, and Simmons went to Chicago. But I want you to have the most accurate version, and that comes from the man who made the call.

And when he was asked why he made that call, his answer was simple. Because you are turning over every stone. You are going to try everything. And then he said it. I’ll put it out there, man. We’re open for any call. Anybody wants to call us, or call anybody, call. He said it was kind of fun to see the outpouring of interest in the story, and that he enjoyed it.

And then he took a playful shot at the way some people think the league works. There is an idea out there that general managers only talk to general managers, and coaches stay in their lane. Harbaugh joked that the Giants are not part of any secret society oath that says you can only talk to certain people. They did not get the memo. They are not in that agreement. And they will not be adhering to it.

Now, I love that, but let me tell you why it matters beyond the jokes. A reporter asked if his relationships in Baltimore make the Ravens an easier trading partner. Harbaugh said he did not know the answer. In some ways it is easier, because it is an easy call to friends. But he said that is how the whole league works. It is a small world. People call people on every level because they know them. To say that only two people can talk to each other is, in his words, false. It is incorrect.

And then he described how the Giants operate inside their building, and this is the part that should make every Giants fan feel good. He said the building is transparent. Everybody is talking to each other. He knows who Joe Schoen is calling. Joe knows who Harbaugh is calling. They are making every call and getting all the information they possibly can, at all times. And they are not apologizing for that, at any point in time, ever. But he also said that the main thing is focusing on the players and getting ready for the game. All the other stuff is going on behind the scenes.

Think about how many franchises have fallen apart because the coach and the general manager were not on the same page. Secret calls. Leaks. Power struggles. Harbaugh is telling you the opposite is happening in East Rutherford. The coach and the general manager know every call the other one makes. That is how a front office is supposed to work.

So why call an owner instead of a general manager? Harbaugh explained that too. He has been in the league a long time. He has been to a lot of owners meetings. He knows the owners, some better than others. They are not texting him every day, but he has good relationships at that level. Then he said something surprising. He probably knows the owners better than he knows the general managers. He does not know many of the general managers that well. Joe Schoen knows those guys better, so Schoen is probably calling them more. Harbaugh said he can call certain people, and he mentioned Steve Bisciotti, the Ravens’ owner, as a great friend. You call who you know.

That makes total sense when you think about it. Harbaugh spent 18 seasons as the head coach in Baltimore. That is a lot of league meetings and a lot of dinners with owners. Schoen came up through scouting and front offices, so his network is made of general managers and personnel people. Put those two networks together, and the Giants can reach just about anybody in the league. That is a real advantage at the deadline.

Now, here is the question everybody wanted answered. A reporter pointed out that Simmons went for a huge haul, and asked what Harbaugh’s call says about how aggressive the Giants are willing to be. And Harbaugh was careful. He said he did not think you could draw that parallel. It definitely says the Giants are aggressive. But it does not say anything about what price they might have been willing to pay. And he said he would not want to divulge that, and that it would be hard to speculate on.

So when people say the Giants were ready to pay two first-round picks for Simmons, Harbaugh is telling you that you cannot assume that. All we know is that they called. We do not know what they would have offered.

Then came the most important exchange of the whole press conference. Would you like to add significant players to the roster in the next week or so? Harbaugh’s answer was instant. Always. Always. That is always the goal. But then he added the reality. It is easier said than done. It is hard to find players who can make you better, because the Giants already have good players, very good players. So it has to be a need. It has to fit your financial profile. It has to fit your price tag.

And then he said the line that I think tells you the most about what is coming. The Giants are not loaded with 12 or 13 draft picks. They do not have draft capital to burn. So they have to be smart shoppers.

And then there was a funny moment that actually revealed something. A reporter asked about using future picks, and when 2028 came up, Harbaugh joked that 2028 is always easier to put on the credit card. It is always an easier spend. Everybody laughed, but think about what that means. Future picks feel cheaper when you are trading them, because the bill does not come due for a while. Harbaugh knows that. And he knows it is a trap if you are not careful.

Because when he was asked about the balance between the present and the future, he gave the answer that I think decides the Jonathan Taylor question. He said any deal would have to be more than a short-term thing. It could not be a one-and-done. If you are going to give up draft capital, you want a short-term win and a long-term win.

So let me apply that to Taylor. Taylor signed a two-year, 44 million dollar extension with the Colts back in August, so he is under contract through 2028. He is not a one-and-done. He would help in 2026, and he would be here when Jaxson Dart, Brian Burns, and Tremaine Edmunds are back healthy next season. By Harbaugh’s own standard, Taylor fits the profile of a player worth giving up picks for. A pure rental does not.

But the other half of Harbaugh’s answer matters just as much. Smart shoppers. No draft capital to burn. Remember, the Giants do not have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks. And the Colts already turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick for Taylor, according to Tom Pelissero. One report this week said a Taylor trade is more likely than not to happen somewhere. So the Colts are going to sell. The question is whether the Giants will be the team that meets the price. Based on everything Harbaugh said, I think the Giants will go hard, but they will not go crazy. That is my read, not a report.

And there was one more answer on trades that I really appreciated. A reporter asked if the Giants consider how a deal might affect the locker room. Harbaugh said yes, absolutely, very important. He said the team has great camaraderie, a great brotherhood, and great synergy right now. And he said the Giants would take that into account, even to the point of probably talking to some of the players before making a move, to make sure everyone understands it and is on board.

That connects directly to Cam Skattebo. If the Giants trade for a star running back, the coaches would want Skattebo to understand his role, especially after the way he answered in Houston. I think that is exactly the kind of conversation Harbaugh is describing. And it tells you this staff cares about the chemistry that got them to 4 and 2 and 1.

Now, let’s get to the injury updates, because there was a hilarious moment here that also gave us real information. A reporter asked about his concern level with Skattebo and Abdul Carter. Harbaugh joked about whether the concern scale was color-coded or number-coded, and said he did not have a scale. But then he gave the real answer. It is going to be day-to-day up to the game for those guys. He said he will probably have more information toward the end of the week.

And then it turned into a comedy routine. On Carter, Harbaugh explained he was sick that day. And then there was some confusion about which running back the reporter meant. Harbaugh started talking about Tyrone Tracy Jr., said Tracy was a tougher one than Skattebo, and then a reporter reminded him that Tracy is on injured reserve. And Harbaugh said, oh, well, there you go, so he won’t be playing. Thanks for clearing that up. He admitted he was trying to be coy and it did not work out so well. And when a reporter said, we finally broke you, Harbaugh laughed and said, yeah, you got me, you cornered me.

I love that moment, because it shows you the mood around this team. Relaxed, confident, and having fun. But let me pull out the real information. Carter was out sick, which is a lot less worrying than an injury. And Skattebo is day-to-day, which lines up with that hamstring he felt grab on his 42-yard run in overtime in Houston. Harbaugh said Tracy is a tougher situation than Skattebo, which I take as good news for Skattebo. But day-to-day with a hamstring on a running back is something to watch closely.

And think about why that matters. If Skattebo cannot go in Philadelphia, the backfield is Devin Singletary, the rookie the Giants signed off the Chargers’ practice squad, and Patrick Ricard at fullback. That is thin for a division game on the road. And if Carter is limited, the Giants lose one of the three pass rushers Dennard Wilson has been stacking on the same side of the formation. Both of those situations make a deadline move more tempting. Especially at running back.

Now, let’s talk about the short week, because Harbaugh gave us a rare look at how he plans it. After Philadelphia, the Giants have a quick turnaround against Washington. He said it is a challenge, especially when you are playing two big division games back to back. And then he laid out the schedule he has developed over the years. The team comes back in on Monday afternoon and practices right away. Tuesday is more of a tempo practice. Wednesday is more of a mental practice, plus a walkthrough. Maybe another walkthrough on Thursday morning, and then they play. He said you are trying to condense a whole week into three and a half days, maybe three days of prep. All hands on deck.

That tells you something important. The Giants cannot afford to come out of Philadelphia banged up. They will not have time to heal. And it also tells you why the timing of any trade matters so much. A player who arrives right before the deadline has to learn the system in the middle of the hardest stretch of the schedule. That is another reason I think the Giants would rather get a deal done early this week, if they are going to get one done.

And that brings us to Philadelphia. Harbaugh opened the press conference by talking about the Eagles with real respect. He pointed out that he was part of that rivalry a long time ago, as an assistant coach in Philadelphia. He spent nearly a decade on the Eagles’ staff, most of it under Andy Reid, before he got the Baltimore job. And now he gets to be on the other side of it. He called it the vaunted Giants against the vaunted Eagles. He said he has a lot of respect for that team and that organization. Defending champs in the division. Recent world champs. And he said the Giants are looking forward to the opportunity.

Then came the question about history. A reporter brought up the fact that this version of the Giants has not won in Philadelphia since 2013. The last Giants win there was in October of 2013. Since then, counting the playoff loss in January of 2023, the Giants have lost 13 straight games in Philadelphia. And the reporter asked if Harbaugh inherits that streak.

And Harbaugh gave one of the best answers I have heard from a Giants head coach. He thought about the word inherit, and compared it to the scene in the movies where everyone sits in the lawyer’s office for the reading of the will. You get the pictures, you get the record collection, and then you find out you also get the million-dollar debt your uncle ran up. And he said he would say, no thanks. I’m not up for that. Then he said he did not even know about the streak, and he probably did not need to know it. And he finished with this. We don’t care about that.

I love that answer. Because the worst thing a team can do is carry somebody else’s history into a game. None of the players on this roster were around for most of those losses. The coaching staff was not here. The quarterback just arrived in September. Harbaugh is telling his team that the streak belongs to the past, not to them. And this is a coach who knows that building in Philadelphia better than most. He knows what it takes to win there, because he spent years on the home sideline.

But I want to be realistic too. The Eagles are without Jalen Hurts, and they just lost 38 to 21 to Dallas. But they are at home, they are desperate, and they will be ready for a Giants team that just showed the league how physical it can be. The Giants have not won in Philadelphia in 13 years for a reason. That place is hard. If the Giants win this game, it will not just be a win. It will be a statement that the old Giants are gone.

So, let me put this all together. Harbaugh confirmed he called the Titans’ owner, but he left a message and never spoke with her. He told the league the Giants are open for any call. He made it clear the Giants want to add significant players. But he also told you the rules. It has to fit a need, the budget, and the price. It cannot be a one-and-done. The Giants do not have draft capital to burn. And they will think about the locker room before they make a move. By that standard, Jonathan Taylor is a fit if the price is right. A big rental is not.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Giants make a move before November 10th, and whether it comes before the Eagles game. Number two, Skattebo’s status at the end of the week. Number three, whether Carter is back at practice after being sick. Number four, whether the Giants add a defensive tackle, because that need has not gone away. And number five, the short-week plan, because the Giants have to come out of Philadelphia healthy.

And here is a prediction I am willing to put on the record. I think the Giants break that streak in Philadelphia. I think it is a close, physical game, and I think the defense makes the play that wins it. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East. Their head coach is answering phones, making calls, and refusing to inherit anybody else’s debts. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
