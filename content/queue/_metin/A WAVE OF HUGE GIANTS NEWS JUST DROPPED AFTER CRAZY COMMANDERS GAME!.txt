A wave of huge Giants news just dropped after that crazy Commanders game, and almost all of it is about the offense. People are asking if offensive coordinator Matt Nagy should be on the hot seat. A former Nagy receiver, Hollywood Brown, is sitting on the open market after getting waived, and fans want him in New York. A rookie receiver the Giants traded up for is quietly turning into one of the most reliable players on the roster. The return game is a problem. And the pass rush, the strength of this team, barely touched Jayden Daniels on Thursday night. So today I am going through every piece of it, and I am going to tell you where I land on each one.

Let me start with the big picture, because it frames everything else. The Giants lost 24-21 to Washington. They were up 21-17 in the fourth quarter. The defense won the game for them at least three times, with a fourth-down stop and two interceptions, one from Jevon Holland at the goal line and one from Ar’Darius Washington late. And the offense had three chances to pick up a couple of first downs and end it. Three-and-out. Three-and-out. Interception. That is why this week is an offense story, not a defense story. The offense did enough to win the game, and then did not win it.

And let’s keep the context in mind, because it matters. This team is overachieving. They have done it without Jackson Dart, who went down against the Rams, and without Brian Burns, who was lost for the season in week three. Most teams that lose their starting quarterback and their top pass rusher fall apart. This one is 5-3-1 and alone in first place in the NFC East. That deserves credit. But I am also done with moral victories. When you are up in the fourth quarter, you win the football game. Period.

Okay. The wave.

News item number one: Matt Nagy’s job.

After a loss like this, the loudest voices always go after the coordinator. And I have seen people asking if Nagy should be fired. My answer is no. Not even close. And here is why.

Think about what Nagy was hired to do. He was hired to build an offense around Jackson Dart. Not Jameis Winston. Not J.J. McCarthy. Dart. And on opening night against Dallas, with Dart at quarterback, the offense looked awesome. The Giants won that game 28-20. Then Dart went down against the Rams, and Nagy has had to rebuild the plan on the fly, twice, for two completely different quarterbacks.

Look at the records. With Winston starting, the Giants are 2-1-1. With McCarthy starting, they are 2-1. With Dart starting, they are 1-1, and the loss was the Rams game where Dart got hurt. That is a coordinator who has kept this offense competitive with three different starting quarterbacks in the first nine games of the season. That is not a fireable résumé. That is a coordinator doing a tough job well.

And on Thursday night, I thought Nagy called a good game for most of it. Skattebo ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns and added 44 receiving yards. McCarthy threw for a career-high 293. Isaiah Likely went for 127 and a 66-yard touchdown. The game plan was good enough to win. That is exactly why this loss is so frustrating. It was the last five minutes that went wrong.

Now, here is the one thing I do not like, and I will say it plainly. The designed quarterback runs for McCarthy. The quarterback crack sweep on third down, the one that was supposed to ice the game and lost two yards, was a play I hated. John Harbaugh defended the design after he watched the tape. He said it is a quarterback-driven run with an extra blocker, that it had a chance to crack them, and that they could have blocked it better. I understand the logic. But McCarthy is still a young quarterback. He has only started 13 games in the NFL. And the team is still figuring out what is best for him. My opinion, not a report: I do not think McCarthy should be asked to win games with his legs in the biggest moments. Let him win with his arm and let Skattebo win with his legs.

And I think part of the issue is that Nagy built the original plan for Dart, who is a natural runner. McCarthy can do some of the same things, but he is not the same type of quarterback. Nagy is not playing him exactly like Dart, but those designed runs are a carryover that I would like to see trimmed down. That is a fix, not a firing.

News item number two: the first half was too horizontal.

Let’s talk about how the game started. The first half looked very sideways. The Giants were trying to get screen passes going, quick throws, horizontal stuff. It felt like the offense was trying not to make a mistake. And I get that, to a point. For a young quarterback, taking what the defense gives you is a good sign. It shows he can read the defense and not force things.

But it also points to what was missing. A real shot down the field. When a defense like Washington’s plays everything in front of it, you have to make them pay deep, or they will keep sitting on the short stuff. And then in the second half, McCarthy started to heat up and the offense opened up. That is when you saw the 66-yarder to Likely. So the lesson is clear. Take the shots earlier. Do not wait until the second half to show the defense you can go over the top.

News item number three: Malachi Fields has been nails.

This is my favorite update of the week. When the Giants traded up to draft Fields, I loved the pick, but I will be the first to admit the trade-up felt a little expensive at the time. I do not feel that way anymore. Fields has been nails. He works hard. He blocks. He catches everything. And he is good after the catch.

And he is filling that role the Giants used to rely on Darius Slayton for, the reliable outside guy who can do the dirty work and still make plays. In the Eagles game, he caught that huge 16-yard pass from Winston on third-and-15 during the touchdown drive. That is a clutch play from a rookie on the road in a division game.

Here is what I want to see next. In college, Fields’ best skill was the 50-50 ball. Going up and winning in traffic. A guy like that should get one or two of those chances every single game. Throw it up and let him make a play. That is the kind of conviction I want from this offense. And it fits perfectly with the deep-shot plan. If the defense is sitting on the short stuff, put the ball up for Fields and let him go get it.

News item number four: Hollywood Brown.

Here is the name that has everybody talking. Marquise “Hollywood” Brown is a free agent after getting waived. And a lot of Giants fans want him signed. There is a Nagy connection, too. Brown was with the Chiefs when Nagy was the offensive coordinator in Kansas City, so Nagy knows him and could speak up for him.

So should the Giants sign him? I do not hate it. But my first question is, who does he play over? He is not playing over Malik Nabers. He is not playing over Darnell Mooney. He is not playing over Malachi Fields, not the way Fields has been playing. And you could argue he is not clearly ahead of Jalin Hyatt either. So on offense, he would be a depth piece, a fourth or fifth receiver. The Giants are a little thin at receiver, so depth does have value, especially in December when injuries pile up.

But here is the bigger question, and to me it is the one that decides it. Can he return punts and kicks? Because the Giants have a real problem there. Charlie Jones muffed a punt in Philadelphia, and at this point, I do not think the Giants can trust him back there. If Brown can give you a reliable returner who also knows Nagy’s system and can step in at receiver if somebody goes down, then signing him makes a lot of sense. If he cannot return, then I would rather keep the roster spot for somebody who can.

My opinion, not a report: I would bring him in for a workout this week and see if he can handle the return job. If he can, sign him. If he cannot, pass.

News item number five: the return game.

This one ties right into Brown. The Giants’ special teams have been an issue all season. We have talked about Dominic Zvada and the right-hash misses. We have talked about the 12-yard punt from Jordan Stout on Thursday. We have talked about the kickoff that went out of bounds. But the return game is part of it too. Charlie Jones’ muffed punt in Philadelphia could have been a disaster. And in a season where so many games are coming down to the last possession, field position matters. A returner who catches the ball cleanly and gets you ten extra yards a game is worth a lot.

And on the kicker, I am still in the same spot as most of you. Harbaugh believed Zvada was better than Ben Sauls, and Sauls is not on a roster right now. I do not think it was the wrong move. I think Harbaugh may see Zvada as his kicker for the next five, ten, twelve years. But you have to make your kicks. You cannot miss a 33-yarder. And you cannot be so erratic that your coach does not trust you to try long field goals. Zvada was money at Michigan in 2024, not as good there in 2025, and he has had great games for the Giants. But the kicking game has cost this team chances in both Washington games. I have one foot on needing a new kicker and one foot on riding with him. Harbaugh says the plant-foot issue is fixable. Jacksonville will tell us a lot.

News item number six: the pass rush went missing.

This is the other side of Thursday night that is getting lost in all the offense talk. The Giants got only two quarterback hits on Jayden Daniels. Two. And the blitzes were not effective. Daniels threw all three of his touchdown passes against cover zero. He was perfect on play action.

Now, to be fair, Daniels is a really hard quarterback to pressure. He gets rid of the ball quickly, and when you do get close, he can escape, improvise and create. Harbaugh also pointed out that Washington lived in third-and-short all night, and that it is hard to get pressure when the offense is in third-and-one or third-and-two and running play action on first down.

But this pass rush is supposed to be the identity of the team. Abdul Carter is an unbelievable football player, and Arvell Reese has been making history as a rookie. But the sacks have to come in big games. In Philadelphia, the Giants had seven sacks. On Thursday, two quarterback hits. That gap is the difference between winning and losing a close game. My opinion, not a report: against Jacksonville, I want to see the Giants rush four more often, win with their front, and save the blitz for third-and-long.

News item number seven: the close-game question.

People are also asking whether the Giants have just been lucky in close games. I would not say luck. The NFL is a one-score league. The Giants showed real skill in close wins over Tennessee, Arizona and Houston, including a game that went to overtime. That is not luck. That is a team that knows how to compete late.

But there is an old saying. If you keep touching a hot stove, eventually you get burned. And the Giants got burned on Thursday night. They have to find a way to stop letting every game come down to the last possession. And that is where the offense comes back in. A stronger offense ends games before the final minutes. Score a touchdown instead of a field goal. Pick up the first down that seals it. Do not leave the door open.

And quickly on Nabers, because the criticism has been loud. Some people are saying he needs to be a better teammate, that the body language against the Eagles, the drop in Philly, the fumble on Thursday, and his comments about not hunting home run balls all add up to a problem. I have already said my piece on this. I am not going to call Nabers a bad teammate, and he has said himself that the label is not true. But I do agree with one part of it. The best way for him to answer is to catch the ball, hold on to it, and be the most reliable player on the field. I think he does that against Jacksonville.

So what does “more conviction” actually look like? Let me give you my own four-point plan for Nagy this week. This is my opinion, not anything the team has said.

Point one: script a deep shot in the first ten plays. Not because it has to hit, but because it tells the safeties they cannot cheat up. Mooney is the obvious guy. He has the speed to separate, and against Washington he did not get enough chances to stretch the field. One deep ball early changes how a defense plays you for the rest of the game.

Point two: build the short-yardage package around Skattebo and the tight ends. Isaiah Likely is turning into a weapon, with nine catches for 127 yards on Thursday after a career game in Philadelphia. Theo Johnson has been a big part of the run game with his blocking. Put both tight ends on the field, put Skattebo behind them, and dare the defense to stop it. If they load the box, play action to Likely. If they do not, hand it to Skattebo. That is a physical identity that does not need tricks.

Point three: have a real blitz answer ready. Against six or seven rushers on Thursday, McCarthy was two for six for 11 yards and an interception. That cannot happen again. Quick game, a hot read, a screen to Skattebo when they bring the house. Nagy has been around long enough to know how to beat pressure. The plan just has to be in place before the pressure comes.

Point four: in the four-minute drill, mix it up. Harbaugh admitted after watching the tape that a good pass might have been the better call than three straight runs. So the next time the Giants have a lead late, I want to see at least one high-percentage throw on first or second down. Make the defense respect it. Keep the chains moving. And then let Skattebo finish it.

Do those four things, and I think a lot of the “fire Nagy” noise disappears in a hurry. Because the talent in this offense is not the problem. Nabers, Mooney, Fields, Likely, Theo Johnson, Skattebo, and a quarterback who just threw for a career high. That is a lot to work with, more than most offenses in this division can say. It is about putting it together for four full quarters, not three and a half.

Now, the schedule, because that is what all of this is building toward. The next four games are Jacksonville, Indianapolis, San Francisco and Seattle, and then Cleveland. Jacksonville will be tough, but they are not unbeatable. The Giants should beat the Colts. San Francisco has to fly cross country to New Jersey. And why can’t the Giants beat Seattle? A four-game losing streak would really disappoint me, and I do not expect it. I think 2-2 is the floor. But for this team to do better than that, the offense has to be better.

And the standings give the Giants some breathing room. They are 5-3-1. Dallas lost badly to San Francisco and is 4-6, two games back. Washington is 3-5-1, also two games back, with the head-to-head edge. Philadelphia is 2-7. The Giants still control their own destiny. The NFL is the king of worst-to-first, and this team has enough to win this division.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. I picked the Giants to beat Washington. Wrong. I said the offense would find a way to close late. Wrong. And I said Malachi Fields would turn into a real piece of this offense. That one I feel good about. So one right, two wrong, and we keep moving.

Here is my prediction for Jacksonville, and hold me to it. Nagy trims the designed quarterback runs and leans on Skattebo in short yardage. The Giants take at least two deep shots in the first half, including a 50-50 ball to Fields. The pass rush gets home at least three times. And the Giants win and get to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A coordinator who has kept the offense alive with three quarterbacks. A rookie receiver who has been nails. A running back playing with fire. A defense that takes the ball away. A coach who expects to win. And a two-game lead in the division. The offense does not need to be blown up. It needs more conviction down the field and smarter calls in the big moments. Give it that, and this team wins the NFC East.
