Alright friends, I have been going back through everything that came out of Giants camp ahead of week four, and honestly, the mood around this team is a lot different than the injury list makes it sound. Going into a home game against Arizona, you would figure it is all bad news, Andrew Thomas missing practices, the full ugly truth about Brian Burns finally coming out, two defensive backs still hobbled. But buried in this batch of updates is a collection of genuinely great news, a league honor for the man who has been swinging our games, a new quarterback making a first impression that has the locker room buzzing, and a history lesson that says Sunday could put this franchise somewhere it has not been since 2012. I am going to walk you through all of it, because I think it changes how we should be looking at this game. Let's get into it.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I'm James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, do not forget to hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing but it helps this channel a lot, and with the way this season keeps moving, you do not want to miss a single day.

Let's start with the thing that jumped out at me first, because the J.J. McCarthy reviews are in, and folks, they are glowing. The man has had just a couple of practices in this building, and the locker room is already talking about him. Think about what that means for a second. Veteran NFL locker rooms do not hand out praise to new arrivals, they wait, they watch, they test you. And instead, the quotes are flowing after two days. The way I read it, he is making a seriously good first impression, and the reasons his teammates give are telling. They like how he got here, a guy who came in with something to prove rather than something to protect. They like that he got straight to work, no entourage energy, no drama, just football. And they know he is not a mystery, they have seen what he looks like at his best, and they know the man is a winner, 27 and 1 in college does not wash off. And here is the bigger picture on McCarthy that I keep coming back to. For a quarterback who could never quite get his feet under him in his first stop, the best medicine in the world is a change of scenery. He gets to reintroduce himself to a team, to the league, and honestly, to himself. That is a full reset, folks, a blank page in a building where the head coach's family has believed in him since he was a teenager. For a 23 year old with his talent, that environment is worth more than any coaching point.

And the best quote of the week came from the man himself, Malik Nabers, who was asked about the new quarterback and did not hold back. Strong arm. Catching on quick. Confident. Targeting receivers well. Four short phrases, and every single one matters, but here is the detail I love most, those two go way back. Nabers talked about a seven on seven event before either of them ever got to college, where he actually worked with McCarthy. Folks, the chemistry conversation we have had all season just got interesting, because if J.J. McCarthy is ever the guy in this offense, his number one receiver will not be starting from zero, they will be picking up a connection that started when they were teenagers. Cam Skattebo chimed in too, in his usual no-nonsense way, said McCarthy is a good player, that he looks good on the scout team throwing it around, and then added the perfect Skattebo disclaimer, that anybody who walks into this building is an NFL quarterback, so of course he looks good. I love that answer, folks, respect without hype, exactly the culture this team runs on.

Now, the one thing I do not love, and look, John Harbaugh knows infinitely more football than I do, but I have to say it, J.J. McCarthy is running the scout team this week. You just traded for your backup quarterback, the man one snap away from your season, and his practice reps are spent imitating Jacoby Brissett for the defense's benefit. I would want him mainlining this offense as fast as humanly possible, and that is hard to do while you are running another team's plays. Now, the fair counterpoint, and I will give it to you straight. Scout team reps are still reps, live reads against a starting defense, and there is a long tradition of young quarterbacks sharpening themselves there. Maybe the staff sees him as a true backup for now and wants Winston's rhythm undisturbed. And you could absolutely use Jake Haener as the practice squad arm to imitate Brissett instead, so I suspect this arrangement is temporary. Either way, it already sounds like the man has hit the ground running, and I would bet everything I own that he is spending his nights at that facility right now with the playbook open. Remember our signals checklist, folks. QB2 on the depth chart, check. The rest is coming.

And speaking of players hitting the ground running, probably harder than anybody in the entire league right now, let's talk about Jevon Holland, because the man was just named NFC Defensive Player of the Month for September. Let me start with some accountability, because this tunnel keeps its receipts. I was critical of Holland last year, genuinely critical, because I hold him to a high standard, I look at his skill set and I see a player who should be one of the most productive defenders in football. Well, folks, that is exactly what he has become. Nobody, and I mean nobody, is playing the safety position better right now. Run the September numbers with me. Three interceptions, one in every single game this season. Twelve total tackles. Two forced fumbles. Two fumble recoveries. Four passes broken up. And the one that puts it in historical context, he is the first player with four takeaways in the first three games of a season since Marcus Peters back in 2016. A decade, folks, that is how long it has been since anybody started a season stealing the football like this. And do not let the award distract you from the real point, which is that these are not empty stats. If Holland does not pick off Dak Prescott at the end of that first half in week one, and if he does not pick off Cam Ward at the front of the end zone in the final seconds against Tennessee, this team is not sitting at 2 and 1 right now. Period. The man is swinging entire football games by himself. This is the player the front office thought it was signing to that big free agent deal, arriving one year late and right on time. We gave him the game ball in this tunnel two weeks running, and now the entire conference has caught up.

Now, the part that keeps this week from being all sunshine, and his name is Andrew Thomas. He missed practice Wednesday. He missed practice Thursday. And honestly, folks, I do not know if he is playing Sunday. Follow the pattern with me, because it matters. Last week he missed one practice, returned the next day, and the team called it a veteran rest day, and we bought it, reasonably. This week it is two straight days, and this groin issue has reportedly been bothering him since week one. And if we are being honest about the bigger picture, this has been the theme of his career, one of the most gifted linemen of his generation who simply cannot shake the injury bug. Watch the tape from this season and you can see it, he looked like he was laboring at moments against Dallas, against the Rams, against Tennessee, gutting through at less than full strength. Maybe this is exactly what the team says it is, workload management for a cornerstone player, because this staff believes it will be playing meaningful football in December and January and wants its best lineman standing when the games get heavy. That is the long-view philosophy we have praised all season. Harbaugh said Wednesday that Thomas should be fine, and look, I am not calling the man a liar, but I am not calling him a truther either, because this coach keeps his cards pressed so close to the vest they are practically inside his jacket. We learned that lesson with the Burns timeline, and I have not forgotten it.

So let's game out the scenario where number 78 sits. First, the silver lining, and it is real. If there were ever a week to rest your franchise left tackle, this is it, because the Arizona Cardinals are one of the worst pass rushing teams in the entire league. No elite edge monster, no exotic pressure machine, just a modest front that this offensive line should handle even shorthanded. Second, the replacement question. My guess is J.C. Davis, the rookie day three pick, steps in at left tackle, because he was the one working there throughout the preseason. Marcus Mbow played some left tackle a year ago and has earned trust with his improved anchor, but the preseason reps usually tell you the staff's plan, and those belonged to Davis. Either way, protect the quarterback's blind side by committee for one week if it buys you a healthy Thomas for the stretch run. And let me say this about the man himself, because it needs saying. Andrew Thomas is arguably one of the finest players this franchise has drafted in a generation, and while his body has betrayed him more than once, his commitment to this team never has. The fight he shows just to be out there week after week deserves more respect than it gets. Hoping we see 78 on Sunday, folks, but if not, now you know why and who.

The rest of the injury report, honestly, is not bad at all, and the comparison with Arizona is closer than you would think. Thomas was the only Giant who did not practice at all. Tyrone Tracy Jr. missed Wednesday but came back limited with the knee, trending the right way. The two I am watching are in the secondary again, Deonte Banks and Tyler Nubin, limited back to back days with those calf issues. Remember the stakes there, Banks has now missed two straight games, Nubin sat out the Titans win, and calves are the injuries that linger and re-bite. We will see if either suits up Sunday. On the bright side, Elijah Campbell, Nic Jones, and Patrick Ricard were full participants on consecutive days, which means the special teams core and the road-grading fullback are good to go. And of course, the wild card in the secondary conversation is the new arrival, because with the Joey Porter Jr. deal getting finalized, every rep he squeezes in this week makes the Banks and Nubin questions a little less scary. Keep your eye on that inactives list, folks. Arizona, for their part, is almost completely healthy, the only non-participant was Dadrion Taylor-Demerson, a depth safety, with Will Johnson already on season-ending injured reserve. So no, the Cardinals will not be handing us excuses. Fine. This roster does not need them.

Now, the part that stings, because the full Brian Burns truth is out, and it turned out worse than anybody expected. Right after the Tennessee game, Harbaugh made it sound like the team might have dodged a bullet. They did not. Burns tore his ACL, and he also tore his meniscus. And folks, forgive me a moment of frustration, because it feels like every other team in this league announces a scary injury that turns out mild, while the Giants announce a mild one that turns out catastrophic. We said it when the messaging was bouncing between nothing serious and day to day, something does not add up, and unfortunately the tunnel was right again. The timeline math is sobering. An ACL alone usually runs nine to ten months. Add the meniscus and you are looking at nine to twelve. Best case scenario, Brian Burns is back rushing the passer at an elite level in week one of next season, fully rebuilt, fully motivated. The season, though, does not stop for anybody, and the sign of a strong team is how fast it gets back up. So here is the roll call. Abdul Carter, you are the alpha now, every protection slides your way, eat anyway. Kayvon Thibodeaux, golden opportunity does not begin to describe it, a contract year, free agency waiting at the end of it, and most importantly, you are back on the edge full time, no more of that interior experiment that the film said loudly was not your position. We have seen the nice moments, now we need the streaky rusher to become a steady one. Chauncey Golston gets his edge opportunities. Do not be surprised to see Arvell Reese, the rookie, rushing off the edge in creative packages. And yes, I will bang this drum one more time, give Trace Ford his game day elevation. You are down your best defensive player, so everyone up front has to rise, and the schedule gives them a soft runway to figure it out.

And the good news on that front seven is that the next generation is already producing. Arvell Reese led this team with twelve tackles last week, adding a tackle for loss and a pass defended, and the rookie is now aiming for his fourth consecutive game with five or more tackles, four games into his career. The record-tying performance was not a fluke, folks, it was a baseline. And next to him stands the perfect mentor, because Tremaine Edmunds quietly posted ten tackles and a pass defended of his own, which was the 24th game of his career with double-digit tackles. And here is the stat that flies under every radar, since 2018, Edmunds leads all linebackers in football with 61 passes defensed. All of them, folks. A young sideline-to-sideline missile playing next to a veteran who has been erasing throwing windows for nearly a decade, that is a linebacker foundation you can build a whole defense on, and with the interior wall of Lawrence and Payne in front of them and Holland cleaning up behind them, the spine of this defense is legitimately strong even without its edge king.

Alright, flip to the offense, because the home matchup numbers are genuinely encouraging. Cam Skattebo is the man I am watching, especially in this building. He went for 100 scrimmage yards last week, 60 on the ground and a team-high 40 through the air, and he has now hit 50 plus scrimmage yards in nine of his last ten games. But the home splits are where it gets fun, folks. In five career games at MetLife, Skattebo has 502 scrimmage yards and five rushing touchdowns, over a hundred yards a game in front of the home crowd, and he is aiming for his sixth consecutive home game with 80 plus scrimmage yards. The man feeds off that stadium. Najee Harris churned out 52 on the ground last week, and in his only career meeting with Arizona he produced 77 scrimmage yards, so the two-headed backfield should have real juice against a defense that just is not built to bully anybody up front. And through the air, here is your fun history nugget, the last time Jameis Winston started a game against the Arizona Cardinals, he threw for 358 yards. The man owns 32 career games with 300 plus passing yards, which is a reminder that the arm talent sleeping inside this conservative game plan is real, and if Arizona loads the box to stop the backs, Winston has the resume to make them regret it. Malik Nabers led the team with five catches last week and has hauled in five or more in nine of his twelve career home games, chasing a third straight to open this home season, and Isaiah Likely has quietly stacked 15 receptions, tied for fourth among all tight ends. The pieces are there, folks. Now finish the drives, because the end zone drought since week one is the only stat on this side of the ball that matters.

Respect to the opponent for a minute, because Arizona at 1 and 2 is dangerous in very specific ways. Jacoby Brissett completed 38 of 52 for 280 yards and two touchdowns last week, and here is the scary split, he has thrown two or more touchdown passes in eight of his nine road starts. The veteran travels well. Trey McBride caught nine balls for 75 yards, leads every tight end in football with 26 receptions, and sits at 373 for his career, one catch from passing Jimmy Graham for the most ever by a tight end in his first five seasons. That is the matchup of the game, folks, McBride against our linebackers and safeties, which means Jevon Holland's award tour gets its hardest test yet. Then there is the rookie, Jeremiah Love, who ran for 90 yards and caught a touchdown last week and leads all rookies in rushing, and Michael Wilson, coming off an eleven catch, 89 yard afternoon. This is a real offense with a real distribution of threats, and the formula against it is the one we always preach, make Brissett hold the ball behind that modest line, collapse the pocket with Lawrence and Payne, and let Holland hunt.

And now the history, because Sunday carries more weight than a week four home game usually does. These two franchises have met 129 times, and the Giants lead the all-time series 81 wins, 46 losses and two ties. The last meeting was a 31 to 28 Giants win back in 2023. And get this, a win on Sunday would give the Giants back-to-back victories over Arizona for the first time since the 2005 and 2008 meetings. The team will do it wearing those gorgeous blue legacy uniforms, the script logo with the underline, an absolute classic look, and my only message to the roster is this, play like the Giants who used to wear them. Because here is the stakes line that should give you chills, folks. At 2 and 1, this team holds at least a share of the best record in the NFC East for the first time this late in a season since 2012. Win Sunday, and depending on the scoreboard elsewhere, the New York Giants might own first place in this division all by themselves. Three and one is sitting right there, guys. Right there.

So that is the full picture, Big Blue. A new quarterback winning the locker room in two days flat. A safety collecting conference hardware and swinging games week after week. A rookie linebacker and a veteran anchor holding down the middle. A franchise tackle hopefully resting toward Sunday instead of rehabbing past it. The full, painful Burns truth finally on the table, and a front seven answering the challenge. And a home date, in the legacy blues, with first place in the division within reach. It has been a season of ups and plenty of downs, folks, but this team keeps getting back up, and that is the only habit that matters in October.
