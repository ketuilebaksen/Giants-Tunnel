The Giants have made their quarterback decision for Thursday night, and it might surprise people who watched J.J. McCarthy limp off the field in Philadelphia. McCarthy is ready. After being listed as limited on Monday with what has been described as a hip pointer, he was upgraded to a full participant in practice on Tuesday. All signs point to McCarthy starting against Washington, not Jameis Winston. And McCarthy is not the only one coming back. Cam Skattebo, who missed the Eagles game with his hamstring, was also a full participant on Tuesday. And on the other big decision of the week, the Giants stood pat at the trade deadline. No Jonathan Taylor. No defensive tackle. No move at all. The Colts kept Taylor after turning down what was reportedly the best offer on the table, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth from the Giants. So the Giants are going into the second division game in five days with the roster they have, a healthy starting quarterback, and their hottest running back back on the field. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with McCarthy, because this is the big one. In Philadelphia, with 12:02 left in the third quarter, McCarthy ran for three yards and took a big hit from Eagles linebacker Zack Baun. He came out with a hip injury and was listed as questionable to return. Jameis Winston came in and threw the go-ahead touchdown to Isaiah Likely. And then McCarthy came back on the very next possession and finished the game. John Harbaugh said after the game that the coaches talked about leaving Winston in, but the trainers and the doctor said McCarthy was good, and McCarthy insisted on playing.

So the question all week was simple. Could McCarthy recover in time for a Thursday night game? On Monday, the Giants listed him as limited. The injury has been reported as a hip pointer, which is a bruise on the top of the hip bone. It is painful, it can make it hard to twist and run, but it is usually something a player can play through once the pain is managed. And on Tuesday, McCarthy practiced fully. Reports say he is ready for Thursday.

So here is the decision. The Giants are not changing quarterbacks. McCarthy is their starter, and he is healthy enough to play. I said in my last video that if McCarthy practiced and the medical staff cleared him without limitations, he should start. That is exactly what happened. And I predicted McCarthy would start on Thursday. It looks like I will get that one right.

And I think this is the right call for a few reasons. First, McCarthy has not thrown an interception in his two starts as a Giant. He protects the football, and that is the trait Harbaugh values most. Second, he has earned the trust of the locker room. One reporter said the players and coaches love him for the way he gutted it out in Philadelphia, including that first down he ran for while he was clearly limping. And third, the Giants want to keep evaluating McCarthy for the future. Jaxson Dart is coming back next year. Every start McCarthy makes is a chance for the coaches to learn more about him. You cannot evaluate a quarterback who is standing on the sideline.

But I also want to be realistic. A hip pointer can still bother a quarterback, especially when he gets hit. And McCarthy was sacked five times before halftime in Philadelphia. If the Giants’ offensive line does not protect him better, he is going to take more shots on that hip. That is the biggest thing I will be watching on Thursday. Not whether McCarthy plays, but how he moves in the pocket, and whether the line keeps him clean.

And Winston will be ready. He has started four games this season. He has come off the bench and won games. On Sunday, he went 6 of 8 for 71 yards and threw the only Giants touchdown. Harbaugh called his deep sideline throw to Malachi Fields the throw of the game. If McCarthy goes down again, the Giants have the best backup situation in the division.

And think about how far this quarterback room has come. In week two, when Jaxson Dart went down on his seventh snap against the Rams, the Giants were in crisis. Winston stepped in and kept the ship steady. He beat Tennessee 12 to 7 and Arizona 36 to 24, and he threw for 326 yards in the tie with Washington. Then the Giants traded for McCarthy, and McCarthy has won both of his starts. Since that loss to the Rams, the Giants are 4 and 1 and 1, with both quarterbacks contributing. And through all of it, they have never lost two games in a row. That is a quarterback room that keeps answering.

And the hip pointer itself is worth explaining, because it tells you why the Giants feel comfortable. A hip pointer is a bruise, not a structural injury. It does not involve a torn ligament or a broken bone. It hurts, and it can limit how a player twists and accelerates. But with pain management and padding, players often play through it within a few days. That is why McCarthy could go from limited on Monday to full on Tuesday. That is my understanding of the injury, not a medical report from the Giants. But it explains the timeline.

Now, Cam Skattebo. This might be even bigger news than McCarthy. Skattebo was doubtful for the Eagles game and did not play. The Giants missed him badly. They ran 31 times for 80 yards, just 2.6 yards per carry, and only 16 yards on 10 carries in the first half. Devin Singletary got his first start since week two of last season and ran 21 times for 31 yards. Without Skattebo, there was no spark.

And on Tuesday, Skattebo was a full participant. That is huge. Remember what he did in Houston. Seventeen carries for 124 yards and a touchdown, plus the 42-yard run in overtime that set up Singletary’s walk-off score. He is the emotional engine of this offense. When he is running hard, the whole offense plays with more energy. And when defenses have to respect the run, McCarthy gets play action and easier throws.

So I want to give you a receipt, and this one is on me. Before the Eagles game, I predicted Skattebo would play on a managed workload. He did not play at all. But now he is back, and I think the Giants were smart to rest him. Two division games in five days, with a hamstring that grabbed on him in overtime in Houston. Sitting him on Sunday gave him the best chance to be ready on Thursday. And it looks like it worked.

And there is more good news on the injury report. Darnell Mooney, who led the Giants with 54 receiving yards in Philadelphia, was upgraded to full with a chest injury. Charlie Jones, the returner who muffed a punt on Sunday, was upgraded to full with his hand. Malik Nabers, Jermaine Eluemunor, Andrew Thomas, and Theo Johnson were full all week. So the offense is about as healthy as it has been in a while.

But there is one name I am a little worried about. Abdul Carter was still limited on Tuesday with knee and back issues. Carter is one of the most important players on this defense. Brian Burns said he has been unblockable at times. And with Jayden Daniels coming to town, the Giants need their pass rushers healthy and fast. If Carter is limited on Thursday, it puts even more pressure on Arvell Reese and Kayvon Thibodeaux.

And rookie cornerback Colton Hood was added to the report with a finger injury after leaving practice with a trainer. Harbaugh said Hood dislocated a finger on a pooch punt, and he said Hood will be fine. Malik Harrison was also still limited with a groin. The Giants held a walkthrough on Wednesday, and it looks like few, if any, players will get a game status for Thursday night.

Now, let’s talk about the trade deadline, because the Giants made a decision there too. They did nothing. And I want to be fair about this. A lot of Giants fans wanted a move. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1, the only team in the NFC East with a winning record, and they have not had a home playoff game at MetLife Stadium in 15 years. This felt like a window. And the Giants did not add anyone.

But I understand why. Look at what happened with Jonathan Taylor. The Giants reportedly offered a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. According to reports, that was the best offer the Colts received. And the Colts still said no. NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo reported that the Colts wanted something like the Christian McCaffrey package, the second, third, fourth, and fifth-round picks that San Francisco sent Carolina in 2022. The Giants were not willing to pay that. Harbaugh effectively confirmed after the Eagles game that the Giants pulled their offer.

And the Colts kept Taylor even while they were selling everything else. They traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle. They traded DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. They also traded Keenan Allen and Josh Downs. But they kept Taylor and quarterback Daniel Jones. Taylor signed a two-year, 44 million dollar extension in August that runs through 2028. And Garafolo reported that the Colts could engage in trade talks again after they hire a new general manager to replace Chris Ballard. But no trade can happen until the new league year opens in March.

So that might not be the end of the Taylor story. The Giants could come back to it in the spring, when the price might be different. But for this season, the Giants are rolling with Skattebo, Singletary, rookie Gregory Desrosiers, and Tyrone Tracy Jr. when he comes back from injured reserve around week 13.

And now, the receipts, because I made two deadline predictions. Prediction number one, Jonathan Taylor would not end up a Giant. I got that one right. A lot of national insiders thought he would be traded, and some thought it could be the Giants. He stayed in Indianapolis. Prediction number two, the Giants would add a defensive tackle before the deadline. I got that one wrong. The Giants stood pat. They did not add Christian Barmore, Grover Stewart, or anyone else. So I went one for two. I will own the miss.

And I think the decision to stand pat says a lot about this front office. Harbaugh said before the deadline that the Giants were not going to go for a short-term fix at an exorbitant cost. He said that when teams do that, it usually does not work out. You end up paying a hefty price, and you do not get the return on it. He said he never thinks a team is one player away. And Joe Schoen has said all along that any move has to make sense short term and long term. They meant it. When the price did not match the value, they walked away.

Now, is that the right call? I think it is. The Giants just won in Philadelphia with their defense. They held the Eagles to 220 total yards and 13 first downs, with seven sacks. Arvell Reese had three sacks, 12 tackles, and a forced fumble. The defense is the strength of this team. And the running game problem in Philadelphia was mostly about Skattebo being out. Now he is back. If the Giants had paid a first-round equivalent for Taylor, and then Skattebo came back healthy, that would have been a lot of draft capital for a position where they already have depth.

But I also understand the other side. Windows do not stay open forever. And the Giants still have a hole in the middle of their defensive line since Dexter Lawrence was traded. They chased Jeffery Simmons, and Harbaugh even called the Titans’ owner. They were interested in Daron Payne before he broke his foot. They did not land anyone. That is a real risk going into December. If the run defense struggles against a physical team, people will ask whether one more defensive tackle would have made the difference.

Now, let’s look at Thursday night. Washington comes to MetLife for the second division game in five days. The first meeting ended in a 27 to 27 tie in week five. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1 after a 20 to 13 loss to the Rams. And on Washington’s injury report, quarterback Jayden Daniels was a full participant with a left elbow issue. Receiver Treylon Burks was limited with a foot injury. Center Lucas Patrick did not practice with a concussion. And Washington is already without Daron Payne, who broke his foot before the Eagles game.

So the Commanders have problems on both lines. A backup center against the Giants’ pass rush is a matchup the Giants should be able to win. And without Payne, Washington is thin on the interior of its defense. That is exactly where Skattebo likes to run. If the Giants can get the running game going early, McCarthy will not have to carry the offense.

And this is why Skattebo’s return matters so much for McCarthy. When the Giants beat Houston, they ran for 188 yards and four touchdowns. McCarthy only had to throw 28 times. He was able to pick his spots, and he hit two big shots down the field. When the Giants played Philadelphia without Skattebo, they ran for 80 yards, and McCarthy got sacked five times before halftime. That is the difference a running game makes. A healthy Skattebo is the best protection McCarthy’s hip could ask for.

But Daniels is the problem. He can beat you with his arm and his legs. And this week, one analyst laid out the dilemma for Dennard Wilson. If you want to spy Daniels, you need a player as fast as he is. And on the Giants, the only player as fast as Daniels is Reese. So does Wilson use Reese as a pass rusher, where he just had three sacks, or as a spy to keep Daniels from running wild? That is the chess match.

And if Carter is limited, that decision gets harder. With Carter at full speed, the Giants can afford to use Reese as a spy, because Carter and Thibodeaux can still get home. Without Carter at his best, the Giants might need Reese rushing the passer more. That is why Carter’s knee and back are the injury story to watch on the defensive side.

And let me give you one more perspective. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 4 and 5. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 7. A win on Thursday would put the Giants at 6 and 2 and 1, and 3 and 0 and 1 in the division. One analyst said the Giants’ magic number is five more wins to clinch the division. A win on Thursday gets them one step closer.

And think about what a win on Thursday would mean for the schedule. After a Thursday night game, the Giants get extra time to rest before their next game. That matters for a team managing McCarthy’s hip, Skattebo’s hamstring, and Carter’s knee and back. It also matters because Tyrone Tracy Jr. is getting closer to coming back from injured reserve. By the time the Giants hit the toughest part of their December schedule, they could have their full backfield, a healthy quarterback, and a defense that is already playing at a high level. That is the best-case scenario. And it starts with taking care of business on Thursday night at home, in front of what Winston said he hopes will be a loud MetLife crowd.

Here is what I will be watching on Thursday night. Number one, how McCarthy moves on that hip, especially when he has to escape the pocket. Number two, how many carries Skattebo gets, and whether the Giants ease him back in or let him loose. Number three, whether Abdul Carter plays and how much. Number four, how the Giants handle Jayden Daniels, rush or spy. And number five, the offensive line, because six sacks in Philadelphia cannot happen again.

And here is my prediction. McCarthy starts and finishes the game. Skattebo gives the running game the spark it was missing in Philadelphia. And the Giants win to move to 6 and 2 and 1. You can hold me to it, and you know I will own the receipt either way.

The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1, first in the NFC East, with a healthy starting quarterback, their best running back back on the field, and a front office that refused to overpay at the deadline. They are betting on the roster they built. And so far this season, every time somebody doubted that roster, it found a way to answer. A starting quarterback lost in week two. Two defensive stars lost for the season. A star defensive tackle traded in the offseason. A running back room hit by injuries. And still, first place. That is not luck. That is a team that believes in itself, coached by a staff that refuses to panic. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
