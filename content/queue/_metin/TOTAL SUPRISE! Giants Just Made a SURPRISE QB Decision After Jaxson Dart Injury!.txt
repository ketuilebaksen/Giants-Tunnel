Guys, the Giants quarterback situation just got a whole lot more interesting. After Jackson Dart went down, everybody expected New York to start shopping for another quarterback. But now there is new information that changes the entire conversation, especially when it comes to the one question that actually matters. How much are the Giants willing to give up? And then there is Jameis Winston, who could have a much bigger role in this whole decision than people realize. So what exactly is New York planning? Let's break into it.

So the first domino is official now. Jackson Dart has been placed on injured reserve, and that completely changes the shape of this roster. Harbaugh has already said the team will bring another quarterback into the building, and he has not ruled out a trade. Now, we talked yesterday about the arms race and the three paths, but here is what is new. The trade chatter has gone from a vague possibility to actual reported news around the league. And once real trade news enters the conversation, this stops being a hypothetical. New York could genuinely make a move. The question is no longer if they add a quarterback. It is what they are willing to pay, and that is where I want to live today, because the price tags tell you everything.

Let's start with the name we floated yesterday, Gardner Minshew, because I dug deeper, and there is a detail that makes this fit even cleaner than I thought. He is sitting on Arizona's practice squad right now, and he already has a connection to Matt Nagy from their time together in Kansas City. Read that again, folks. Our offensive coordinator has literally coached this man before. The Giants would not be bringing in a stranger who needs six weeks to learn the verbiage, they would be bringing in a guy with a running start on the system. Now, the honest résumé. Minshew has had genuinely productive stretches. That Indianapolis season stands out, 15 touchdowns against nine interceptions across 17 games, and a winning record as the starter. But the full career picture is a lot less shiny. He went 7 and 13 in Jacksonville. One and three in Philadelphia. Seven and six in Indy. Two and seven with the Raiders. A couple of starts in Kansas City before leaving with an injury. Add it all up and you get 17 wins and 30 losses across 47 career starts. So we have to ask the uncomfortable question out loud. Does Gardner Minshew actually give the Giants more than they already have in Jameis Winston? Maybe a little, maybe not at all. And that, folks, is exactly why the price is the entire story. If New York can get him for a late pick swap or some other low-cost arrangement, fine, that is depth insurance with a coaching connection, you do it. But spending a genuinely valuable selection on a quarterback who might just be a fancier version of what you have? That is opportunity cost eating your future for no reason.

Then there is the bigger swing, Mac Jones, and I think that door is a lot heavier than people want to admit. The Giants can absolutely make the call to San Francisco, but the 49ers have their own reasons for holding on. Remember what Jones did when Brock Purdy was unavailable last season. Eight starts, nearly 70 percent completion, 13 touchdowns against six interceptions, and a 5 and 3 record. That is a legitimately solid stretch of quarterbacking, and it is exactly why John Lynch keeps talking about how much he likes that room with Purdy and Jones together. Teams do not sell insurance policies they just watched pay out. So if San Francisco even picks up the phone, the number being whispered is something around a third round pick. And folks, a third rounder for a quarterback who might start eight games and walk? For a team still building its future? That is the kind of move that feels good in October and hurts for three years. The Giants have too many roster decisions coming down the road to be donating day two picks to the salary cap gods.

Which is why the third lane matters so much, the no-cost lane. Jimmy Garoppolo is sitting at home unsigned right now. I am not expecting Jimmy G to walk in and transform a season, but adding that much starting experience for nothing but money, no draft capital, that has real value. The same logic applies to names like Joe Milton and Hendon Hooker. If the Giants can improve the room through free agency, a practice squad deal, or some other bargain arrangement, they protect every asset they own. And keep the long view in mind too, because I would love to see this team eventually add a young developmental quarterback on top of it all. Jameis is not the answer forever, and even with Dart locked in as the future, a young arm developing quietly behind the veterans is just good franchise hygiene.

But here is the truth underneath every one of these options, and it is the same truth we landed on yesterday. Jameis Winston is the fulcrum. The Giants extended this man for a reason, and Sunday against Tennessee is his referendum. For the first time, the offense is actually being adjusted around his strengths instead of asking him to operate a system designed for a completely different player. If Winston performs, New York gets to shop calmly, no desperation tax, maybe no move at all beyond cheap insurance. If he struggles again, the front office has to get serious about the outside options, and every price we just discussed goes up. That is how much is riding on one o'clock Sunday.

So let's talk about how you actually help him, because the Titans front is no joke. Jeffery Simmons is a wrecking ball. John Franklin-Myers is having a real start to the season. Jermaine Johnson can bend the edge. That group can collapse a pocket, and here is the honest part, Jameis does not give you the same escape-hatch improvisation that Jackson Dart does. Keeping him clean is not a preference, it is the whole game plan. That starts with the offensive line doing its job, but the real protection is philosophical. Go back to the Dallas formula. Stay committed to the run, get Patrick Ricard on the field moving bodies, feed Skattebo and Najee, and make Tennessee defend multiple dimensions instead of letting every snap become a passing situation. And there is one stat from the first two weeks that has to change. The Giants have had 17 offensive drives this season, and only four of them lasted ten or more plays. Four. That is not who this team was built to be. Long, grinding possessions keep the Titans defense on the field, take the hero pressure off Winston, and keep our own defense fresh on the sideline. Time of possession is not a boring stat, folks, it is this roster's entire identity.

Now flip it over, because the defensive matchup this week is genuinely fun. Cam Ward is a talented young quarterback, but the numbers say he gets a lot less efficient when you get in his face, and here is the thing, the Giants have barely blitzed through two weeks. And that made sense! You do not go blitz-crazy against Dak Prescott and Matthew Stafford, veterans who eat aggressive defenses for breakfast. But this week is a different animal. This is the week Dennard Wilson gets to turn the pressure dial and force a young quarterback into fast, uncomfortable decisions. And if Deonte Banks cannot go, that aggression becomes even more important, because the last thing you want is Ward sitting in a clean pocket for four seconds picking at a shorthanded secondary. There is also a wrinkle on the other side. Tennessee is banged up at running back, two backs dealing with injuries including the lead dog Tony Pollard, and if those guys are limited, the Titans may have to lean on the passing game more than they want. And do not forget who is calling that offense, folks. Brian Daboll. Yes, that Brian Daboll. You know he would love nothing more than to walk into this matchup and light up his old franchise, so the Giants better arrive ready for everything.

Quick spin around the injury report, because there is real news. The encouraging one first, Malik Nabers' shoulder X-rays came back clean and he was upgraded to full participation in Thursday's practice. Exhale, everybody. That is the best sentence of the week. On the other side, Andrew Thomas got downgraded in practice with that groin issue, and if he cannot go, Marcus Mbow steps in, and honestly the kid has shown he can hold up, the work he has put into his lower body strength shows up in his anchor, which matters a lot against a power front like this one. But the bigger Thomas conversation is about the long run. Ankle, knee, now groin, all lower body, and the Giants have to manage the man's workload with more than one Sunday in mind. And to address the noise directly, yes, there has been trade speculation floating around Andrew Thomas. I would pump the brakes hard. Any team calling would have to sort through the health, the contract, and the availability questions before real compensation ever gets discussed. Maybe the offensive line picture gets revisited in the offseason, but an in-season Thomas trade is not something a serious franchise does casually. On the edge, if Brian Burns misses time, watch for Kayvon Thibodeaux sliding back outside for more one-on-one opportunities, because Abdul Carter is already drawing so much attention that somebody is going to eat single blocks, and for a man in a contract year, those are gold. And in the secondary, Greg Newsome becomes an even bigger piece while Colton Hood keeps adjusting to NFL speed, plus keep an eye on the slot, where Andru Phillips brings all that physicality, sometimes too much of it, and Darius Washington could quietly keep growing into a bigger role depending on the matchups.

One last thing, and I want to say this clearly, because the takes have been flying. I do not think one injury should erase everything this organization believed about Jackson Dart a month ago. And when people throw around the words injury prone, look at the actual situations. His previous issue came when he hit his head scrambling to recover a loose ball. This knee happened on a regular dropback when two defenders sandwiched him. Those are two completely different, completely random football events, not a pattern in his body. And Giants history says early injuries do not define careers. Phil Simms got beat up early in his run and became a Super Bowl MVP. Amani Toomer dealt with injuries at the start and turned into the most productive receiver this franchise ever had. Perspective, folks. Dart's only job right now is to heal properly. The evaluation happens when he is standing on a healthy knee, not a day before.

And before we wrap, let me define what a passing grade actually looks like for Jameis on Sunday, so we are all judging the same test. It is not 300 yards. It is not highlight throws. Here is my checklist. Zero turnovers, first and always, because that was the entire difference between the Dallas win and the Los Angeles disaster. A third down conversion rate back above 50 percent, because that is what keeps drives alive and keeps Tennessee's offense in street clothes on the sideline. At least two of those long, soul-draining ten play drives, because we have had only four all season and that number is a scandal for a team built like this. And decisiveness, the ball coming out on time to the flat, to the checkdown, to Likely over the middle, no hero ball into coverage. If Winston checks those boxes and this team wins ugly, 20 to 13 ugly, I am completely satisfied, and so should you be. Style points are for teams with healthy quarterbacks. Substance points win Octobers.

So here is where everything lands, family. The decision in front of this front office comes down to three things. The cost, the fit, and above all, what Jameis Winston puts on tape starting Sunday. Beat Tennessee behind a real run game and a clean pocket, and the panic ends, the market cools, and this season stays alive on schedule. Come out flat, and the phones get loud in a hurry.
