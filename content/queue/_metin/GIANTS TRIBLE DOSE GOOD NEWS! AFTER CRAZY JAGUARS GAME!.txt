Good news number two: the red zone wall.

This was the stat that came up again and again on the national shows. Jacksonville made six trips inside the Giants' 20 and came away with one touchdown. One. One analyst said the Giants were all over them like back pockets, and that most of the yards Jacksonville piled up came late while they were trying to catch up. Let me walk you through how that wall held up, because it was not one play. It was a series of plays, and each one tells you something about this team.

Start with Abdul Carter. One analyst said Carter was unblockable the whole game and compared him to a young Micah Parsons. That is an opinion, and it is a big one, but go back and watch the tape. That is not just talent. That is preparation. That is a second-year player studying tendencies and trusting what he sees. And when you combine that kind of film study with his first step, you get a guy who can wreck a game plan.

For weeks, the critics said Carter was a bust because the sack total was low. Harbaugh kept saying the sacks would come. Against Jacksonville, they came.
