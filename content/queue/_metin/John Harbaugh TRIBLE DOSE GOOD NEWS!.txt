Dose number one: Harbaugh shuts down the Abdul Carter controversy.

To understand why this has become such a big deal, you have to look at the expectations around Carter. The Giants used a premium draft pick on him because they wanted a difference-maker on the defensive line. Someone who could get after the quarterback every week and make big plays. And so far, the sack numbers have not matched those expectations. He has only two sacks. That is where the frustration started. Some fans have even started calling him a bust.

Now, I understand wanting more production from a highly drafted player. But I think the criticism has gone way too far, because sacks do not always tell the full story of how a pass rusher is playing. And Harbaugh made that crystal clear this week.

He explained that he studies the film, grades every player himself, and compares his grades with what his position coaches see. And according to Harbaugh, Carter is grading out really well on his sheet and on the coach’s sheet. He called him disruptive. He said, I promise you they’re accounting for him. He described him as a game changer and a game wrecker, and he said there are going to be a lot of game wrecks in Carter’s future. And he made it clear he believes the sacks are coming.

He also said something I think a lot of people need to hear. You can have a bunch of sacks and really not be playing that well. We’ve seen that before. In other words, the sack column is one piece of the puzzle, not the whole picture.

And here is where Harbaugh’s argument gets really strong. The numbers outside the box score are on his side. Pro Football Focus has Carter with an 89.3 overall grade, which ranks third among all edge defenders in the league. Third. With only two sacks. His pass-rush grade is 84.9, and he has 28 pressures. That tells you exactly what Harbaugh is seeing on tape. Carter is beating blockers, collapsing the pocket and forcing quarterbacks off their spots. The finish just has not shown up in the sack total yet.

Think about what happens when an offensive line has to give extra attention to one defender. The quarterback gets hurried. The pocket moves. The timing of the play changes. And somebody else gets an opening. That is a big reason why Arvell Reese was able to put up three sacks in Philadelphia. When the protection slides toward Carter, a guy like Reese gets a one-on-one. That impact never shows up next to Carter’s name, but it shows up on the scoreboard.

I have also seen people compare Carter to Kayvon Thibodeaux, and I do not think that comparison settles anything. Thibodeaux has had productive moments of his own, and every player develops on his own timeline. What matters is whether Carter keeps improving and starts turning those pressures into finishing plays. Harbaugh is not promising instant perfection. He is saying the film gives him real reasons to believe.

And against Jacksonville, this matters a lot. Trevor Lawrence gets the ball out in about two-point-seven seconds, and the Jaguars have allowed only 14 sacks all season. So the Giants may not get a ton of sacks on Sunday, even if they are getting pressure. But pressure still wins. Hurry Lawrence, get hands in the throwing lanes, force a bad throw, and you change the game. My opinion, not a report: Carter is due for a breakout game, and I think it could come Sunday.

And think about the whole front together. Carter on one edge. Thibodeaux, who the Giants kept at the trade deadline instead of moving him, on the other. Reese inside, blitzing from the linebacker spot. That is three different players an offensive line has to account for on every snap. Since Brian Burns went down in week three, this defense has leaned into pressure, and it has been one of the most blitz-heavy units in the league. But on Sunday, against a quarterback who gets rid of the ball this fast, I think the best plan is to let those three win their one-on-ones with a four-man rush and drop more players into coverage. If Carter keeps creating pressure at the rate the numbers say he does, the sacks will follow. My opinion, not a report.

Before we get to dose two, there is a related controversy I want to touch on, because it ties into Skattebo.

Former Giants defensive coordinator Wink Martindale took a shot at the old team culture this week. He suggested the previous regime cared too much about headlines, attention, and which players could get popular online. And he used Cam Skattebo as an example, even though Martindale was gone before Skattebo got to New York. He questioned whether basic football jobs were getting enough attention, and he brought up things like picking up blitzes, flashy celebrations, and what happened on a quarterback sneak.

Now, I understand the concern about distractions. But I do not agree with all of that. Take the sneak. On that short-yardage quarterback sneak with Jameis Winston against Washington, people were blaming Skattebo for not pushing from behind. But Harbaugh already explained after watching the tape that it was not a tush push. It was a normal quarterback sneak with a check built in, and Skattebo was not supposed to push. So blaming him for standing exactly where the play told him to stand is not fair.

Still, the bigger point is worth talking about. Fans do not want to hear more about viral moments than about missed assignments and losses. The Giants need personality, sure. But personality cannot replace execution. And that brings me to the decision Skattebo just made.

Skattebo is stepping away from his touchdown backflips, at least for now. And the reason is hard to ignore. Aidan Hutchinson suffered a devastating knee injury while celebrating a sack, and watching that happen made Skattebo rethink the risk. You hate to see a great player lose his season over a celebration. There is nothing wrong with being excited. But protecting your body matters. And remember, Skattebo already dealt with a serious ankle injury that ended his previous season. That is why those flips made people nervous. Malachi Fields and Darnell Mooney have had their own backflip moments too. I love the energy. But I would much rather see a healthy running back making big plays every single week.

And that is the thing about Skattebo. He can really play. He has been productive, physical and exciting with the ball in his hands. He just ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns against Washington, plus 44 receiving yards. The fumbles earlier in the season created real concerns, and the side stories kept piling up, the pretzel-wrapped hot dog before the game, getting sick on the sideline, all of it. But I want people talking about Skattebo’s runs, his toughness, and how he helps the Giants win, not about whether his next celebration goes viral. And this decision tells me he gets it. That is a mature move from a young player.

Dose number two: the injury report is encouraging.

Let’s go through the latest practice report before Jacksonville.

The best news first. Cam Skattebo practiced fully despite a hamstring issue. That is huge, because he is the engine of this offense right now. Deonte Banks was a full participant with a shoulder problem. And another player practiced fully after dealing with an illness.

The Banks shoulder actually answers a question from last week. Remember when Harbaugh explained why the Giants chose to receive the opening kickoff against Washington, even though they have been a defer team all season? He said a defensive player got dinged on his shoulder in warmups and was not going to be ready for the first series. That was Banks. So the Giants took the ball first to buy him time, and he was able to play. And now he is practicing fully. Against Jacksonville’s receivers, Parker Washington, Jakobi Meyers, Brian Thomas Jr., the Giants need Banks and the whole secondary ready to compete.

Now, the injuries to watch. Nick Jones was the only player who did not practice, because of a knee injury. That matters for depth at corner and on special teams, especially since Jones has been one of the gunners on the punt team. And that makes the Tavierre Thomas signing even more important. Thomas led the Vikings with 19 special teams tackles last season, and ESPN’s Jordan Raanan suggested he could help as a gunner. If Jones cannot go, Thomas could step right into that role.

Malik Harrison was limited with a groin injury after missing the previous game. And safety Jason Pinnock was limited with a hip problem. Depending on how Pinnock progresses, the Giants may have to think carefully about their safety rotation, including Tyler Nubin and Ar’Darius Washington. Washington has been playing well lately, including that fourth-quarter interception of Jayden Daniels, so if he needs to step up, I am comfortable with that.

Overall, this is a good report. The key players on offense are healthy. The secondary is mostly intact. And the Giants had the long break after the Thursday night game to recover. That is a real advantage going into a game against a 7-2 team.

Dose number three: letting Wan’Dale Robinson walk is looking smarter by the week.

This one involves a former Giant, and the consequences could show up in next year’s draft instead of on Sunday. Wan’Dale Robinson went on injured reserve with Tennessee because of an ankle injury, and his season is over. First of all, I hate to see that happen to him. He gave everything he had to this team.

But from the Giants’ side, there is a draft angle here. Over the Cap’s Nick Korte had projected a fourth-round compensatory pick for the Giants after they lost Robinson in free agency. With his playing time cut short, that pick could slide to the fifth round. It is only a projection, not an official award, but it is still a little disappointing.

Here is how compensatory picks work. They reward teams that lose more qualifying free agents than they sign. The formula looks at the size of the contracts, how much those players actually play, and certain honors. The Giants lost Robinson, Cordale Flott, Daniel Bellinger, Dane Belton and Austin Schlottmann. They added Isaiah Likely, Greg Newsome II, Jordan Stout and Patrick Ricard. After those moves cancel each other out, Robinson is the departure driving the projected extra pick. His injury does not erase it automatically, but it could lower the round.

And that difference matters, because the Giants are short on middle-round picks in 2027. Right now, they have their first, second and third-round picks, their own sixth, and an extra sixth from Jacksonville. Their fourth went to Cleveland in the trade up to draft Malachi Fields. Their fifth went to Minnesota in the J.J. McCarthy trade. That leaves a noticeable hole between the early rounds and the late rounds.

And we saw why that matters at the trade deadline. The Giants had interest in defensive help, including DeForest Buckner and Grover Stewart. Buckner ended up going from Indianapolis to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth-round pick. And Jeremy Fowler reported the Colts wanted a fourth for Stewart. The Giants had already traded their 2027 fourth and fifth, which made those deals more complicated. Picks are not just for drafting rookies. They are also currency when a veteran becomes available. Sometimes having the right pick matters as much as the player you would use it on.

So why do I call this good news? Because when you step back and ask whether the Giants made the right call letting Robinson go, the answer is clearly yes. And not because he suddenly became a bad player. In 2025, Robinson gave the Giants 92 catches for 1,014 yards and four touchdowns. He built real chemistry with Jackson Dart. I appreciated everything he brought to the offense. But Tennessee gave him a contract worth about 17.5 million dollars a year, and his production with Cam Ward has not matched what we saw in New York. Different quarterback, different offense, different situation. I do not put all of that on him.

But look at what the Giants found in Isaiah Likely. He is officially a tight end, but a lot of his work comes from the slot, the same area Robinson used to work. He can catch in traffic, line up in different spots, and create mismatches. And he has been a major receiving weapon without costing anywhere close to the same annual amount. Over the last two games, he set a career high in catches in Philadelphia and then went for nine catches, 127 yards and a 66-yard touchdown against Washington.

That flexibility also works perfectly with Malik Nabers. Instead of asking one slot receiver to do everything, the Giants can move Likely around and create opportunities for both of them. And with Mooney and Fields on the outside, the receiving group is deeper than people think. So when I weigh the savings, the receiving production, and the possible extra draft pick, I still love the Giants’ decision. I wish Robinson a full recovery. But losing him has not held this offense back. If anything, it opened the door for Likely to become one of the best moves of the Giants’ offseason.

There is one more piece to this, and this part is my opinion, not a report. Not paying a slot receiver 17.5 million a year is the kind of discipline that leaves room to take care of your own. They just extended Greg Newsome II, a three-year deal worth 46 million dollars with 32 million guaranteed, keeping him through 2029. They also kept the core of this defense together at the deadline instead of selling off pieces. When you make a smart call on one contract, it lets you make bold calls on others. That is how a front office builds a contender that lasts more than one season.

So those are your three doses. Harbaugh and the numbers say Carter is playing at an elite level, and the sacks are coming. The injury report is encouraging, with Skattebo and Banks both full. And the Robinson decision looks better every week, thanks to Likely. Plus, Skattebo is putting his body and his team first by retiring the backflips for now.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Last week I said the Giants’ pass rush needed to show up after only getting two quarterback hits on Daniels. I still believe that, and I am holding them to it on Sunday. I also said Likely was turning into a real weapon, and the numbers keep backing that up. And I picked the Giants to beat Washington, which was wrong. I own it, and we keep moving.

Let’s keep the big picture in mind. The Giants are 5-3-1, alone in first in the NFC East. Dallas is 4-6. Washington is 3-5-1, two games back, with the head-to-head edge. Philadelphia is 2-7. One playoff model has the Giants at about 72 percent to make the playoffs, and a win over Jacksonville would push that to around 85. This Sunday is huge.

Here is my prediction, and hold me to it. Carter gets at least one sack and a handful of pressures on Trevor Lawrence. Skattebo runs hard, scores a touchdown, and celebrates without a backflip. Banks plays a clean game with no flags. Likely has another big day out of the slot. And the Giants beat Jacksonville to move to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A pass rusher who is one of the highest-graded edge players in football. A running back who is playing physical and smart. A tight end who replaced a 1,000-yard receiver without missing a beat. A coach who trusts the tape over the noise. And a two-game lead in the division. The critics are focused on the sack column. The Giants are focused on winning. I know which one I care about.
