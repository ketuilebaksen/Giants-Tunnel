Jonathan Taylor has made his decision, and it might be the most important piece of the Giants’ trade deadline puzzle. With his name all over the trade rumors, Taylor sat down with Colts owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon to talk about his future. And he told her he loves the organization. He has also said publicly that he takes pride in playing for Indianapolis and that he has not asked for a trade. So the best running back on the market is not pushing his way out. But here is the twist. According to CBS Sports, the owner did not give him any assurances that he is definitely going to stay. And with the November 10th deadline less than a week away, the Giants are one of five teams that have shown interest, along with Denver, Green Bay, Minnesota, and Seattle. So, what does Taylor’s decision mean for the Giants? Does it make a deal harder, easier, or more expensive? Today, I am going to break it down, and I am going to tell you exactly where I land. Let’s get into it.

If Taylor wants to stay in Indianapolis, should the Giants still chase him?

Quick note before we dive in. A lot of the Taylor material going around this week is the same stuff we already covered, the Pelissero report about a rejected second-round offer, the Simmons phone call, and the Art Stapleton trade idea. I am not going to repeat all of that. Instead, I want to focus on what is actually new. And there is a lot that is new.

Let’s start with Taylor himself. A player’s own stance matters more at the deadline than people think. When a star asks for a trade, the team loses leverage. Everyone in the league knows the player wants out, so the price goes down. That is not what is happening here. Taylor has not asked for a trade. He told the owner he loves the organization. That means the Colts are under no pressure to move him. They can hold out for their price, and if nobody meets it, they can keep him and say, he never wanted to leave anyway.

And that changes how I read the market. When Taylor met with Irsay-Gordon, she reportedly did not promise him he would stay. That tells you the Colts are open to a deal, but only on their terms. And here is where the reports start to conflict. Adam Schefter reported that the Colts are open to trading Taylor and that talks are active. But CBS Sports’ Aditi Kinkhabwala reported over the weekend that the Colts told her they are not soliciting offers for him. So which is it?

I think both can be true. The Colts are not shopping Taylor. They are not calling teams and asking, what will you give us? But they are listening when teams call them. That is a big difference. A team that is shopping a player wants to move him. A team that is listening wants to be blown away. And when you are trying to blow a team away, the price goes up.

And we already know the price is high. Ryan Dunleavy of the New York Post reported this week that the asking price is high, and that the Giants will look at other offensive playmakers on the trade market. ESPN reported that the Colts would want something similar to what San Francisco gave Carolina for Christian McCaffrey in 2022. That was a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth-round pick. And we know at least one offer, a second plus more, was turned down.

Now, let me give you the contract side, because there is a new detail here that I think matters a lot. Taylor signed a two-year, 44 million dollar extension in August, so he is under contract through 2028. According to The Athletic’s Daniel Popper, if the Colts trade him, they would be left with about 10 million dollars in dead money in 2027. That is real money for a team that is rebuilding. It is one more reason the Colts are not in a rush. Trading Taylor costs them something too, so they want to be paid properly for it.

And let me remind you how good Taylor still is, because this is not a declining player. Heading into week 8, he had 587 rushing yards and a league-high eight rushing touchdowns, on a Colts team that was 2 and 5. He has 40 touchdowns over the past three seasons, the third most in the NFL. He is 27 years old. He led the league in rushing in 2021. Popper called him one of the best running backs in the league, and said he has been for some time. Popper also ranked him as the top trade piece left in the entire NFL now that Jeffery Simmons is in Chicago.

So the Giants are chasing the best player on the market, from a team that does not have to sell, with a player who does not want to leave, and four other teams in the mix. That is the hardest kind of trade to make.

Now, let’s look at the competition, because it has changed since I last talked about it. ESPN listed five teams with at least some interest. Denver, Green Bay, Minnesota, Seattle, and the Giants. And Popper’s trade board added Cincinnati as a possible landing spot. But he also said Seattle looks less likely now, because Jadarian Price and Zach Charbonnet have returned to that backfield. And Minnesota, as Jonathan Jones reported, cannot fit Taylor under its salary cap right now without some major work. So the realistic field might be smaller than it looks. Denver and Green Bay are the names I would watch most closely, because they both have reasons to want a lead back.

And here is the thing about a smaller field. It does not automatically mean a lower price. Because the Colts do not have to sell. If only two or three teams are serious, the Colts can simply wait. And if nobody meets their price by November 10th, they keep a great running back who loves the team and is signed for two more years. That is the leverage a happy player gives his team.

So, let me walk you through how my own thinking has changed over the last couple of weeks, because I want to be transparent with you. When the Taylor rumors first started, I said I would be stunned if the Giants gave up a first-round pick for a running back in the middle of a season. Then I said my absolute ceiling was one first-round pick, and my ideal deal was a second plus a mid-round pick. Then I looked at the Giants’ history with second-round picks, and I got more comfortable with trading picks for a proven star. Now I have new information. Taylor does not want to leave, the Colts are not shopping him, and the price is a McCaffrey-type package.

And here is where I land today. If the Colts hold firm at a McCaffrey-style package, I would walk away. Not because Taylor is not worth it on the field. But because the Giants do not have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks, so a deal like that would mean emptying the cupboard or reaching deep into 2028. Harbaugh joked last week that 2028 is always easier to put on the credit card. But he also said the Giants are not loaded with draft capital and have to be smart shoppers. A McCaffrey package is not smart shopping for a team in the Giants’ situation.

But if the Colts come down to a second-round pick plus a mid-round pick, or a single first-round pick with nothing else, I would make the deal. That is my line. And I want to be clear, this is my opinion, not a report.

And there is a timing piece that I think a lot of people are missing. The Giants play Philadelphia on Sunday. The deadline is Tuesday. That means if the Giants want Taylor available for the Eagles game, they have to get the deal done by the end of this week. If they cannot, the next realistic window is Monday and Tuesday, after the Eagles game. And that game could change everything. A win in Philadelphia, with Cam Skattebo looking healthy, could make the Giants more comfortable walking away. A loss, or another Skattebo setback, could push them to pay more.

And Skattebo is the key to all of this. Harbaugh said on Monday that Skattebo is day-to-day with the hamstring he felt grab in Houston. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve. The Giants just brought back Eric Gray to the practice squad, and they have rookie Gregory Desrosiers on the active roster. If Skattebo practices fully by Friday, the urgency at running back drops. If he does not, the Giants may feel like they have to make a move.

Now, let me give you the case for sticking with what the Giants have, because it is a strong one. Go back to overtime in Houston. Skattebo broke off a 42-yard run. On the very next snap, Devin Singletary went 30 yards for the walk-off touchdown. That was a 27 to 21 road win, and Houston never got the ball in overtime. The Giants ran for 188 yards that day, with four rushing touchdowns from four different players. That is not a team that is desperate at running back. That is a team that is thin at running back for a few weeks.

And remember what Schoen said about Tracy. His injury is not season-ending, and it will not be something long-term. He can come back around week 13 against San Francisco. So if the Giants trade a big package for Taylor, they would be paying for a position where they already have two young backs who will be healthy for the stretch run. That is the argument against.

And the argument for is just as clear. When the weather turns in December and January, the team that can run the ball usually wins. Taylor would give the Giants a lead back who can turn a two-yard run into a seven-yard run when nothing is blocked perfectly. He would make life easier for J.J. McCarthy, because defenses would have to respect play action. And he would give Malik Nabers more one-on-one chances, because a defense cannot keep a safety deep to help on Nabers while also stopping Taylor. That combination could win a playoff game.

And history tells us midseason running back trades can work. In 2010, Seattle traded for Marshawn Lynch in October, for a fourth and a fifth-round pick. A few months later, he ran through the entire Saints defense in the playoffs on the Beast Quake run. In 2017, the Eagles traded a fourth-round pick for Jay Ajayi at the deadline, and he became part of a backfield that won the Super Bowl. And in 2022, San Francisco traded that big package for Christian McCaffrey, and the 49ers went all the way to the NFC Championship Game. So, the idea that a running back cannot change a season is not true. The right back, in the right system, at the right time, can be the final piece.

But look at the prices in those examples. Lynch and Ajayi cost mid-round picks. McCaffrey cost a lot more, and he was a different kind of weapon, a back who could line up as a receiver and was signed long term. The Colts are asking for McCaffrey money. The Giants would be much happier paying something closer to Ajayi money. That gap is the whole negotiation.

And think about the coaching staff that would be using Taylor. Greg Roman is on this staff. In 2019, with Harbaugh in Baltimore and Roman running the offense, the Ravens set the NFL record for rushing yards in a single season. Matt Nagy is working with McCarthy. This is a staff that knows how to build a run game around more than one back. Give them Taylor and Skattebo, with Patrick Ricard leading the way at fullback, and I think they would build one of the most physical rushing attacks in the league. That is the dream scenario, and I understand why the Giants are at least making the call.

So what is the alternative if Taylor does not happen? Dunleavy said the Giants will look at other offensive playmakers. That could be a cheaper running back. It could be a receiver or a tight end who gives McCarthy another reliable target. Remember, Theo Johnson missed the Houston game. And I still think the Giants need help at defensive tackle, with Dexter Lawrence gone and Simmons in Chicago. Jonathan Jones reported that the Giants were looking at both running back and defensive tackle, and that league sources expect them to make a move before the deadline. So even if Taylor stays in Indianapolis, I expect the Giants to add someone.

And here is a fun wrinkle if Taylor does stay in Indianapolis. The Giants play the Colts later this season. Tracy is going to miss that game while he is on injured reserve. So if the Colts keep Taylor, the Giants might have to stop him instead of using him. A team that just held Houston to 84 rushing yards would get a chance to prove itself against one of the league leaders in rushing touchdowns. That is not the worst outcome in the world, but I would rather have him on my side.

And if he does come, let me tell you how I think the backfield would work. Taylor would be the lead back, with 15 to 18 carries a game. Skattebo would be the hammer, the change of pace who punishes defenders late in games. Singletary would be the veteran who can step in on third downs and protect McCarthy. And when Tracy comes back around week 13, he would add a receiving and return element. That is four backs who all bring something different. It would be a good problem to have. But the coaches would have to manage it carefully, and Harbaugh has already said he cares about how every move affects the locker room.

And let me add one more thing about Taylor’s decision that I respect. In a league where players often push their way out when a team starts rebuilding, Taylor is doing the opposite. He is saying he loves the place, even as the Colts trade away Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner and fire their general manager. That tells you something about his character. And if he does end up in New York, I think he would be exactly the kind of person Harbaugh wants in that locker room. Remember, Harbaugh said the Giants would think about how a trade affects their locker room, and might even talk to players before making a move. A player with Taylor’s attitude would fit that culture.

And let me say this clearly, because I think it gets lost in all the trade talk. The most important thing the Giants can do this week is not trade for Jonathan Taylor. It is beat the Philadelphia Eagles. The Giants have lost 13 straight games in Philadelphia, counting the playoffs, and they have not won there since 2013. Harbaugh said the team does not care about that streak, and I love that answer. But it is still a hard place to win. The Eagles are 2 and 6 and desperate, playing with Tanner McKee at quarterback after losing six in a row. If the Giants protect the football, run the ball with whoever is healthy, and let Abdul Carter and the pass rush go to work, they can win that game with or without a new running back.

Now, let’s talk about the division, because it is part of why the Giants might stretch. While the Giants were on their bye, Dallas lost at home in overtime to Arizona, and Philadelphia lost 27 to 10 to Washington for its sixth straight loss. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. The Giants have a three-loss cushion over Dallas and a four-loss cushion over the Eagles. When the division is sitting there for you to take, it is tempting to push your chips in. But Harbaugh and Schoen have been consistent. Short term and long term. Not a one-and-done. Smart shoppers.

And that is why I think the Giants’ final decision on Taylor will come down to one number. If the Colts will take a package the Giants can live with, I think they pull the trigger. If the Colts insist on a McCaffrey-style haul, I think the Giants walk away and add a cheaper piece. And I think either decision can be the right one, as long as the price matches the value.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Colts lower their price as the deadline gets closer. Number two, whether Denver or Green Bay makes a bigger push. Number three, Skattebo’s practice reports on Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. Number four, whether the Giants pivot to a defensive tackle or a pass catcher. And number five, whether Taylor himself says anything else about his future.

And here is a prediction you can hold me to. I do not think Jonathan Taylor will be a Giant by November 10th. I think the Colts’ price stays too high for this front office, and I think the Giants add a smaller piece instead. If I am wrong and Taylor is wearing blue next week, I will own it, and I will be thrilled to be wrong.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East, with a disciplined front office, a coach who is not afraid to make calls, and a backfield that has already proven it can win big games. With Taylor or without him, I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
