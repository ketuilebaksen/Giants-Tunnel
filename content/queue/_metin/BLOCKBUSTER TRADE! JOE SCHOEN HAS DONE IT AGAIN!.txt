Who has been the best Giants pass rusher this season, Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux, or Arvell Reese?

Let me start with Brian Burns, because this is the part of the story that hit me the hardest. Burns is on injured reserve after tearing his ACL. He is not going to play again this season. And yet there he was, on a national show, breaking down film of his teammates and hyping them up like a proud older brother. When he was asked about his recovery, he said it is going good, and he is taking it day by day. But then he said what every injured player feels. He wishes he could be out there with them, especially with where the team is right now. He said injuries hit the Giants hard, not just him but some other key players too, and there is nothing he wants more than to be on the field with his guys.

I respect that a lot. It would be easy for a veteran star to disappear during a season-ending injury. Instead, Burns is out there defending his teammates in public. And the teammate he defended the hardest was Abdul Carter.

Here is what Burns said about Carter. He has been unblockable at times. He ruins plays. But Burns said he is not doing anything Burns has not seen before. He has seen it time and time again in practice. The rest of the league is just now seeing it. And then Burns took on the critics directly. Everybody has something to say about Carter’s numbers being low. But if you watch the game, you can see the impact, the speed, and the tenacity he plays with. Offenses have to account for him no matter what. And Burns ended it with two words. Watch ball.

I love that, and I think he is right. Sack numbers are the most famous stat for a pass rusher, but they do not tell the whole story. A pass rusher can win his matchup, collapse the pocket, and force the quarterback to throw early without ever getting credited with a sack. And when a defense has to send a guard or a running back to help on one player, someone else gets a one-on-one. That is impact. It does not always show up in the box score, but it shows up in the result of the play.

And the results are real. According to the numbers shown during that broadcast, the Giants rank third in the NFL in pass rushing. They are allowing the third fewest yards per completion in the league. And they have the fourth most pass breakups. Think about what those three numbers mean together. The pass rush is getting home fast, quarterbacks are being forced into short throws, and the secondary is closing on the football. That is a defense where the front and the back are working as one unit.

And remember what this defense has lost. Tremaine Edmunds is out for the season with a shoulder injury. Brian Burns is out for the season with a torn ACL. Dexter Lawrence was traded in the offseason. In Houston, the Giants were down five starters on defense, and they still held the Texans to 237 total yards, with C.J. Stroud throwing for just 153. That does not happen by accident. That happens because the people in charge built a deep, fast, versatile defense, and that is the part of this story that belongs to Joe Schoen.

Let me explain the scheme, because it is really clever. The Giants’ defensive coordinator, Dennard Wilson, has been putting three of his best pass rushers on the same side of the formation. Kayvon Thibodeaux, Abdul Carter, and Arvell Reese. On the broadcast, the film showed it clearly. Thibodeaux and Carter lined up on one side of the offense. Reese came off the ball on a cross dog, a linebacker blitz through the line. Suddenly, Carter and Thibodeaux were rushing together. Thibodeaux ran a stunt, got through, and Carter dropped out into coverage. On another snap, Reese was on one side, with Thibodeaux and Carter on the other, and Carter wrapped underneath on a stunt.

Why is that so hard to block? Think about it from the offensive line’s point of view. Linemen are taught to pass off stunts. When two rushers cross, the guard and tackle have to switch who they are blocking in a split second. That is hard enough when the rushers are average. When the rushers are three of the fastest players on the field, it becomes almost impossible. Burns said it himself. When you put Carter and Reese together, somebody gets held, and it becomes a problem. When you put all three of them together in a passing situation, he said, he does not know what an offense can do against it.

And one of the former players on that show made a great point. Some teams used to call this kind of thing a rabbit package. A group of speed rushers you only put on the field on obvious passing downs. But the Giants do not have to take anyone off the field to run it, because all of these guys can also play the run at a high level. That is the secret. The Giants do not have to choose between stopping the run and rushing the passer. Their best pass rushers are good enough against the run to stay on the field for every down.

And he added one more thing that I think is important. Speed changes everything. When guards are trying to pass off stunts, and that much speed is coming at them, it is very difficult. And he pointed out that if Burns were healthy and lined up on the other side, the tackle over there could not pay attention to anything else. That is a scary thought for 2027. But even without Burns, the speed is doing the damage.

And then Burns explained something about how the rush works that most fans never think about. Is it fixed, or do the rushers decide among themselves? Burns said it is a little bit of both. Sometimes it is a called stunt. Sometimes it is a pattern. Sometimes it is a simulated pressure or a blitz. But in obvious passing situations, the rushers can play off each other, and one guy can give another a signal. Think about how much trust that requires. Young players communicating with each other, in real time, before the snap, in front of a loud crowd. That is not just athletic ability. That is football intelligence.

And Burns made sure to give credit to the guy who does not always get it. Kayvon Thibodeaux. Burns called him the unsung hero of this whole thing. He said even when Burns was on the field, Thibodeaux was the glue. He is the guy you can kick inside to a three technique. He can balance the rush and play in the A gap. He was strong enough to play a lot of positions that Burns and Carter did not usually play, unless they were coming off the ball. Thibodeaux also does a lot of the dirty work in the run game. And since Burns went down, Thibodeaux has taken over the Sam linebacker role, so he has been dropping into coverage too.

I want to sit on that for a second, because I think Thibodeaux’s season has been underrated. He was the fifth overall pick in 2022. He has heard a lot of criticism over the years. And now, without Burns, he is moving all over the formation, playing inside, playing outside, dropping into coverage, and doing the jobs that nobody notices. That is a player putting the team first. And when the guy who used to play next to him calls him the glue, you should believe it.

Now, let’s talk about versatility, because Joe Schoen talked about it during the bye too. He called it somewhat of a positionless defense. And he gave an example. Last year against Kansas City, the Giants had an injury at linebacker, and Abdul Carter played off the ball as an inside linebacker. Schoen reminded everyone that Carter did that at Penn State too. In fact, Carter is only in his third year playing edge rusher. Think about that. A player who was a linebacker not long ago is now one of the most feared edge rushers on this team, and he can still move back to linebacker when the team needs it.

And then Schoen talked about Arvell Reese. He said the clear vision for Reese was inside linebacker. That would be his role. The Giants thought he was a really good player. But they also wanted to know if he could play off the edge. And the answer is yes. The Giants are moving him around a lot. Schoen said losing Brian Burns really hurt, but Reese’s ability to blitz and also play on the edge, plus Dennard Wilson’s ability to move these guys around, has been awesome.

And then Schoen said this about Reese. As big as he is, as fast as he is, and as productive as he has been, the kid just turned 21 a couple of months ago. It is insane, Schoen said. The sky is the limit for him. And he said the Giants are really happy with where Reese is.

So let me connect the dots on why this is a Joe Schoen story. Schoen drafted Thibodeaux fifth overall in 2022. He drafted Abdul Carter third overall in 2025. He drafted Arvell Reese this year. He traded for Brian Burns. That is four premium pass rushers in four years. A lot of people questioned it. Why keep spending top picks on the same position? Well, this is why. When one goes down, the defense does not fall apart. The others slide over and keep going. That is roster building. And when people say Schoen has done it again, this is the part they should be talking about.

And this matters for the trade deadline too. Last time, I told you that if I were Schoen, I would look at a defensive player before a running back. I still feel that way. But after watching this film, I think the need is not another edge rusher. The Giants have enough speed off the edge. If they add anyone on defense, I think it makes more sense to add a veteran linebacker, someone who can take some of the off-ball snaps that Edmunds used to take. That would free up Reese to keep rushing the passer. That is my opinion, not a report. But it fits exactly what Schoen described. A short-term move that does not hurt the long term.
