Joe Schoen has pulled off something almost no general manager in this league ever gets to do. Back in the 2024 draft, he had a chance to take J.J. McCarthy, and he passed, taking Malik Nabers instead. Two years later, he got McCarthy anyway, for a fifth-round pick. And on Sunday in Houston, the quarterback Schoen passed on will be throwing the football to the receiver Schoen picked over him. Folks, the Giants ended up with both. That is the good news heading into the Texans game, and it is only the beginning. Because when you look at how this quarterback room was built, a young franchise quarterback for the future, a veteran safety net who has already won games this season, and now a hungry starter who just threw for 243 yards in a single quarter, you realize Schoen has quietly built one of the most flexible quarterback situations in football. Today, I am going to walk you through exactly how he did it, all the good news surrounding this team right now, and the one big test waiting in Houston. Let’s get into it.

Let me start by setting the scene, because a lot has happened this week. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that J.J. McCarthy will start for the Giants against the Texans on Sunday. Not a package, the start. And in my last video, I had to own a mistake, because I had predicted Jameis Winston would get one more start. Harbaugh went bold instead. So today, I want to step back and look at the bigger picture. Not just the decision Harbaugh made this week, but the decisions Joe Schoen made over the last two years that made it possible.

Go back to April of 2024. The Giants had the sixth overall pick, and that draft was loaded with quarterbacks. J.J. McCarthy was on the board, a national champion at Michigan, coming off a title run under Jim Harbaugh. A lot of people thought the Giants should take a quarterback. Schoen took Malik Nabers instead. McCarthy went four picks later to Minnesota at number ten. At the time, plenty of fans were frustrated that the Giants did not get their quarterback of the future. Now look at it. Nabers has become one of the best young receivers in football. He just put together his third straight 100-yard game, with 12 catches for 165 yards against the Saints. He passed Puka Nacua for the second-most receptions by any player through his first 25 career games, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. That pick was right.

Then came 2025. Schoen took Abdul Carter third overall, a pass rusher who is already making a difference on this defense. And then he made the move that defined the future of this franchise. He traded back into the first round, at pick 25, to take Jackson Dart. And here is the part that makes this week even better. The team Schoen traded with to get back into the first round for Dart was the Houston Texans. The same Houston Texans the Giants play on Sunday. You cannot make this stuff up. Houston got extra picks to move down, and the Giants got the quarterback they wanted. On Sunday, those two franchises meet, and the quarterback on the field for New York will not even be the one that trade was made for. That is how fast things change in this league.

Now, Dart got hurt this season, with a knee injury that will keep him out for the rest of the regular season. And that is where the depth Schoen built became so important. Because he had already signed Jameis Winston, a veteran with years of starting experience, as the bridge and the backup. And when Dart went down, Winston stepped in and went unbeaten in his first three starts. Schoen even gave Winston a two-year extension back on September 7th, before the season really got rolling. That is a general manager planning for what could go wrong before it goes wrong.

And then, on September 28th, after Dart’s injury, Schoen made the move that is paying off right now. He traded with Minnesota for McCarthy, giving up a 2027 fifth-round pick. At the time, it looked like an insurance policy. A young quarterback who never found his footing in Minnesota, brought in to sit and learn. Less than a month later, he is the starter, and he arrives with real momentum.

So, let me count it up. For a sixth overall pick, the Giants got Malik Nabers. For a trade back into the first round, they got Jackson Dart. For a veteran contract, they got Jameis Winston. And for a late-round pick, they got J.J. McCarthy. That is a franchise quarterback for the future, a star receiver, a proven veteran backup, and a young starter with a big arm and something to prove. Folks, that is what I call doing it again.

Now, let’s break down the good news, one piece at a time, starting with what this means on Sunday.

Good news number one. The Giants have a safety net. This is the part that people are not talking about enough. When a team turns to a young quarterback in the middle of a season, the scariest part is what happens if it goes badly. Most teams in that situation have nobody behind him. The Giants have Jameis Winston. A quarterback who threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games, the first Giant to do that since Eli Manning in 2016. A quarterback who led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback in Washington. And a quarterback who handled being pulled against the Saints with total class. He praised McCarthy, said he was proud of him, and said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. If McCarthy struggles, Harbaugh can turn back to a veteran who has already won games for this team. That is not a quarterback controversy. That is insurance most teams would kill for.

And we have seen how much that kind of depth can matter. Think about the 2017 Eagles. Their front office brought in Nick Foles as a backup behind a young franchise quarterback in Carson Wentz. When Wentz tore his ACL in December, Philadelphia did not have to panic. Foles stepped in and won the Super Bowl. Nobody remembers the backup signing in March. Everybody remembers the parade in February. Schoen has put the Giants in a position where, no matter what happens at quarterback the rest of this season, there is a capable player ready to step in. That is a luxury most teams do not have.

Good news number two. McCarthy and Nabers finally get a full week together. Think about what happened against the Saints. McCarthy came in cold, in a 24-point hole, with no first-team practice reps as the starter. He still threw for 243 yards in the fourth quarter, the third-most passing yards by a backup quarterback in a single quarter since 1978. Now he gets an entire week of preparation, with a game plan built for him, and reps with Nabers and Isaiah Likely. McCarthy wants to attack downfield. Nabers wins downfield. Likely has already shown real chemistry with McCarthy, catching the two-point conversion and the 24-yard touchdown against New Orleans, and he was McCarthy’s target on fourth down at the end. If those connections grow with a week of practice, this offense could look very different in Houston.

But here is the challenge. Houston’s secondary is one of the better units the Giants will face this year. They are not going to let Nabers run free the way some defenses have. Expect safety help shaded his way, physical coverage at the line, and a plan to make somebody else beat them. That is why the third and fourth options matter so much. Malachi Fields, Charlie Jones, who made that 49-yard catch from McCarthy against the Saints, and Darnell Mooney if his neck allows him to play. If McCarthy can spread the ball around early, Nabers gets his chances later.

Good news number three. The roster moves this week rewarded the right players. Trace Ford, the outside linebacker this channel has been talking about for weeks, was signed to the active roster on October 20th. And here is another Schoen detail. Ford was on the reserve/future list back in January. He was waived at the end of August, then went right back onto the practice squad the next day. The Giants kept him close, elevated him three weeks in a row, and now he has earned his spot. That is patience. That is a front office that knew what it had and waited for the right moment. A lot of teams would have let a player like that walk in August and never thought about him again. The Giants kept him one phone call away, and now he is a part of the rotation in one of the biggest games of the season. And with Brian Burns and Chauncey Golston on injured reserve, Ford gives the pass rush another body that can win, right before a game against C.J. Stroud.

Good news number four. The division. The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, still in first place in the NFC East. The Eagles are expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the rest of the season, and I hope he makes a full recovery, because health comes first. The Cowboys are 2 and 4. Washington has not found its footing since that tie against the Giants. It might only take eight or nine wins to take this division. And the Giants have two division games right after the bye, at Philadelphia and at home against Washington. A win in Houston, and the Giants walk into those games with momentum and a new quarterback.

Good news number five. The timing. The bye week comes right after Houston. That means McCarthy gets a start, then two full weeks to fix whatever needs fixing. Harbaugh said earlier this week that the Giants are still building an offense that McCarthy has a feel for. The bye gives the coaching staff the perfect window to finish that build. And it gives injured players like Andrew Thomas, Darnell Mooney, and the two cornerbacks dealing with concussions, Colton Hood and Greg Newsome II, extra time to get healthy. If you had to design a schedule for a quarterback change, this is close to what you would draw up.

Now, I promised you I would always give you both sides, so here is the other side of the Schoen story. Not every move has worked. The Darius Slayton contract, 36 million dollars for a receiver the Giants released before week one, did not work. Najee Harris signed as a free agent on August 18th and was released two months later after averaging 3.2 yards per carry. Jihad Ward lasted less than three weeks. The offensive line has been inconsistent all season, and the run game has struggled badly. Harbaugh called it not even close to good enough after the Saints game. And the run defense gave up 175 yards to New Orleans. So, no, this roster is not perfect, and Schoen has made mistakes.

But here is what I keep coming back to. The big swings, the ones that shape a franchise, have mostly landed. Nabers at six. Carter at three. A trade up for a young quarterback. A veteran bridge who won games. A low-cost backup who is now starting. And late-round finds like Tyrone Tracy Jr. and rookie tackle J.C. Davis, who both played well against the Saints. When the big decisions are right, you can survive the misses. And in a weak division, that might be enough.

And this week also showed something important about how this front office and this coaching staff work together. This is John Harbaugh’s first season in New York, and the partnership between a new head coach and a general manager can be tricky. But look at the moves this week. Najee Harris was released after Harbaugh said nobody had earned the running back job. Jihad Ward was released, and Trace Ford, the player who kept earning elevations, got the spot. McCarthy, the quarterback Schoen traded for, got the start when Harbaugh decided he had earned it. The coach is setting the standard, and the front office is backing it up with roster decisions. When a head coach and a general manager are on the same page like that, players notice. They know production gets rewarded, and they know the coaching staff has the power to make changes.

And think about what this means for the future too. If McCarthy plays well over the rest of this season, Schoen has created a valuable problem. Jackson Dart is still the franchise quarterback when he comes back next year. But a young quarterback with real starting tape is an asset. He could be one of the best backups in football, or he could become a trade chip worth far more than the fifth-round pick it cost to get him. Either way, a late-round investment turns into something real. That is how smart front offices create value.

Now, the test. And it is a real one. The Texans are 2 and 4, but they have won two straight, including a 34 to 31 win over the Jaguars in London on a 49-yard field goal by Ka’imi Fairbairn as time expired. C.J. Stroud is second in the NFL in passing yards, with 1,714 through six weeks. Marvin Harrison Jr. just made his Texans debut after being traded from Arizona, and his last game before that trade was at MetLife against the Giants. And Tank Dell is coming back on Sunday after not playing since December 2024 because of a serious knee injury. That is an offense with weapons, and a quarterback who knows how to use them.

And on the other side, DeMeco Ryans is a defensive head coach who built his reputation as a defensive coordinator in San Francisco. He is going to throw everything at McCarthy. Disguised coverages, simulated pressures, looks McCarthy has not seen in a Giants uniform. That is the real test for a quarterback making his first start for a new team.

So, what does McCarthy need to do? First, protect the football. Turnovers are the reason this change happened. Four of them against the Saints turned a 10-point lead into a 24-point hole. McCarthy cannot give the ball away the way the offense did last week. Second, start clean. He threw an interception on his first drive against the Saints. On the road, in his first start, a calm opening series matters. Third, use his legs, but wisely. He scrambled for 28 yards on the final drive against the Saints, and his mobility can bail out a banged-up line. Fourth, trust his weapons. Nabers and Likely have carried this passing game. Get the ball to them early.

And the rest of the team has to help him. The run game needs to give him something to lean on, with Cam Skattebo, Tyrone Tracy Jr., and Devin Singletary. Tracy’s pass protection against the Saints was the best on the team, and that matters for a quarterback who likes to push the ball downfield. The defense has to fix the run fits, because the Saints ran for 175 yards, and Houston will test the middle. D.J. Reader has been a monster, but he needs help. And the pass rush has to make Stroud uncomfortable. Kayvon Thibodeaux has the highest pass-rush win rate on the team over the last two weeks. Abdul Carter is a game-changer. And Trace Ford finally has his roster spot. If those three can speed up Stroud, the Giants’ banged-up secondary has a chance.

And here is one more piece of good news that I think matters more than any stat. This team does not quit. Two weeks in a row, it fell behind big and fought back. In Washington, it came back from 14 down in the fourth quarter and walked away without a loss. Against the Saints, it came back from 24 down and had a throw into the end zone at the end. That kind of fight does not come from nowhere. It comes from a locker room that believes in each other and a head coach who demands it. Now the Giants need to turn that fight into a full 60 minutes.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. Number one, McCarthy’s first two drives. Does he look comfortable, and does the offense look built for him? Number two, the turnover column. Zero has to be the goal. Number three, the pass rush against Stroud, including Trace Ford’s first game on the active roster. And number four, the run game. Who emerges as the lead back now that Najee Harris is gone?

Joe Schoen took a lot of criticism when he passed on a quarterback in 2024. Two years later, the Giants have the receiver, the young quarterback of the future, the veteran who saved this season, and the quarterback they passed on, all in the same building. That is not luck. That is a plan. And on Sunday in Houston, we find out if it is ready to pay off. A win sends the Giants into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, with all the momentum in the world. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
