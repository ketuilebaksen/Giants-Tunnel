John Harbaugh just went back, watched the tape from the Washington loss, and came out with a decision that should get every Giants fan fired up before Jacksonville. He said the Giants are going to be more aggressive. He admitted they could have thrown the ball to close out that game. He said he is kicking himself for not going for it on that fourth-and-one deep in their own end. He revealed exactly what is wrong with Dominic Zvada’s kicks. He cleared Cam Skattebo on that sneak everybody was yelling about. And he said something about J.J. McCarthy that might be the strongest thing he has said about his quarterback all season. He said he feels more strongly than ever that McCarthy is capable of winning games. Let’s break all of it down.

Before we get into it, a quick reset. The Giants lost to Washington 24-21 last Thursday night. They led 21-17 late. The defense picked off Jayden Daniels twice in the fourth quarter, once by Jevon Holland at the goal line and once by Ar’Darius Washington with a little over two minutes left. The offense went three-and-out after both interceptions. Daniels hit Stefon Diggs for the winning touchdown with 57 seconds left, and Rasul Douglas picked off McCarthy on the final drive. The Giants are 5-3-1, still alone in first place in the NFC East, and they host Jacksonville this Sunday, November 22.

Now, the first time we heard from Harbaugh, it was right after the game. He said he would have to see the tape on a lot of plays. Now he has seen the tape. And some of his answers changed. That is what makes this press conference so important.

Let’s start with the biggest question. After each interception, the Giants ran the ball and went three-and-out. A reporter asked if he wished they had been more aggressive on offense.

And Harbaugh’s first answer was simple. Sure. Yeah, we could have thrown the ball.

Then he explained the thinking. He said, we thought we had good runs, we thought we could get it with the runs, and also keep the clock running and make them burn their timeouts. We thought we could do both. But then he said this, and this is the part I want you to hear: looking back on it, I think a good pass there might have been good too.

Now, he also gave the other side, and I think it is a fair point. He said if you throw the ball and you throw three incompletions, the question gets flipped. Why didn’t you run the ball? You stopped the clock for them. So being successful is really the bottom line. And he made a point of saying, it’s not like we’re trying to play it safe or anything like that. We’re not afraid to throw the ball there.

Here is my take. I love that answer, because it is a coach being honest without throwing anybody under the bus. He is admitting that the mix could have been better. He is also reminding everybody that if a pass falls incomplete, the critics would be screaming about stopping the clock. Both of those things are true. But the real message is that he is going to be thinking about this differently going forward.

Then he talked about the quarterback crack sweep, the one where McCarthy lost two yards on third down. Remember, right after the game Harbaugh said he had to see the tape to see how Sonny Styles got through. Now he has seen it. And he said, that run actually has a chance to be a really good play. It has a chance to crack them. It’s a quarterback-driven run with an extra blocker. Washington had everybody up on the line. And then he said, in that one play, we can block that play better.

So that is the answer. The design was fine in his eyes. The blocking was not. When the defense puts everybody on the line, the extra blocker has to win his matchup, and on that snap, somebody did not. I still think Skattebo should have been the one carrying the ball there, and that is my opinion, not a report. But at least now we know Harbaugh sees it as an execution problem, not a design problem.

And then came the McCarthy question, and this was the moment of the press conference for me.

The reporter asked if any of the run-heavy decisions had to do with McCarthy, with him still being a new quarterback for the team, and maybe not wanting to put the ball in his hands. And Harbaugh said no. He said it was a very fair question, and then he said this: I think he’s proven that he’s capable of winning games and making the plays, and I probably feel more strongly about that now than ever.

More strongly than ever. After a loss. After the interception on the final drive. That is a head coach planting his flag next to his quarterback.

Harbaugh also said they are definitely in the process of growing that confidence, since it is still early in McCarthy’s run as a starter here. He said, I understand what you’re saying, Paul, for sure. But he’s earned that right. And I’m excited about where he’s going going forward, and our chance to close out some games.

And then he said the part that tells you what bothers him most. We had a chance to close the game out on offense twice, and we didn’t do it. That’s probably the thing that disappoints us the most, at least me. I want to win it on either side of the ball, but we had a chance to win it there and we didn’t. We’ve got to find a way to do that in the future.

That is the shocking decision, if you ask me. Harbaugh is putting the closing job on the offense, and on McCarthy. Next time the defense hands them the ball with a lead late, I do not think we are going to see three straight runs. I think we are going to see McCarthy get a chance to finish it.

Now, the second big update. Dominic Zvada.

A reporter pointed out something really sharp. If memory serves, all of Zvada’s misses this season have come from the right hash. And even some of his makes from the left hash have a little curve to them, but it is worse from the right hash. So the question was, is there something recurring?

And Harbaugh said yes. He said there’s a technique issue there with his plant foot that we’re looking at, and he needs to clean that up from a consistency standpoint. He said Zvada knows it. He said it popped up again on that kick on Thursday. He watched it that morning. And then he said, so there is an issue on the right hash. No question.

This is a big deal, and I think it is actually good news. Think about it. If Zvada was missing randomly, from everywhere, that is a scary problem. You do not know how to fix it. But a plant-foot issue on one side of the field is a specific, coachable problem. You know where it is. You know what it is. You work on it all week. And remember what Harbaugh said right after the game. He is not going to knee-jerk it with a rookie who has made a bunch of kicks. Zvada went four for four in Philadelphia, including a 53-yarder. He has six field goals of 50 or more yards this season. The leg is there. The fix is specific.

And there is a coach on that sideline who knows a thing or two about kickers. Harbaugh came up as a special teams coach. If anybody can help a young kicker clean up a technique issue in the middle of a season, it is him.

Then the third update, and this one is for everybody who was yelling at Skattebo.

After the game, there was a lot of talk about the Jameis Winston quarterback sneak where it looked like Skattebo stayed in his stance instead of coming up to push. Right after the game, Harbaugh said he would have to see the tape, and he was not sure if the fullback was even in the game. Now he has seen it. And he cleared it up. He said, that wasn’t a tush push. That was a different kind of play. It was a normal quarterback sneak, with a certain check involved in it that is part of the play. It wasn’t a tush push, so he wasn’t supposed to be up there pushing.

So there it is. Skattebo did his job. He was not supposed to push. All the people blaming him on that play can let it go. And I am happy about that, because Skattebo was a house on fire on Thursday. Two touchdowns, 78 rushing yards, 44 receiving yards, running through people in the rain. That man does not deserve to be blamed for a play where he did exactly what he was told.

Now the fourth update. The offensive line.

A reporter brought up that Andrew Thomas took a lot of the blame on himself and the offensive line for the four-minute offense, for not being able to run out the clock. I respect that a lot from a veteran leader. But Harbaugh did not let the line take all of it. He said, I thought the offensive line played a really good game this last game. Much better probably than the week before. We didn’t play as well against the Eagles. I thought they were better against Houston. And then he said the biggest thing right now is probably chasing consistency.

He said he appreciated Thomas saying what he said, and that the line feels good about running the ball in that situation. They want to run the ball and close the game out. They want the game on their shoulders. And Harbaugh said you want to be able to do that. He said overall they played a good game, they ran the ball well, so much better than the Eagles game, and that was a big point of emphasis going in. So now they just need to keep building and become more consistent.

That is a really important point. Remember the Eagles game. The Giants ran 31 times for 80 yards, and McCarthy got sacked five times before halftime. On Thursday, the line opened holes for Skattebo and gave McCarthy time to throw for a career-high 293 yards. The line is trending up. The final four-minute drill did not work, but the overall trend is in the right direction.

Now the fifth update, and this is where Harbaugh surprised me the most. The fourth downs.

A reporter asked if, after watching the tape, he wished he had gone for it on any of the fourth downs. It felt like the Giants were stuck on the edge of field-goal range all game, around the 41 or the 46. And Harbaugh explained that when they talked about it during the game, they felt those were out of their range to go for, and a lot of it was the analytics. But also, they felt comfortable punting because they had a chance to pin Washington deep, and they did that on all of them except the last one, which he called disappointing. He said longer-yardage fourth downs are a little bit more of a crapshoot. And then he said, we may go for it in the future. Maybe more aggressive in the future than we were.

And then he brought up a play nobody even asked him about. He said, I thought you were going to ask me about the fourth-and-one quarterback sneak, deep in our own end. You didn’t ask me about that one. We could have gone for that one, and that would have been gutsy. Ballsy. A reporter said, that’s the “Who let the dogs out” one, right? And Harbaugh said, exactly. We could have gone for that one. And you know what? I’m kind of kicking myself. Maybe we should have just gone for that one too.

He said you can get as aggressive as you want, and they are not afraid, they have done it before, history would tell you that. We want to be aggressive. We want to be smart. I’ll probably second-guess all those. But they also were longer distances, as I recall.

Now here are the receipts, because I always own them. In my last breakdown of Harbaugh’s decisions, I gave the fourth-down calls a B, and I specifically defended the punt on that fourth-and-one deep in their own end. I said going for it there would have been a huge gamble, and that trying to draw them offside and then punting was the smart play. And now the head coach himself is saying he is kicking himself, and maybe he should have gone for it. So I will own that. I was more conservative than the coach. And if Harbaugh is going to lean more aggressive going forward, I am going to update my view too. With a defense that had just taken the ball away three straight times, maybe the bold call was the right call.

Then the last question was about Zvada and those fourth downs. Did his struggles play into the decision to punt instead of kick? And Harbaugh asked the reporter for the distances. The answer was that those field goals would have been 55-plus. Harbaugh said, yeah, they all would have been in the 50s. And then he said it is a risk-reward thing. He said he is not saying Zvada cannot make those kicks, but it was a windy night, and it is a field-position game in some ways. He called them toss-up calls, probably leaning toward punting. And then he made a great point. If you kick a 56-yarder and miss it, the next question is, why didn’t you punt them deep and play the field-position game?

I think that is a fair answer. A 55-plus yard kick in wind and rain is a coin flip for almost any kicker, let alone a rookie working through a plant-foot issue on the right hash. Punting there and trusting your defense was reasonable. The problem was the one punt that did not go where it was supposed to.

So let me sum up the wave of decisions coming out of this press conference.

One, Harbaugh admits the Giants could have thrown the ball to close out the game, and he is not afraid to do it.

Two, the quarterback crack sweep was a blocking problem, not a design problem.

Three, he feels more strongly than ever that McCarthy can win games, and he wants to close games out on offense.

Four, Zvada has a specific plant-foot issue on the right hash, and they are working on it.

Five, Skattebo did his job on the sneak.

Six, the offensive line played a good game, and Andrew Thomas stepped up as a leader by taking responsibility.

And seven, Harbaugh says the Giants may be more aggressive on fourth down going forward, and he is kicking himself about the fourth-and-one.

That is a coach who watched the tape, owned his mistakes, protected his players, and set a new direction. That is exactly what you want to hear before a big game.

And here is why this press conference matters more than the one right after the game. Right after a loss, every coach is emotional. Every coach says he has to see the tape. The real test is what he says after he has seen it. Some coaches dig in and defend every call. Some coaches throw players under the bus. Harbaugh did neither. He admitted the pass might have been the better call. He admitted he might have gone for it. He explained the kicker’s problem in plain words. And he defended the players who did their jobs, like Skattebo on the sneak and the offensive line in the run game. That is a coach building trust inside the building, and trust is what wins close games in December.

Now let’s talk about what this means for Jacksonville.

The Giants have had ten days to get ready. McCarthy’s hip has had time to heal after he played through that hit in Philadelphia. Zvada has had a full week of practice to work on that plant foot. Skattebo has had time to rest after a big workload. And Harbaugh has had time to look at the late-game playbook and think about how to close.

My prediction, and hold me to it: we are going to see a more aggressive Giants team on Sunday. If the Giants have a lead late in the fourth quarter, I think McCarthy throws the ball to finish it. I think Harbaugh goes for it on at least one fourth down that he would have punted on before. And I think the Giants beat Jacksonville to get to 6-3-1.

And let’s keep the big picture in view. The Giants are 5-3-1. Washington is 3-5-1 and two games back, with the head-to-head edge after the tie and the win on Thursday. Dallas was 4-5 heading into its game with San Francisco, a game and a half back at that point. Philadelphia came into the weekend at 2-7. The Giants still have the second games against Dallas and Philadelphia ahead of them, and they already beat both of those teams once. Tracy is on IR and lined up to come back around week 13 against San Francisco. This defense was top five in the league heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. Arvell Reese is one of the best rookies in football. The pieces are here.

And I am going to say it again, because this press conference made me believe it even more. This team can go all the way. A head coach who owns his mistakes and gets more aggressive. A young quarterback his coach trusts more than ever. A kicker with a fixable issue. A running back playing with fire. An offensive line that is getting better. And a defense that takes the ball away. That is a team ready to bounce back.
