Let me start with the defense, because it deserves its flowers. Dennard Wilson’s group was wildly impressive in Philadelphia. The Eagles had 220 total yards. They had 13 first downs. They averaged 3.3 yards per play. They converted just 5 of 18 third downs. And the Giants’ defense was credited with seven sacks, with a lot of blitzing. Think about that. Seven sacks, against a team whose offensive line was one of the best in football for years.

And remember who was missing. No Brian Burns. No Tremaine Edmunds. Dexter Lawrence was traded in the offseason. The interior of the defensive line has been a weakness all year. And the secondary is not the most talented group in the league. But one reporter put it perfectly. He said this defense is playing above its means. The coaching staff is getting the most out of these players. That is what good coaching looks like.

And the Giants’ defense has been doing this for weeks. Against Houston, without five starters, it held the Texans to 237 total yards. Against Philadelphia, it allowed 220. That is two straight games where the defense has kept an opponent under 240 yards. When your offense is still finding its rhythm with a new quarterback and a banged-up backfield, a defense like that buys you time. It gives you a chance to win every single week.

And then there is Barkley. Saquon Barkley has hurt the Giants ever since he left for Philadelphia. Giants fans have had to watch him do it again and again. On Sunday, he had 13 carries for 22 yards. He did score a short touchdown. But the Giants’ defense took away the one weapon the Eagles had left, with their top receivers out and a first-time starter at quarterback. That was the plan all week. Stop Barkley, and make Joe Milton beat you. Milton went 18 of 34 for 169 yards. Plan executed.

Now, Arvell Reese. Harbaugh said after the game that Reese could have gotten two or three game balls. He gave him the first one. And look at the stat line. Twelve tackles. Three sacks. A forced fumble near midfield on a hit on Joe Milton that one reporter said walloped him into another dimension. Reese said that might be the hardest he has ever hit a player in a game. Micah McFadden recovered the fumble.

And one of the national analysts talked about what makes Reese so dangerous. His versatility. He covers. He plays the run. And then on Sunday, he showed he can go sack the quarterback. When you have a player like that, it makes your defense unpredictable. Offenses do not know where he is going to line up, or what he is going to do. Is he dropping into coverage? Is he blitzing? Is he spying the quarterback? That is a nightmare to game plan against.

And think about where Reese started this season. The Giants drafted him as an inside linebacker. Joe Schoen said during the bye that the clear vision was for him to play off the ball. But when Brian Burns went down, the coaches started using him as a pass rusher too. And now he is doing both at a high level. Dennard Wilson has been stacking Reese with Abdul Carter and Kayvon Thibodeaux on the same side of the formation to create problems for offensive lines. On Sunday, that plan worked to perfection. Three sacks for Reese, seven for the team. And this is a player who is still learning the position.

And the comparison that keeps coming up is the Defensive Rookie of the Year race. One reporter said Reese is playing at that level right now. And he went further. He said if Reese keeps playing like this, he could be in the Defensive Player of the Year conversation. That is a big statement about a 21-year-old rookie. But after three sacks in Philadelphia, it is hard to argue.

And remember what Harbaugh said about him after the game. He said he and Joe Schoen were giddy when Reese fell to them in the draft. He called Reese humble, very tough, hard-working, coachable, and a great leader in his own quiet way. That combination of talent and character is rare. And the Giants have it in a rookie linebacker.

The Giants had only 16 rushing yards in the first half.

And McCarthy did not have a great passing day either. He went 12 of 19 for 127 yards, and he was sacked five times before halftime. He hurt his hip in the third quarter, came out, and then came back. But that same analyst made the most important point. The Giants found a way to win. And he said why. They have a real head coach over there. They are finding ways to win.

And Harbaugh called his deep sideline throw to Malachi Fields, after a sack put the Giants in a long-yardage spot, the throw of the game.

Now, the trade deadline. Today is the day. And we finally know what the Giants offered for Jonathan Taylor. According to Jay Glazer of Fox Sports, the Giants offered a second-round pick and a fifth-round pick. The Colts said no. And the Giants said fine, that offer is off the table. Harbaugh said after the game that there is some truth to that report. But he would not rule anything out. He said things change, and he said nothing is imminent right now.

And here is a detail I want to point out. The Giants traded their 2027 fifth-round pick to Minnesota in the McCarthy deal. So that fifth-round pick in the Taylor offer would most likely have been a future pick, probably in 2028. That fits what Harbaugh has said all along. He would rather use 2028 picks for any deadline deal.

So, second plus fifth. That was the Giants’ number. And remember what Jeremy Fowler reported. Executives around the league believed Taylor was worth about a second-round pick plus another pick. That is almost exactly what the Giants offered. The problem was the Colts. They reportedly wanted something closer to a first-round pick, or a McCaffrey-style package of a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth. The Giants offered market value. The Colts wanted a premium. And Harbaugh said it plainly. We are not going to go for a short-term fix at an exorbitant cost.
