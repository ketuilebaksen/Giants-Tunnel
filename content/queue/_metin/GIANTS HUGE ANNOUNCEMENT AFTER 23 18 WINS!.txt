The Giants beat Jacksonville 23-18, and the biggest announcement from that game was not the final score. It was Abdul Carter. For weeks, the Giants' young pass rusher had been winning his matchups without finishing. People were calling him a bust. John Harbaugh stood at the podium and said, the sacks will come. And on Sunday, against a 7-2 team, in front of the whole country, the sacks came. A career-high two sacks and four quarterback hits. That is the perfect news, because with the schedule about to get a lot harder, a pass rusher who finishes changes everything. But this win also told us a lot about the offense, and not all of it is good. So today I am going to break down this game through the numbers that matter most, and what they mean for Indianapolis and the stretch run.

Let's set it up. The Giants beat the Jaguars 23-18 at MetLife. They are 6-3-1, still on top of the NFC East. Jacksonville came in at 7-2 on a three-game win streak, and here is a number a lot of people missed. The Jaguars came in with the best turnover differential in the league, plus nine. That is the kind of number that usually wins you games like this. Teams that protect the ball and take it away usually beat teams that do not. And the Giants flipped it on them. The Jaguars fell to 7-3, and the Giants were the team that stopped them.

Number one: two. Abdul Carter's sacks.

All season, the conversation around Carter was about one column on the stat sheet. Before Sunday, the deeper numbers told a different story. He had a 29 percent pass-rush win rate. He had created 16 incompletions off his pass-rush wins, the most in the NFL. Pro Football Focus had him with an 89.3 overall grade, third among all edge defenders. Harbaugh said this week that Carter was grading out really well on his sheet and on the coaches' sheet, that teams were accounting for him, and that he was a game wrecker. He said the sacks would come.

And on Sunday, Carter had two sacks, a career high, and four quarterback hits. Those two sacks doubled his season total in one afternoon. After the game, he called himself the "best in the world." One report also credited him with eight total pressures and two tackles for loss, and he drew several holding penalties. That is a full day of wrecking a game, exactly the way his head coach described him.

Here is why this is the perfect news. A pass rusher who wins his matchup but does not finish forces the quarterback to move, throw early, or check down. That is valuable. But a pass rusher who finishes ends drives. Sacks lose yards, kill third downs, and sometimes force fumbles. When Carter starts finishing, offensive lines have to slide protection toward him every single snap. And that opens up one-on-ones for everyone else, including Arvell Reese, who had three sacks in Philadelphia, and Kayvon Thibodeaux on the other side. My opinion, not a report: if this is the start of Carter finishing plays, this defense goes from very good to scary.

And Carter was not alone. DJ Reader got his second sack in three games. The Giants had three sacks and 11 quarterback hits on Trevor Lawrence, against a team that had allowed only 14 sacks all season coming in.

Number two: zero. Giants interceptions thrown, against the league's best takeaway team.

Remember, Jacksonville came in at plus nine in turnover differential. Their defense averaged about 1.8 takeaways per game. And J.J. McCarthy did not throw an interception. Not one. That matters a lot, because if you give that team extra possessions, they will make you pay.

But I will be fair. The Giants did lose a fumble. Late in the third quarter, Josh Hines-Allen forced a fumble from Cam Skattebo, and Antonio Johnson scooped it up and returned it 42 yards before McCarthy tackled him at the Giants' 31. That was a scary moment. But the defense held, and Jacksonville turned it over on downs. That stop kept the lead at 20-6, and I think it saved the afternoon.

And on the other side, the Giants took the ball away twice in regular play, plus a huge two-point interception. Deonte Banks picked off Lawrence in the first quarter and returned it 19 yards to set up a game-tying field goal. Paulson Adebo picked off Lawrence in the fourth quarter, his first interception of the season, and that turned into a field goal that pushed the lead to 23-9. And after Lawrence hit Brian Thomas Jr. for an 18-yard touchdown to make it 23-15, Kayvon Thibodeaux intercepted the two-point try to keep the lead at eight. Against the best turnover team in football, the Giants won the turnover battle. That is the game.

Number three: three. Deonte Banks' pass breakups.

I want to give Banks his own spot, because his week was a story. Less than two weeks earlier, he was getting crushed for the 33-yard pass interference against Washington that set up the game-winning drive. The critique was all about technique, watching the receiver instead of turning his head to find the football. He also hurt his shoulder in warmups against Washington.

On Sunday, he answered all of it. Banks finished with six tackles, five of them solo. He broke up three passes. And he had the interception. It was his first interception since his 98-yard return against Arizona back in week four. When Banks is locked in, he can be a difference-maker, and the Giants needed exactly that against a quarterback like Lawrence. Football players remember the bad plays. Good ones answer them.

Number four: fourteen. The red zone streak the Giants ended.

Before the game, one of the biggest concerns about the Giants' defense was the red zone. They had been near the bottom of the league at preventing touchdowns once opponents got inside the 20. And Jacksonville came in on a streak of 14 straight red zone drives that ended in points. Liam Coen is aggressive on fourth down, and on Sunday, he went for it on fourth-and-one at the Giants' 11. Dru Phillips broke up the pass. Streak over. That is the kind of play that tells you a defense is growing up in the moments that matter.

Number five: eight. Dominic Zvada's 50-yard field goals.

All week, the story was the right hash. All six of Zvada's misses this season had come from that side, and Harbaugh said there was a plant-foot technique issue. On Sunday, Zvada made a 53-yarder and a 54-yarder in the first half, both from the right hash. He also made a 43-yarder in the fourth quarter. He did miss a 32-yarder wide left to open the second half, and that one stung. A miss that short can cost you a game, and I am sure it is the one he wants back.

But here is the number. Zvada now has eight field goals of 50-plus yards this season. That ties Graham Gano's franchise record from 2022. And to give you some perspective on how high the ceiling goes, Brandon Aubrey set the NFL record with 14 in 2024. So there is room to grow. But an undrafted rookie tying a franchise record in his first season, with seven games left, is impressive.

And the punting was a weapon too. Jordan Stout averaged 60.3 yards on four punts, with one downed inside the 20. He now has 20 punts inside the 20 this season, second in the league. And Zaire Barnes added four special teams tackles. After a few rough weeks in the third phase, that is real progress.

Number six: one hundred ninety-two. Malik Nabers' career catches.

Nabers was the main man in the passing game on Sunday. Seven catches and an eight-yard touchdown from McCarthy with 6:04 left in the first half that made it 17-6. It was his third touchdown of the season. And with those seven catches, he now has 192 career receptions. Only Odell Beckham Jr. had more catches through his first 29 career games. That is the company Nabers is keeping. After all the noise about body language and the fumble against Washington, that is a great response.

Number seven: six hundred. Isaiah Likely's receiving yards.

Likely became the first Giants tight end with 600 receiving yards through the first 10 games of a season since Mark Bavaro in 1987. Think about how long ago that was. Likely has turned into one of the most important weapons on this offense, and he has done it after the Giants let Wan'Dale Robinson walk in free agency. That move looks better every week.

Okay. Now the number that worries me.

Number eight: four point five five. McCarthy's yards per attempt.

McCarthy finished 18 of 38 for 173 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. I love that he protected the football. But 173 yards on 38 attempts is about 4.55 yards per throw. That is not good enough. The Giants need more rhythm from him before the schedule gets harder. He did add something with his legs, with four runs for 38 yards, including a big 22-yard scramble on the touchdown drive. That is a strong number for a quarterback, and it shows the Giants can lean on his legs when the passing game is not clicking.

And the run game was quiet. Skattebo had 14 carries for 34 yards, with a 10-yard run as his longest, and he added an 11-yard catch and run. But he also had the fumble, his fifth of the season. Devin Singletary had one carry for five yards. As a team, the Giants ran for 77 yards on 25 carries. That is 3.1 yards per carry. Not a lot of push.

And here is the stretch that worried me most. In the second half, the Giants went four straight drives without a first down while Jacksonville cut a 14-point lead down to five. That is the same closing problem we saw against Washington. The good news is who broke it. Rookie Malachi Fields, who had already drawn a 37-yard pass interference on Montaric Brown to set up Jameis Winston's one-yard touchdown sneak, caught a 19-yard pass with under three minutes left that finally moved the chains. Then Skattebo picked up another first down at the two-minute warning, and McCarthy kneeled to end it.

Nine different Giants caught a pass, including Jalin Hyatt, who caught his first pass of the season, a four-yard gain on third down. I like that spread. But the offense has to be more efficient against the better teams coming up.

And here is a number that ties into something Harbaugh said all week. He promised an aggressive game plan. He said he hoped everyone would be singing the praises of that aggressive game plan on Monday. So did we see it? In some spots, yes. Think back to the Washington game. Harbaugh was asked about field goals in the mid-50s on a windy night, and he said those were toss-up calls, probably leaning toward punting, and that he chose to play the field-position game. Against Jacksonville, he sent Zvada out for a 53-yarder and a 54-yarder, and Zvada made both. That is a coach trusting his kicker's leg instead of punting it away. He also went right back to the Jameis Winston sneak at the goal line, after the "Who let the dogs out" sneak had become a punchline against Washington, and it worked. And McCarthy took a shot downfield to Fields that drew a 37-yard pass interference. Those are aggressive choices.

Where we did not see it was the second half. Four straight drives without a first down, and one report pointed out the Giants barely ran the ball in that stretch until the final two minutes. That part still looks like a team trying to protect a lead instead of extending it. My opinion, not a report: the Giants have to find a four-minute offense they trust, because this is now two straight games where a big lead nearly disappeared late.

So let me put the whole thing together. The defense won this game. The pass rush won this game. The kicker's long field goals won this game. And the offense did just enough. That formula beat a 7-2 team. But it will not always be enough, especially against the stretch that is coming.

Now let's talk about the injuries, because there is one to watch. Greg Newsome II left with an ankle injury, and Paulson Adebo replaced him. Adebo was great, with that fourth-quarter interception. But depth at corner matters a lot against good quarterbacks, and the Giants just extended Newsome through 2029, so they need him back. On the other side, Jacksonville lost cornerback Jarrian Jones with a chest injury and left tackle Walker Little with a knee injury. Both teams were banged up.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Before this game, I predicted Giants 23, Jaguars 20, with Zvada making a big kick. The Giants scored exactly 23, and Zvada made two huge ones. I said Carter would get home, and he got home twice. I said McCarthy would not turn it over, and he did not. I also said Zvada would go perfect, and he missed a short one. And I said Skattebo would carry the offense, and he did not. So more right than wrong, and I own the misses.

Next up is Indianapolis on the road in week 12. The Colts are 3-8. They just lost 26-3 to Houston on Thursday night. They waived Daniel Jones, and they will start Anthony Richardson, a former fourth overall pick, in his first start since 2024. That is a team in a really tough spot. And these are the same Colts who turned down the Giants' Jonathan Taylor offer at the trade deadline. Taylor will be on the other sideline.

And there is a big matchup in this game that concerns me a little. Jonathan Taylor against the Giants' run defense. Before the Jacksonville game, the Giants were allowing about 4.48 yards per carry and 121.6 rushing yards per game, which ranked 26th in the league. Taylor is one of the best running backs in football, and when your quarterback is making his first start since 2024, the easiest thing to do is hand the ball to your best player over and over. So this game might come down to whether the Giants can stop the run on first down. If they do, Richardson has to beat them from the pocket, and that is where Carter, Reese and Thibodeaux can take over.

And Richardson is a different kind of quarterback than Lawrence. He has a huge arm and he can run, but he has struggled with accuracy and consistency, and he has not started a game in a long time. Against a young quarterback like that, I would expect the Giants to mix in more pressure than they did against Lawrence. Confuse him before the snap, make him hold the ball, and let the pass rush finish. And keep a spy on him, because if the Giants lose contain, he can hurt you with his legs. Reese is the perfect player for that job. He can rush, he can cover, and he can chase.

And let's keep the bigger picture in mind. Before the Jacksonville game, Pro Football Focus projected the Giants' playoff chances at about 76 percent with a win, and they got the win. Now come the Colts, and then San Francisco at home, Seattle on the road, Cleveland at home, Detroit on the road, and then Dallas and Philadelphia to finish. The Colts game is the kind of game a playoff team has to win. Lose it, and all the good feelings from Sunday disappear. Win it, and the Giants are 7-3-1 heading into the toughest part of the schedule with real momentum.

Here is what I want to see in Indianapolis. Get the run game going early, because the Giants need confidence on the ground before the tougher games. Trust McCarthy to throw on early downs and find a rhythm. Keep Carter rolling against a young quarterback who can be rattled. And close the game with a first down, not a punt.

And a defense that just shut down the best turnover team in football. The offense still has work to do. But this team just beat a 7-2 opponent without its best stuff on offense.
