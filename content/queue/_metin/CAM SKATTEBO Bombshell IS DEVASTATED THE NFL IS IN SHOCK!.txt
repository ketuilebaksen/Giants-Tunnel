The real fix is technique, not a new player

Here's the most important thing I'll say today. Signing Gibson doesn't solve the real issue. The real issue is ball security. And that's on coaching and on Skattebo himself.

Let me explain what ball security actually means, because fans hear it all the time and rarely hear it broken down.

There are five points of pressure on a football when a running back carries it properly. The fingertips over the tip of the ball. The palm against the side of the ball. The forearm pressing the ball. The bicep squeezing it. And the ball tight against the chest. When all five are in place, it's very hard to knock the ball loose.

Fumbles usually happen when one of those points breaks. Most often, it's when a back is fighting for extra yards and the ball drifts away from his body. His arm swings out to keep his balance, and the ball is exposed. A defender rips at it, or a helmet hits it, and it's gone.

And here's the thing about Skattebo. His whole style is fighting for extra yards. He spins, he bounces, he keeps his legs moving in a pile. That's what makes him great. But it's also exactly the situation where fumbles happen. So the fix isn't to stop him from fighting. It's to get two hands on the ball when he's in traffic. High and tight. Every time.

The coaching staff already took responsibility for game management issues a few weeks ago. Now they need to own this too. Drills every day. Ball security in every walkthrough. Because a fumble in the third quarter of a playoff game ends seasons.

Why the fear approach doesn't work

Let me add one more point here, because I think it's the one people miss.

You can't coach a running back by making him afraid. A back who's scared to fumble runs with his eyes down, both arms wrapped around the ball, and no burst. He stops making people miss. He stops falling forward. He becomes a guy who gets three yards and goes down.

And that defeats the whole purpose of having Cam Skattebo. The reason he's the starter is that he runs angry. He makes something out of nothing. If you coach that out of him, you've lost the player.

So the balance is this: teach the technique, hold him accountable when he breaks it, but don't make him play scared. The Week 6 benching was the accountability. Now it's about the daily work.
