Jaxson Dart is off crutches. Just a few weeks ago, he was moving around the Giants’ locker room on crutches, not putting any weight on his surgically repaired knee. Now there is video of him walking around New York City on his own, no crutches, no help. And Dart himself still believes he can come back if the Giants make the playoffs. That is a huge update. But it also sets up one of the most difficult decisions John Harbaugh could face this season. Because if J.J. McCarthy keeps this team winning and gets them to January, and Dart says he is ready, what do you do? Today, I am going to walk you through the injury, the real timeline, the playoff calendar, and where I land on the biggest quarterback question in New York.

Let’s start with the update itself.

Dart’s girlfriend, Marissa Ayers, shared a video of the two of them out celebrating in New York City. And in that video, Dart is walking around on his own. No crutches. Nobody helping him with every step. For a guy who was dealing with a serious knee injury and surgery, that is a real milestone.

And what makes it so encouraging is how different the picture looked just a few weeks ago. On November 6, Dart was back around the Giants’ locker room, and he was still on crutches. During the bye week, Joe Schoen said Dart was not putting weight on that injured knee yet. Now he is walking without crutches. That is real progress in a short amount of time.

But I want to be careful here, because I do not want to turn one video into a promise. Walking without crutches and practicing are two very different stages of recovery. Patience matters just as much as hope.

Let’s remember how serious this injury was.

It happened in the week two loss to the Rams. Dart was standing in the pocket, doing his job, trying to make a throw, and he got caught between defenders. He was not doing anything reckless. He was not taking some unnecessary run. He was just playing quarterback. And the initial scans showed damage to his MCL, his PCL and his meniscus. The good news was that his ACL stayed intact. But he still needed surgery to repair the meniscus and the MCL, and that surgery happened in late September, around September 25. The PCL did not need surgical repair.

The estimated recovery timeline after that surgery was roughly four to six months, depending on how the knee heals and how Dart responds to rehab. Count four months from September 25, and you land on January 25. Six months takes you into late March.

And here is the important part. Walking without crutches is not the same as planting your foot in the ground, changing direction, escaping pressure, and absorbing contact from NFL defenders. Getting normal movement back is one step. Being cleared to play in an NFL playoff game is a much, much bigger test.

That is why I keep coming back to what Harbaugh said early on. He never completely slammed the door on a postseason return. He acknowledged there might be a chance, depending on how the recovery went. But he also called that idea ambitious. And I think that one word tells you a lot. Harbaugh understands how badly Dart wants to play. But wanting to come back and actually being ready are not the same thing.

And Schoen added another interesting piece during the bye week. He said Dart still believed he could return if the Giants made the playoffs. But Schoen made a point of saying that was Dart’s mindset. To me, that sounds like a young competitor refusing to give up on his season, not the team announcing a return date. Dart has reportedly been eager to work on football movements around the building. And the team has said it hoped Dart and Brian Burns might travel with the group later in the season. That is great for their involvement and for the locker room. But it is not a medical clearance.

Let me walk you through what the next stages usually look like for a knee like this, in general terms, because it helps explain why patience matters. First, you get off crutches and walk normally. That is where Dart is now. Then you rebuild strength in the leg, the quad, the hamstring, everything around the knee. Then you start jogging in a straight line. Then you start running at speed. Then come the cuts, the plants, the change of direction. Then position drills. Then individual work with the team. Then team practice. And only after all of that do you take contact. Every one of those steps can take weeks, and any one of them can bring a setback. So when you see a video of a guy walking around the city, that is a big step. But it is one of the first steps, not one of the last.

And there is also the roster process to think about. When a player is on injured reserve, the team has to open a practice window before he can come back to the active roster. That window gives the team a few weeks to see him practice before they have to make a decision. So if Dart ever gets close, you would hear about that practice window opening first. That will be the real sign. Not a video. Not a quote. The practice window. Until that happens, I would treat any playoff return talk as hope, not a plan.

So where does that leave the playoff question? Let’s look at the calendar, because the calendar is brutal.

The wild card round starts January 16. The divisional round is January 23 and 24. The conference championship games are January 31. And the Super Bowl is in February. Now line that up with the four-to-six-month window. Even the divisional round would come just before the four-month mark from his surgery. The wild card round would be well before it. That leaves almost no room for a setback in rehab. And that is before anybody even talks about putting on pads, taking hits, or practicing at full speed.

Could he recover faster than expected? Maybe. Young athletes sometimes do. But there is no reason to assume an early return. My opinion, not a report: if Dart plays at all this postseason, I think it would be late in the playoffs, and only if the Giants go on a real run. And I would not count on it.

Before anybody starts imagining Dart running out of the tunnel in January, there is a much bigger problem the Giants have to solve first. They actually have to make the playoffs.

Right now, the Giants are 5-3-1. And that loss to Washington hurt. It was not just another game on the schedule. It cut into their NFC East cushion and handed a division rival the head-to-head edge, after the 27-27 tie and the 24-21 loss. The Giants are still alone in first, two games ahead of Washington at 3-5-1 and two games ahead of Dallas at 4-6. But the margin for error is smaller than it looked a week ago.

And that is why Sunday against Jacksonville is so big. According to Pro Football Focus’ latest playoff projections, the Giants’ playoff chances jump to 76 percent with a win over Jacksonville, but fall to about 49 percent with a loss. Other models have it a little different, but they all tell the same story. This is a swing game. Those are projections, not guarantees, but they show you exactly how much is riding on one Sunday.

And Jacksonville is not an easy opponent. The Jaguars come in at 7-2 with a lot of confidence after a 40-23 win over Tennessee. Trevor Lawrence is playing at an MVP level. Their defense allows the second-fewest points per game in the league. And after Jacksonville, the Giants still have Indianapolis, San Francisco, Seattle, Cleveland, Detroit, and then the late division games against Dallas and Philadelphia. That is not an easy road at all. So if we are seriously talking about Dart returning for January football, the team in front of him has to keep winning in November and December.

And that brings me to the person who makes this whole story so complicated. J.J. McCarthy.

When Dart went down, it felt like the Giants had lost the quarterback they wanted to build around. Jameis Winston stepped in first and kept the team afloat. Then McCarthy took over. And McCarthy has given this team a reason to keep believing.

I know fans are split on him, especially after the Washington game. I watched the mistakes too. But what I cannot understand is acting like those mistakes erase everything good he did before them.

Look at what McCarthy did against Washington. He threw for 293 yards, a career high. And it was not empty passing yardage. He made a beautiful throw to Isaiah Likely over the middle where the ball had to clear one defender and get there before another one could close. He showed real touch on throws to Malik Nabers and Malachi Fields along the sideline. Those plays take timing, confidence and trust in your receivers. To me, that shows a young quarterback building real chemistry with this offense. And of course, there was the 66-yard touchdown to Likely that gave the Giants a 21-17 lead.

Yes, the fourth quarter matters. McCarthy’s late interception came on a desperation throw with the clock running down. Nobody should pretend the ending was not frustrating. But for me, the bigger problem was what happened before that. The defense picked off Jayden Daniels twice in the fourth quarter, and the offense went three-and-out after both of them. That is where the play calling deserves criticism too. Harbaugh himself admitted after watching the tape that a good pass might have been the better call than three straight runs. Expecting perfection from a young quarterback in his third start with a new team is unfair.

And Harbaugh has been very clear about where he stands. He said he feels more strongly than ever that McCarthy is capable of winning games. He said McCarthy has been playing winning football and he is really happy with the way he is playing. He wants him confident, aggressive and smart, and he wants him to handle all the blitz checks.

And let’s not forget how this team has survived without Dart. Look at the records. With Dart starting, the Giants are 1-1, beating Dallas and then losing that Rams game where he got hurt. With Winston starting, they went 2-1-1. With McCarthy starting, they are 2-1, including that huge 19-11 win in Philadelphia, the first win there since 2013. Three quarterbacks, and every one of them has found a way to win games. That says a lot about the coaching staff, about this defense, and about the culture Harbaugh has built. It also tells you the Giants do not need to rush anybody back.

And Dart can still help this team right now without taking a single snap. He can be in the quarterback room, watching film with McCarthy and Winston. He can be on the sideline on Sundays, talking through what he sees. He can be a leader in the locker room. A young quarterback who stays engaged while he is hurt is building the respect he will need when he comes back. That matters too.

So now, imagine this. The Giants finish strong. McCarthy wins enough games to get them into the postseason. And then Dart reaches a point where he feels ready to play. What exactly should Harbaugh do? Bench the quarterback who helped get the team there? Or hand the offense back to the player the Giants originally saw as their future?

That is not an easy answer. And that is why this recovery update is a lot bigger than a happy video in New York City.

Let me give you both sides.

The case for Dart. I still believe Dart has a special ceiling. We saw flashes of it against Dallas in week one, when the Giants won 28-20. His confidence. His ability to escape pressure. His willingness to make plays outside the pocket. He gives this offense a different kind of energy. He can turn a broken play into a first down, and he does not panic when the pocket gets crowded. And remember, Matt Nagy built this offense around Dart in the first place. That is the kind of upside that makes fans believe they found their long-term quarterback.

The case for McCarthy. Think about what makes Dart so dangerous. So much of his game depends on his legs. If he is protecting a recovering knee, can he move the same way? Can he plant hard, cut away from a defender, and extend a play without thinking twice? Maybe he could stand in the pocket and throw. But taking away his movement takes away a huge part of what makes him special. And a limited Dart is not automatically better than a healthy McCarthy who is playing with confidence and rhythm.

And there is the locker room to consider. Players see who is practicing every week. Who is taking the hits. Who is learning from mistakes. Who is helping the team win on Sundays. If McCarthy earns a playoff start by getting this team there, pulling him for someone who has not played since September could create a difficult situation in that building.

So here is where I land. My opinion, not a report: it has to be about who gives the Giants the best chance to win on that particular Sunday, not whose name was on top of the depth chart in September. If McCarthy gets this team to the playoffs, he starts the first playoff game. Period. And unless Dart is fully cleared, fully practicing, and clearly moving like himself, I do not think you make a change in the middle of a playoff run.

And my biggest concern goes beyond who gets the first playoff snap. It is the risk of rushing a serious knee injury. When several parts of the knee have been damaged, coming back before strength and movement are fully restored can expose a player to another injury. The consequences are too serious to ignore, especially for a young quarterback the Giants still see as part of their future. The Giants need him healthy for the next ten years, not for one game in January.

So what is the ideal outcome here? I think it looks like this. McCarthy keeps growing and leads this team into the playoffs. Dart keeps rehabbing, gets stronger, travels with the team, and is around the building as a teammate and a leader. And then this offseason, the Giants have the best problem in football. Two young quarterbacks with real ability. Maybe there is a competition in training camp. Maybe another team sees McCarthy’s progress and makes an offer. Remember, the Vikings let McCarthy go for a fifth-round pick. If he finishes this season strong, his value goes way up. Either way, the Giants win.

One more thing on the timing. Even if Dart cannot play in January, the fact that he is walking normally in November is great news for his offseason. A full offseason of strength work, a normal training camp, and a fresh start next September. That is the real win here. Whatever happens in the playoffs, the Giants are going into 2027 with a healthy Dart and a battle-tested McCarthy. Very few teams in this league can say that about their quarterback room.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. When Dart went down in week two, a lot of people wrote this season off. Through three quarterbacks, the Giants are 5-3-1 and in first place, and everyone who wrote them off has had to eat those words. Every time this channel has said this team can go all the way, the doubters have laughed, and every week this team keeps proving it belongs in that conversation. I also picked the Giants to beat Washington, and that was wrong. And I have been saying McCarthy’s next step is handling the blitz. Sunday is the next test.

Here is my prediction, and you can hold me to it. McCarthy plays a clean game against Jacksonville, no turnovers, at least one touchdown pass. The Giants win and move to 6-3-1, and their playoff odds jump back up into the mid-70s or higher. And Dart is on the sideline cheering for him. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A franchise quarterback who is off crutches and fighting to get back. A young quarterback who is playing winning football right now. A coach who will make the right call when the time comes. A top-five defense. And a two-game lead in the division. Most teams would kill to have one young quarterback with a future. The Giants might have two. That is not a problem. That is an opportunity, and it is one the Giants have earned.
