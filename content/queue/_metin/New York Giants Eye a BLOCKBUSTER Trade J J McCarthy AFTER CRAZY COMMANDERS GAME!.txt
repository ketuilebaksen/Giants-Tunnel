So the question everybody is asking this week is simple. Is McCarthy the answer, or do the Giants need to go get a big name at quarterback? First, let’s clear up the trade part of this, because I do not want anybody walking away from this video confused. The trade deadline was November 10. It is gone. The Giants stood pat. They put an offer on the table for Jonathan Taylor, a 2027 second and a 2028 fifth, the Colts did not bite, and that offer got pulled. There is no in-season trade coming. Nobody is getting traded for, and nobody is getting traded away until the new league year. So when you see the word blockbuster attached to McCarthy right now, here is what it really means. It is the conversation about the offseason. It is the question of whether this front office, Joe Schoen and John Harbaugh, will look at what they have in McCarthy and decide he is the long-term answer, or decide they need to make a big move at the position next spring. So let me give you the quick version of the game, and then we get to the film, because the film is where the real answer lives.

Giants lost 24-21 at MetLife in the rain. Cam Skattebo scored twice and finished with 78 rushing yards plus 44 receiving yards. Likely had nine catches for 127 yards, a career high. The defense picked off Jayden Daniels twice in the fourth quarter, including Jevon Holland at the goal line. The Giants led 21-17 with under two minutes left. And then Washington went 71 yards in three plays, helped by a 33-yard pass interference call on Deonte Banks, and Daniels hit Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds left. McCarthy got one last chance, needing only a field goal to force overtime, and his deep shot for Malik Nabers was intercepted. That is the game in a paragraph.

Now here is the stat that was floating around all week before this game, and I want you to hear it, because it explains why this game matters so much for McCarthy. Of the 31 quarterbacks with multiple starts since week seven, McCarthy was the only one without a turnover. Clean. Not one. And he was also the only one of those 31 without a passing or rushing touchdown. Read that again. The safest quarterback in the league in that stretch, and the least productive in the end zone. That is the whole McCarthy story in two numbers. He was protecting the football, and he was not creating enough.

So what happened on Thursday? Both numbers broke. He finally got the touchdown, the 66-yarder to Likely, the biggest throw of his young Giants career. And he finally had the turnover, the interception on the final drive. So in one night, McCarthy showed you the ceiling and showed you the risk. And that is why people are screaming about the quarterback position this week.

Let’s go to the film, because this is the part I really want you to understand.

There was a breakdown going around this week of one specific McCarthy snap, and it explains everything. Under center, ball fake, and the design of the play is to get the ball to Nabers. Nabers is the number one read. That makes sense. He is the best player on the offense. But Nabers is not open. Two defenders are sitting underneath him. And the running back is wide open, all alone, on second down. The right play is easy. Check the ball down. Take the free yards. Stay on schedule. And McCarthy did not get there.

That is the flaw. And this is not a new flaw. The point that was made on that breakdown is that this was a problem for him in Minnesota, too. When the first read is open, he looks great. That is why it did not get exposed in the Houston game or coming in against the Saints, because the number one guy was there. But defensive coordinators are not dumb. They watch the same film we watch. And what they are going to do, more and more, is take that number one guy away and make McCarthy beat them with the second and third option. If he cannot get to that second read, the drive stalls. And if he tries to force it to the first read, that is when the turnovers start.

Now connect that to Thursday night. The final throw was a deep shot for Nabers. Was there something underneath? I am not going to sit here and claim I know exactly what every receiver was doing on that snap, because I do not have the all-22 in front of me, so I will not invent it. But I will say this. When you need a field goal with under a minute left, the smart football is usually to get the chunk you need in pieces. Ten yards, out of bounds, another ten yards, spike, kick. The deep shot to your favorite guy is the high-risk, high-reward play. McCarthy chose high risk. It did not work. And that fits right into the film breakdown. Under stress, his eyes go to the number one guy.

Here is the good news, though, and I mean this. That flaw is coachable. It is not an arm problem. It is not an accuracy problem. He threw for 293 yards, the most of his career, on a wet ball, with a hip that was not 100 percent a week ago. The arm is there. The toughness is there. What he needs is reps, comfort, and a coaching staff that keeps drilling the progression. The phrase that stuck with me from that breakdown is that he has got to play stress free. When the guy he expects to be open is not open, he has to calmly get to the next guy. The more comfortable he gets doing that, the more this offense opens up.

So now let’s answer the blockbuster question directly. Should the Giants go get a big-name quarterback this offseason?

My opinion, not a report: no. Not yet. And here is why.

Number one, look at the timeline. McCarthy has a handful of starts as a Giant. He has not had an offseason in this system with this coaching staff. He took a hip pointer in Philadelphia and played through it four days later on a short week and threw for a career high. You do not give up on a young quarterback halfway through his first season in a new building because of one throw.

Number two, look at the alternative. A blockbuster trade for a quarterback costs you premium picks, and premium picks are exactly what built this defense. Arvell Reese was the fifth overall pick. You do not trade the next Arvell Reese for a quarterback who might be marginally better than what you already have.

Number three, the Giants already have a safety net. Jameis Winston bailed this team out in Philadelphia, a 14-play, 76-yard drive that ended in a touchdown to Likely. If McCarthy gets hurt, the season is not over. That is a luxury. That gives Harbaugh the patience to let McCarthy grow.

Now, I will be fair to the other side. If McCarthy finishes this season still struggling to get off his first read, still stalling drives when the number one guy is covered, then the offseason conversation gets real, and I would not blame Schoen for exploring every option. That is the standard. This second half of the season is McCarthy’s audition. Every week from now until January, he is answering the question.

Now, the flip side. Nabers had a tough Thursday. Five catches for 44 yards, a lost fumble deep in Washington territory in the third quarter that swung the game, and he was the target on the final interception. Go out against Jacksonville on November 22 and take over the game. My opinion, not a report: he does exactly that.

And this connects right back to McCarthy, by the way. Nabers being the number one read is the right design. He is the best weapon on the team. But the McCarthy and Nabers connection needs a second piece. When Nabers is doubled, McCarthy has to trust Likely, trust Skattebo out of the backfield, trust Mooney, trust Fields. If McCarthy learns that, Nabers gets more one-on-ones, not fewer, because defenses cannot cheat to him. That is how a young quarterback and a star receiver grow together.

Okay. Now I want to flip to the other side of the ball, because the biggest story of this Giants season is not the quarterback. It is the rookie who is making people bring up the name Lawrence Taylor.

Arvell Reese. Let me give you the history from the Eagles game, because some of these numbers came out after we covered that win, and they are wild.

With three sacks in Philadelphia, Reese became the youngest player in NFL history to register three sacks in a game. Twenty-one years and seventy days old. He became the only NFL rookie with three sacks and ten or more total tackles in a game since 1994, when tackles became an official stat. His strip sack of Joe Milton came in 2.29 seconds, the fastest time any player has registered a sack in the NFL this season, according to Next Gen Stats. And here is the one that tells you how he is being used. His other two sacks came when he was lined up at inside linebacker, and that made him the only player in the league this season with two sacks in a game from that alignment. He was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week. He is at four and a half sacks on the season, tied for the league lead among inside linebackers.

Think about that. An inside linebacker, a rookie, 21 years old, tied for the lead at his position in sacks, while also racking up tackles like a traditional linebacker. That is not normal. That is a player who can do everything.

Now here is the stat that ties it all together, and it is my favorite stat of the season. Last year, in 2025, the Giants allowed the fifth-most yards per game in the league. Heading into week 10, they ranked fifth in total defense, allowing 301.4 yards per game. Bottom five to top five in one offseason. And according to NFL Research, the last time the Giants made that kind of jump in one offseason was from 1980 to 1981. That was the year the Giants drafted Lawrence Taylor second overall.

So that is where the comparison comes from. Nobody is saying Arvell Reese is Lawrence Taylor. Taylor is the greatest defensive player who ever lived, in my book, and you do not hand that out after eight games. But the impact pattern, a rookie flipping a defense from bad to elite in one year, the only other time it happened for this franchise was number 56. That is the company Reese is keeping. And the way people around the team talk about him, he plays like a machine, he does not play like a 21-year-old, and athletically, he is just getting started. He is going to get bigger, faster and stronger. That should scare every offense in the NFC.

And there is a scheme story here, too. In the fourth quarter of the week three game against Tennessee, the Giants lost Brian Burns for the season. A huge loss. And instead of folding, this defense changed. Since the week four Cardinals game, the Giants have been one of the most blitz-heavy teams in the league. The Vikings under Brian Flores are number one, and they always will be while he is running that defense, but the Giants are right up there behind them. Reese is the key to all of it. Put yourself at center. Reese is standing over an interior gap. If you slide your protection to him, someone else gets a clean path. If you leave him one-on-one, Philadelphia just showed you what that costs.

Now, here is the honest truth about Thursday night, and I am going to say it plainly. Washington did a good job against that pressure on the final drive. The two big touchdowns, the 31-yarder to Antonio Williams and the 32-yarder to Diggs, came on plays where Daniels had time. Before the game, the warning was all about Daniels as a runner. In the first meeting, he ran for 103 yards and two touchdowns on just nine carries. That was the blueprint for Washington, use his legs, zone reads, get him on the perimeter, make Reese choose between the running back and the quarterback. And with the blitz-heavy approach, the risk is always the same. When you send extra, and they pick it up, somebody is one-on-one in the back end. That is what happened on the final drive.

So is the blitz approach wrong? No. This defense was top five heading into week 10 because of it. It forced two fourth-quarter interceptions on Thursday. It got a fourth-and-one stop with nine minutes left. But Harbaugh and the defensive staff have ten days now to look at the final two minutes and ask one question. In a four-point game with under two minutes left, do you keep sending pressure, or do you drop eight and make Daniels throw underneath? There is no perfect answer, but it is the question this coaching staff needs to get right in December.

Now let’s put the whole picture together. Where does McCarthy stand right now, and what does the blockbuster conversation really look like?

Here is what McCarthy has going for him. He protected the ball in his first stretch of starts better than anybody in the league. He played through an injury on a short week. He threw for a career high against a division rival in bad weather. He made a big-time throw, the 66-yarder, in the biggest moment of the night. And he is playing next to a defense that can win games for him, a run game with Skattebo that just scored twice, and a tight end in Likely who is turning into a weapon.

Here is what is working against him. The end zone production is still light. The film shows a pattern of locking onto the number one read. And the first real turnover came at the worst possible time, with the game on the line.

So the answer, the way I see it, is this. The Giants are not going to make a blockbuster quarterback move this season, because they cannot. The deadline is gone. And they should not chase one this offseason unless McCarthy shows he cannot grow out of that first-read habit. This second half of the season is the test. If he plays like he did on the 66-yarder, the blockbuster conversation dies. If he plays like he did on the final throw, the blockbuster conversation gets louder.

And one more thing about the standings, because some people are acting like this season is over. It is not even close. The Giants are 5-3-1 and still alone in first place in the NFC East. Washington is 3-5-1. Dallas is 4-5. Philadelphia is 2-7. The Giants are two games up on Washington and a game and a half up on Dallas. Washington now has the head-to-head edge after the tie and this win, so that matters if it ever gets tight, but the Giants still control their own path. They have ten days off before hosting Jacksonville on November 22. Tracy is on IR and lined up to return around week 13 against San Francisco. And they still have the second Dallas and Philadelphia games ahead of them.

So here is my prediction, and you can hold me to it. McCarthy uses these ten days well. He comes out against Jacksonville and gets the ball out to his second and third reads. The Giants win, and they get to 6-3-1. And Reese keeps adding to a rookie season that has people saying the name Lawrence Taylor out loud. If I am wrong, I will tell you right here, in this tunnel.

A young quarterback with a big arm and a coachable flaw. A star receiver with something to prove. A division lead with eight games to go. That is not a team that needs to blow it up. That is a team that needs to finish.
