Okay. Now the blueprint, because this is the part that should scare the rest of the league.

The Giants' defense did not just win this game. It rattled a quarterback who has looked comfortable all season. Lawrence came in with a 112.8 passer rating, 21 touchdowns, only five interceptions, and only 14 sacks taken in nine games. Jacksonville had built the whole offense around getting the ball out quickly and using play action. And on Sunday, Lawrence was sacked three times, hit 11 times, and pressured on about 40 percent of his dropbacks. He threw for 301 yards, but it took him 47 attempts, he threw two interceptions, and Jacksonville kept settling for field goals in the red zone.

So the question one analyst asked is the right one. Was that about the Giants' personnel, or did the Giants find a game plan that other teams can copy?

The answer is a little bit of both.

Start with the personnel. Abdul Carter had been getting criticized all season for not finishing his rushes. On Sunday, he finished. Two sacks, a career high, and four quarterback hits. And here is the detail I love. Jacksonville's left tackle, Walker Little, went down with a knee injury. They put in a backup, and Carter beat him up. Then Carter moved over to the other side and beat up the right tackle too. Pick your poison. And DJ Reader added a sack, his second in three games. The defensive backs played well too, with interceptions from Deonte Banks and Paulson Adebo, and Kayvon Thibodeaux picking off the two-point try.

Then the scheme. Defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson deserves a ton of credit here. One analyst broke down what Wilson does. He mixes and matches guys. He moves them around. He shows a look with five guys across the front, which forces the offensive line into one-on-one blocks. And then he mixes up the coverage behind it. Sometimes man. Sometimes quarters, which he loves. So the quarterback sees the same look before the snap, but he gets something different after the snap.

Think about why that works against Lawrence. Jacksonville's offense is built on timing. Quick throws. Play action. The ball comes out in under three seconds. When the defense shows five across, the protection has to commit to one-on-ones. If one of your rushers wins quickly, like Carter did, the timing breaks. And when the coverage behind it changes, Lawrence has to hold the ball for that extra half second to figure out what he is seeing. That half second is when the pressure gets home.

And remember how this defense got here. Brian Burns, the Giants' top pass rusher, was lost for the season with a torn ACL back in week three against Tennessee. A lot of teams would have folded. Instead, Wilson changed the identity. Since the week four Cardinals game, the Giants have been one of the most blitz-heavy teams in the league, right behind the Vikings under Brian Flores. He leaned on Reese as a rookie inside linebacker who can rush. He kept moving Carter around. And this defense went from one of the worst units in the league last season to fifth in yards allowed and third in passing yards allowed this season. Losing your best pass rusher and getting better is not normal. That is coaching.

So did the Giants give the rest of the league a blueprint to beat Jacksonville? Maybe. But here is the catch, and I think it is the most important point. Drawing up the pressure is the easy part. You still need players who can win those one-on-one blocks. Not every team has an Abdul Carter. Not every team has a Kayvon Thibodeaux on the other side, or a rookie like Arvell Reese, who came into this game with a 30 percent pass-rush win rate, fifth in the league among players with at least 50 pass rushes. The Giants have the personnel to make that blueprint work. Most teams do not.

And let me add one more thing about how the defense flipped a weakness. Before this game, the Giants had been near the bottom of the league at preventing touchdowns in the red zone. And Jacksonville came in on a streak of 14 straight red zone drives that ended in points. On Sunday, the Giants made Jacksonville settle for field goals over and over, and Dru Phillips broke up a fourth-and-one pass at the Giants' 11 to end that 14-drive streak. That is a defense that took its worst tendency and turned it around in the biggest game of the year.

Now, the play nobody is talking about. J.J. McCarthy's tackle.

Late in the third quarter, with the Giants up 20-6, Cam Skattebo fumbled. Josh Hines-Allen forced it, and Antonio Johnson scooped it up and took off. He returned it 42 yards. And the guy who stopped him from scoring was the quarterback. J.J. McCarthy ran him down and made the tackle at the Giants' 31.

Think about that. If McCarthy does not make that tackle, Jacksonville scores a defensive touchdown, and it is 20-13 with the whole momentum of the game flipping. Instead, the Jaguars had to drive, the Giants' defense stood them up, and Jacksonville turned it over on downs. No points. One analyst called it an enormously big play from McCarthy, and I agree. The Giants might owe their biggest win of the season to a quarterback making a tackle. A quarterback cleaned up the offense's mistake, and the defense made sure that tackle mattered.

And that brings me to the quarterback conversation, because it came up again after this game.

One panel had a really interesting take. They said Jaxson Dart and McCarthy are stylistically pretty similar. Both are young. Both are athletic. Both like to run. Both throw the ball down the field. One person said Dart has a little more touch on the ball. And the big question they asked was simple. Why can't the Giants go into next season with both of them?

Now, to be clear, nobody from the Giants has announced any kind of competition. In that discussion, Dart was the projected starter long term. And the idea of keeping both and letting them compete next season is speculation. But I like the logic. Both quarterbacks like to run, and running quarterbacks take hits. Dart is coming back from a serious knee injury, with surgery on his MCL and meniscus in late September. He was just seen walking without crutches. Having a second quarterback who can win games is not a luxury for this team. It is insurance. My opinion, not a report: I would keep both, no question.

And McCarthy has given the Giants a lot since the trade. In the first half on Sunday, he was moving the ball, he scrambled for 22 yards on the touchdown drive, and he hit Malik Nabers for an eight-yard touchdown. He finished 18 of 38 for 173 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions, and he ran four times for 38 yards. He protected the football against a defense that came in with the best turnover differential in the league, plus nine. That matters.

Now, the problem. The one thing that could still ruin all of this. The four-minute offense.

Here is the critique, and I think it is fair. Against Washington, the complaint was that the Giants got too conservative. With a lead late, they ran the ball three straight times after each interception, went three-and-out, and handed the ball back. Harbaugh admitted afterward that a good pass might have been the better call.

So against Jacksonville, with a lead in the second half, the Giants went the other direction. They leaned hard on the pass. And they went four straight drives without a first down. One analyst said the problem with that is a pass-heavy three-and-out can burn barely 30 seconds off the clock. That is how a lead starts to feel like a countdown to disaster. Jacksonville cut the lead from 14 to five. And then he said the line that stuck with me. You need to be able to play some semblance of the four-minute game if you are going to win games in this league with a late lead. And right now, the Giants are about as far from that as any team could be.

That is harsh. But it is the right thing to focus on. The Giants have now nearly blown two straight fourth-quarter leads, one they lost and one they survived. The fix is not run every down or throw every down. It is balance. Mix runs and high-percentage throws. Use Isaiah Likely, who just became the first Giants tight end with 600 receiving yards through the first 10 games of a season since Mark Bavaro in 1987. Use quick throws to Nabers, who now has 192 career catches, second only to Odell Beckham Jr. through 29 career games. Get Skattebo the ball in space. And make the defense defend everything. Malachi Fields' 19-yard catch with under three minutes left finally broke the streak. That is the kind of play the four-minute offense needs earlier.

There is also something the Jacksonville tape tells future opponents about the Giants, and the Giants need to be ready for it. Teams are going to look at that game and see a Giants offense that averaged 3.1 yards per carry, with Skattebo at 14 carries for 34 yards, and a quarterback who averaged about 4.5 yards per pass attempt. So I would expect defenses to keep crowding the line of scrimmage and dare McCarthy to beat them over the top. The Giants have to answer that. That means hitting the deep shots when they are there, like the throw to Fields that drew a 37-yard pass interference, and finding ways to get Nabers and Likely the ball on the move. One national voice said he loves Harbaugh's formula of running the rock and finishing games. That is the right formula. But the run game has to actually work for it to close out games, and on Sunday, it did not.

And let me be fair to the other side of this debate. One analyst said the Giants held on for dear life in a game where Jacksonville played a stinker. He said if Jacksonville plays a normal game, the Jaguars probably win a close one. He said the Giants were headscratchingly bad at times, and that is why people look at them and do not believe in them. I understand that take. But I also think a big reason Jacksonville played a stinker is that the Giants' defense made them play one. Three sacks, 11 hits, three takeaways, including the two-point pick, and a fourth-down stop in the red zone. You do not get credit for the other team's bad day when you caused it.

And here is the thing he said at the end, which I think is the whole point. You believe in the record right now, because the record is damn good.

The Giants are 6-3-1. Dallas already has six losses. The Giants have seven games left. Dallas has six. Even if Dallas wins on Thanksgiving to get to 6-6, the Cowboys would need the Giants to collapse down the stretch and would need to win the rematch to have any shot at the head-to-head. Washington still has the head-to-head edge on the Giants after the tie and the 24-21 win. That is the one team I would keep an eye on. But the Giants have three losses. And with the Jacksonville win, they have proof they can beat a top team when it matters.

And do not forget the special teams piece of this. The Giants overhauled that unit before Jacksonville, bringing in Xavier Gipson to return kicks and Tavierre Thomas to help on coverage. Gipson had no muffs. Dominic Zvada made a 53-yarder and a 54-yarder, tying Graham Gano's franchise record with eight 50-yard field goals this season. Jordan Stout averaged over 60 yards a punt. A team that wins with defense needs special teams that do not give games away, and for the first time in a while, they did not.
