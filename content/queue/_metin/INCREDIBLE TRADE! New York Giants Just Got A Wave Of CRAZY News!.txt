Alright, the big one first. Jackson Dart. The latest report from Adam Schefter is that the knee injury is worse than anticipated, and it is possibly season ending. And I want to slow down on that word, possibly, because I think a lot of people saw the headline and went straight to season over. That is one of the outcomes, absolutely, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But listen to what John Harbaugh actually said. Evaluations are still ongoing. No final decisions have been made. They have learned a lot more, and he does not want to share too much until decisions get made. Now here is my read on that, and I want to be clear, this is me speculating, not me reporting. It sounds like the MRI came back worse than they hoped, and the conversation now is a classic one. Is this something he could safely play through later this season, maybe in six or eight weeks, with surgery waiting in the offseason? Or is the risk of making it worse high enough that the smart move is surgery now, and surgery now means the season is over? My guess is that is exactly the discussion happening in that building today. And folks, I will say this with my whole chest. Priority number one is Jackson Dart being healthy for the next ten years, not for this season. If the choice is between a compromised quarterback limping through December and a fully healed franchise guy attacking next offseason, you make the long term choice every single time. It hurts. But you make it. And yes, the sting is worse because of how it happened. We told you before the game the door was not fully shut on him playing. He walked through that door, he stood in against pressure, and he got hurt delivering an absolute dime. The kid wanted to keep playing after it, too. The training staff basically had to take his jersey away. That is who he is. And that is exactly why the organization has to protect him from himself right now.

Okay, now the rest of the injury report, and believe it or not, this is where the news gets better. Start with Brian Burns. It is a low ankle sprain, not a high one, and that distinction is everything. The high ones are the six week nightmares. The low ones, guys play through. Burns was in a boot Monday night, but he was putting full weight on it, and the man himself said it plainly. Nothing serious, I have dealt with this before, we will see how the swelling looks, the recovery starts now. Realistic range, a week or two, and do not be shocked if he is out there next week. Exhale, folks. Then Malik Nabers, and I will admit, that was a bizarre scene, because it did not look like anything violent happened on the play. His shoulder came out, and they popped it back in on the spot. Now, the online doctors, and some of them genuinely know their stuff even without access to the scans, point out that he has had this before, which means it pops out easier, and every time it happens there is a chance of new damage. So he is getting an X-ray to check for exactly that, and the range of outcomes runs from playing next week with a brace, to missing a couple of games, to the small chance the adrenaline let him play through something worse. But here is my common sense read. This is a superstar coming off a major injury last year. This training staff just physically took the jersey away from their franchise quarterback in the middle of a football game. There is no world where those same people send Nabers back onto the field if they secretly feared a fracture. The fact that he went back out and played tells me he is probably going to be okay. And finally Andrew Thomas, who pulled his groin. This is the one with the least information out there, but Harbaugh was asked about it and said, we will see, none of these are career threatening, those guys will be back. It reads like a week to week thing, not a significant tear. Honestly, it was so quiet I did not even notice he left the game until somebody brought it up. So take a breath. The quarterback news is scary. The rest of it, genuinely, could have been so much worse.

Now let's talk about the game itself, because I owe you some honesty here. I am man enough to admit when I am wrong, and I was wrong about the Rams. I looked at them after week one and I said it out loud. Stafford is getting old. Adams is getting old. No Myles Garrett. Maybe this team is washed. They are not washed. Even Sean McVay admitted he botched the Australia travel plans and it wrecked them in week one. Monday night, we saw the real Rams, the team everybody expected in August. And on top of that, we caught them at the single most dangerous moment possible. Aaron Donald, out of retirement after two years, making his return in that building. That is a Michael Jordan coming back to the Bulls level event, folks. That crowd, that sideline, that team was playing on pure inspiration. And here is the part that gets buried in the blowout. Early in that game, we still punched them in the mouth. We were driving right down the field. And Jackson Dart, with pressure bearing down on him, stood tall and threw an absolute dime to Theo Johnson. And Theo dropped it. And look, I do not want to spend this whole video hating on our own guys, love your neighbor as yourself and all of that, but I have to get this off my chest. If a man ruins his knee delivering you a beautiful football, you have to bring it down. You have to. The drops are getting under my skin, and that one might haunt this whole season.

Which brings us to the hardest part of the film. Jameis Winston was atrocious Monday night. I do not love pinning a team loss on one player, and to be fair to him, the circumstances were brutal. You spend all week holding the clipboard while the starter takes the reps, and suddenly you are thrown into a hostile building against an inspired team that half the league considers a Super Bowl favorite, with Aaron Donald hunting you. That is genuinely hard. But the throws that were there simply did not get made. Fourteen nothing, and he floats an absolute duck into the end zone that gets picked off, on a play where Malik Nabers had a step, a play where, if that is Dart throwing it, we are probably talking about a touchdown. Later, fourteen nothing again, third and eight, the offense schemes Darnell Mooney wide open on the edge, a catch and turn upfield that moves the chains at minimum and maybe goes the distance with one block. The ball arrives at his shoe laces. Drive over. And that is the thing I need you to see. On both of those plays, the offense worked. The design won. The scheme beat the Rams defense. The football just did not arrive. Bobby Skinner put out a take that I am fully on board with. I refuse to believe what Jameis was Monday night is what he will be going forward. He might throw his interceptions, that has always been part of the deal, but the juice in that arm did not die overnight. Give the man a full week of starter reps with the game plan built around him, and I think he steadies.

And that matters, because the foundation of this team did not get blown out Monday. It is still here. This offense is built on the power run game, on the offensive line, on Cam Skattebo running behind Pancake Pat Ricard, and every bit of that survives no matter who is at quarterback. Remember, in the barely more than four quarters Jackson Dart played this season, he posted the highest EPA per play of any quarterback in football. That is not just a quarterback stat, that is proof this system, with these weapons, works at an elite level when the ball goes where it is supposed to go. So the way I see it, one of two things happens now. Option one, and it is the one I believe in, this team galvanizes around the adversity, the backup settles, the defense holds the line, and this roster is still good enough to fight for a playoff spot, because those third and eights and those red zone trips are going to start converting again, and when they do, the defense stops living on the field. Option two, the bottom falls out, and even then, listen to me, the sky is not falling. We still have our franchise quarterback under contract and healing. We still have a young core that just dominated a playoff caliber Dallas team two weeks ago. And if this season truly collapses into a tank year, it collapses into the perfect draft class for it. Imagine, and I am just dreaming out loud here, a season bad enough to put us in range of a receiver like Jeremiah Smith out of Ohio State, a kid people compare to Ja'Marr Chase, Julio Jones, Justin Jefferson, lining up across from Malik Nabers for the next decade. I am not rooting for that. But if the worst happens, that is what the worst looks like. That is a pretty soft landing, folks.

Now, the conversation nobody wanted to have in September. If Dart is done for the year and Jameis does not bounce back, do the Giants go get a quarterback? I think you have to at least be prepared, so let's run through the names being floated. Jimmy Garoppolo, the classic steady veteran option. Josh Johnson, who has been on practically every roster in the league and, importantly, spent three separate stints with Harbaugh in Baltimore, so he knows the bones of this offense already. There is even the petty option, and I admit I smiled at it, signing Joe Milton right off the Cowboys practice squad and sticking it to Dallas. Jake Browning spent years in the AFC North going toe to toe with Harbaugh defenses. But the name that genuinely intrigues me is J.J. McCarthy. I know, I know, he gets roasted online, and some of that alter ego stuff was strange, fine. But think about the football fit for a second. The Michigan offense he ran under Jim Harbaugh is practically a cousin of what John Harbaugh runs here. Power run game, play action off of it, bootlegs, quarterback movement. McCarthy has a strong arm, he is genuinely mobile, and here is the kicker, he is sitting third on a depth chart where Carson Wentz got the call ahead of him. That tells you the price might be as low as a seventh round pick. For a scheme fit that clean at that price, I make that phone call yesterday. He interests me more than any other name on the list.
