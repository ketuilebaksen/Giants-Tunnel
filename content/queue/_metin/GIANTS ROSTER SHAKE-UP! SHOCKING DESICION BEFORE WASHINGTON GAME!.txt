A team sitting at 3 and 1, alone on top of the NFC East, is supposed to coast through a quiet week. That is the normal script, folks. You protect the locker room, you keep the roster the same, you ride the momentum into the next game. The New York Giants did the exact opposite. In the days leading up to their first divisional road trip under John Harbaugh, this front office and this coaching staff tightened every single bolt on the machine. They added a veteran pass rusher. They locked a third quarterback onto the active roster. They moved a former top-ten pick up to the number two spot. They put a receiver on injured reserve and signed another one. They opened up a real competition at center. And they did it all while their best receiver and their left tackle were sitting on the injury report. Today is game day in Washington, and I want to walk you through the whole week the way the coaching staff lived it, Monday to Saturday, because when you put it all together, you see a staff that is planning for the worst while believing in the best. And then I am going to give you my five keys to walking out of Washington at 4 and 1, and my final score prediction. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start on Monday, because that is where the week’s story began. John Harbaugh stepped to the podium the day after the Arizona win and gave us three storylines that would carry through the whole week. First, the quarterback room. Harbaugh said there was a possibility that J.J. McCarthy would be the number two quarterback at Washington, that they would see how the week went, but that it was definitely on the table. Think about how fast that is, folks. A former top-ten pick arrives in a trade from Minnesota, and within days, he is being discussed as the backup in a division road game. Second, the center position. Brian Hudson had just made his first career start in place of John Michael Schmitz, who had missed time because his wife had just had a baby. Harbaugh said Hudson played really well and that it might create some options, but that Schmitz had been playing really well too, and they had not had their personnel meeting yet. And third, Braxton Berrios. Harbaugh said he was not on injured reserve at that point, that he got twisted up with an ankle and foot issue on a punt return, and that they would know more soon. So on Monday, the staff was still hoping, still evaluating, still keeping every door open.

Then came the move that told us how aggressive this front office still is. Joe Schoen went out and signed Jadeveon Clowney. The former number one overall pick, a veteran edge rusher who had 9.5 sacks for John Harbaugh in Baltimore in 2023, on a staff that included Dennard Wilson, the man now running this Giants defense. A team that had been searching for a proven pass rusher since Brian Burns tore his ACL found one with deep roots in this building. And with Jayden Daniels expected back for Washington, a veteran edge setter who knows how to keep a mobile quarterback in the pocket arrived at the perfect moment.

Then Wednesday, and that is when the moves came in bunches. The Giants signed Jake Haener from the practice squad to the active roster. Against Arizona, Haener had been one of three quarterbacks in uniform, but only because he was elevated for the day, and an elevation is temporary. The player goes back down afterward. By signing him outright, the Giants made it permanent. And folks, I read that as a team that refuses to be thin at the most important position on the field ever again. They already lost one quarterback to a serious injury this season. Nobody in that building wants to be scrambling on a Sunday morning in a hostile stadium. With three quarterbacks on the active roster, there is no emergency phone call and no panic. If McCarthy is the number two, Haener is the safety net, so nobody has to be pushed into a role before he is ready. And let me say this clearly again, that is not the same as McCarthy taking the job from Winston. It is depth, planning, and a coach covering every possibility.

The same afternoon, the Berrios decision came down. Injured reserve. On Monday, they were hoping. By Wednesday, the decision was made, and when a team puts a player on IR, it is telling you it does not expect him back soon. That is a real loss, both at receiver and in the return game. To fill the gap, the Giants signed receiver Dante Fleming to the practice squad. Most people will scroll right past that name, and on its own, it does not move the needle. But when you lose a receiver, you want a body in the building, somebody ready if something else goes wrong.

And then the first injury report of the week dropped, and it explained exactly why the Giants wanted that extra receiver around. Malik Nabers did not practice, with a knee. Andrew Thomas did not practice, with a groin. Jason Pinnock was out with a concussion, Chauncey Golston with a neck, and Najee Harris for a personal reason. When I saw Nabers and Thomas on the same list, I took a long breath. Your number one receiver and your left tackle. And in the limited group, Isaiah Likely with a knee and groin, Chris Manhertz with a shoulder, and Cam Skattebo with a shoulder. Tight ends and a running back, the players who make this offense run the way it wants to run. On the bright side, Deonte Banks was a full participant with his calf, and so was Brian Hudson with his ankle.

And Wednesday also brought the best news of the week. Deonte Banks was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week, his first weekly award, after the 98-yard pick six as time expired that beat Arizona. The second-longest touchdown with no time left on the clock since the 1970 merger, behind only a 100-yard return by Brock Marion, whose team was already up by 20. Banks did it with the game on the line. He also had four passes defensed, the most of any player in week four. And Arvell Reese was nominated again for Pepsi Rookie of the Week, after nine solo tackles, three passes defensed, a tackle for loss, and his first career interception. He leads the entire NFL with 26 solo tackles through four weeks. He is only the fifth Giants rookie with three passes defensed and an interception in a game, and the only one since 1994 to add nine tackles to that line.

Then Thursday, and the week started to turn. Nabers and Thomas both returned to practice as limited participants. That is a step in the right direction, folks. Remember what we said on Wednesday, the first report is the least reliable, and Thursday tells you more. Matt Nagy gave J.J. McCarthy an A-plus for how fast he is learning the offense. The published depth chart listed McCarthy as the backup behind Winston. And on the other side, Jayden Daniels was a full participant for Washington on both Wednesday and Thursday, with that brace on his left elbow, his non-throwing arm. So barring a setback, the Giants are preparing for the real Jayden Daniels.

So step back and look at the whole week, folks. A team on top of its division could have coasted. Instead, this staff added a veteran pass rusher, locked in quarterback depth, accelerated its young quarterback, replaced an injured receiver, opened a competition at center, and managed a crowded injury report without panic. That is what planning for the worst while believing in the best looks like. Every single move had a backup plan built into it. And that is exactly the mindset you want walking into a division road game.

Now, my five keys to walking out of Washington at 4 and 1.

Key number one, keep Jayden Daniels in the pocket. This is the whole game on defense, folks. Daniels can turn a perfectly good defensive play into a disaster. You cover the first read, you get pressure, everything looks fine, and then he slips outside and suddenly defenders are making decisions in space. The answer is rush discipline. Get after him, but do not run past him. Clowney setting one edge, Abdul Carter on the other, Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne collapsing the middle, and Arvell Reese, the fastest linebacker on the field, ready to mirror him. If Dennard Wilson blitzes, and I think he will after how aggressive he was against Arizona, somebody has to account for the escape route. Make Daniels beat you from the pocket with a brace on his elbow. Do not hand him the edge.

Key number two, win the turnover battle. This is the Jameis Winston key, and you know I have to say it. Against Arizona, Winston threw three touchdowns and two interceptions, and the Giants survived because the defense came up with three takeaways. On the road, in a division game, with a desperate team and a revived home crowd, you cannot count on the defense bailing you out every week. Remember the Winston benchmarks we updated this week? Zero-turnover game, check, he did it against Tennessee. Touchdowns, check, three against Arizona. Wins, check. The final benchmark is both at the same time. Touchdowns and no turnovers in the same game. If Jameis gives us that version in Washington, folks, this game is over by the fourth quarter. And remember, McCarthy is the official backup now, with an A-plus grade and a package of plays already built. Winston controls that timetable, and the best way to keep it that way is to protect the football.

Key number three, fix the run game. Against Arizona, Skattebo and Harris combined for 88 yards on 27 carries. Harbaugh admitted the ground game has to get better. The answer is not just handing it off again and hoping. It is play action. Make the linebackers pay for crashing downhill. Fake the handoff, freeze the second level, and hit Nabers over the middle, or take a shot deep like the 40 and 38-yard throws Winston hit against Arizona. If Najee Harris is limited after missing time this week, give Tyrone Tracy Jr. some real opportunities, because his speed adds the explosive element this run game has been missing. Balance helps everybody, the backs, the protection, and the quarterback.

Key number four, win the communication battle up front. This is the one nobody talks about. The center sets the protections and communicates with everyone around him. On the road, in a division game, with a crowd that can make the snap count difficult, that matters a lot. Whether it is Schmitz back in the lineup or Hudson getting his second straight start, and I do not think we will know for sure until we see the starting lineup, the Giants need clean snaps, clean protection calls, and no false starts. And if Andrew Thomas is out there after working back to limited practice, protecting Winston’s blind side, this offensive line has a chance to control the game the way it controlled Dallas in week one. With Thomas, we have learned all season that Friday is the day that matters, and the final word comes when the inactives are released ninety minutes before kickoff.

Key number five, keep winning the hidden phase. The Giants have the best special teams unit in football, and they have to keep it that way without Braxton Berrios. That means Charlie Jones, a returner who scored a return touchdown in each of his first three NFL seasons, could get a bigger role. It means Dominic Zvada, the undrafted rookie who is still perfect and just became the first Giants rookie with three field goals of 50-plus yards in a season, has to keep being automatic. And it means Jordan Stout has to keep flipping the field with those booming punts. In a close division road game, field position is gold, and special teams are where the Giants mine it. John Harbaugh starts every meeting with special teams for exactly this reason.

Now, let me flip the script for a second, because you always have to respect what the other side is going to try. If I am Dan Quinn, I know my team is 1 and 3, I know my season is on the line, and I know my franchise quarterback just came back. So I am going to use that energy. Expect Washington to come out fast, script some designed runs and bootlegs for Daniels early to test that elbow and get the crowd involved, and try to put the Giants on their heels before the defense settles in. On defense, Quinn has always loved to bring pressure, and he will look at Jameis Winston and think he can bait him into the same kind of mistakes he made against Arizona. Disguised coverages, late rotations, a robber sitting in the middle of the field waiting for Winston to stare down Nabers. That is the chess match, folks. The Giants have to weather the opening punch, stay patient, and remember that a desperate team playing on emotion usually runs out of answers in the second half.

And then there is Terry McLaurin. He was held out of the Colts game with a hamstring, and Quinn said he was still being evaluated, working off to the side with trainers. Whether he plays or not changes Washington’s passing game completely. If he is out there, this is the first marquee test for Joey Porter Jr. in a Giants uniform. A six-foot-three corner who allowed a 73.6 passer rating last season, matched up against a veteran route runner who has tormented this division for years. That is the matchup I will be watching on every third down. If Porter wins it, Washington is left with a quarterback in a brace and nowhere to throw. And do not forget the homecoming. Daron Payne spent his entire career in Washington before the Giants brought him to New York. Today he walks back into that stadium in blue, knowing that offensive line, knowing their calls, knowing how Daniels likes to escape. You know he has this one circled.

So those are my five keys. Keep Daniels in the pocket. Win the turnover battle. Fix the run game with play action. Win the communication battle up front. And keep winning the hidden phase. Do four out of five, and I think the Giants win. Do all five, and I think they win comfortably.

And here is why this game matters so much, folks. The Giants have a little cushion in the NFC East. The Eagles are 2 and 2 after losing to the Rams, and they are heading across the ocean to play Jacksonville in London, without Lane Johnson, who just retired. The Cowboys, the team the Giants beat in the opener, are 2 and 2 after a shootout with Houston. Washington is 1 and 3. But a cushion in October is only worth something if you protect it, and division road games are exactly where cushions disappear. A win today makes it 4 and 1, with a three-game lead over Washington in the loss column, the first head-to-head win over the Commanders in the pocket, and the Saints and Texans still to come before the bye. And it would be Harbaugh’s first division road win as Giants head coach. All three Giants wins so far have come at MetLife. Winning in a hostile building is the next step for a real contender.

And folks, I want you to remember something before kickoff. A month ago, this team lost its franchise quarterback on a Monday night in Los Angeles. A week later, it lost its best pass rusher. The national media was writing eulogies. The power rankings put them in the bad team tier. And since then, this team has won two straight, taken over first place, ended back-to-back home games with defensive interceptions, watched a former bust become NFC Defensive Player of the Week, and added Jadeveon Clowney and Joey Porter Jr. to a defense that was already rising. Whatever happens today, this is not a fluke. This is a real football team with a real identity, and I believe it is built to go all the way this season.

So here is my prediction. I think the Giants defense makes Jayden Daniels uncomfortable from the first series. I think Clowney makes an impact in his Giants debut if he is active, maybe not on the stat sheet, but in the way Washington’s edges get squeezed. I think Winston has a cleaner day than he did against Arizona, with the play action game opening up shots for Nabers and the tight ends. I think Arvell Reese leads the team in tackles again, chasing Daniels sideline to sideline, and I think this secondary, with Banks riding the confidence of a Player of the Week award and Porter getting his first real test, comes up with at least one takeaway when Washington gets desperate late. I think Zvada stays perfect. And I think the Giants leave Washington at 4 and 1. Giants 24, Commanders 17. That is my call. Hold me to it.
