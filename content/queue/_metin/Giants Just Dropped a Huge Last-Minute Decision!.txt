First place, at home, in front of the whole country, and still nobody believes in the New York Giants. They are about five and a half to six-point underdogs at MetLife on Sunday against a 7-2 Jacksonville team. And with kickoff almost here, the Giants are facing one last-minute decision that could decide this whole game: how do you defend Trevor Lawrence? Blitz him like they have blitzed everybody since Brian Burns went down, or sit back, rush four, and make him earn it? And there is a late twist on the Jacksonville side too. Their lead running back missed practice on Thursday. Let’s get into it, and I will give you my final score prediction at the end.

Let’s set the stage. Sunday, November 22, 4:25 Eastern, CBS, MetLife Stadium. The NFL moved this game into the late national window for a reason. Two first-place teams. The Giants at 5-3-1, leading the NFC East. Jacksonville at 7-2, leading the AFC South. One field, one national window.

The Giants are coming off a painful loss. On Thursday night, November 12, they lost at home to Washington 24-21. They had the lead late. They had chances. They could not finish. And here is the question every critic keeps asking. The Giants have not yet beaten a team with a winning record this season. Jacksonville is the first one they will face since that week two loss to the Rams. Beat Jacksonville, and that argument is gone. Lose, and the critics will say it again, louder. They will say the record was built on a soft schedule. So this game matters for the standings and the playoffs. But more than that, it matters for respect.

And I respect Jacksonville. Seven wins do not happen by accident. They beat Tennessee 40-23 on the road last week. The week before, they beat Indianapolis 42-7. They have also beaten Philadelphia and Baltimore. Jacksonville is scoring 29.9 points per game, fourth best in the league. And they are allowing only 16 points per game, second best. That is a complete football team.

So let’s start with the decision. How do you defend Trevor Lawrence?

Lawrence is playing the best football of his career. Through nine games, he has thrown for 2,284 yards, 21 touchdowns and only five interceptions. He is completing about 66 percent of his passes, averaging 8.8 yards per attempt, and his passer rating is 112.8. He has been sacked only 14 times. That last number is the one I want you to remember. Fourteen sacks in nine games. Jacksonville gets the ball out of his hands quickly, and that quick release neutralizes pressure.

Now think about the Giants’ defense since Brian Burns tore his ACL in week three. This defense has been one of the most blitz-heavy in the league, right behind the Vikings under Brian Flores. And most of the time, it has worked. The Giants rank fifth in the league in yards allowed, at 301.4 yards per game. They are third in passing yards allowed, at 177.8 per game. That is with Burns out for the season, and with another key starter lost to a torn chest muscle. Last season, this was one of the worst defenses in the league. Now it is top five in yards allowed.

But we saw the other side of the blitz against Washington. Harbaugh confirmed after the game that the last two throws, including the game-winning touchdown to Stefon Diggs, came against zero blitzes. All three of Jayden Daniels’ touchdown passes came against cover zero. And the Giants only got two quarterback hits on him all night.

So here is the decision. Against a quarterback with a 112.8 rating who gets the ball out fast, do you keep blitzing?

My opinion, not a report: no, not as your base plan. You cannot live in an all-out blitz against Lawrence. He will find the open man, and with nobody deep, one missed tackle turns into a touchdown. The smarter plan is to rush four, win up front, and save the big pressure for the right moments, mostly on third-and-long. Be patient. Be disciplined. Make him earn it.

And the Giants have the guys to win with four. Abdul Carter has only two sacks through 10 weeks, but he is still among the league’s most effective edge rushers at winning his matchup and creating pressure. Pro Football Focus has him with an 89.3 overall grade, third among edge defenders, with 28 pressures. Harbaugh called him a game wrecker this week. Kayvon Thibodeaux is on the other side. And then there is the rookie.

Arvell Reese. The fifth overall pick out of Ohio State. He leads the Giants with 81 tackles. He has four and a half sacks, an interception, a forced fumble and a fumble recovery. And on November 8 in Philadelphia, in that 19-11 win, he had 12 tackles, three sacks and a forced fumble. At 21 years and 70 days old, he became the youngest player in NFL history to record three sacks in a game since sacks became an official stat in 1982. He was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week.

So the decision might look like this. Rush four with Carter, Thibodeaux and the interior. Use Reese as a spy, a run-stopper and a surprise blitzer, not every down, but at the right moments. That keeps more defenders in coverage, keeps a safety deep, and still gives you a chance to get to Lawrence when it matters.

Now, the late twist. The Jacksonville running game.

Here is what people miss about this Jaguars offense. Everybody talks about how they run the ball and control the game. But they are averaging just under four yards per carry, which ranks 22nd in the league. Their lead back, Bhayshul Tuten, has 609 yards on 148 carries. That is 4.1 yards per carry. Chris Rodriguez Jr. has 233 yards on 59 carries. Tuten is tough, he breaks tackles, and he is also a weapon as a receiver, with 18 catches for 206 yards and three receiving touchdowns. But this run game is steady, not explosive.

And here is the twist. Tuten was limited in practice on Wednesday, and he did not practice at all on Thursday. I am not saying he will miss the game. I do not know that, and I am not going to pretend I do. But his status is worth watching all the way up to kickoff. If Tuten is out or limited, Jacksonville loses its best runner and one of Lawrence’s best check-down options. That would put even more on Lawrence’s arm and make Jacksonville a little more one-dimensional.

And that matters because of how Jacksonville wants to play. They want to run on first down, stay ahead of the chains, and then use play action to hurt you deep. Stop the run on first down, and the whole approach gets thrown off. Second down becomes harder. Third down gets longer.

But this is not a weakness the Giants get for free. They are allowing 4.48 yards per carry and 121.6 rushing yards per game. They have to set the edge, make clean tackles, and force outside runs back inside. Remember what Harbaugh said after the Washington game. The biggest problem was letting Washington live in third-and-short. Stop the run on early downs, and you get the third-and-longs where the pass rush takes over.

Now the most dangerous weapon on the other side. Parker Washington.

Washington leads the league with 17.9 yards per catch. He has 645 receiving yards and six touchdowns. Against Tennessee last week, he had seven catches for 152 yards, a career best. He is the guy who turns a play-action fake into a 40-yard gain. And after the Giants gave up a 31-yard touchdown and a 32-yard touchdown against Washington last week, the deep ball is the one thing this defense cannot allow. That is another reason to keep a safety deep and not live in cover zero.

And Lawrence has more help than just Washington. Jakobi Meyers brings experience and reliable route running. Brenton Strange can hurt you at tight end. Brian Thomas Jr. can still make a big play if the defense loses focus. And Travis Hunter is a rare talent who plays on both sides of the ball.

Now let’s talk about the stat that scares me most for the Giants. Red zone defense.

The Giants have been near the bottom of the league at preventing touchdowns once opponents get inside the 20. That is painful, because this defense has been so good between the 20s. And Jacksonville’s red zone offense is strong, and head coach Liam Coen is not afraid to go for it on fourth down.

Let me put it in simple math. If Jacksonville drives the field three times and scores three touchdowns, that is 21 points, and the Giants are in trouble. If Jacksonville drives the field three times and gets two field goals and one touchdown, that is 13 points, and the Giants are right in this game. That eight-point difference is the difference between winning and losing. So when Jacksonville gets inside the 20, every snap is a playoff snap.

And the Giants will need it, because points will be hard to come by on the other side.

Jacksonville’s defense allows 16 points per game, second fewest in the league. They rank ninth in yards allowed. They allow just 3.9 yards per rush. And they added help at the trade deadline, defensive tackle Jordan Davis and safety Jessie Bates III. Neither played against Tennessee, so both could debut against the Giants. Davis adds size and strength inside. Bates brings experience in the back end. Jacksonville does not need to blitz constantly to get pressure, and they average about 1.8 takeaways per game.

So what is the Giants’ offensive plan? Protect the football. Protect J.J. McCarthy. Keep the chains moving. And finish drives with touchdowns, not field goals. Harbaugh said this week he wants McCarthy confident, aggressive and smart, and he wants him to handle all the blitz checks. Against a defense like this one, the quick decisions matter even more. Get the ball to Isaiah Likely over the middle. Lean on Cam Skattebo, who practiced fully this week after running for 78 yards and two touchdowns against Washington. Take a couple of calculated deep shots to Malik Nabers, Darnell Mooney and Malachi Fields. And do not hand Jacksonville a short field.

And special teams cannot be a disaster again. The Giants overhauled that unit this week. Xavier Gipson is the new returner, with a clean record on kickoffs. Tavierre Thomas is the new special teams ace, with Pro Football Focus grades in the mid-80s year after year. And Harbaugh said Dominic Zvada was dead on in practice, even from the right hash, where all six of his misses this season have come.

Let me give you three individual matchups I will be watching that we have not talked much about yet.

Matchup one: Deonte Banks against Parker Washington. Banks practiced fully this week after hurting his shoulder in warmups against Washington, the reason the Giants chose to receive the opening kickoff. But the bigger story with Banks is technique. The 33-yard pass interference on Treylon Burks in the final two minutes set up the game-winning drive. The criticism is that Banks watches the receiver instead of turning his head to find the ball, and that makes any contact look worse to the officials. Against a receiver averaging 17.9 yards per catch, Banks has to turn, locate and play the ball. If he does, he can make a big play. If he does not, Jacksonville will go right at him.

Matchup two: the Giants’ interior offensive line against Jordan Davis. If Davis plays, he is a massive body in the middle who can collapse the pocket and clog the run lanes Skattebo needs. The Giants have to double him on early downs and get the ball out quickly on passing downs. Harbaugh said the Giants’ line has already faced Davis this season, so they know what they are dealing with. Now they have to win that fight.

Matchup three: Xavier Gipson against Jacksonville’s coverage units. This will be Gipson’s first game of the season. He has 51 career kick returns for a 25.9-yard average with no muffed kicks. But he also has six muffed punts in his career, according to Pro Football Focus. In a game this close, one clean return that starts a drive at the 40 could be worth three points. One muff could be worth seven for Jacksonville. Every punt is going to be a nervous moment for Giants fans.

And then there is the turnover battle, which might be the biggest stat of all on Sunday. Jacksonville’s defense averages about 1.8 takeaways per game. The Giants’ defense averages about one. That is a gap the Giants cannot afford to widen. Remember, earlier in his Giants run, McCarthy was the only quarterback among the 31 with multiple starts since week seven without a turnover. That version of McCarthy, careful with the football, combined with the version that threw for 293 yards and a 66-yard touchdown against Washington, is exactly what the Giants need. Protect the ball, take the right shots, and make Jacksonville’s defense work for every stop. On defense, the Giants got two fourth-quarter interceptions against Washington, from Jevon Holland and Ar’Darius Washington. One more takeaway like that on Sunday, and I think the Giants win this game.

And let’s talk about what is on the line in the standings, because it is a lot. The Giants are two games ahead of both Washington and Dallas in the NFC East. Pro Football Focus projects the Giants’ playoff chances at about 76 percent with a win and around 49 percent with a loss. That is a 27-point swing in one afternoon. Another model has the Giants at about 85 percent with a win and 67 percent with a loss. Different numbers, same message. This is the biggest game of the season so far.

And the Giants have a real home-field advantage here. MetLife in the late window, a national audience on CBS, and a fan base that has been waiting for a statement win all year. One big defensive stop early can wake the whole building up. And after a week and a half to prepare, after the long break since Thursday night, this team should be rested, healthy and ready. Harbaugh promised an aggressive game plan. He said he hopes everyone is singing the praises of that game plan on Monday. Now it is time to see it.

There is one more thing I keep thinking about, and it is how this team responds to a loss. After the Rams game in week two, when Jaxson Dart went down, the Giants came back and beat Tennessee 12-7 in awful weather. After that frustrating New Orleans game, they went to Houston and played one of their best games of the season. This team has a habit of answering when people doubt them. And right now, everybody is doubting them. Underdogs at home, after a loss, against a 7-2 team. That is exactly the kind of spot where this group has played its best football.

So here is how I see the whole game coming together.

If the Giants make the right decision on defense, rush four, keep a safety deep, and save the blitz for the right moments, they can slow down Lawrence and Parker Washington. If Tuten is out or limited, Jacksonville’s run game gets even more manageable. If the Giants win in the red zone, they keep this game within one score. And if McCarthy protects the football and special teams is clean, the Giants have a real shot to win it late.

If the Giants blitz too much, give up explosive plays, and let Jacksonville score touchdowns in the red zone, this gets away from them.

That is the game. That is the decision.

The Giants hold Jacksonville to one touchdown in three red zone trips. McCarthy plays a clean game with no turnovers. And it comes down to the final minute. Giants 23, Jaguars 20, with Dominic Zvada hitting the game-winning field goal. Yes, from the right hash. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A top-five defense that makes you earn every yard. A rookie linebacker making history. A pass rusher grading out as one of the best in football. A young quarterback playing winning football. A running back playing with fire. And a coach who knows how to win big games. Everybody is picking Jacksonville. Good. Let them. The Giants have been doubted all season, and they are still sitting in first place.
