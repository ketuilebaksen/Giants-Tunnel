Because when you look at what that result did to the standings, to the record books, to the tiebreakers, and to the road ahead, you realize the Giants did not just win a football game on Sunday. They put themselves in a position almost nobody imagined when Jackson Dart went down. They are 4 and 2 and 1, alone in first place in the NFC East, unbeaten in the division, on a six-game winning streak against Houston, and heading into a bye week with a quarterback who just won his first start. Today, I am going to give you five pieces of good news that came out of that game, and then one honest warning, because I always give you both sides. Let’s get into it.

If you are new here, hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now and tell me, what is the best news you have heard about this team all season?

Good news number one. History. Before Sunday, the Giants led the all-time regular-season series with the Texans 5 to 1, with five straight wins going back to 2006. The last meeting before this one was week 10 of the 2022 season at MetLife, a 24 to 16 Giants win. Now the series is 6 to 1, and the winning streak is six straight. That makes it the Giants’ longest winning streak against any team outside the NFC East since they beat the Rams seven straight times from 2002 to 2016. Think about that. Coaches have come and gone, quarterbacks have come and gone, entire rosters have turned over, and the Giants just keep beating Houston. I know history does not catch passes or sack anybody. But streaks like this say something about a franchise’s ability to rise to the moment, and on Sunday, this team rose to it on the road.

Good news number two. The standings and the tiebreakers. And this is the part people are not talking about enough. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Coming into this weekend, the Eagles and the Cowboys were both 2 and 4, and Washington was 1 and 4 and 1. But here is the hidden piece. The Giants are unbeaten in the division. They beat Dallas 28 to 20 in week one. They tied Washington 27 to 27 in that wild game. That is no losses in the NFC East so far. And in a division race this tight, division record can become one of the most important tiebreakers there is. If two teams finish with the same record, head-to-head results and division record are among the first things the league looks at. The Giants already own a head-to-head win over Dallas. They did not lose to Washington. And the next two games after the bye are both in the division, at Philadelphia and at home against Washington. If the Giants win those two, they would be 3 and 0 and 1 in the division, and that kind of tiebreaker cushion could decide who wins the NFC East in January.

Let me quickly explain why this matters so much. When two teams in the same division finish with the same record, the NFL goes through a list of tiebreakers. First is head-to-head record. Then division record. Then record in common games. Then conference record. So, every division game counts twice, once in the standings and once in the tiebreakers. A win over the Eagles in Philadelphia is not just a win. It is a head-to-head edge and a division record edge at the same time. And the tie with Washington, which felt so frustrating at the time, actually means the Giants have not lost to them, which keeps the division record clean. That is why I keep telling you every game in this division is worth more than it looks.

And here is the stake that makes it all real. The Giants have not won the NFC East since 2011. That was the season they went 9 and 7, won the division, and then won the Super Bowl. Fifteen years without a division title is a long time for a franchise with this kind of history. This team has a real chance to end that drought, and Sunday made that chance a lot bigger.

Good news number three. The pace. Let me do some quick math, and I want to be clear this is just math, not a prediction. The Giants have four wins through seven games. If they keep winning at that rate over a 17-game season, they would finish with somewhere around nine or ten wins. Now, a few weeks ago, analysts were saying it might only take eight or, dare I say, nine wins to take the NFC East this year, with the Eagles losing Jalen Hurts for the season and the Cowboys struggling. The Giants are on pace to clear that number. That does not mean they will. Paces change fast in this league. But it means the Giants control their own destiny. They do not need help. They just need to keep winning the games in front of them.

Good news number four. The defense answered the challenge. Remember the week leading into this game. Dennard Wilson was frustrated after the Saints game, especially about players not running to the ball. The Saints had run for 175 yards. Alvin Kamara had turned a manageable catch into a 31-yard touchdown because of missed tackles and poor pursuit. Harbaugh said it is nobody’s fault, it is everybody’s responsibility, and that the players were mad at themselves. Well, here is how they responded. Houston came into this game having scored 34 points the week before. C.J. Stroud was second in the NFL in passing yards, coming off 312 yards and three touchdowns, and he was going for his fourth straight game with a rating over 105. He had Nico Collins, who was going for his 14th straight game with 55-plus scrimmage yards. Jaylin Noel, coming off a career-high 107 receiving yards. Foster Moreau, coming off two touchdown catches. Marvin Harrison Jr., newly acquired. Tank Dell, back from his knee injury. And the Giants held all of that to 21 points. On the road. With a secondary that had been dealing with concussions all week. That is a defense that heard the message. And it matters for more than one game, because it tells you the players respond when their coaches challenge them. Not every locker room does.

And think about who was on that defense. D.J. Reader, playing against the franchise where he spent 2016 through 2019, with 154 tackles, 6.5 sacks, and 16 tackles for loss in 61 games for Houston. Abdul Carter and Arvell Reese, two young players who have been around the football all season. Deonte Banks, tied for second in the league in passes defended coming in. Jason Pinnock, who forced his first fumble of the season against the Saints. Paulson Adebo, who was set to return from injured reserve. I will go through the full film during the bye and give individual credit where it is due, but as a unit, this defense did exactly what it had to do. It kept one of the league’s most productive passing offenses in check and gave its young quarterback a chance to win.

Good news number five. The quarterback. J.J. McCarthy is now 1 and 0 as a Giants starter. And I want you to remember everything that was stacked against him. A road game. A loud stadium. A defensive head coach in DeMeco Ryans who had a full week to prepare for him. And two of the best edge rushers in football, Will Anderson Jr., who came in leading the NFL with 7.5 sacks, and Danielle Hunter, who came in third in the league in sacks since 2015, a guy who had two sacks and a forced fumble the last time he faced the Giants back in 2022. McCarthy had been on this team for less than a month. He had 100 percent of the playbook for the first time only this week. And the Giants won.

And here is a parallel that gives me real hope. Back in 2008, John Harbaugh was a first-year head coach in Baltimore, and he started a rookie quarterback named Joe Flacco. Nobody expected much from that team. They went 11 and 5, won two road playoff games, and made it all the way to the AFC Championship Game. A first-year Harbaugh team with a young quarterback outperforming every expectation. Now, the 2026 Giants are not the 2008 Ravens, and McCarthy is not a rookie. But Harbaugh has done this before. He knows how to build a winner around a young quarterback in a hurry. And so far, in his first season in New York, he is 4 and 2 and 1 with three different quarterbacks having played meaningful snaps this season. That is coaching.

And do not forget what the next two weeks mean for McCarthy specifically. Matt Nagy told us McCarthy had about 50 percent of the playbook against Washington, about 90 percent against the Saints, and 100 percent for the first time in Houston. He won a road game in his first week with the full offense. Now he gets two more weeks of practice with all of it, plus the film from Houston to learn from. The version of McCarthy that shows up in Philadelphia should be more comfortable, more decisive, and more connected with his receivers than the version that showed up in Houston. That is the scary part for the rest of the division.

And the safety net is still there. Jameis Winston went unbeaten in his first three starts this season, threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games, and handled losing the job with total class. He praised McCarthy, said he was proud of him, and said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. If anything happens to McCarthy, the Giants have a veteran who has already won games for them this year. Not many teams in the league can say that about their backup.

And the weapons around McCarthy are only getting started. Coming into Sunday, Malik Nabers had 168 career receptions, the second-most by any player through his first 25 games in NFL history, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. He had become just the third player in league history with 10 or more catches in five of his first 25 games, and he had three straight 100-yard games, including 12 catches for 165 yards against the Saints. Isaiah Likely came in with six or more catches in three straight games and touchdowns in back-to-back weeks, and he had already built real chemistry with McCarthy in that fourth quarter against New Orleans. Those two give any quarterback a security blanket, and they give a young quarterback a chance to look a lot better than his experience level would suggest.

And the backfield has a direction now too. With Najee Harris released, Harbaugh said this week that Tyrone Tracy Jr. has earned more carries, praising his assignments, his physical running, his acceleration, and his speed. Tracy scored two touchdowns against the Saints and graded as the best pass protector on the team. Cam Skattebo had 71 scrimmage yards in week five and had a heck of a week of practice, according to Harbaugh. And Devin Singletary gives them a third-down option. A clearer backfield, with fewer backs splitting the work, should help this run game find a rhythm in the second half of the season.

Now, a lot of you asked about the Texans last time, so let me add a few thoughts on Houston. This loss drops them to 2 and 5 and snaps the two-game winning streak they built after an 0 and 4 start. The Texans made a big swing trading for Marvin Harrison Jr. in the middle of the season. They got Tank Dell back. They have the league’s sack leader. And they still lost at home to a team starting a quarterback making his first start for a new franchise. That is going to raise hard questions in Houston. But I want to be fair. This is not a bad team. Stroud, Collins, Anderson, Hunter, Stingley, that is real talent. Which is exactly why this win means so much. The Giants did not beat a broken team. They beat a dangerous one, in its own building.

And there is one more piece of good news that comes with the calendar. The bye week. This team has been beaten up all season. Brian Burns and Chauncey Golston are on injured reserve. Andrew Thomas has been working through a groin injury. Jermaine Eluemunor has been dealing with a calf. Greg Newsome II, Colton Hood, and Jevon Holland have all been dealing with concussions. Tremaine Edmunds has a shoulder injury. Two weeks without a game gives a lot of those players a real chance to heal. And for McCarthy, it means two full weeks of first-team reps with Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely before the trip to Philadelphia. Coming out of a bye with a healthier roster and a quarterback who has had time to grow into the offense is about as good as it gets at the midpoint of a season.

And think about what is waiting on the other side of that bye. The Eagles, expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the rest of the season. I hope he makes a full recovery, because his health comes first. But from a football standpoint, the Giants are walking into Philadelphia with a real opportunity. Then Washington comes to MetLife, the team the Giants tied in that unforgettable game earlier this year, the one that ended with Washington wasting 42 seconds and the clock running out on them. Two division games, back to back, with the Giants in first place. That is the stretch that will define this season.

And here is what I expect the coaches to do with the bye. First, a self-scout. Every staff in the league uses the bye to look at its own tendencies, what it calls on certain downs, out of certain formations, and where an opponent could predict it. With a new quarterback, that matters even more. Second, tackling and pursuit. Harbaugh admitted the defense might be leading the league in three-and-outs but is still giving up too many explosive plays, especially on scrambles and long runs. That is a fixable problem, and two weeks of practice is the perfect time to fix it. Third, building out the McCarthy offense. More designed runs, more bootlegs, more of the run-pass option looks that disappeared when Dart went down. And fourth, rest. Sometimes the most important thing a coach can do during a bye is get his players off their feet.

And give Joe Schoen some credit here too. The quarterback who won in Houston cost a 2027 fifth-round pick. And here is a fun twist. Back in 2025, the trade Schoen made to move back into the first round for Jackson Dart was with the Houston Texans. So, the Giants have now beaten Houston with the quarterback they got for a late-round pick, while the quarterback they traded with Houston to draft is recovering for next season. When Dart comes back, the Giants could have a real decision on their hands, a young franchise quarterback and a young quarterback who has proven he can win. That is the definition of a good problem, and the kind of problem every general manager in this league would love to have.

Now, the warning, because I always give you both sides. One win does not fix everything. A week ago, this team gave up 34 straight points to the Saints, turned the ball over four times, and allowed 175 rushing yards. Harbaugh himself said before this game that if this team has been anything, it has been inconsistent. A lot of spectacular plays, but not enough consistency. Sunday was a great step. But the Giants have to prove they can stack these performances, week after week. The run game still needs a back to take the job, whether that is Tyrone Tracy Jr., Cam Skattebo, or Devin Singletary. The offensive line still needs to get healthy. And the defense still has to cut down on explosive plays. The good news is real. The work is not done.

But here is why I am so excited. Think about everything this team has been through in seven weeks. They lost Jackson Dart for the rest of the regular season. They lost Brian Burns. They lost Paulson Adebo for a month. They changed quarterbacks twice. They tied a game they should have won and lost a game they should have won. They came back from 14 down in Washington and from 24 down against the Saints. And through all of it, they are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, unbeaten in the division, and riding a winning streak against Houston that goes back two decades. A team that can survive all of that, and still be on top, is a team with something special in that locker room.

So, here is what I will be watching during the bye. Number one, the injury report, and who is ready for Philadelphia. Number two, the McCarthy chatter, and how the coaches talk about the offense expanding around him. Number three, the running back room. And number four, the rest of the NFC East, because every loss by the Eagles, Cowboys, and Commanders makes the Giants’ lead a little more comfortable.

I have been saying it all season. Through the injuries, the tie, the Saints loss, and the quarterback change. And after Sunday in Houston, I am saying it louder than ever. This team can go all the way.
