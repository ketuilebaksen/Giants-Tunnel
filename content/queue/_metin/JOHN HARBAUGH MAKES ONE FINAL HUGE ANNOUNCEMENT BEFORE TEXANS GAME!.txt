John Harbaugh saved the best news of the week for his final press conference before Houston. Paulson Adebo is ready to go. Harbaugh said the cornerback looks good, called him a legit cover corner who tackles, a high-end corner in this league who is finally, probably, healthy, and said he cannot wait to see it happen, potentially this week. Folks, that is a starting cornerback coming back from injured reserve on the exact day the Giants face C.J. Stroud, Nico Collins, Marvin Harrison Jr., and Tank Dell. And that was not the only announcement. Harbaugh said his starting tackles should have a good chance to be ready to go against two of the best pass rushers in football. He told us exactly why the Giants released Najee Harris. He gave Tyrone Tracy Jr. the strongest endorsement any running back has gotten from him all season. And he dropped one line about accountability that I think explains this entire team. Today, I am going to walk through all of it before kickoff in Houston. Let’s get into it.

First, a small moment from the start of the press conference that I think says a lot about this coach. Harbaugh came out late, and he apologized for the wait. He explained that there had been guests in the building, including family and a group of students from a criminal justice college, and he took a moment to say those young people are going to make a difference in the world. It is a small thing. But I have noticed it all season. Harbaugh talks about people before he talks about plays. That matters in a locker room, and it matters when you are asking players to hold themselves to a high standard.

Now, the big news. Paulson Adebo. Remember, the Giants put him on injured reserve back on September 18th, and this week they designated him to return. He practiced, and on Thursday, Harbaugh was asked about him directly. He said Adebo looks good and is ready to go. He called him a legit cover corner who tackles, and a high-end corner in this league who is finally probably healthy. He said he cannot wait to see it happen, potentially this week. And he mentioned that Dennard Wilson had apparently already told reporters Adebo would be out there. Folks, when the head coach and the defensive coordinator are both saying it, it looks like Sunday.

And the timing could not be better. Greg Newsome II has been in the concussion protocol and is unlikely to play, according to reports. Colton Hood has been dealing with a concussion too. Jevon Holland was limited in practice while working through the protocol. The Giants secondary has been held together with tape for weeks. Now they get back a starting-caliber corner the Giants signed in free agency in 2025, a long, physical player who can match up with bigger receivers. Think about what that does against Houston. Nico Collins is going for his 14th straight game with 55-plus scrimmage yards. Jaylin Noel just had a career-high 107 receiving yards. Harrison Jr. is settling into a new offense. Having Adebo outside, next to Deonte Banks, who is tied for second in the NFL with eight passes defended, and Joey Porter Jr., gives Dennard Wilson options he has not had in a while.

But let me add one caution, because I always want to be honest. A player coming back from a long absence often has a snap count in his first game. So, I would not assume Adebo plays every snap. He might rotate, especially early. But even 30 or 40 snaps from a corner of his caliber is a huge upgrade over what the Giants would have had without him.

Now, the second announcement. The tackles. A reporter asked how the Giants’ tackles looked, and Harbaugh’s answer went straight to the challenge waiting in Houston. He referred to number 51 and number 55, two of the best in the league. That is Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter. Anderson leads the NFL with 7.5 sacks this season. Hunter ranks third in the NFL with 117.5 sacks since 2015. Harbaugh said the Giants have played them a lot and understand what they can do. And then he said that, based on that day’s practice, his tackles should have a good chance to be ready to go.

That is huge for J.J. McCarthy. Andrew Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor have both been dealing with injuries, and they returned to practice as limited participants this week. If both are available, McCarthy has his starting tackles for his first start, against the best pair of edge rushers he has seen all year. And if one of them is limited, rookie J.C. Davis is ready, after not allowing a single pressure in 25 snaps against the Saints. Check the final inactives, but Harbaugh sounded optimistic.

Now, the running backs, and this is where Harbaugh revealed a lot. He was asked first about Cam Skattebo, and whether he had done enough this week to earn a bigger role. His answer was careful. He said Skattebo was always going to be a big part of this season. He said Skattebo had a heck of a week of practice, like all the backs, and that the whole team had a good week. And then he said the focus is on playing their best football against a tough opponent in a tough environment. He did not say Skattebo is back as the lead runner. He did not say he is not. I read that as deliberate. Harbaugh did not want to turn a practice report into a depth chart announcement.

But when the conversation turned to Tyrone Tracy Jr., Harbaugh was much more direct. He was asked whether Tracy has shown enough to earn more carries, and he said yes. Asked what Tracy has shown, he said Tracy has practiced well, he has been right on with his assignments, and the physical nature of his running, his acceleration, and his speed have all been very good. Folks, notice what came first in that answer. Assignments. Before the speed, before the highlights. A coach who talks about assignments first is telling you what he values in a running back. And it suggests the difference between Tracy and Skattebo right now is not just talent. It is trust. Tracy scored two touchdowns against the Saints and graded as the best pass protector on the team. That is the kind of back a coach trusts in front of a young quarterback in his first start.

And then came the Najee Harris question. A reporter introduced it by saying the topic had slipped through the cracks on Wednesday, and Harbaugh joked that somebody should beat themselves up about that. Then he gave a real answer. He said the decision was not about production. It was about setting a course. He said it is hard to carry four backs, and there were other moves the team had to make to get players on the roster. He said he told Harris it was nothing against what he did. Harris ran super hard, Harbaugh said, but he did not think Harris had a lot of room in the plays he got over the last couple of weeks. And this was simply the direction the staff decided to go. The players who remain are the ones who will get the shot.

That is an honest answer, and it fits the math of a 53-man roster. A running back who is not getting touches is taking a spot from a player who might help on special teams or fill a need somewhere else. And I appreciated that Harbaugh did not criticize Harris. He said Harris ran hard and had little room, which is a fair assessment of an offensive line that has struggled in the run game. But it also reads as a message to the rest of the locker room. If this staff will make a hard call in the running back room, it will make one anywhere. The one caution I would add is depth. With Harris gone, if an injury hits the backfield, there is less margin. Skattebo, Tracy, and Devin Singletary have to stay healthy.

Now, the line that I think explains this entire team. The next question was about effort, after Dennard Wilson’s strong comments earlier in the week about players not running to the ball against the Saints. Harbaugh said certain plays were shown in the team meeting, in all three phases, and that most teams probably have plays like that, but they were not going to be acceptable. He said the players were more accountable than anybody, that they acknowledged where they needed to do better, and that he thought they were mad at themselves.

And then he said this. It is nobody’s fault. It is only your fault if you consciously object, or if you are sabotaging. Otherwise, it is everybody’s responsibility. Folks, that is the philosophical center of how Harbaugh coaches. He separates two kinds of failure. A missed tackle or a missed assignment is a failure of execution, and the whole group shares it, because every unit is responsible for the standard. A deliberate lack of effort is a different thing. That is a choice, and he will not tolerate it. He said Wilson told the media exactly what he told the players, and the players respected it and are determined to improve. He said the team is chasing perfection, knowing it is hard to be perfect, and he pointed to the craziness around the league as proof. I think that framing is smart. It lets him demand a standard without pretending mistakes will magically vanish.

And then he made the most honest statement of the whole session. Asked how the team responded, he said really well. They practiced well again, and practices have been improving every week. But then he said that if this team has been anything, it has been inconsistent. A lot of spectacular plays on both sides of the ball, but the consistency is not there yet. I respect that. A coach who praises practice but refuses to claim the results is telling his players that practice habits only matter if they show up on Sunday.

And folks, Harbaugh knows better than anybody that inconsistent teams can catch fire. In 2012, his Ravens lost four of their last five regular-season games. People wrote them off. They went on to win the Super Bowl. The difference was that the consistency showed up at the right time. That is exactly what Harbaugh is chasing with this team. He does not need the Giants to be perfect in October. He needs them to be consistent in December and January, when the games decide everything.

He also gave some specifics about the defense that I found really interesting. He said the Giants might be leading the league in three-and-outs, which he called phenomenal defense. And yet, they are giving up too many explosive plays, especially on quarterback scrambles and some runs that go for too many yards. That is a revealing combination. A defense that forces a lot of three-and-outs is winning on early downs and on third down most of the time. The explosive plays are the exceptions, the moments when gap integrity breaks down, or a scrambling quarterback extends a play. That fits exactly what Wilson was complaining about. If the effort and discipline hold on the plays that go wrong, those explosive numbers should drop.

Then he described Houston’s offense, and this is where it gets hard. He said the Texans have a quarterback who can run and make throws while scrambling, and a team that is determined to run the ball, even though they have not had a lot of success with it yet. But he noted they were pretty good last week against Jacksonville, which he described as the league’s number one run defense heading into that game. Asked how the Giants focus on limiting explosive plays and gap integrity, he said you have to chase it, because offenses do a good job of challenging gap integrity. They change the schemes, use motions, shifts, and inserts, and create confusion. The defense needs a high level of communication and shared understanding, and the players have to know what each other will do.

And the last part of that answer was my favorite. He said it would be hard to run the ball against any defense if everyone were in the right place every time, because the players are good, but coaches find ways to create running lanes. That is a respectful description of what a good offensive staff does. And it explains why gap integrity is a constant chase, not a one-week fix. Every week, the opponent brings a new wrinkle, and the defense has to adapt before the first snap.

There was also a roster question I found useful. A reporter asked why the Giants now carry an emergency quarterback when they did not in the first couple of weeks. That is Jake Haener, who had been the number two quarterback for a while earlier this season, before the Giants added McCarthy. Harbaugh said it was probably circumstances. Haener is still on the roster, so the team can afford it. If it could not afford it, or wanted to add a player who could help more, it would do that. He even wondered aloud how often an emergency quarterback has actually played since the rule changed. But he said it is really important if it ever comes up, because you do not want a wide receiver playing quarterback. With Dart out, McCarthy starting, and Winston as the backup, Haener is the last line of defense, and in a season this crazy, I am glad he is there.

The most thoughtful question of the session was about the short term versus the long term. A reporter asked how Harbaugh balances the one-game-at-a-time mentality with the bigger picture, and whether he ever worries a decision comes too early or too late. Harbaugh said every situation is different. You have to consider the short term, the middle term, and the long term, the best interest of the player and the best interest of the organization. Sometimes a player who wants to play has to be held out, because you have to protect the player from himself. But in the end, he said, you have to look at what gives you the best chance to win this game. And then he joked that if Tom Brady decided to come out of retirement, he would not play him in the first week, because it would not be in the best interest of the team.

There is a serious point behind that joke. The best chance to win this game is a principle that is easy to say and hard to apply, because every decision has hidden costs. And it tells you exactly how he made the quarterback decision. With McCarthy named the starter, Harbaugh is telling you the staff believes that gives the Giants the best chance to win in Houston, even if it comes with long-term questions about how the quarterback room develops. He will not let the calendar make decisions for him.

One more depth note. Harbaugh was asked about linebacker Micah McFadden, with Tremaine Edmunds dealing with a shoulder injury. He said he had said from the start that the team has three starters at linebacker, so if McFadden is needed this week, he is a starter and he will play really well. That is a quiet but strong endorsement, and it matters next to rookie Arvell Reese, who has nine or more tackles in three of his last four games.

So, what do I take from all of it? First, Harbaugh values clarity. He tells you plainly why a player was released, what the standard is, and where the team has fallen short. Second, the staff is creating competition without panic, in the running back room and everywhere else. Third, the team is preparing for a physical test, with a defense that must tighten its gap integrity and an offense that hopes to have its tackles back against Anderson and Hunter. And fourth, the coach keeps coming back to one question. What gives the team the best chance to win this game?

My concerns are real. A coach who admits inconsistency is telling you the team has not yet shown on Sunday what it shows in practice. The explosive plays are a real issue, and Houston is exactly the kind of offense that creates them. The backfield has less margin with Harris gone. And the quarterback is making his first start for this team. But the opportunities are real too. If the tackles are healthy, if Adebo returns, and if the defense plays with the effort this staff demands, the Giants have a chance to look like the team they believe they are.

I am sticking with my prediction. Giants 24, Texans 21. And with Adebo back in the secondary, I feel a little better about it than I did yesterday. A win, and the Giants go into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, on top of the NFC East, with the Eagles missing Jalen Hurts and the Cowboys at 2 and 4. I believe in this team, and I have said it all season. This team can go all the way.
