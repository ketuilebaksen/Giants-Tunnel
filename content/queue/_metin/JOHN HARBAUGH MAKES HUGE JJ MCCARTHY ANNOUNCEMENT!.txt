John Harbaugh just made the J.J. McCarthy announcement every Giants fan has been waiting for. When McCarthy took over, the question was always the same. How much of the playbook does he really know? He arrived in late September. He came off the bench against the Saints. He started in Houston after barely a week in the building. And now Harbaugh says that is over. He said McCarthy is pretty much caught up. And then he went even further. In terms of anything the Giants would be doing in the game plan, McCarthy will be as fully prepared as any quarterback would be at this stage. Folks, that means the training wheels are coming off. And it comes in the same week Philadelphia made a stunning quarterback change of its own. The Eagles benched Tanner McKee and named Joe Milton their starter for Sunday. So the Giants walk into Philadelphia with a fully loaded McCarthy, facing a quarterback making his first start for the Eagles. Today, I am breaking down everything Harbaugh said, what it means for McCarthy, what Milton changes, and why Harbaugh says the word favorite means nothing to him. Let’s get into it.

Now that McCarthy has the whole playbook, how many touchdowns does he throw in Philadelphia?

Before the football, I want to mention how Harbaugh opened his press conference, because it mattered. He started by sending the team’s thoughts and prayers to the family of Rachel Apa, the 24-year-old runner who died during the New York City Marathon last weekend. He said he could not imagine the pain the family is going through, and also the pride they must feel for a life well lived. He said the New York Giants are thinking about her and her family. That was a classy way to start, and our thoughts are with her family too.

Now, let’s get to McCarthy. A reporter pointed out that Harbaugh did not hesitate to put McCarthy in when he felt he was ready, and that McCarthy has now had a bye week, some downtime, and more time in the playbook. Should the Giants expect a different, better, more prepared quarterback? Harbaugh said yes, and walked through it. McCarthy had a practice during the bye week. Another practice on Monday. Then Wednesday. So three practices, with three more to come before Sunday. And then he said McCarthy is at the stage now where he is pretty much caught up. In terms of anything the Giants would be doing in the game plan, he will be as fully prepared as any quarterback would be at this stage.

Let me tell you why that is such a big deal. Think about what McCarthy has done without being fully caught up. He was acquired from Minnesota on September 28th, for a 2027 fifth-round pick. In relief against the Saints, he threw for 243 yards in the fourth quarter alone. Then, in his first start, in Houston, he went 20 of 28 for 252 yards, with no touchdowns, no interceptions, and no fumbles, in a 27 to 21 overtime win. He was sacked three times, but he never turned the ball over. He hit two big shots early, including a 47-yarder to Malachi Fields. And he did all of that with only a slice of the playbook.

Now imagine what the coaches can do with all of it. When a quarterback is new, the play-callers keep it simple. They use plays he knows cold. They limit his pre-snap decisions. They do not ask him to check into a lot of different plays at the line. Once he is fully up to speed, Matt Nagy and the staff can open everything up. More formations. More motion. More options at the line of scrimmage. More ways to attack a defense. That is what Harbaugh just told you is coming. This is my read on what caught up means, not a play sheet from the Giants, but that is how it usually works.

And then a reporter asked something interesting. McCarthy seems to be in a really good headspace. How much does that contribute to his confidence, after he did not have much success at other points in his career? And Harbaugh said, it’s a fair question, but really not thinking about it and really don’t care. He said there is no comparison made to anything. It doesn’t matter. All they are looking at is this week and getting ready for this game, and that is all McCarthy is locked in on. History does not factor into this game.

I love that answer, and I think it is exactly what McCarthy needs. Remember where he came from. In Minnesota, he threw 11 touchdowns and 12 interceptions in 10 starts, and he had fallen to third on the depth chart behind Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz. A lot of people wrote him off. Harbaugh is refusing to let any of that follow McCarthy into this building. Nothing from Minnesota matters. Only this week matters.

And another reporter asked about McCarthy’s experience in big games, and Harbaugh brought it right back to the present. He said McCarthy has been in huge games, the biggest games in front of the biggest crowds in college football. Remember, McCarthy won a national championship at Michigan, coached by Harbaugh’s brother Jim. And Harbaugh said he has done it in the NFL too. So the crowd in Philadelphia is not going to be an issue. The issue is going to be the Eagles, and how the Giants operate, play, and execute. The outside noise is not going to be a factor.

Now, here is the receipt I need to own. Before Houston, I predicted McCarthy would throw two touchdown passes. He threw zero. I owned it then, and I am owning it again. But I want to make a different point now. If McCarthy did all that in Houston with only part of the playbook, I think the touchdowns are coming. I am not going to give you a number this time, because I learned my lesson. But I think the version of McCarthy we see in Philadelphia will be more aggressive than the one we saw in Houston.

Now, let’s talk about the other quarterback. The Eagles are starting Joe Milton. Harbaugh joked that word travels fast, and said the Giants heard about it before practice. And this is a stunning reversal. On Monday, Nick Sirianni said the Eagles were sticking with Tanner McKee. Then, on Wednesday, they flipped. Milton will be Philadelphia’s third different starter this season.

Let me give you Milton’s background, because a lot of fans do not know much about him. He was a sixth-round pick by the Patriots in 2024. New England traded him to Dallas in April of 2025, where he backed up Dak Prescott and played in four games off the bench. This summer, he lost the backup job in Dallas to Sam Howell, got waived, and then went back to the Cowboys’ practice squad. And on October 16th, the Eagles signed him off that practice squad, one day before they placed Jalen Hurts on injured reserve. So, three weeks later, he is starting a division game.

And why did the Eagles make the change? Because McKee struggled. In his three starts, he completed 63.2 percent of his passes for 638 yards, with two touchdowns and two interceptions. The Eagles lost all three, and their losing streak is now at six. They are 2 and 6, last in the NFC East. And Jalen Hurts, according to ESPN’s sources, is expected to miss the rest of the season with a serious medical issue. So this is a team searching for anything that works.

So how do the Giants prepare for a quarterback with so little film? Harbaugh said it is not so different from the previous quarterback. You watch the plays. The Giants have seen Milton in college, in the preseason, and in the little he has played in the regular season. They will try to anticipate what the Eagles want to run with him, the same way other teams had to figure out what the Giants would run with McCarthy. But Harbaugh said it is still going to be their offense, their structure, and the Eagles team. And then he repeated a message he said one of the veterans shared with the team. Our concern first and foremost is about us. If the Giants play the way they are supposed to play, they will be in really good shape.

And I have to admit something here. In my last couple of videos, I spent a lot of time breaking down how the Giants’ pass rush could attack Tanner McKee, after Washington sacked him four times. That plan just changed. Milton is a different kind of quarterback. He is known for a huge arm, and he can move. He can create plays outside the structure of the offense. That means the Giants’ pass rushers cannot just pin their ears back. They have to keep him in the pocket, because a big-armed quarterback who escapes can hit a deep shot at any moment. Contain matters as much as the sack.

But a first-time starter also brings mistakes. Milton has very little NFL experience. He will be facing one of the best pass rushes in football, with Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux, and Arvell Reese, and a defensive coordinator, Dennard Wilson, who loves to disguise pressure. Young quarterbacks struggle with disguised looks. If the Giants can show him one coverage before the snap and play another after it, they could force some bad throws. And Greg Newsome, fresh off his new contract, would love to be waiting for one.

And do not forget about Saquon Barkley. Harbaugh was asked about him too. He said he does not really know Barkley personally, maybe bumped into him at a Pro Bowl, but he has nothing but respect for him. First class, from everything he has been told, and obviously a great player. And then he described what makes Barkley dangerous. He said Barkley is an elusive back, that the Eagles are among the league leaders in making tacklers miss, and that Barkley is a big part of that. He can take the ball from the A gap to the edge to the backside edge in a flash. He is still a threat catching the ball out of the backfield on checkdowns. All hands on deck, Harbaugh said, when he is out there.

That is the real danger on Sunday. With a new quarterback, the Eagles are going to lean on Barkley. Even though he only ran for 30 yards on 13 carries against Washington, a back like Barkley can break a game open on one run. If the Giants let him get to the edge, that is where the trouble starts. The defense has to tackle well, and it has to keep him from becoming Milton’s security blanket.

Now, let’s get to the injury updates. Harbaugh said both Cam Skattebo and Abdul Carter are day-to-day. But he gave two very different tones. On Carter, he said he should be fine. It is an illness, so he should be fine. On Skattebo, he said Scat’s got a hamstring, so you know how that goes. We’ll see. I take that as good news on Carter and a real question on Skattebo.

But Harbaugh also said he feels very confident about the running back situation. He likes the guys the Giants have. He mentioned Devin Singletary, who he called Motor. He mentioned Eric Gray, who the Giants brought back. And he mentioned the young back the Giants claimed off the Chargers, Gregory Desrosiers, who he called a very talented young guy. Those players have had two practices, and if they have to go, Harbaugh said, they will definitely be ready to roll.

Then came the complacency question, and this was one of my favorite parts of the press conference. A reporter said this is not a question he would have asked earlier in the year, but does Harbaugh have to talk to his players about being complacent? Harbaugh said it is definitely something to get locked in on, but he does not believe it is an issue this week. He said the team’s leaders have talked to the players about it. And then he said this. We’re not really anywhere close to that yet. We’re fighting for our lives too. We’ve got everything to gain. We know how big this game is. We know who we’re going against. We’ll be ready.

And then he was asked about being considered the favorites in the division, after years of the Eagles being in that spot. And Harbaugh said it is all kind of meaningless. If you are betting, it is important, but it does not matter to the Giants, because there are still ten games left. He said every team in the division believes it can make up ground in ten games. And he said he has had experience with that in his coaching career. He knows how fast a lead can disappear. It is about what you do, not what anybody says about you or where you are ranked. You have to go win games.

And then he went deeper, and this is the heart of the Harbaugh philosophy. He said if a team even thinks for one second that a game is not going to be a challenge, shame on them. The opportunity is not the division. The opportunity is the game. And within the game, the opportunity is the next play. This is the play where everything is on the line. Then once that play is gone, it is behind you, and the next play is the play where everything is on the line. Same thing with the games. This is the game where everything is on the line. And once it is done, the next game is the one where everything is on the line. He said that is what the team’s one-and-oh t-shirts are all about.

I love that. And it is exactly the right mindset for a team that has a three-loss lead in the division. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. It would be easy to look at those standings and relax. Harbaugh is making sure nobody does. One and oh, every week.

A reporter also asked about the Giants’ long losing streak in Philadelphia. The Giants have lost 13 straight games there, counting the playoffs, and have not won there since 2013. The reporter asked if Harbaugh addresses the psychology of that, and the idea of that stadium being a house of horrors. Harbaugh’s answer was short. That would probably be a good question for the players, and he does not have anything to say about it. Last week, he said the team does not care about the streak. This week, he did not even want to talk about it. That is a coach who refuses to give that history any power.

Now, let’s talk trades, because the deadline is on Tuesday. A reporter asked if Harbaugh prefers to get a trade done early, say Monday or Tuesday, so the new player has the rest of the week to prepare. Harbaugh said that is a great point. You would rather make a trade as early as you can, because you have the player longer to prepare for the next game. But you do not always have that luxury.

And that matters a lot this year, because the Giants play Washington on Thursday night, just two days after the deadline. A player traded on Tuesday would have almost no time to learn the system before that game. That is one more reason I think, if the Giants are going to make a move, they would love to make it before Sunday.

And Jonathan Taylor himself spoke this week. He said the trade rumors have been dragging for a while, and at this point, it just becomes noise. He said no one wants uncertainty, and that clarity should come by Tuesday. He confirmed he has not requested a trade, and that he is aligned with Colts coach Shane Steichen. And SNY’s Connor Hughes reported that NFL sources disagree on whether the Colts will trade him at all, with some believing the asking price is so steep that Indianapolis may not really want to deal him.

That lines up with what I have been saying. I do not think Taylor will be a Giant by Tuesday. I still think the Giants are more likely to add a smaller piece, maybe a defensive tackle like Christian Barmore, who Albert Breer said is available for something around a fourth-round pick.

And there was one more answer about contracts that connects to Greg Newsome. A reporter asked if the Giants have a policy about negotiating contracts during the season. Harbaugh said no, there is no policy. It goes back to turning over every stone. He said the Giants believe they can do two things at once. If it becomes a distraction, they do not do it. If it is not a distraction, they do it. He said the bye week was a good opportunity, and that is exactly when the Giants got Newsome’s three-year, 46 million dollar extension done. And he said the Giants do not want to close the door on any opportunity, at any point, just because of a deadline. There is no deadline, he said, on the opportunity to make yourself better.

So, maybe there are more extensions coming. Deonte Banks has turned his career around. Others could be next. Harbaugh just told you the door is never closed.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. Number one, how much of the playbook the Giants open up for McCarthy now that he is caught up. More motion, more audibles, more shots downfield. Number two, how the defense handles Joe Milton, especially keeping him in the pocket. Number three, Saquon Barkley, and whether the Giants keep him from breaking a big one. Number four, whether Skattebo plays, and how many carries Singletary gets. And number five, whether the Giants make a move before the deadline.

And here is my prediction, and I am standing by it. The Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. I think McCarthy throws his first touchdown pass as a Giant, and I think the pass rush forces Milton into at least one big mistake. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, with a quarterback who is finally fully caught up, a head coach who refuses to let anyone get complacent, and a division lead they are treating like it does not exist.
