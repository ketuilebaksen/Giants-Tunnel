Today in Houston, two trades collide. On one side, the Giants’ trade for J.J. McCarthy, a deal that cost them only a 2027 fifth-round pick on September 28th, and a quarterback who now makes his first start for New York. On the other side, the massive trade Houston just made for Marvin Harrison Jr., the former top-five pick who was playing against the Giants at MetLife only weeks ago, and is now lining up for the Texans. And there is a third trade hanging over this game, because the deal the Giants made in 2025 to move back into the first round for Jackson Dart was with these same Houston Texans. Folks, everybody is saying the Giants are the team that has to survive today. Stroud is coming off 312 yards and three touchdowns. Will Anderson Jr. leads the entire NFL in sacks. But when you go through the numbers, the Giants have a huge opportunity sitting right in front of them, and it has been building for twenty years. Today, I am going to break down that opportunity, every key number for this game, and why this could be the afternoon McCarthy’s trade becomes the steal of the season. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with the opportunity that almost nobody is talking about. The Giants and Texans have played six times in the regular season, and the Giants lead the series 5 to 1. The Giants have won five straight against Houston, going all the way back to 2006. The most recent meeting was week 10 of the 2022 season at MetLife Stadium, and the Giants won that one 24 to 16. Now, I know history does not catch passes or sack anybody. But here is why it matters today. If the Giants win, it becomes their longest winning streak against a team outside the division since they beat the Rams seven straight times from 2002 to 2016. So, this is not just a random road trip to Houston. There is a real streak on the line. Kickoff is 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX.

And think about that 2022 win for a second. That was a Giants team nobody expected much from either. New head coach, a roster people called a rebuild, and yet the win over Houston pushed them to 7 and 2. That team went on to make the playoffs and win a playoff game in Minnesota. Sound familiar? A new head coach, a team nobody believed in, stacking wins at the midpoint of the season while the experts waited for the collapse. The 2026 Giants are not that team, and this roster is built very differently, but the parallel is hard to miss. And this year’s version is sitting in first place in its division.

And it all starts with the quarterback. In week six against the Saints, McCarthy threw for 243 yards and a touchdown in his relief appearance, finished with a 90.0 passer rating, and ran for 26 yards. Honestly, that 90.0 rating tells me there is still another level for him, and I mean that in a good way. Remember, he threw an interception on his first drive and still finished at 90. And those 26 rushing yards matter, because a defense that has to worry about the quarterback pulling the ball down gets a lot less comfortable. Now he has the full playbook. Matt Nagy said this week that McCarthy had about 50 percent of the offense against Washington, about 90 percent against the Saints, and now he has all of it.

But here is the challenge, and it is a big one. The Texans have two edge rushers who can take over a game. Will Anderson Jr. leads the NFL with 7.5 sacks this season. He has at least half a sack in five of his last six games, and a tackle for loss in four of his last five at home. And on the other side, Danielle Hunter ranks third in the NFL with 117.5 sacks since 2015. He has half a sack or more in four of his last five games. And here is a fun fact that is not so fun for Giants fans. The last time Hunter played the Giants, on December 24th, 2022, with Minnesota, he had seven tackles, two sacks, and a forced fumble.

So, this is the test for McCarthy and the offensive line. Andrew Thomas, Jermaine Eluemunor, and Marcus Mbow all got back to practice as limited participants this week, so check the final inactives, because the left tackle spot matters a lot against this pass rush. And if Thomas cannot go the full game, rookie J.C. Davis, who did not allow a single pressure in 25 snaps against the Saints, might be the guy dealing with one of the best edge rushers in football. So, how do the Giants handle two edge rushers like that? You cannot block them one-on-one all afternoon and hope for the best. You chip them with tight ends and running backs before they release into routes. You use the quick game, so the ball is out before the rush gets home. You move the pocket with bootlegs and rollouts, ideally away from whichever side Anderson is lined up on. And you make them respect the run, because edge rushers who are pinning their ears back on every snap are vulnerable to draws, screens, and quarterback keepers. That is where McCarthy’s legs could be a real weapon. If Anderson and Hunter crash hard upfield, a quarterback who can step up and take off turns their aggression against them. If McCarthy goes into Houston and plays clean football against the team that leads the league in sacks, that is a statement game. That is how a quarterback starts earning real trust from a fanbase.

The good news is who McCarthy gets to throw to. Malik Nabers had 12 catches for 165 yards against the Saints to lead the team, and that was only his 25th career game. He became just the third player in NFL history with 10 or more receptions in five of his first 25 career games. He is up to 168 career receptions, the second-most through a player’s first 25 games in NFL history, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. with 176. He has a touchdown catch in two of his last three games. And now he is going for his fourth straight game with at least 110 receiving yards. When you are a quarterback trying to find your rhythm, having Nabers on the field is about the best security blanket you could ask for.

And it is not just Nabers. Isaiah Likely is turning into a real problem for defenses. Against the Saints, he caught six passes for 99 yards and a touchdown, including that 24-yarder from McCarthy. Now he is going for his third straight game with 90-plus receiving yards and a touchdown catch, and his fourth straight with six or more receptions. A defense can lean toward Nabers all day, but then Likely is sitting there over the middle, ready to punish you. For McCarthy, that is huge. When the first read is covered, he has a second option who has been producing every single week. And against a pass rush like Houston’s, a tight end who can win quickly over the middle is exactly the kind of answer a young quarterback needs.

Now, the backfield. Cam Skattebo had 71 scrimmage yards in week five, 59 rushing and 12 receiving, and he has 65-plus scrimmage yards in three of his last four. He did not get an offensive touch against the Saints, so today is a chance to rebuild trust, and ball security is the first step. Tyrone Tracy Jr. had a season-high two touchdowns against the Saints, one rushing and one receiving, and graded as the best pass protector on the team. Against Anderson and Hunter, Tracy’s blitz pickup might be one of the most important things nobody sees on the highlight reel. And Devin Singletary is there on third downs.

On the other side, the Texans’ lead back, Woody Marks, had 81 scrimmage yards last week, but only 56 of them came on the ground. He is going for his third straight game with 80-plus scrimmage yards. That is solid, but it is not a run game that should scare a defense. And after the Saints ran for 175 yards against the Giants, this is the week the run defense has to answer Dennard Wilson’s challenge about running to the ball.

And that is where one Giant has a little extra motivation today. D.J. Reader. He had seven tackles and a tackle for loss against the Saints, and he is going for his third straight game with five or more tackles. But here is what stands out. Reader spent 2016 through 2019 in Houston. In 61 career games for the Texans, he had 154 tackles, 6.5 sacks, and 16 tackles for loss. He knows that building, he knows that organization, and I would not be surprised if he plays with an extra edge today. When Reader is on the field this season, the Giants allow around 4.5 yards per carry. When he is off, it jumps to 5.2. Keep him on the field, and this run defense has a chance.

Behind him, Jevon Holland has been one of the best ball hawks in football. He tied for the team lead with nine tackles against the Saints, with a tackle for loss and a pass defended, and he is tied for first in the NFL with three interceptions this season. But remember, he was limited this week while working through the concussion protocol. If he is cleared, he is a game-changer against Stroud. If he is not, that is a big hole in the secondary. That is one of the first names I will be checking on the inactives list.

And then there are the young guns. Abdul Carter is going for his third straight road game with a sack. Road games are where pass rushers have to show up, and Carter has. Arvell Reese had five tackles and a fumble recovery against the Saints. He has nine or more tackles and a tackle for loss in three of his last four games, and he ranks fourth among all rookies with 47 tackles. That tells you he has been around the ball constantly. These two have to step up today, because Houston has two edge rushers who can take over a game, and the Giants cannot let that pressure be one-sided. If Carter, Reese, Kayvon Thibodeaux, and Trace Ford can bring heat of their own, the Giants can fight fire with fire.

Now, the matchup I am watching closest. Deonte Banks against Nico Collins. Banks had a pass defended against the Saints, and he is going for his fifth straight game with at least one. He is tied for second in the league with eight passes defended this season. That is a corner playing with confidence, and remember, this is the same Deonte Banks people called a bust before his 98-yard pick six earlier this season. Nico Collins had 74 receiving yards last week and is going for his 14th straight game with 55-plus scrimmage yards. He has 75-plus scrimmage yards and a touchdown catch in three of his last four home games. And he caught a touchdown in the last meeting between these two teams, back in 2022. That is a real test for Banks.

And it does not stop with Collins. Jaylin Noel led Houston with a career-high 107 receiving yards and a touchdown last week, and he is going for his third straight game with a receiving touchdown. Foster Moreau, the tight end, had a career-high two touchdown catches. Add Marvin Harrison Jr., who had three catches for 34 yards in his Texans debut, all of them for first downs, and Tank Dell, who is returning from his serious knee injury, and Houston has weapons everywhere. That is why I liked what Jason Pinnock did against the Saints, with a pass defended and his first forced fumble of the season. The entire secondary has to be disruptive, not just Banks, because Houston is not going to throw to one guy. And with Greg Newsome II and Colton Hood both dealing with concussions, and Paulson Adebo still working his way back from injured reserve, depth is going to be tested.

Which brings me to C.J. Stroud. And there is a storyline here that Big Ten fans are going to love. Stroud played his college ball at Ohio State. McCarthy played at Michigan. In 2022, in the biggest rivalry game in college football, McCarthy’s Michigan went into Columbus and beat Stroud’s Ohio State 45 to 23. Now, four years later, they meet again, this time in the NFL, with a first-place team on the line. I guarantee Stroud has not forgotten that afternoon. And I guarantee McCarthy is not thinking about it at all, because he has enough to worry about with Anderson and Hunter coming off the edge. But it adds a little spice to an already great matchup.

So, back to Stroud’s numbers. Last week, he completed 24 of 34 passes, 70.6 percent, for 312 yards and three touchdowns, with a 116.3 rating. He is going for his fourth straight game with a completion percentage of 65 or higher, 260-plus passing yards, a touchdown pass, and a rating over 105. And in his 16 career starts against NFC teams, he has 3,993 passing yards, 249.6 per game, and 22 touchdown passes. I am not going to sit here and tell you stopping him is simple. It is not. The Giants have to make him uncomfortable early, mix coverages, and disguise their pressure, because a quarterback that hot will carve up a predictable defense.

And Houston’s defense is not just the two edge rushers. Safety Calen Bullock had five tackles, two passes defended, and his first interception of the season last week. Cornerbacks Kamari Lassiter and Derek Stingley Jr. both made plays. Linebacker Azeez Al-Shaair has seven or more tackles in five straight games. And Henry To’oTo’o had six tackles, a tackle for loss, and a pass defended. This is a defense with playmakers at every level, coached by a defensive head coach in DeMeco Ryans. McCarthy will see disguised looks all afternoon.

And do not sleep on special teams in a game like this. Houston just won in London on a 49-yard field goal by Ka’imi Fairbairn as time expired. They know how to win close games late. On the Giants’ side, Dominic Zvada bounced back from his rough day in the mud in Washington with a clutch 41-yarder against the Saints. Jordan Stout has been flipping the field all season, and against an offense as dangerous as Houston’s, every extra ten yards of field position matters. And the return game has to protect the football. The Charlie Jones fumble right before halftime against the Saints started a 17-point swing. One mistake like that on the road, and the building gets loud fast. If this game comes down to the final two minutes, and I think it will, the team that wins the hidden yards probably wins the game.

Now, let’s go back to the trades, because I think that is the story of this game. Houston went out and made a massive move for Marvin Harrison Jr., a player this channel once floated as an idea for the Giants. The Texans paid for a big name to add firepower to an offense that was already second in the league in passing yards. The Giants went the other way. They spent a fifth-round pick on a quarterback the Vikings no longer wanted. If McCarthy outplays expectations today, while Harrison Jr. is still learning a new offense, Joe Schoen’s quiet trade could end up looking better than Houston’s loud one. And if Harrison Jr. has a big day against his old MetLife opponents, well, the Texans will be the ones celebrating their deal. Either way, a trade is going to look good tonight.

And let’s not forget what this team has already shown it can do. The Giants beat the Cowboys 28 to 20 in week one. They beat the Cardinals 36 to 24 in week four. They came back from 14 down in the fourth quarter in Washington to earn a tie. And they came back from 24 down against the Saints to get within one play. This is a young core that keeps making plays on both sides of the ball. A win in Houston keeps them in first place in the NFC East heading into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, with the Eagles missing Jalen Hurts and the Cowboys at 2 and 4.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, McCarthy against Anderson and Hunter. Does he get the ball out on time, and does he use his legs when the pocket breaks down? Number two, Banks against Collins, and how the secondary holds up against all of Houston’s weapons. Number three, Reader against his old team, and whether the run defense answers last week’s 175 yards. And number four, the turnover column. The Giants turned it over four times against the Saints. They cannot do that against Stroud.

I said it yesterday, and I am sticking with it. Giants 24, Texans 21. I think McCarthy throws two touchdowns, Nabers gets his fourth straight 110-yard game, and the defense makes the play that seals it late. Five straight wins over Houston becomes six. You do not get many chances to extend a streak like this against a team people think is going to beat you. That is why I keep calling it an opportunity. We have the history. We have the playmakers. And we have a quarterback with a real chance to prove himself. This team can go all the way.
