The Giants did not even play this weekend, and they just had one of the best Sundays of their entire season. Double dose, folks. First, the Dallas Cowboys lost at home, in overtime, to an Arizona Cardinals team that benched its starting quarterback in the middle of the game. Then, on Sunday night, the Philadelphia Eagles went to Washington and got rolled, 27 to 10. That is six straight losses for Philadelphia, its first six-game losing streak since 2012. So while the Giants sat on their couches during the bye, both of their biggest division rivals lost, and the team that won is still buried in the standings. Look at the standings now. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. The Giants have two losses. Nobody else in the division has fewer than four. And I am going to tell you today why that double dose does more than change the standings. I think it makes a big Giants move before the November 10th deadline more likely. Let’s get into it.

If you are new here, hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now. Which loss made you happier, Dallas or Philadelphia?

Let’s start in Dallas, because that one was wild. The Cardinals came into AT&T Stadium at 3 and 5, and things went bad fast. Arizona fell behind 17 to 3, and in the second half, the Cardinals benched Jacoby Brissett. He had gone 10 of 23 for 119 yards with an interception. In came Gardner Minshew. And Minshew was fantastic. He went 17 of 23 for 188 yards, three touchdowns, and no interceptions. He threw touchdowns to Tyler Allgeier and Trey McBride in the fourth quarter to pull Arizona within three. Dallas kicked a field goal to go back up six. And then, with six seconds left, Minshew found Michael Wilson for a touchdown to tie it.

And here is the part that should have saved Dallas. Arizona kicker Chad Ryland missed the extra point that would have won the game. The Cowboys got a second life. Overtime. A chance to win it at home. And they could not do it. Both defenses traded stops, and then Ryland came back out and hit the game-winning field goal. Cardinals 33, Cowboys 30.

Think about how bad that is for Dallas. You are at home. You are up 17 to 3. The other team benches its starting quarterback. The other team misses a game-winning extra point. And you still lose. That is not bad luck. That is a team that does not know how to close. And it is not the first ugly home loss for this team this season either.

And here is a fun connection for Giants fans. Remember who the Giants beat earlier this season? The Arizona Cardinals, 36 to 24, with Jameis Winston at quarterback. That same Arizona team just walked into Dallas and won. And after the game, the Cardinals named Minshew their starter going forward. So the team the Giants handled is the same team that just exposed the Cowboys at home. That makes the Giants’ win over Arizona look a lot better than it did at the time.

And the questions in Dallas are getting louder. After the loss, Dak Prescott was out there defending his offensive coordinator, Brian Schottenheimer. When your quarterback is defending the play-caller after a home loss to a backup, you know the heat is on. There has also been a lot of national chatter about how much confidence the Cowboys’ organization really has in Prescott. I take some of that with a grain of salt, because the Cowboys are always the loudest story in football. But losses like this have a way of snowballing. Is it the coaching? Is it the quarterback? Is it the defense? Those questions do not go away in one week, and they follow a team into the next game. For a division rival, that is exactly the kind of turmoil you want to see.

Now, let’s go to Sunday night, because the Eagles game might have been even more telling. Philadelphia actually jumped out to a 10 to 0 lead in the first quarter. And then they never scored again. Tanner McKee, making his third start in place of Jalen Hurts, was sacked four times, and Washington recorded nine official quarterback hits. Saquon Barkley ran 13 times for just 30 yards, though he did score a short touchdown. Jake Elliott missed a 41-yard field goal off the upright. And on the other side, Jayden Daniels went 16 of 29 for 217 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions. It was Daniels’ first win since October of 2025. Washington kicker Drew Stevens hit four field goals, including a 56-yarder. Commanders 27, Eagles 10.

Six straight losses. The Eagles are 2 and 6. Let that sink in. This is the team that has owned the NFC East in recent years, the team that won the Super Bowl not long ago. And right now, they cannot score after the first quarter, they cannot protect their quarterback, and their star running back cannot find room to run. And guess who they host next? The New York Giants, on November 8th.

And I have to say something about Saquon Barkley, because I know a lot of you were thinking it. Giants fans watched Barkley leave in 2024, run for over 2,000 yards in Philadelphia that season, and win a Super Bowl. That one hurt. And now, on Sunday night, he had 30 yards on 13 carries behind a line that could not open anything. I am not celebrating a player struggling. Barkley gave this franchise a lot of great years, and he is still a great back. But it tells you how far this Eagles team has fallen. When even Barkley cannot get going, the problem is bigger than one player.

Now, let me give you the full picture of the division, because it is wild. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. Look at the loss column. The Giants have two losses. Washington has four. Dallas has five. Philadelphia has six. That is a three-loss cushion over Dallas and a four-loss cushion over Philadelphia, with ten games to go. And remember, the Giants are unbeaten in the division. They beat Dallas 28 to 20 in week one, and they tied Washington.

And now that tie with Washington looks a lot different. When it happened, it felt like a loss. A lot of us were annoyed by it, me included. But in a tight race, a half game can decide a division. Right now, that half game is part of the Giants’ cushion, not a hole they have to climb out of.

And here is a receipt I need to own. A few videos ago, when the national projections still had Dallas as the favorite to win the division, I said I understood the projection. I said the Giants’ point differential was small, McCarthy only had a couple of games of experience, and Dallas had a lot of offensive talent. I gave the model too much credit. The standings were telling us the truth the whole time. The Giants were the better team, and Dallas just proved it again by losing at home to a backup quarterback.

And the betting markets have noticed. Before the Giants played Houston, one prediction market had the Cowboys at about a 45 percent chance to win the division, with the Giants around 27 percent. After the win in Houston, it moved closer to even. And then, somehow, people talked themselves back into Dallas during the bye week, the way they always seem to. Now, after the loss to Arizona, that same market reportedly has the Giants around 51 percent and the Cowboys around 28 percent. That is a massive swing. I would not treat prediction markets like gospel. But they tell you how people are reading the race, and the consensus has finally caught up to what the standings were saying for weeks.

And I think the market is still too low on the Giants, and here is why. Look at the remaining schedule. The Giants still play Philadelphia twice, Dallas once, and Washington again. Every one of those teams has at least four losses. They also play Jacksonville, Indianapolis, Cleveland, and San Francisco. There are a lot of winnable games on that schedule. And because the Giants hold the lead, every division win does double duty. It adds a win for the Giants and it adds a loss for a direct rival.

The two Eagles games are especially interesting. Philadelphia is playing with a backup quarterback, its offense has stalled, and it is on a six-game skid. That is not the Eagles team that dominated this division. If the Giants take care of business against Philadelphia twice, they will be in an extremely strong position to win the NFC East for the first time since 2011.

But let me be careful here, because I do not want anyone getting complacent, and I know John Harbaugh will not let this team get complacent either. Remember what he said about the streak in Philadelphia. The Giants have lost 13 straight games there, counting the playoffs, and they have not won there since 2013. Harbaugh said, we don’t care about that. Good. But a 2 and 6 team at home, embarrassed on national television, with its season slipping away, is a dangerous team. The Eagles are going to come out swinging. The Giants have to match that energy from the first snap.

Now, let me make the bigger point. Even if you ignore the division and look at the Giants on their own, this is a good team. According to the numbers I have seen, coming into the bye, the Giants ranked in the top 12 in both yards gained and yards allowed per game, with a top-ten defense in yards allowed. Not many teams can say that. Teams that are good on both sides of the ball are usually teams that make the playoffs and have a real shot at a run.

And the comparison with Dallas is interesting. The Cowboys’ offense is not the juggernaut its reputation suggests. Statistically, it is closer to the middle of the pack, and pretty similar to the Giants. And since J.J. McCarthy took over, and with Malik Nabers getting back into the full swing of things, I would argue the Giants’ offense has been a little better. McCarthy has given this offense a steady hand. And when Nabers is fully involved, defenses have to roll coverage toward him, which opens everything else underneath. Then look at the defense. The Giants are top ten in yards allowed. By most accounts, the Cowboys are near the bottom of the league. These teams are not close, no matter what the reputations say.

Of course, there is always that voice in the back of your head. No Brian Burns. No Tremaine Edmunds. No Jaxson Dart. Tyrone Tracy Jr. on injured reserve. Cam Skattebo day-to-day with a hamstring. With that many injuries, can this team really compete with the best teams in the conference? It is a fair question.

And here is how I think that question connects to the trade deadline. If Dallas and Philadelphia were both sitting at 5 and 3, I think the Giants might take a more conservative approach. They might protect their picks and wait for next season, when everyone is healthy. That would not be crazy. The Giants are projected to have a lot of cap space next year, and key starters are coming back. The future looks bright either way.

But that is not the situation. Dallas just lost at home to a backup. Philadelphia has lost six in a row. Washington, even after a big win, is 2 and 4 and 1. This division is sitting there for the Giants to take. And windows do not always stay open as long as you expect. That is why I think this double dose makes a big Giants move more likely, not less.

Think about it from Harbaugh’s point of view. If he was not confident about winning the division, he probably would not push his chips in. But right now, that confidence has to be very high. And we already know he is willing to be aggressive. He personally called the Titans’ owner to try to get Jeffery Simmons. He told reporters the Giants are open for any call. He said the Giants want to add significant players, always. A three-loss lead over Dallas with ten games to go is exactly the kind of situation where a coach like Harbaugh wants to add.

But he also told us the rules, and they still apply. Any deal has to fit a need, fit the budget, and fit the price. It cannot be a one-and-done. If the Giants give up draft capital, they want a short-term win and a long-term win. The Giants do not have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks. They are not loaded with draft capital to burn.

So what does that mean for Jonathan Taylor? The Colts are open to trading him, and the Giants are reportedly one of the favorites. Taylor is under contract through 2028 after signing a two-year extension in August, so he fits Harbaugh’s long-term rule. And the running back room needs help right now, with Tracy out and Skattebo’s hamstring a question mark. With the division looking this good, I think the Giants are more likely to stretch for Taylor than they were a week ago. Not to a crazy price. But a little further than before. That is my read, not a report.

And I still think defensive tackle is a real need. With Dexter Lawrence gone, the Giants need a big body in the middle, especially against teams that want to run on third and short. A veteran defensive tackle in a smaller deal, the kind of price San Francisco paid for DeForest Buckner, would fit perfectly. That is my opinion, not a report.

And here is one more angle that I think people are missing. The other teams in the division are not going to be buyers. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. Dallas is 3 and 5 and full of questions. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. If any of those teams decide to sell before the deadline, the Giants get stronger while their rivals get weaker. And if they hold, they are holding with teams that are already four or more losses deep. Either way, the Giants are the only team in the NFC East that should be thinking about adding right now.

And do not forget what comes right after Philadelphia. Washington, on a short week. And Washington just showed us something on Sunday night. Its defense sacked McKee four times and hit him nine times. Jayden Daniels played a clean game with no interceptions. That is the team the Giants tied 27 to 27 earlier this season, in a game where Winston threw for 326 yards. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1, but it is coming off its best performance of the year, and it will come to New Jersey with confidence. Harbaugh called these two games the most important of the season before the bye. After this weekend, I think that is even more true. Win both, and the Giants would be at least three losses clear of everybody in the division, and five clear of Philadelphia, with eight games to go.

And tiebreakers matter too. The Giants already beat Dallas head to head. A win in Philadelphia would make the Giants 2 and 0 and 1 in the division, with every rival already buried in losses. In a race this lopsided, head-to-head results and division record could decide everything if the Giants hit a rough patch in December. Every division game is worth more than a regular win right now.

Now, let’s get back to Philadelphia, because that is the game that matters most right now. The Eagles’ problems on Sunday night are things the Giants can attack. Washington sacked McKee four times. The Giants have one of the best pass rushes in football, with Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux, and Arvell Reese, and Dennard Wilson loves stacking them on the same side. If Carter is healthy after being sick early this week, that matchup looks very good for the Giants. And Barkley ran for 30 yards on 13 carries. If the Giants’ run defense holds up the same way, the Eagles will be in long-yardage situations all day, with a backup quarterback, against a defense built to rush the passer.

On offense, the Giants want to run the ball. They ran for 188 yards in Houston. If Skattebo’s hamstring is okay, and Singletary keeps running the way he did on that walk-off touchdown, the Giants can control the clock and keep McKee on the sideline. That is the formula. Protect the football, run it, and let the pass rush do the rest.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Giants make a move before November 10th now that the division picture is this good. Number two, Skattebo’s status at the end of the week. Number three, whether Carter is back at full strength. Number four, how the Eagles respond to six straight losses, because desperate teams can be dangerous. And number five, whether Dallas makes any big changes after this loss.

And I will stand by the prediction I made in my last video. I think the Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. And after this weekend, I feel even better about it. You can hold me to that.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, with a three-loss lead over Dallas and a four-loss lead over Philadelphia. The division is theirs to take. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
