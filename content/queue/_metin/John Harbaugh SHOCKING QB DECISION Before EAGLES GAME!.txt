Four different Giants ran for a touchdown in Houston. Tyrone Tracy Jr. Cam Skattebo. Devin Singletary. And Jameis Winston. According to the Associated Press, the Giants had not had four separate players score rushing touchdowns in one game since December 3rd, 1950. And that historic afternoon was built on two decisions John Harbaugh made in a single week, the kind of decisions most head coaches avoid. He pulled Jameis Winston in the middle of a home loss and named J.J. McCarthy his starter, telling reporters, we will be starting J.J., Jameis will be the backup. And he gave Cam Skattebo, his leading rusher, zero carries for an entire game after a costly fumble. Both of those calls went on trial in Houston, against a team that made the playoffs last season. And both of those calls won. Now, with the bye week here and the Eagles next, Harbaugh’s quarterback decision is locked in. McCarthy is his guy. Today, I am going to show you why this upset was no fluke, why the numbers matter more than the score, and the honest warning signs that come with it. Let’s get into it.

Which Harbaugh decision was bigger, McCarthy or Skattebo?

First, a receipt, because I owe you one. Before the game, I predicted the Giants would win 24 to 21, and I said McCarthy would throw two touchdowns. I got the win right. I got the Texans’ 21 right. But McCarthy threw zero touchdown passes. Instead, the Giants scored four rushing touchdowns. I also told you Will Anderson Jr. did not record a sack or a quarterback hit, which is true, but I want to be complete. McCarthy was sacked three times by the rest of that Houston defense. So, the day was not perfect. As Harbaugh said afterward, McCarthy sure did not play perfect, but he played a heck of a football game.

Now, let’s look at the numbers, because they tell you this was not luck. The Giants outgained Houston 420 total yards to 237. That is a difference of 183 yards, on the road. They ran for 188 yards on 34 carries, against a Texans run defense that came into the week ranked third in the league, allowing only 83 rushing yards per game. The defense forced four three-and-outs, including Houston’s first possession of overtime. And the Texans managed only 84 rushing yards. C.J. Stroud, who came in second in the NFL in passing yards, finished 17 of 28 for only 153 yards and two touchdowns, with his full group of receivers available, including Tank Dell in his first game since December 2024. A fluke winner does not win the yardage battle by 183 yards on the road. A lucky team does not run for 188 yards on a top-three run defense. Harbaugh said it himself. That was the best game the Giants have played all year.

But let me give the skeptics their due, because they have a real point. Through seven games, the Giants have scored 163 points and allowed 161. That is a point differential of plus two. That is the profile of a coin-flip team. A 4 and 2 and 1 record built on a two-point margin usually means a team has lived on luck, and a skeptic has every right to say so.

But let me add some context that the skeptics usually leave out. A big chunk of that number comes from one game. The 28 to 6 loss to the Rams in week two, the game where Jackson Dart went down on his seventh snap. That is a 22-point loss in a game where the Giants lost their starting quarterback almost immediately. Take that game out, and the Giants have outscored their opponents by 24 points over the other six. That does not make the skeptics wrong. Every game counts. But it does tell you this team has been more competitive than one number suggests.

And honestly, this game should never have gone to overtime. Rookie kicker Dominic Zvada missed two first-half field goals, including one from 44 yards in perfect indoor conditions, and the coaches never sent him back out after halftime. Add those six points back, assume nothing else changes, and regulation ends with the Giants ahead 27 to 21. Here is the number that worries me. Zvada made his first nine field goal attempts of the season. Since then, he has made only four of nine. That started in the mud in Washington, where he missed three. He bounced back with a clutch 41-yarder against the Saints. But now this. Harbaugh trusted him after Washington, and I agreed with that. But a contender cannot keep leaving points on the field. Zvada’s confidence is something the coaches have to fix during the bye, because in a close division race, a missed kick can decide the season.

And remember, Harbaugh knows kickers better than almost any coach in the league. In Baltimore, he had Justin Tucker, an undrafted rookie who beat out a veteran in training camp in 2012 and became one of the most accurate kickers in NFL history. Harbaugh has shown he will trust a young, undrafted kicker. He has also shown he will make a change when he has to. Zvada already has a franchise record as the first Giants rookie to make three field goals of 50 or more yards in a season. The talent is there. The question is whether the bye can get his mind right before Philadelphia. I would not be surprised to see the Giants bring in a kicker or two for workouts, just to keep the pressure on. That is not a lack of faith. That is how a contender handles a slump.

Now, to understand how big Sunday was, you have to remember how close this season came to falling apart. In week one, Jackson Dart threw for 230 yards and three touchdowns in a 28 to 20 win over Dallas. The Harbaugh plan looked ahead of schedule. Eight days later, everything changed. On his seventh snap against the Rams, Dart took a hit from two defenders and suffered a season-ending knee injury, and the Giants lost 28 to 6. The franchise quarterback was gone before October even arrived.

Jameis Winston stepped in and steadied the season. He beat Tennessee 12 to 7 in week three. He beat Arizona 36 to 24 in week four. He threw for 326 yards in the 27 to 27 overtime tie in Washington. And in that game, Skattebo lost a fumble in overtime with the Giants driving toward field goal range, and a likely win turned into a tie. Skattebo said later that taking care of the ball was not in his mind at that moment. Winston finished his run as the starter at 2 and 1 and 1, and he kept the Giants on top of a weak division.

Then came the Saints in week six, and the bottom almost fell out. Winston threw two interceptions. New Orleans built a 34 to 10 lead. Harbaugh pulled his starter with under 12 minutes left, and McCarthy came in and nearly erased the whole deficit before the Giants lost 34 to 27. And Skattebo, the team’s leading rusher, finished that game with zero carries. Harbaugh insisted it was not by design. But Skattebo admitted he knew he was being punished. The fallout from that loss forced the two decisions that won Sunday’s game.

Let’s start with the quarterback decision. Harbaugh said the front office started working the phones as soon as Dart went down. And on September 28th, the Giants sent a 2027 fifth-round pick to Minnesota for McCarthy, the tenth overall pick of the 2024 draft. By then, the Vikings had buried him at third string, behind Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz. His only season of real action in Minnesota produced 11 touchdowns and 12 interceptions across 10 starts. A former top-ten pick turned into a fifth-round discount, and the move barely made national headlines.

And part of the bet was personal. John’s brother, Jim Harbaugh, coached McCarthy at Michigan, where he won a national championship. So, the Giants knew the person before they trusted the stat sheet. And John Harbaugh said something about McCarthy that I think explains this whole story. Guys have scars. He has scars. He has been wounded. He has been broken. He has been battered. But you heal stronger, and you become better because of it.

Then McCarthy’s Giants debut came in relief against the Saints, where he threw for 243 yards in the fourth quarter alone. According to ESPN research, only one quarterback who did not start a game has thrown for more yards in a fourth quarter over the past 25 seasons. Three days later, Harbaugh named him the starter over a veteran who had kept the season afloat. That is the decision a lot of coaches would never make. And in Houston, McCarthy completed 20 of 28 passes for 252 yards, with no interceptions and no fumbles. He took three sacks, but he never put the ball in danger. After a week where the Giants turned it over four times against the Saints, that is exactly what Harbaugh needed. A quarterback who protects the football.

And here is a twist that makes this even better. Jameis Winston, the quarterback Harbaugh benched, was one of the four Giants who ran for a touchdown in Houston. Think about that. The veteran who lost his job did not sulk. He came in when his number was called and found the end zone. That tells you everything about the culture in that quarterback room, and why this change did not break the locker room.

Now, the second decision. Skattebo. One week after zero carries, he ran 17 times for a career-high 124 yards, his first 100-yard game as a pro. He scored on a 16-yard run with 8:55 left in regulation. And he broke off a 42-yard run in overtime to set up the winner. He owned the fourth quarter and the extra period, and he ran like a player with something to prove.

He also drew an unnecessary roughness flag for lowering his helmet into safety M.J. Stewart on a run, and he refused to back down afterward. He said, if I am going to keep getting flags or fines for it, I guess I am going to play for free. That is Skattebo. And Harbaugh, the coach who benched him a week earlier, said his back played with a fierce joy on Sunday. And then he explained what he meant. There is a fierce joy in football. That is what football is. It is not happy joy. It is a fierce joy. Folks, I love that line. That is the identity Harbaugh wants for this entire team.

But the case against is real too, and I will not hide it. According to ESPN, Skattebo fumbled five times in his first 13 career games. That is a lot. And the Washington tie still stings. The physical style that makes him great also puts the football at risk. Harbaugh’s message last week was about protecting the ball first. Skattebo answered in Houston. Now he has to keep answering every week.

And then there is the man who finished it. Devin Singletary actually played for the Texans in 2023. And in overtime, he broke a 30-yard touchdown run to beat his former team. After the game, a conversation from earlier in the season came out. Singletary had been waiting for his chance, and he asked what more he could do. The answer he got was that there was nothing more he could do, that he was doing everything right, and that his time would come. And look where it came. Right there, in overtime, in Houston. If you want a picture of what this team is about, that is it.

Now, before you judge this win, look at who did not play in it. Brian Burns, the best player on the defense, tore his ACL and landed on injured reserve in late September. And then, the day before kickoff, middle linebacker Tremaine Edmunds, a free agent signing who had played every defensive snap this season and called the signals, went on season-ending injured reserve with his shoulder injury. According to The Athletic, two of the three most important pieces of the Giants’ front seven are gone for the year. Plenty of teams fold under losses like that.

And the secondary took hits too. Jevon Holland, the NFC Defensive Player of the Month for September after three interceptions in the first three games, sat out with a concussion. Greg Newsome II missed the game with a concussion of his own. Tight end Theo Johnson stayed home with a neck injury. And during the game, Tracy left in the first half with a hamstring injury, and Skattebo said he felt his hamstring grab on his biggest run of the day.

Somebody had to fill those holes, and the answers came from deeper on the depth chart. Micah McFadden replaced Edmunds and led the team with 10 solo tackles, two tackles for loss, and a quarterback hit. Remember, just a few days earlier, Harbaugh said the Giants have three starters at linebacker, and if McFadden was needed, he was a starter and would play really well. He was right. And Paulson Adebo, activated off injured reserve after missing every game since the opener, started at corner and avoided the big mistake. That is a defense held together by next-man-up football.

But let me be honest about what the Edmunds injury means long term. A middle linebacker who calls the signals is the quarterback of the defense. He gets everybody lined up, makes the checks against motion and shifts, and fixes problems before the snap. Harbaugh said earlier this month that gap integrity is a constant chase, because offenses use motions, shifts, and inserts to create confusion. Losing the player who organizes all of that is a real blow. McFadden stepped in beautifully in Houston. But one game is one game. And that is why I keep coming back to the trade deadline. With Burns and Edmunds both out for the season, a veteran defender who can help organize the back end, someone like the Budda Baker type we talked about, would make even more sense now.

And the young core showed up too. Abdul Carter, the third overall pick in 2025, did not record a sack, but Pro Football Focus credited him with six pressures, and he drew holding penalties on Houston’s blockers. And Harbaugh said the second-year edge rusher took command of the locker room at halftime. That is a leadership moment you cannot measure. Rookie receiver Malachi Fields caught four passes for 83 yards, including a 47-yard catch and run on an out route. And rookie linebacker Arvell Reese helped force Stroud out of bounds on a first-quarter third down, forcing a punt. Zoom out, and the core of this roster looks younger and hungrier than it has in years.

So, what does all of this mean for the decision going forward? It means Harbaugh’s quarterback call is not an experiment anymore. McCarthy is the starter coming out of the bye. He protected the football, he made the throws when he needed to, and the offense around him ran for 188 yards. And the backfield decision is settled too. Skattebo earned his role back. Singletary proved he can close games. And Tracy, when healthy, has been one of the most complete backs on the roster.

And here is the question this win dragged into the open. 2027. Jackson Dart is still the young quarterback this franchise traded back into the first round to draft, and he will be back next year. But if McCarthy keeps doing this, the Giants will have a real decision to make. Two young first-round quarterbacks, both 23, both mobile, both able to win games. That is a question nobody expected to be asking in October. It is a good problem. But it is a problem.

Now, the road ahead. The Giants are on their bye week. Then they go to Philadelphia, to face an Eagles team that is expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the season and just lost 38 to 21 to Dallas on Monday night. Then they come home to face Washington. Two division games, back to back, with first place on the line. And after that, the rest of the schedule, including teams like the Chargers, will test whether this is a real contender or a team that got hot for a week.

And the bye could not come at a better time. Tracy’s hamstring. Skattebo’s hamstring. Jermaine Eluemunor’s calf, which had him in a walking boot. Holland and Newsome in the concussion protocol. Theo Johnson’s neck. And the Giants have to figure out the middle of the defense without Edmunds for the rest of the season. Two weeks of rest and preparation might be the most important stretch of this entire season.

So, here is what I will be watching. Number one, Zvada. Does he get his confidence back during the bye, or do the Giants bring in competition? Number two, the hamstrings. Tracy and Skattebo are a big part of this run game, and the Giants need both healthy in Philadelphia. Number three, the linebacker room. McFadden was great in Houston, but can he call the signals and hold up for the rest of the season? And number four, whether the Giants add help at the trade deadline, because with Edmunds and Burns gone, the defense could use a veteran.

The scoreboard says 4 and 2 and 1. The point differential says plus two. But the way they won in Houston says this team is better than the skeptics think. Harbaugh made two hard decisions in one week, and both of them won. He called it the best game his team has played all year. I agree. And I believe the best is still ahead. This team can go all the way.
