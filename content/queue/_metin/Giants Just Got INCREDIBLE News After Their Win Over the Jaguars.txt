The Giants just lost a starting cornerback for at least a month, and somehow the story coming out of East Rutherford this week is good news. Greg Newsome II is headed to injured reserve with a high ankle sprain. That hurts. But the man who replaced him against Jacksonville walked onto the field cold, picked off Trevor Lawrence, and broke up a career-high four passes. And then the Giants made a practice squad move that looks strange at first glance, until you notice where that player spent his summer. Indianapolis. The very team the Giants play on Sunday. So today I am going to show you why a bad injury turned into some of the best news this team has had all season, and why the most interesting roster move of the week might have nothing to do with cornerback at all.

Let me start with the bad part, because you deserve to know exactly where things stand.

The Giants officially placed Greg Newsome II on injured reserve after he suffered a high ankle sprain against the Jaguars. According to Jordan Schultz, Newsome is set to undergo tightrope surgery and is expected to miss four to six weeks, possibly longer. Being on IR means he has to sit at least four games no matter what, even if the recovery goes faster than expected.

Now, a quick word on that surgery, because a lot of fans hear the word surgery and panic. Tightrope surgery for a high ankle sprain is a procedure teams use more and more these days, because it can stabilize the ankle and help a player get back sooner than if they just let it heal on its own. That is why the timeline is four to six weeks and not the whole season. So yes, this is bad news. But it is not the worst-case version of bad news.

Let's do the math on the calendar. The Giants are 6-3-1. There are seven games left in the regular season, starting with the Colts. Four to six weeks puts Newsome's return somewhere around the final two or three weeks of the regular season, and that is if everything goes well. Possibly longer, Schultz said. So there is a real chance we do not see him again until the very end of the season, or until the playoffs, if the Giants get there. And here is what I want to say about that: do not rush him. I would much rather have a healthy Newsome in January than a limping Newsome in December. A high ankle sprain that gets rushed can turn into a nagging problem for months.

And let's not forget how important Newsome has been. He has been a big part of why this secondary went from a question mark to a strength. The Giants already gave him a three-year extension, which tells you how much they think of him. This is not a depth piece. This is a starter. And losing a starter in the secondary, right before the hardest part of the schedule, is a real hit.

But this is where the story flips. Because the Giants did not just lose a starter. They found out exactly what they have behind him.

So let's talk about the incredible news, and there are three parts to it.

Incredible news number one: Paulson Adebo is back.

When Newsome went down early in the Jacksonville game, Adebo had to step in with no warning. No full week of first-team reps. No game plan built around him. Just, you are in, go cover. And against one of the best passing offenses in the AFC, Adebo came away with an interception and a career-high four passes defended.

Think about that. Trevor Lawrence threw the ball 47 times in that game. He went looking for the matchup he thought he could win. And a lot of the time, the matchup he went after was the guy who just came off the bench. And that guy won. Adebo got his first interception of the season in the fourth quarter, the kind of play that keeps a two-score lead a two-score lead.

And this matters for Adebo personally too. A lot of Giants fans had started to lose faith in him. He has had injuries. He has had ups and downs. Back in Week 2, the Giants had to put him on injured reserve, and there were people who wrote him off right then. Now, on the biggest stage of the season so far, he showed up. In my opinion, that performance can do a lot to win back the fans who had started questioning him.

But here is my challenge for Adebo, and I mean this with love. One great game is a great game. Two is a trend. Three is who you are. Sunday in Indianapolis is his chance to show this was not a one-time thing. The coaches clearly trust him, or they would have gone out and signed another cornerback this week. And they did not. That says a lot.

Incredible news number two: the secondary has become a strength, and the coaching staff deserves the credit.

Go back to the preseason. Think about the conversations we were having. People said the cornerback room did not have enough quality. They said there was not enough dependable depth. The secondary was supposed to be one of the weakest parts of this roster. Some people had it as the weakest.

Now look at it. Against Jacksonville, the Giants had 14 pass breakups as a team. Fourteen. They intercepted Trevor Lawrence twice. Dru Phillips jumped a route on fourth-and-one to save points in the red zone. And a backup cornerback filled in for a starter and played like a starter. A position group everybody was worried about in August is now one of the reasons people believe in this defense in November.

That is coaching. Defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson, John Harbaugh and the defensive backs coaches have gotten more out of this group than almost anybody expected. And it is not just the individual plays. It is the system. When one guy goes down, the defense does not have to change how it plays. The next guy steps in, plays the same technique, and the scheme holds up. That is what good teams do.

And look at the long-term picture. Newsome is locked in on a three-year extension. Deonte Banks has given this team plenty of reasons to believe he can be a big part of this defense going forward. My opinion, not a report: I would love to see the Giants find a way to keep Banks around too. When you have three or four cornerbacks you can trust, you can play any kind of coverage you want, and you can survive an injury like this one without panic.

And there is one more name I do not think we talk about enough: Korie Black. He has been on injured reserve since the beginning of the season, but the Giants designated him to return when they placed him there. Black made a nice impression this summer. He broke up four passes in the preseason before he got hurt, and he looked like a guy with a real shot at making the roster. Now, with Newsome out, this might be the opening for him to get back into the mix, if he is healthy enough and the Giants can make room. I am not saying he walks in and plays like Newsome. That would be asking way too much from a player who has not had a chance to play this season. But as depth behind Adebo, Banks and Phillips? That could matter a lot over the final stretch.

And here is a big reason the Giants might have felt comfortable not bringing in an outside cornerback. Between Adebo, Banks, Phillips and the possible return of Black, they believe they already have the answers in the building. I like that confidence. I just hope it is justified, because the schedule after Indianapolis gets a lot harder.

Incredible news number three: the Vandermark move, and the Indianapolis connection.

So here is the roster move that had a lot of fans scratching their heads.

First, the Giants signed Dawuane Smoot from the practice squad to the active roster. I have no problem with that at all. Smoot is a veteran edge rusher, and the Giants had already elevated him for three straight games. He has been contributing when he gets the chance, and with all the bumps and bruises on this defense, keeping him around makes sense. And with Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux and the rest of that front getting after the quarterback, a veteran rotation piece who knows how to set an edge is exactly what you want for the stretch run.

But then came the practice squad move. You might expect the Giants to use that open spot on another cornerback after losing Newsome. They did not. Instead, they brought back Vandermark, an interior offensive lineman who was already with the organization earlier this season. With his return, the Giants have five offensive linemen on the practice squad. That is a lot of spots dedicated to the offensive line, at a time when there might be other places where depth would be useful.

So why do it? I see two possible reasons.

Reason one is the simple one. Maybe the Giants just like him. Vandermark first joined the Giants in early September, right around the time there were questions at center. Brian Hudson eventually took over the starting job from John Michael Schmitz Jr., and Vandermark was a guy who could potentially give them interior depth. The Giants let him go, and now he is back. Sometimes a young player is not ready to help you on Sundays yet, but the coaches see something in his size and his tools and want to keep working with him. I am not saying he is going to take over at center anytime soon. But keeping him on the practice squad lets the staff keep developing him, and I do not think you give up on a young lineman just because he is not ready yet.

Reason two is where it gets interesting. Before he joined the Giants, Vandermark signed with the Indianapolis Colts as an undrafted free agent and spent the summer with them. He went through their camp. He was in their meetings. He was around their offense. And who do the Giants play on Sunday? The Indianapolis Colts.

Now add the quarterback situation. Colts head coach Shane Steichen has officially named Anthony Richardson the starter. Richardson has not started a regular season game since Week 16 of the 2024 season. So there is not much recent game film of him running this offense with this group of players. The Giants' coaches can watch old tape all week, but it is not the same as knowing what Indianapolis worked on this summer with Richardson in the building.

My opinion, not a report: I think it is entirely possible the Giants could pick up a little useful information from Vandermark about how Indianapolis likes to do things. Not the whole playbook. Not every answer for Sunday. Offenses change during the season, and a practice squad lineman is not going to hand over the game plan. But even small things, like how the Colts like to run certain looks with Richardson, or what they emphasized in camp, can help a coaching staff know what to look for during preparation.

And look, this happens all over the league. Teams sign players who were just with their next opponent all the time. And knowing what kind of coach John Harbaugh is, I would not be surprised if he is willing to explore any reasonable edge to get his team ready. He is one of the most detail-oriented coaches in football.

But let me be very clear, because this is the kind of thing that gets twisted online. I do not know if the Indianapolis connection had anything to do with this signing. Nobody has reported that. It could be a straight football decision about a young offensive lineman the Giants already know and like. I just find the timing interesting, and I think it is worth talking about.

And there is one more way to look at the offensive line side of this. Five linemen on the practice squad tells you the Giants are thinking about the long haul. Offensive linemen get banged up in December. One sprained knee, one bad ankle, and suddenly your backup center is starting on a Sunday night in the cold. We have watched enough Giants football over the years to know what happens when the depth up front runs out. The quarterback gets hit, the run game stalls, and the whole season goes sideways. So even if the Indianapolis angle turns out to be nothing, stacking bodies on the offensive line is not a bad idea for a team that wants to play deep into January. Protecting J.J. McCarthy, and eventually Jaxson Dart when he is ready, is the most important thing this offense can do.

And think about what the Giants are doing as an organization here. They are not panicking. They lost a starting cornerback, and instead of grabbing the first veteran they could find off the street, they trusted their own guys and used their spots on players they believe fit for the long run. That is the kind of patience this franchise did not always have. My opinion, not a report: that patience is a sign of a front office and a coaching staff that are on the same page.

Now, let's talk about the game, because this is the part that worries me the most.

On paper, the Giants are the better team. The Colts are 3-8. They lost 26-3 to Houston. They waived Daniel Jones the next day. And they are turning to a quarterback who has barely played in almost two years. But this is exactly the kind of game where a good team can get itself in trouble.

Here is why. Indianapolis played on Thursday night, so by Sunday, the Colts will have had 10 days between games. That is a lot of time to install a new plan around Richardson. Meanwhile, the Giants are coming off an emotional win over one of the best teams in the AFC. That is a classic letdown spot. And when a struggling team changes quarterbacks, it sometimes gets a little extra energy, even if the change does not fix all of its problems. A new quarterback, a fresh week of practice, a locker room that wants to prove something. Do not sleep on that.

And Richardson is not a normal quarterback to prepare for. He has a huge arm. He can run. He can turn a broken play into a big gain. The Giants' pass rush has to stay disciplined. You cannot just pin your ears back and run past him. Collapse the pocket, keep him in front of you, and make him beat you with his arm from inside the pocket.

Some fans point to the loss to Washington as an example of the Giants losing a game they should have won. But Washington has beaten Seattle and Cincinnati this season. That is a team capable of playing good football when things are working. Losing to Washington and overlooking a 3-8 Colts team are two very different things. This is a game the Giants should win, and they have to play like it.

For me, this is another test of the culture John Harbaugh has built. Good teams do not spend the week celebrating the last win. They take what worked, fix what did not, and get ready for the next opponent. If the Giants go into Indianapolis and take care of business, that tells you this is a team that is built for the stretch run.

So here are my keys for Sunday.

One: Adebo has to back it up. Indianapolis is going to test him. They have watched the Jacksonville tape too, and they know he just stepped in. Expect them to throw at him early. If he wins those early battles, the Colts' passing game shrinks.

Two: stop Jonathan Taylor. If Taylor gets going, the Colts can control the clock, keep Richardson in easy situations, and keep the game close. Make them one-dimensional.

Three: contain Richardson. Rush lanes, discipline, and tackling. No free scrambles on third down.

Four: take the ball away. A quarterback who has not started in a long time, against a secondary that just broke up 14 passes? The chances will be there. Catch them.

Five: no letdown. Start fast. Do not give a 3-8 team any reason to believe.

And I will say it again, Big Blue. This team can go all the way. Not because nothing goes wrong. Because when things go wrong, this team does not fall apart. A starting quarterback goes down, and the team keeps winning. A starting cornerback goes down, and the backup grabs an interception. The kicker struggles from the right hash, and then hits from the right hash. The pass rusher gets called a bust, and then he breaks out. That is the pattern of this season. That is what a real team looks like. And if Newsome comes back healthy for January, this secondary could be even better than it was before he got hurt.
