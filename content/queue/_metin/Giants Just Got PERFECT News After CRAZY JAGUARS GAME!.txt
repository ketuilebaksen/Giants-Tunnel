And there is a storyline in this game too. These are the same Colts who turned down the Giants' Jonathan Taylor offer at the trade deadline, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth. The Giants kept their picks. Taylor is still in Indianapolis. And now the Giants get to go into that building and show they did not need him.

But I want to be fair about the danger here. Taylor is one of the best running backs in football. And before the Jacksonville game, the Giants' run defense ranked 26th, allowing about 4.48 yards per carry and 121.6 rushing yards per game. When your quarterback is making his first start in a long time, the easiest plan is to hand the ball to your best player. So this game may come down to whether the Giants can stop Taylor on early downs. If they do, Richardson has to beat them from the pocket, and that is where Carter, Reese and Kayvon Thibodeaux can take over. If they do not, the Colts can shorten the game and keep it close.

Perfect timing reason number three: the defense is taking the ball away.

Against the best turnover team in the league, the Giants won the turnover battle. Deonte Banks intercepted Lawrence in the first quarter and returned it 19 yards to set up a game-tying field goal. That was his first interception since his 98-yard return against Arizona back in week four. He also had six tackles, five of them solo, and three pass breakups. Paulson Adebo got his first interception of the season in the fourth quarter after replacing Greg Newsome II, and that turned into a field goal that made it 23-9. And Kayvon Thibodeaux intercepted the two-point try after Brian Thomas Jr.'s touchdown, keeping the lead at eight.

And do not forget Dru Phillips. On fourth-and-one at the Giants' 11, he broke up the pass, ending Jacksonville's streak of 14 straight red zone drives that ended in points. A defense that was near the bottom of the league at stopping red zone touchdowns just shut the door on one of the best offenses in football.

Now think about facing a quarterback making his first start since 2024 with that kind of ball-hawking secondary behind a pass rush that is finally finishing. That is why this is perfect timing. My opinion, not a report: I think the Giants get at least two takeaways in Indianapolis.

Perfect timing reason number four: the special teams are finally helping.

Dominic Zvada made field goals from 53 and 54 yards in the first half against Jacksonville, both from the right hash, the side where all six of his earlier misses had come. He did miss a 32-yarder wide left to open the second half, and that one stung. But he now has eight field goals of 50-plus yards this season, which ties Graham Gano's franchise record from 2022. And here is a fun number for you. Brandon Aubrey set the NFL record with 14 in 2024. Zvada has seven games left. He would need six more to tie it. That is a lot, but with that leg, it is not impossible.

Jordan Stout was a weapon too. He averaged 60.3 yards on four punts, with one downed inside the 20. He now has 20 punts inside the 20 this season, second in the league. Zaire Barnes added four special teams tackles. And Xavier Gipson handled kick returns in his first game back for his third stint with the team, with no muffs.

After weeks of special teams costing the Giants, this unit finally did its job. And in a road game against a struggling team, special teams can be the thing that keeps an underdog alive, or the thing that buries it. The Giants want to bury it.

Perfect timing reason number five: the Giants' weapons are hitting milestones.

Malik Nabers had seven catches and an eight-yard touchdown from McCarthy, his third score of the year. He now has 192 career receptions. Only Odell Beckham Jr. had more catches through his first 29 career games. And Isaiah Likely became the first Giants tight end with 600 receiving yards through the first 10 games of a season since Mark Bavaro in 1987. Those are two players the Colts' defense has to account for on every snap.

Perfect timing reason number six: every player Harbaugh backed delivered.

This is the part of the Jacksonville win I keep thinking about, because it tells you something about this coach and this locker room. Go back through the week before that game. Harbaugh backed Carter when people were calling him a bust, and said the sacks would come. Carter had two. Harbaugh backed Zvada when people wanted a new kicker, and refused to bring in anyone for a tryout. He said Zvada was dead on in practice from what he called the "amazing, scary right hash." Zvada made a 53-yarder and a 54-yarder from that exact hash. Harbaugh told Paulson Adebo, after making him a healthy scratch, that in his mind Adebo is a starter in this league and would have to start again. Adebo came in for an injured Newsome and got an interception. Harbaugh said he felt more strongly than ever that McCarthy could win games, and McCarthy protected the ball, threw a touchdown, and saved a touchdown with a tackle. And Banks, who was getting ripped for the pass interference against Washington, came back with an interception and three pass breakups.

That is not a coincidence. When a head coach publicly backs his players, and then they go out and deliver, the whole locker room notices. The trust goes both ways. My opinion, not a report: that kind of trust is exactly what you need in December and January, when every game is a dog fight, which is the exact phrase Harbaugh used after the Washington loss.

Now, the part that worries me. Because if the Giants are going to take advantage of all this perfect timing, the offense has to be better.

J.J. McCarthy was 18 of 38 for 173 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. I like that he protected the ball against a team with a plus-nine turnover differential. But 173 yards on 38 attempts is under five yards a throw. That is not a lot. The Giants need more rhythm from him before the schedule gets harder. He did add four runs for 38 yards, including a 22-yard scramble on the touchdown drive. And he made one of the biggest plays of the game when Josh Hines-Allen forced a Cam Skattebo fumble late in the third quarter. Antonio Johnson scooped it up and returned it 42 yards, and McCarthy ran him down and tackled him at the Giants' 31. If McCarthy does not make that tackle, Jacksonville likely scores, and the whole game changes. Instead, the defense held, and Jacksonville turned it over on downs, keeping the lead at 20-6.

The run game was quiet. Skattebo had 14 carries for 34 yards, with a 10-yard run as his longest, plus an 11-yard catch and run. And the fumble. Devin Singletary had one carry for five yards. There was not much push from the backfield, and that is a problem if you want to close games.

And the closing problem showed up again. The Giants went four straight drives without a first down while Jacksonville cut the lead from 14 to five. Malachi Fields finally broke the streak with a 19-yard catch with under three minutes left. Fields had already drawn a 37-yard pass interference on Montaric Brown that set up Jameis Winston's one-yard touchdown sneak. Jalin Hyatt caught his first pass of the season, a four-yard gain on third down. Nine different Giants caught a pass. I like that spread. But the Giants cannot keep needing one big catch to survive the fourth quarter.

So here is what I want to see against Indianapolis. And again, this is my opinion, not anything the team has said.

One, start fast on offense. Against a team that just lost 26-3 and is starting a quarterback who has not started since 2024, an early lead puts the pressure right on Richardson. Force him to throw. That is when Carter and this pass rush feast.

Two, get the run game going. The Giants need confidence on the ground before San Francisco, Seattle and Detroit. Skattebo has to hold on to the ball. Five fumbles is too many. But he is also the most physical runner on this team, and he needs to get rolling again.

Three, practice the four-minute offense. If the Giants have a lead in the fourth quarter, I want to see a real drive that runs clock. Mix the run with quick, high-percentage throws to Likely and Nabers. Move the chains. Finish the game with the offense on the field, not with the defense holding on.

Four, keep the takeaways coming. Make Richardson uncomfortable, make him hold the ball, and let the secondary make plays on bad throws.

And keep an eye on the injury front. Greg Newsome II left the Jacksonville game with an ankle injury, and Adebo replaced him. Whoever plays has to be ready, because depth at corner matters. The Giants just extended Newsome through 2029, so they need him healthy for the stretch run.

And let me add one more piece to the defensive plan for Sunday, because Richardson's legs are a real threat. If the Giants lose contain, he can turn a broken play into a big gain, and that is how a struggling team stays in a game. That is why I think Arvell Reese is going to be one of the most important players on the field. He can rush, he can drop into coverage, and he can spy the quarterback. He came into the Jacksonville game leading the Giants with 81 tackles, with four and a half sacks, an interception, a forced fumble and a fumble recovery, and he became the youngest player in NFL history with three sacks in a game in Philadelphia. Use him as a spy on some downs, let him blitz on others, and Richardson never knows where he is. That is how you take away a mobile quarterback's best weapon.

Now let's zoom out to the NFC East. The Giants are 6-3-1. Dallas already has six losses and plays on Thanksgiving. Washington holds the head-to-head edge on the Giants after the tie and the 24-21 win, so that is the team I would watch most closely. But the Giants have only three losses, and they just proved they can beat a top team. Every win from here makes it harder for anyone to catch them.

And after Indianapolis, the schedule gets much tougher. San Francisco at home. Seattle on the road. Cleveland at home. Detroit on the road. Then Dallas and Philadelphia. That is why this Colts game matters so much. It is the last game before the gauntlet where the Giants should be clear favorites. Win it, and you go into the tough stretch at 7-3-1 with confidence. Lose it, and the Jacksonville win starts to feel like a one-off.

And think about where a win would put this team in the bigger picture. Before the Jacksonville game, Pro Football Focus projected the Giants' playoff chances at about 76 percent with a win, and they got the win. A road win in Indianapolis would push them even higher, and it would mean that even a split in the tough stretch that follows keeps them firmly in control of the division. This is the kind of game that does not get you headlines when you win it, but gets you buried when you lose it. Good teams take care of business in spots like this.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Before the Jacksonville game, I predicted the Giants would win 23-20 with Zvada making a big kick. They won 23-18, and Zvada made two huge ones from the right hash. I said Carter would get home, and he got home twice. I said McCarthy would not turn it over, and he did not. But I also said Skattebo would carry the offense, and he did not, and I said the four-minute offense would be fixed, and it was not. So I own those.

Here is my prediction for Indianapolis, and you can hold me to it. Carter gets at least two more sacks against Richardson. The Giants force at least two turnovers. Skattebo gets back on track with no fumbles. McCarthy throws for over 200 yards with a better completion rate than he had against Jacksonville. Zvada makes at least one more 50-yarder, moving him past Gano into sole possession of the franchise record. And the Giants win comfortably and move to 7-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A pass rusher who just found his finish. A secondary that takes the ball away. A kicker chasing franchise history. A punter who is one of the best in the league at pinning teams deep. A star receiver on an Odell Beckham pace. A tight end doing what nobody has done here since Mark Bavaro. And now, a road game against a team in chaos, right when this team is gaining momentum. That is perfect timing. Now go take advantage of it, and make the rest of this division chase you.
