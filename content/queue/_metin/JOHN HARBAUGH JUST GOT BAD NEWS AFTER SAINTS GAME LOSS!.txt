The Giants lost to the Saints at MetLife, and the bad news did not stop when the clock hit zero. It got worse at the podium. Ten minutes after the game, John Harbaugh was asked if J.J. McCarthy is now his starting quarterback, and he would not say no. He said he cannot answer that right now, that it is not even up for discussion, and that the process of deciding starts, in his words, right now. He called his running game not very good, not even close to good enough. He admitted his defense is not stopping the run at all and is giving up way too many quarterback scrambles. He watched a fumbled kickoff return at the end of the first half let the Saints back into the game. And for the first time all season, the Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, with a road trip to a Houston team that Harbaugh says is getting healthy. Folks, that is a lot of bad news for one Sunday. So today, I am going to walk through every piece of it, what Harbaugh said, what he really meant, and whether this season is in trouble or just got a wake-up call. Let’s get into it.

Then he said the kickoff return was big, the whole sequence was big. We let him back in the game, he said, and then we come back out and we do not stop him after the half. That was 17 points that started it. Folks, think about that. A team that felt in control of the game suddenly hands the ball back late in the half, and then the defense comes out of the locker room and cannot get a stop. Momentum in the NFL is not a myth. It is a sequence of plays, and the Giants lost that sequence badly.

And the hole got deep. Harbaugh himself mentioned that the Giants were not planning on being down by 24, and then added, whatever we were down by. When your head coach does not even want to say the number, you know it got ugly. But here is the part I do not want anybody to miss. This team did not quit. They kept fighting back until the very end, and that is where the biggest story of the day began.

Because the Giants turned to J.J. McCarthy. Harbaugh called it desperation time, and he said it is different than normal football. But he also said McCarthy came in and did a heck of a job given the circumstances, made some plays, got the Giants back, and gave them a chance at the end to win the game. We are throwing the ball in the end zone at the end, Harbaugh said, so that was good, you have got to compliment him on that. And he confirmed that if the Giants had scored, they were going for two. So, think about how close that was. A team that was buried by a big deficit had a throw into the end zone at the end, with a two-point try waiting behind it.

Then came the question everybody wanted to ask. Is McCarthy your starter going forward? And here is how Harbaugh answered it. I cannot answer that now. The game just ended ten minutes ago. It is not even up for discussion or conversation right now. We have got to go back and go to work. And then he went out of his way to say, I have no problem with Jameis whatsoever. I love the way he competes, love the way he fights, all those kind of things. He plays, you know, up and down today for sure, but those things will all be decided, not ten minutes after a game.

Folks, read between the lines with me, but carefully. Harbaugh did not name McCarthy the starter. He also did not do what a coach does when he wants to shut a controversy down instantly, which is say, Jameis is our quarterback, period, end of story. He left the door open. And when he was asked later when the process of deciding starts, tonight or tomorrow, his answer was three words. Starts right now. That is the bad news for Jameis Winston. Not that he lost the job, because he has not. But for the first time since he took over, his job is officially under review.

Now, let me be fair to Jameis, because this channel has been in his corner all season, and I am not jumping off after one bad afternoon. Look at what he has done. He took over when Jackson Dart went down. He went unbeaten in his first three starts, two wins and a tie. He became the first Giant since Eli Manning in 2016 to throw three touchdown passes in back-to-back games. He threw for 326 yards in Washington and led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback on the road. That is not nothing. That is a quarterback who saved the season once already. Harbaugh’s own word was up and down, not bad. Up and down is what Jameis has always been. The question is whether the down was low enough to make a change.

And here is the case for McCarthy, because it is real too. We have been tracking his signals all season on this channel. He was named the backup. He earned an A-plus from Matt Nagy for his practice progress. He had a package designed for him that we saw against Arizona, and Harbaugh said back then that what we saw was not all they have for him. Now, add a fourth signal. When the Giants needed somebody to rescue them in a real game, with real pressure, McCarthy came in and nearly did it. A reporter also asked Harbaugh how much McCarthy’s mobility and athleticism are an asset, and whether that was part of the reason the Giants acquired him. Harbaugh said it is a big asset, absolutely, he is a very mobile quarterback, it is a big part of his skill set. Think about that in context. The offensive line has been banged up. Both starting tackles were on the injury report all week. A quarterback who can escape pressure and make plays with his legs suddenly looks a lot more valuable behind a shaky line.

Now, let me give you the other side of the McCarthy case, because it matters. Coming in during desperation time is not the same as starting a game. When you are down by multiple scores, the defense is often playing soft, protecting against the big play, and letting you take the underneath stuff. Plenty of backup quarterbacks in NFL history have looked great in garbage-time comebacks and then struggled when they had to start and face a full game plan. Harbaugh himself said it, it is different than normal football. So I am not going to tell you McCarthy is the answer based on one comeback. I am telling you he has earned a real look, and Harbaugh clearly agrees.

And folks, we have talked about this before on this channel. John Harbaugh has been through this exact decision. In Baltimore, back in 2018, he had a veteran starter in Joe Flacco and a young, mobile quarterback in Lamar Jackson. When Flacco got hurt, Lamar took over, the team started winning, and when Flacco was healthy again, Harbaugh stuck with the young quarterback. That season ended with a division title. Now, I want to be careful here. That situation was not identical. Lamar started because of an injury, not because of one comeback. But it shows you that Harbaugh is not a coach who is afraid to make the hard call at quarterback, even midseason, even with a veteran he respects. And it also shows you he does not make it in ten minutes. He makes it after he sees the tape.

So, what do I think happens? Here is my honest read, and I am labeling it as my opinion. I think Harbaugh watches the full tape of both quarterbacks before he decides anything. I think Jameis still has a real chance to start in Houston, because his body of work this season is strong. But I also think McCarthy’s package is about to grow, no matter who starts. Now, the second piece of bad news, and honestly, it might be the bigger long-term problem. The running game. The very first question of the press conference was, what did you see from your running game today? And Harbaugh’s answer was four words. It was not very good. Then he said it again. Not very good. Later he said, we are not running the ball well enough at all, not even close to good enough.

And then came the exchange that is going to be talked about all week. A reporter asked when Harbaugh made the decision to bench Cam Skattebo, who ended up playing only six snaps. And Harbaugh pushed back hard. He said they did not bench Skattebo. He said it was not by design. All the backs were taking equal reps in the first half. They put four running backs up, and they were trying to see who could get a hot hand and earn it. Then in the second half, down big, after the first series, they went to a no-huddle approach, and that is when you use your third-down backs. When Skattebo was in the game, they did not call runs. He said it was not about any one back. It was about giving them all an equal shot.

But then Harbaugh said something that I think is the most important quote of the entire press conference. He said, you can argue that. You can say, I do not like you divvying up the snaps between four backs. I can tell you, I do not like it either. One of those guys needs to step up. Somebody needs to step up and be the back, and none of those guys have done that yet. When one of those guys steps up to be the back, then they will be the back. That is your answer. And that is as honest as I can be about it. Believe me, they are all hungry. Somebody is going to step up and take that job.

Folks, that is a head coach telling the whole world that his running back room is an open competition. Think about who is in that room. Cam Skattebo, the fan favorite, who fumbled in overtime in Washington. Breece Hall, who the Giants just traded for to upgrade the backfield. Najee Harris, the veteran. And one more back the Giants wanted to get into the mix. That is a lot of talent, and right now, none of them has grabbed the job. When a reporter followed up and asked whether this was about the fumbling, Harbaugh got a little fiery. He said it has been pretty clear the whole story, that it is the best answer you could ever get in a thousand years, and that if you are not thrilled with it, he has got nothing else for you. That is Harbaugh. He gave a direct answer, and he was done answering the same question twice.

Here is why this matters so much. A shotgun, tempo offense built around a quarterback still needs a running game that can keep a defense honest. If defenses do not respect the run, they can drop more players into coverage, sit on Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely, and rush the passer without worrying about the draw or the handoff. And if McCarthy ends up playing more, the run game becomes even more important, because the read-option and quarterback movement only work when the defense has to respect the running back. So whoever wins this running back job is not just winning carries. He is unlocking the whole offense.

And now the third piece of bad news, the defense. Harbaugh did not hold back here either. We are not stopping the run like we need to at all, he said. We are not keeping the quarterback where he needs to be at all. Way too many quarterback scrambles. And a couple of things in coverage that were not good. Defensively, we have got to play way better, and our guys know it, way more consistently. Folks, that is a direct hit on the exact thing Harbaugh warned about before the game. All week, he talked about Tyler Shough as a young quarterback playing with confidence. And on Sunday, Shough made the Giants pay with his legs. When a pass rush gets too far upfield, a mobile quarterback finds the lanes, and that is a discipline issue, a rush-lane issue, and maybe a depth issue too, with all the bodies the Giants have been missing at edge rusher this season.

And on offense, Harbaugh said the same thing in a different way. We are not executing well. We are not hitting basic plays like we need to. So, so many stretches that are not winning football, he said. That is the phrase I keep coming back to. Not one bad play. Stretches. Long periods of a game where this team does not look like the team that took first place in the NFC East.

Now, there was also some debate about the clock at the end, and I know a lot of you are going to bring it up after everything we talked about in Washington last week. Harbaugh explained that the Giants wanted to get their field goal done by 2:13, before the two-minute warning, because they needed two scores and wanted the two-minute warning to count for them as an extra stop of the clock. He said they had a chance to hit a couple of shots and maybe score a touchdown first, but an eight-yard sack got in the way. He was asked why they spiked the ball on third down instead of running a play, and he said getting what they call a warp play communicated and executed with a tired team would have been really hard, and it could have been chaos, it could have been a disaster, it could have run a bunch of time off the clock. And on the kickoff, he said the goal was to kick it out of the back of the end zone, and sending it out of bounds would have given up five more yards.

Now, you can debate those choices, and the reporters clearly did. But here is the difference from what we saw in Washington. Every one of Harbaugh’s answers had a plan behind it. You may not agree with spiking on third down, but nobody on that field was confused. Nobody wasted 42 seconds. Harbaugh called it good common sense clock management, and said, we did the best we could with it. That is the Harbaugh we talked about yesterday. You do not have to love every call, but this team knows what it is trying to do at the end of games.
