The Giants just made two shocking roster cuts, and the timing is not an accident. Najee Harris, the running back who started against the Saints only days ago, has been released. Jihad Ward, the veteran edge rusher the Giants brought in off Minnesota’s practice squad, is gone too. And in the same week, the Indianapolis Colts fired their general manager, Chris Ballard, in the middle of the season. Folks, put those two stories together, and you have the setup for a blockbuster trade. Because the Colts have exactly the kind of player the Giants need most, and a front office shake-up is the moment when teams start listening to offers. I am talking about Quenton Nelson, one of the best guards in football, at the exact position where this offense is falling apart. Now, let me be clear from the start. There is no deal done, and I have no report that one is close. But today, I am going to break down both cuts, what they tell us about where Harbaugh and Schoen are heading, and the blockbuster move I would make before the trade deadline, along with two other Colts names and the price that would make sense. Let’s get into it.

If the Giants could make one trade with the Colts, who are you calling, Quenton Nelson, Jonathan Taylor, or DeForest Buckner?

Let’s start with Najee Harris, because this is the move that makes you stop and think about what the Giants are building. He started at running back against the Saints. It was his first start since January 2025. And now, days later, he is gone. He finished that game with four carries for two yards, so you understand why the team wanted a change. But this was not a player buried on the depth chart for weeks. He had just been handed the chance to start, and the Giants moved on anyway.

And the timing gets more interesting when you look at what Harris said after the loss. He talked about how hard it is to find a rhythm when four backs are sharing the work. I understand that. A runner wants to feel how the defense is reacting, learn which lanes are opening, and adjust as the game goes on. That is tough to do with a couple of carries and then a long stretch on the sideline. Now, to be fair to Harris, he was talking about his own experience, not taking a shot at the coaches. But when a player voices frustration about his opportunities and then gets released, people are going to connect the two. I cannot tell you his words caused the decision. I can tell you the timing stands out.

The production explains a lot, though. Harris carried 31 times for 99 yards over five games. That is 3.2 yards per carry. He broke only three tackles. His longest run went for 11 yards. And he fumbled against the Cardinals. That is not what you want from a back you are trying to build a ground game around, especially on a team whose head coach has called ball security the most important thing with his offense.

But I will not put it all on Harris. The offensive line has not opened consistent lanes, and the constant rotation made it hard for anybody to settle in. Cutting Harris fits the numbers, but it does not touch the bigger problem behind them. Hold onto that, because it is the whole reason the trade I am about to pitch makes sense.

And let’s give Najee Harris his due, because this is not a player without a résumé. In Pittsburgh, he ran for more than 1,000 yards in each of his first four seasons in the league. That is a durable, productive back who carried a heavy load for years. So when a player like that averages 3.2 yards a carry in a new offense, you have to ask whether the problem was the back, or what was happening in front of him. My answer is that it was probably a little of both. Harris was not breaking tackles the way he used to. But a back with his track record does not suddenly forget how to run. Sometimes the situation around a player matters as much as the player himself, and that is exactly why the Giants need to look at the line before they look at another runner.

And remember Harbaugh’s stance on this. After the Saints game, he said he does not like dividing the snaps between four backs either. He said one of those guys needs to step up and be the back, and when somebody does, that is your answer. He understood Harris wanting more work, but the message was clear. Nobody has earned the job yet. I agree with that in principle. Competition is healthy, and production should be rewarded. But coaches also have to put players in a position to succeed, and telling somebody to take the job does not help much when the blocking breaks down and the rotation keeps shifting.

So, with Harris gone, here is what I want to see. Give Cam Skattebo and Tyrone Tracy Jr. a clear path to most of the carries, with Devin Singletary in a supporting role. Singletary has value in pass protection and as a receiver, and that matters on third down. Tracy just showed against the Saints that he can catch, score, and pick up the blitz better than anybody on the roster. And Skattebo has the toughness and the motivation to earn his role back. The Giants do not need to rotate four backs just because they have four available. Now they have three. Decide who gives you the best chance to move the ball, commit to that plan, and let those players build some continuity.

Now, the second cut. Jihad Ward. And this one points to a different problem. Ward played 23 snaps against Washington and 18 against New Orleans. That is only 41 snaps since he arrived, and the Giants were not getting enough from him. He came in without a full training camp after time on the Vikings practice squad, and getting into game shape takes time. At 32 years old, he is also not a young player with room to grow into a bigger role. I do not blame him for the defense’s problems. But the real issue is that the Giants have struggled to stop the run, and swapping one veteran body does not repair the front.

And here is the part of this move that this channel has been waiting for. With Ward gone, the Giants promoted Trace Ford from the practice squad to the active roster. Folks, we have been talking about Trace Ford for weeks. He earned game-day elevations twice already this season. Now he has a real roster spot. With Brian Burns and Chauncey Golston on injured reserve, the Giants need every edge rusher who can win, and Ford finally gets his chance to prove he belongs. I love that move.

The Giants also worked out nose tackles this week, and that tells you exactly what is on their mind. The Saints ran for 175 yards. D.J. Reader has been a monster in the middle, and the numbers show the run defense is a lot worse when he is off the field. Since Dexter Lawrence left this building, the Giants have been searching for more help on the interior, and these workouts tell me they know it.

And there is one more depth problem that might be the most urgent of all. The receiver room. Darnell Mooney left the Saints game with a neck injury and did not return. If he cannot go in Houston, the Giants would have only Malik Nabers, Malachi Fields, and Charlie Jones as active receivers. That is thin, especially against a Texans defense with a strong secondary. Jalin Hyatt is on the practice squad, so promoting him is one option. A free agent, a trade, or a receiver off another team’s practice squad are others. But if Mooney misses time, I would be stunned if the Giants ignored it.

So, the Giants have a running game that is not working, a run defense that is giving up big chunks, and a receiver room that is one injury away from a crisis. And that is exactly why the news out of Indianapolis matters so much.

The Colts fired Chris Ballard in the middle of the season. And remember, these are the same Colts who just released Darius Slayton after three games without a single offensive snap. A midseason front office change usually means a team is about to reevaluate everything. I am not saying the Colts are heading into a full rebuild. But a change at the top can open the door to roster moves nobody expected, especially when it comes to expensive veterans. And if Indianapolis starts listening to offers before the trade deadline, Joe Schoen and John Harbaugh need to be on the phone.

And when I look at what this roster needs, one name stands out above the rest. Quenton Nelson. If the Giants are serious about fixing the run game, he is the player I call about. He is 30 years old, widely regarded as one of the best guards in the league, and he would give the Giants a proven starter exactly where they need help in the run game.

Here is why I put him ahead of any running back. Look at the left guard spot right now. PFF has Jon Runyan Jr. ranked 63rd out of 75 guards overall, and 72nd out of 75 in run blocking. His pass protection has been better, and rankings never tell the whole story about any player. But when a guard is struggling that badly in the run game, no running back is going to find consistency behind him. And at center, Brian Hudson has moved ahead of John Michael Schmitz. So, the interior of this line is still being sorted out in the middle of the season.

What makes Nelson so appealing is not the name. It is that he fixes a problem that touches the entire offense. Better line play gives the quarterback more time. It opens cleaner lanes. It helps every running back on the roster. A trade for another running back might upgrade the talent in the backfield, but it would not fix why the runs are not developing. Nelson would raise the ceiling of the players the Giants already have. Skattebo, Tracy, and Singletary would all look better with him pulling in front of them. Honestly, Najee Harris might have looked better too.

And think about the quarterback situation. Whether it is Jameis Winston or J.J. McCarthy, a stronger interior line changes everything. Winston needs a clean pocket to step into his throws. McCarthy wants to attack downfield, which takes time. And the quick game only works if pressure is not coming straight up the middle. Every quarterback in the league plays better when the interior holds.

And think about who would be coaching him. John Harbaugh spent years in Baltimore with Marshal Yanda, one of the best guards of his generation, an eight-time Pro Bowler who set the tone for that entire offense. Harbaugh knows exactly what an elite guard does for a run game, and for a locker room. The Ravens built their identity on physical, nasty blocking up front, and that is the identity Harbaugh has been trying to build here. Quenton Nelson plays the game the same way. He finishes blocks, he pulls with violence, and he makes defenders think twice about the next snap. If you want to turn this into a Harbaugh offense, that is the kind of player who makes it happen.

Now, the price. Nelson is nearing free agency, so any team trading for him has to plan for what comes after this season. That is both the risk and the opportunity. The risk is that you give up picks for a few months of a player. The opportunity is that you get him in your building and get the first chance to sign him long term. I could see the Giants opening with a second-round pick, though the Colts might ask for more depending on how many teams call. And yes, there is an awkward part. The Giants have a commitment to Runyan at that same position. But if the team decides it made a mistake or found a better answer, it cannot let one contract hold it back. Sticking with a decision just because you already made it does not help anybody.

And Giants fans, we have been here before. Back in 2019, the Giants traded for Leonard Williams in the middle of the season, giving the Jets a third-round pick and a fifth-round pick for a player near the end of his contract. A lot of people hated it at the time, because the Giants were not contending and they would have to pay him. They franchise-tagged him, then signed him to a big extension, and he became one of the best players on the defense for years. The lesson is simple. When you trade for a player nearing free agency, you have to be ready to pay him. If the Giants believe Nelson can anchor this line for the next several years, a second-round pick could look like a bargain.

And if you want proof that a midseason trade can change a season, look at the 2021 Rams. They traded a second-round pick and a third-round pick for Von Miller at the deadline. A few months later, Miller was a huge part of their Super Bowl win. A proven veteran at the right position, at the right time, can change everything. I am not saying Nelson would do that alone. But the right trade can turn a team in first place into a team that stays there.

Now, the second Colts name, and I understand why it is tempting. Jonathan Taylor. He is 27, he weighs 226 pounds, and he has been one of the best running backs in the league for years. Harbaugh wants one runner to establish himself as the primary option. With Taylor, the Giants would not need to guess who deserves the next series. They could build a clear plan around one proven lead back.

But the price is what makes me cautious. Taylor is on a big contract, so the Giants would be taking on both the trade cost and the money. A second-round pick and maybe a fifth could be a starting point, but the Colts might hold out for a first-rounder. And I would not give up a first-round pick for a running back while the offensive line is this big a concern. Put Jonathan Taylor behind this line as it stands right now, and you are paying a premium for a back who would still be running into crowded lanes. Fix the blocking first.

The third name is DeForest Buckner, and here I take a different approach. He is a good player, and his pass rush makes him an easy fit for any team trying to strengthen its defensive front. He already has four sacks this season, with strong totals in previous years. Next to D.J. Reader, he would give the Giants a dangerous interior. But he is 32 and nearing free agency. So, I think hard about what he is really worth to this team. If the price is reasonable, he helps right away, especially against the run. But I would not empty the cupboard for a 32-year-old on an expiring deal, not when the Giants have younger needs to fill and a future to protect.

So, where do I land? If I am Joe Schoen, Quenton Nelson is my first call. The offensive line is the root of the running game problem, and fixing the root is smarter than buying another symptom. Second, I keep an eye on receiver depth, because one more injury could leave this offense with almost nobody behind Nabers. And third, if the price on a defensive tackle is right, whether that is Buckner or somebody from those nose tackle workouts, I make that move too. But I would not chase a running back with a first-round pick.

And here is the bigger picture. The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, sitting on top of the NFC East ahead of the Cowboys, Eagles, and Commanders. The Eagles just lost Jalen Hurts for the season. The Cowboys are 2 and 4. The division is wide open. The Giants head to Houston with the quarterback situation still unsettled, and then they get the bye. If this team believes it can win the division, and I do, then the next two weeks are the moment to strengthen the roster. But a trade just because a player is available is not a strategy. The player has to solve a real problem, and the price has to make sense.

The Harris and Ward releases tell you changes are coming. But the cuts alone do not fix what led to them. The Giants still need a more consistent ground game, better run defense, and enough receiver depth to survive injuries. Those are the three problems. Quenton Nelson addresses the biggest one.

Here is what I will be watching this week. Number one, whether the Giants add a receiver, from the practice squad or anywhere else, depending on Mooney’s neck. Number two, whether a nose tackle gets signed after those workouts. Number three, how the carries are split between Skattebo, Tracy, and Singletary in Houston. And number four, any whispers out of Indianapolis about who the Colts are willing to move.

I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team has the pieces to make a real run. The right move at the right time can turn a team in first place into a champion. This team can go all the way.
