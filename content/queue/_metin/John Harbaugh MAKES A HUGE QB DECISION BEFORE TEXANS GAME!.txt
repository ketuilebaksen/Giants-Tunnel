John Harbaugh has made his quarterback decision for Houston, and it is the bold one. According to a report from ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, J.J. McCarthy will start for the Giants against the Texans on Sunday. Not a package. Not a few series. The start. The quarterback who walked into a 24-point hole against the Saints and threw for 243 yards in one quarter now gets a full week of preparation and the keys to the offense, with the Giants sitting in first place in the NFC East. And folks, before we go any further, I owe you a receipt, because in my last video I told you my read was that Jameis Winston would start in Houston. I was wrong. Harbaugh went the other way, and today I am going to tell you exactly why I think he did it, what it means for Winston, the wild story of how McCarthy got here, and why this decision could define the rest of the Giants’ season. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with the receipt, because that is how this channel works. In my last video, I looked at Harbaugh’s Monday press conference, where he said he admires and respects Jameis Winston and that McCarthy just got here a couple of weeks ago, with the Giants still building an offense he has a feel for. I read that as Harbaugh leaning toward Winston for one more week. I even said Harbaugh would not make a switch after one quarter of football. Well, he did. And I think the lesson is that when Harbaugh says the staff is honing in on a decision, he means it. He was not leaning, he was deciding. So I own that one. And honestly, I am glad I was wrong, because this is the more exciting path.

Now, here is what makes the decision so interesting. There was a very logical plan sitting right in front of the Giants. Start Jameis one more time in Houston. Give McCarthy one more week to get comfortable. Then use the bye week to install the offense around him, and turn it over to him for the Eagles game. A lot of people, me included, expected something like that. One analyst said it perfectly this week. Throw that plan out the window, because of what McCarthy did against the Saints. And another said it this way. We all saw it. We damn near won a football game. We were all energized by it. If a performance like that does not earn a full week of preparation and a start, then why did you bring him here in the first place?

And let’s remember what that performance actually was. Eleven of 21 for 243 yards, all of it in the fourth quarter. That ties for the most passing yards by a Giant in a single quarter since at least 1991, alongside Eli Manning. And it is the third-most passing yards by a backup quarterback in a single quarter since 1978. Seventeen unanswered points. A 49-yard shot to Charlie Jones. A touchdown to Isaiah Likely. A 28-yard scramble on the final drive. And a throw into the end zone on fourth down that would have tied it, with a two-point try waiting to win. That is not a normal relief appearance. That is the kind of quarter that changes how a whole building feels.

And that is the part I underestimated. Harbaugh is not just evaluating a stat line. He is reading his team. When a quarterback comes in and the energy on the sideline changes, when the defense starts getting stops because it believes again, when the whole stadium wakes up, a head coach feels that. And with the division wide open, the Eagles without Jalen Hurts, and the Cowboys at 2 and 4, Harbaugh decided he could not afford to wait. If McCarthy is the spark, you light it now, not after the bye.

So, let me compare it to what Harbaugh did in Baltimore, because I brought it up last time and I want to be accurate. In 2018, when Joe Flacco got hurt, Lamar Jackson took over, the Ravens went on a run, and Harbaugh stayed with the young quarterback even when the veteran was healthy. That season ended with a division title. I said this time would be different because it was only one quarter. But the principle is exactly the same. Harbaugh trusts what he sees, and when a young quarterback gives his team life, he is not afraid to ride it, even with a respected veteran in the room. That is who he is.

Now, the McCarthy story, because it is one of the wildest quarterback journeys in the league right now. He was a first-round pick by the Vikings in 2024, the tenth overall selection, fresh off a national championship at Michigan. Then he got hurt and missed his entire rookie season. In his absence, Sam Darnold went out and won 14 games for Minnesota. And then the Vikings let Darnold walk and handed the job to McCarthy. He struggled. He got hurt again. Meanwhile, Darnold went somewhere else and won a Super Bowl. And by this fall, Minnesota was ready to move McCarthy for a fifth-round pick. As one analyst put it this week, the it factor did not show up enough in Minnesota for them to stick with him.

But here is the thing. New York is a completely different environment for him. In Minnesota, he was the first-round pick, the franchise’s future, carrying all of that weight. In New York, he is playing with house money. He came in as the backup. He is technically keeping the seat warm for Jackson Dart, who is out for the rest of the regular season with his knee injury. Nobody expects him to be the long-term answer. Which means every good game is a bonus, and if he keeps playing well, he gets to write his own script. One analyst said it best. He is a placeholder for Jackson Dart, unless, dot dot dot. And that unless is the story he gets to write now.

And think about the irony with Sam Darnold. The man who replaced McCarthy in Minnesota was himself a quarterback a lot of people had given up on. Darnold was written off in New York with the Jets, bounced around the league, and then found new life somewhere else, all the way to a championship. That is the blueprint for McCarthy. Sometimes a quarterback just needs a new building, a new coach, and a fresh start without the pressure of being the savior. That is exactly what the Giants are giving him.

And you could see it after the Saints game. One analyst noticed that when McCarthy spoke to reporters, he looked bigger, like a guy who has been through it and is coming out the other side. And listen to what McCarthy said himself. He said there was something special about this group from the moment he walked in. He talked about how Harbaugh builds confidence through work, not just talk. And he talked about how this team responded when Brian Burns and Jackson Dart went down. That is not a quarterback who sounds like a temporary rental. That is a quarterback who already feels like he belongs in this locker room, and teammates feel that too.

And then there is the Harbaugh connection, and Giants fans who went to Michigan are going to love this. McCarthy won that national championship at Michigan playing for Jim Harbaugh, John’s brother. So now John Harbaugh is handing the keys to the quarterback his brother won a title with. You cannot write this stuff. And here is another twist. Back in the 2024 draft, the Giants had a chance to take McCarthy. They took Malik Nabers instead, sixth overall, and McCarthy went to Minnesota at ten. The year after that, they drafted Jackson Dart. And now, two years later, J.J. McCarthy is going to be throwing the ball to Malik Nabers. If you had told someone that in 2024, they would have assumed the Giants drafted McCarthy. They did not. They just ended up with him anyway.

And speaking of Nabers, low-key, he is having a monster season and not enough people are talking about it. Twelve catches for 165 yards against the Saints. His third straight game with at least 100 receiving yards. He just passed Puka Nacua for the second-most receptions by any player through his first 25 career games, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. Now imagine what that connection looks like with McCarthy after a full week of practice reps together, instead of a desperate fourth quarter. McCarthy wants to attack downfield. Nabers wins downfield. The potential there is real. But potential means nothing until they do it on Sunday.

Now, let’s talk about Jameis Winston, because I do not want anybody forgetting what he did for this team. He took over after Dart went down. He went unbeaten in his first three starts. He threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games. He led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback in Washington. And the Giants signed him to a two-year extension back on September 7th. That is a lot of good football. But the turnovers against the Saints were the turning point, and in a season where Harbaugh has called ball security the most important thing with his offense, that ended up deciding it.

And here is what gives me confidence that this will not break the locker room. Remember how Winston handled it after the Saints game. He praised McCarthy. He said he was proud of him. He said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. He said he is looking internally, not outwardly. A veteran who talks like that is going to be on the sideline helping the young quarterback, play by play, the same way he did in that fourth quarter. And if McCarthy struggles, the Giants have a quarterback who has already won games for them this year, ready to step back in. That is not a controversy. That is depth.

And Jameis has done this before. Remember, he spent a season in New Orleans as Drew Brees’s backup, learning, preparing, and by every account being one of the best teammates in the building. He knows how to be ready when his name is called. And in a season like this one, with injuries everywhere, there is a very real chance his name gets called again. A quarterback room with a hot young starter and a veteran who has already won games for you is exactly what a contender wants in November and December.

Now, the other big story this week, and it ties directly into all of this. Najee Harris. The Giants released him after he started against the Saints and finished with four carries for two yards. And after that game, Harris spoke honestly about the rotation. He said it is hard to get into a rhythm with one or two runs. On the first couple of runs, you are figuring out how the defense is playing you, and on the next ones, you know how to attack it. But if you are playing here and there and getting taken out for a series, he said, there is nothing you can do. He said he did not think he had ever been through something like that before.

And his comments sparked a big debate among former players this week. One of them argued that a player in Harris’s position has to be careful. He said Pittsburgh moved on, the Chargers moved on, and when you come into a new team as the newcomer, you are not in the same position as the superstar backs who can publicly ask for more touches. His point was that sports are cruel, and the newest guy without production is the first to go. Another said that conversation is supposed to happen behind closed doors with the coaches, not in front of the cameras. And a third, who was a starter for years, said he was frustrated too when he was not getting the ball, but he kept working in practice until the coaches finally saw it.

Here is my take. I do not think Harris said anything wrong. He was honest about his own experience, and he did not attack his coaches. But the numbers did not help him. Thirty-one carries for 99 yards over five games, 3.2 yards per carry, and a fumble against the Cardinals. And the bigger message from this week is the same one behind the McCarthy decision. Harbaugh is rewarding production and energy right now, not résumés. Tyrone Tracy Jr. scored two touchdowns and graded as the team’s best pass protector against the Saints. McCarthy threw for 243 yards in a quarter. Trace Ford earned his active roster spot after three straight weeks of elevations. That is the standard, and everybody in that building knows it.

And that running back room matters even more now, because a young quarterback in his first start needs a running game to lean on. With Harris gone, it is Cam Skattebo, Tyrone Tracy Jr., and Devin Singletary. Tracy picking up the blitz the way he did against the Saints is huge for McCarthy. And a quarterback who can keep the ball on read options gives those backs a little more room. The run game and the quarterback have to help each other.

Now, the test, and it is a real one. The Texans are 2 and 4, but they have won two straight, including that 34 to 31 win over the Jaguars in London on a walk-off field goal. C.J. Stroud is second in the NFL in passing yards. Marvin Harrison Jr., who played against the Giants at MetLife right before he was traded to Houston, is now a Texan. And Tank Dell is coming back from his serious knee injury. That is an offense that can put up points. Which means McCarthy cannot just manage the game. He may have to win it.

And do not forget who is on the other sideline. DeMeco Ryans is a defensive head coach. Before he took over in Houston, he was the defensive coordinator in San Francisco, and his defenses are built on speed, pressure, and discipline. A defensive-minded head coach facing a quarterback making his first start for a new team is going to throw everything at him. Disguised coverages, simulated pressures, looks McCarthy has not seen on film with the Giants. That is the real test. Not whether he can throw the deep ball, we know he can. Whether he can see it coming before the snap, get the protection right, and get the ball out on time.

So, what does McCarthy need to do in Houston? Number one, protect the football. That is the whole reason this change happened. If he turns it over the way the offense did against the Saints, all the energy goes away fast. Number two, start fast. Against the Saints, he threw an interception on his first drive. On the road, in his first start as a Giant, he needs a clean opening series to settle in. Number three, use his legs, but wisely. His mobility is a weapon, especially behind a line that has been banged up, with Andrew Thomas’s status still in question and rookie J.C. Davis ready if needed. And number four, feed Nabers and Likely. Those two have carried this passing game, and Likely has already shown real chemistry with McCarthy.

And let me give you the honest risk too. This is McCarthy’s first start for a team he joined less than a month ago, on the road, against an offense that can score. Harbaugh himself said they are still building an offense McCarthy has a feel for. There will be mistakes. There will be plays where he holds the ball too long or misreads a coverage. The question is not whether he will be perfect. It is whether he can bring the same energy for 60 minutes that he brought for 11.

Giants fans know how this can go. In 2004, Tom Coughlin turned the offense over to a young Eli Manning in the middle of the season, with the Giants in the playoff race. It was rough at first. Eli took his lumps, the losses piled up, and plenty of people questioned the timing. But that experience built the quarterback who would win two Super Bowls. Now, McCarthy is not a rookie, and this is not the same situation. But the lesson stands. When you hand the ball to a young quarterback, you have to be ready for some bumps, and you have to judge him over weeks, not one afternoon. If Harbaugh made this call, he made it knowing that.

Here is what is on the line. The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East. After Houston, they get the bye. Then they go to Philadelphia, against an Eagles team without Jalen Hurts, and then they come home to face Washington. If McCarthy wins in Houston, the Giants go into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, with a new quarterback, real momentum, and two division games coming against teams that are reeling. That is a dream setup. And if it goes the other way, the Giants still have the bye to regroup, and a veteran quarterback who has already won games for them waiting on the sideline.

Harbaugh made the bold call. He trusted his eyes, he trusted his team, and he trusted a quarterback who came to New York with something to prove. I love it. I was wrong about the timing, but I am all in on the decision. And I still believe what I have been saying all season. This team can go all the way.
