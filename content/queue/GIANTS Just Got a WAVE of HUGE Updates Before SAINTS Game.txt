A wave of updates just hit the Giants before the Saints game, and some of them are going to make you nervous while others should make you smile. Five Giants did not practice on Wednesday or Thursday. Abdul Carter, the young star pass rusher, with a calf. Both starting offensive tackles, Andrew Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor. And two pieces of the secondary, rookie corner Colton Hood and safety Tyler Nubin. But on the other side of that same report, a safety who was ruled out last week practiced fully on Thursday, the running back room is healthy, and the Saints’ own top receiver, Chris Olave, did not practice either day. Folks, this is the injury report that is going to shape Sunday at MetLife, so today I am going to walk through every name, tell you who steps in if the worst happens, and explain exactly what I will be watching for on Friday. Let’s get into it.

First, a quick reminder of how to read this report, because it matters. A DNP means the player did not participate. Limited means he got some work in. Full means he practiced without restrictions. And right now, this report only covers Wednesday and Thursday. The Friday column is still empty, and so is the game status column. That means nobody has been ruled out. Nobody is listed as doubtful. Nobody is officially questionable yet. So before anybody panics, understand that Friday is the day that tells us the most. That said, two straight days without practice for five players is not something I am going to brush past.

Let’s start with the name that caught my attention most, Abdul Carter. Calf injury, did not practice Wednesday, did not practice Thursday. Now, John Harbaugh told reporters earlier this week that Carter was doing very well, and I believe him. But I want you to understand why this one matters so much. Look at what has happened at outside linebacker this season. Brian Burns went on injured reserve back on September 29th with his torn ACL. Chauncey Golston went on injured reserve on October 10th with his neck. The Giants have been working that position nonstop. Jihad Ward was signed off Minnesota’s practice squad on October 1st. Trace Ford, the practice squad edge rusher this channel has been championing for weeks, was elevated for game days on October 3rd and again on October 10th. Jadeveon Clowney was added. Dawuane Smoot came in as a practice squad veteran on October 13th after the Yannick Ngakoue workout. Folks, that is not what a team does when everything is fine at a position. That is a team patching holes, week after week, just to keep the rotation alive.

So when Carter shows up with a calf and misses two straight days, I start asking the obvious question. If he cannot go, who is left? Here is how I see it. Kayvon Thibodeaux becomes the lead edge rusher, and remember, Harbaugh just told the media they are underestimating what Thibodeaux brings to the table. Clowney takes a bigger share of the snaps, with his edge-setting experience from Baltimore. Jihad Ward rotates in. And Trace Ford, who has earned game-day elevations twice already, could get his biggest opportunity yet. Smoot is the emergency depth if the Giants need another body. And folks, this is also where Dennard Wilson’s blitz-heavy approach becomes even more valuable. If the four-man rush is missing its best player, Wilson can manufacture pressure with blitzes, like the ones that let nickel corner Andru Phillips record two sacks in Washington. I am not saying Carter is out. A calf can turn around quickly. But I want to see him at least limited on Friday, because the margin for error at that position is thin.

And let me add one more thought on how the defensive game plan could change without Carter at full strength. When your best edge rusher is missing, the pressure has to come from somewhere else, and the Giants have two answers. The first is the interior. Daron Payne can collapse the pocket from the inside, and when the middle caves in, a quarterback cannot step up away from the edge rushers. That matters against a young quarterback like Tyler Shough, who has been playing well but has not seen every pressure look this league has. The second answer is Arvell Reese. The rookie linebacker just recorded his first career sack in Washington, he leads the entire league in solo tackles, and he is the only Giant in the last 30 seasons to reach 40 tackles through his first five career games. Dennard Wilson can send Reese on delayed blitzes, line him up on the edge, or drop him into coverage, and the quarterback never knows which one is coming. A defense without Carter is a lesser defense, no question. But it is not a defense without answers.

Now the offensive line, and this is the part that really made me stop. Two offensive tackles on the same report, and neither one practiced on Wednesday or Thursday. Jermaine Eluemunor, the right tackle, with a calf, after leaving the Washington game. And Andrew Thomas, the left tackle, with a groin. And Thomas is not new to this list. He was in the headlines of the week four injury report too, with that same groin. So this is not a one-off. It has been sitting in the background for weeks. Now, we have learned all season that with Thomas, Friday is the day that matters. He has missed Wednesdays before and played on Sunday. But missing Thursday too is a different story.

So who plays tackle if both are out? Harbaugh was asked about this earlier in the week, and he talked about two young tackles who could be pressed into action. He said he has seen a lot of diligent work, that they are both very good players trying to prove themselves in this league, and that if and when they get the chance, they will be very determined to play well. And here is something worth thinking about, folks. Rookie first-round pick Francis Mauigoa has been lining up at guard, but he played tackle at Miami. If the Giants are desperate, sliding him outside is at least an option the coaches could consider. I have no idea how the staff would actually line it up, and I am not going to pretend I do. But I can tell you why it matters so much this week. The Giants just decided to lean into a shotgun, tempo offense built around Jameis Winston. That kind of offense needs a clean pocket. If both starting tackles are out, the quick passing game becomes even more important, because Winston cannot hold the ball and wait for deep routes to develop. Expect more quick throws to Malik Nabers, Isaiah Likely, and Breece Hall out of the backfield, and maybe some help from the tight ends and backs in pass protection. The Giants also have some interior depth they have been building, with Kendrick Green added as a practice squad veteran back on October 2nd and Josh Gesky added to the practice squad on October 14th.

And let me be honest about Thomas, because this is a pattern Giants fans know too well. He is a talented left tackle, one of the best pass protectors in the league when he is healthy, but he has dealt with injuries before, including a foot injury that ended his 2024 season. That is why the staff has been so careful managing him with rest days all season. The goal is not to win one practice. The goal is to have Andrew Thomas healthy in December and January, when the games matter most. So if the Giants decide to hold him out against a 1 and 4 Saints team to protect him for the long run, I would understand that decision, even if it makes Sunday a little tougher.

And think about how this connects to the tempo offense. In a no-huddle attack, the linemen do not get to rest between plays, and fatigue becomes a real factor. With backup tackles in the game, the coaches might want to mix in more huddle drives than usual, use extra tight ends to help on the edges, and keep the play calls simple enough that a young tackle is not getting stressed by a complicated protection scheme. Breece Hall and Cam Skattebo will have to be sharp in blitz pickup too. The Saints will absolutely look at two backup tackles and send pressure early to test them. The Giants need an answer ready, whether that is a quick slant to Nabers, a screen to Hall, or a chip block from Likely before he releases into his route. This is where Matt Nagy earns his money.

Now the back end of the defense. Colton Hood, the rookie cornerback, has a concussion and did not practice either day. Hood was the Giants’ second-round pick, number 37 overall back in April, so he is a young player with a big future. But a concussion is a concussion. It follows the league protocol, it takes as long as it takes, and I am not a doctor, so I am not going to guess the timeline. Tyler Nubin has a neck injury and did not practice either day. And Greg Newsome II has a groin and was limited both Wednesday and Thursday. So that is a corner, a safety, and another corner all on the same report.

But here is the good news in the secondary, and it is real. Jason Pinnock, who has been dealing with his own concussion, was limited on Wednesday and then a full participant on Thursday. And remember, Pinnock was ruled out for the Washington game. So seeing him practice fully tells me he is very close to being back. If Nubin cannot go, Pinnock and Ar’Darius Washington, who made some impressive plays in Washington, can step in next to Jevon Holland. Elijah Campbell, with a thumb injury, was full both days too. And at corner, the Giants still have Joey Porter Jr., Deonte Banks, and Andru Phillips. Remember, this position group has been dealing with injuries all season. Paulson Adebo went on injured reserve back on September 18th. So depth has already been tested, and the Giants have survived it.

Now, the limited group, the names to monitor. Malik Harrison, the linebacker who was just signed to the active roster on October 13th as a special teams contributor, had a dash next to his name on Wednesday and was limited on Thursday with a groin. Theo Johnson was limited both days with a neck injury. Limited is better than not practicing at all, but it is not the same as full go. And Theo matters more than people realize right now. He has been making important catches in back-to-back games, and he has turned into a weapon on kickoff coverage. If the offensive tackles are banged up, a healthy tight end who can help in pass protection is extra valuable.

Now for the better news, because there is plenty of it, especially on the offensive skill side. Najee Harris, with a ribs injury, was a full participant on Wednesday and Thursday. Charlie Jones, the return man, has an Achilles listing, and I know that word makes everybody nervous, but he was a full participant on Thursday. And Darnell Mooney, with a neck injury, went from limited on Wednesday to full on Thursday, which is exactly the direction you want. And here is the best part, folks. Look at who is not on this report at all. Malik Nabers is not on it. Isaiah Likely is not on it. Remember, two weeks ago, both of them were dealing with knee issues and missing practice. Now they are off the report entirely after becoming only the second receiver and tight end duo in NFL history to each post eleven catches, 90 yards, and a touchdown in the same game. Add Breece Hall, the newest weapon in the backfield, and Cam Skattebo, and this offense has every major skill player available. If the line can hold up, the weapons are there. And with Jameis Winston coming off back-to-back three-touchdown games, the first Giant to do that since Eli Manning in 2016, this is the healthiest and most dangerous group of playmakers he has had since he took over.

Now, you cannot only look at one side, so I went through the Saints report too, and it is not exactly clean over there either. Chris Olave, the Saints’ top receiver, is dealing with an illness and did not practice Wednesday or Thursday. Folks, this matters a lot. Earlier this week, John Harbaugh specifically pointed to the connection between young quarterback Tyler Shough and Olave, saying those two guys are a connection right now. If Olave is limited or out, the Saints’ passing game loses its best weapon. Defensive lineman Nathan Shepherd is also dealing with an illness and did not practice either day. And linebacker Kaden Elliss, with a calf, did not practice either. An illness is a different kind of situation than a calf or a groin. Players can bounce back from a bug in a day or two, or it can spread through a locker room. Either way, it is something to watch on Friday.

But do not think the Saints are falling apart. Tyler Shough has a left hand injury listed, but he was a full participant both days, so the young quarterback is good to go. A couple of their other players with ankle and toe injuries moved from limited to full during the week. So the Saints have their own list, but their quarterback is healthy, and that means this is still a real football game. At 1 and 4, New Orleans is a team with nothing to lose, and Harbaugh has been telling his players all week that this is a hungry, talented, well-coached opponent.

So when I put it all together, here is how I see it. The Giants’ concerns are concentrated in three places. The edge, with Abdul Carter. The offensive tackles, with Andrew Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor. And the secondary, with Colton Hood, Tyler Nubin, and Greg Newsome. Those are not random spots, folks. Those are exactly the spots where the Giants have already been moving bodies through the practice squad all season. That is the part that sticks with me. Not because I think this season is going sideways, the Giants are 3 and 1 and 1 and alone in first place in the NFC East, but because the depth has already been tested, and it is about to be tested again.

At the same time, the Saints are 1 and 4 with their own key players missing practice, including their best receiver. So I would not call this game automatic, but I would call it very winnable. And this injury report will decide how comfortable it gets.

Here is exactly what I will be watching for on Friday. Number one, Abdul Carter. I want to see him get on the field, even limited, because two straight missed days with a calf makes me want to see movement. Number two, the tackles. If Andrew Thomas or Jermaine Eluemunor gets back to limited work, I will feel a lot better about the tempo offense having the protection it needs. If both stay out, expect the Giants to get creative up front and lean even harder on quick throws. Number three, Colton Hood and Tyler Nubin, and whether Jason Pinnock gets fully cleared to take Nubin’s spot. And number four, Chris Olave on the other side. If Olave cannot go, the Saints’ offense loses its most dangerous connection. If I see at least two of those Giants names move up to limited, I will feel great. If all five are still out on Friday, the coaches are going to have to get creative, and that is where this roster’s depth gets another real test.

And here is one more reason for perspective. After the Saints, the Giants travel to Houston, and then they get their week eight bye. That bye is coming at the perfect time for a roster this banged up. If the Giants can get through these next two games with their depth players holding the fort, they will have an extra week to get Carter, Thomas, Eluemunor, Hood, and Nubin healthy before the second half of the season, when the NFC East race really heats up. So every decision the coaches make this week has to balance two things, winning on Sunday and protecting the roster for November and December. A smart staff manages both, and I trust this one to do it.

But folks, remember what this team has done all season. They won without Jackson Dart. They won without Brian Burns. They came back from 14 down in the fourth quarter on the road with a banged-up roster and walked off the field without a loss, something this franchise had failed to do 230 straight times since 1970. Next man up is not a slogan in this building. It is the identity. And with the Eagles and Cowboys both at 2 and 3, and Washington at 1 and 3 and 1, a win on Sunday would keep the Giants in control of the NFC East heading into Houston and the bye week. I still believe what I have been saying all season, injuries and all. This team can go all the way.
