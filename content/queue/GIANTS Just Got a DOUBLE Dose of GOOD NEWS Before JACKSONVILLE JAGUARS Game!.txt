The New York Giants just got a double dose of good news before the Jacksonville game, and it is all about the little things that decide close games. First, the Giants completely reshaped their special teams this week, and when you dig into the numbers on the new guys, it is a lot more impressive than the headlines make it sound. Second, the front office is still hunting for upgrades. They brought in a veteran running back for a workout, and they made a statement by not bringing in a kicker. Four roster moves, one workout that has everybody asking questions, and one decision they chose not to make. Let’s break it all down, because Sunday is going to be decided by details like these.

First, let’s set the stage. The Giants are 5-3-1 and still lead the NFC East. They lost a close one to Washington, 24-21, last Thursday night. And now Jacksonville comes to town at 7-2, fresh off a 40-23 win over Tennessee. The game is Sunday, November 22, at 4:25 Eastern on CBS at MetLife Stadium. It was flexed out of the 1:00 window, so the whole country is going to be watching.

Because of that Thursday night game, the Giants had close to a week and a half between games. And when a team has that much time, it can take a hard look at its roster. That is exactly what happened on Monday. The Giants made four moves. Two players were released. Two players were signed. On the surface, that sounds small. But when you look at the details, you see a team fixing a problem it could not ignore anymore.

Dose number one: a special teams overhaul with real numbers behind it.

The biggest problem was at returner, and that means Charlie Jones. I want to thank him for what he gave the Giants. But when you are a return specialist and you cannot hold on to the football, a change has to come. Jones fumbled on a kickoff return against New Orleans, and then he fumbled again on a punt return in Philadelphia. Both times, the ball ended up as a turnover. He also has been dealing with a hand injury that has reportedly had him in a cast or a splint at times. Too many times the ball hit the turf, and a turnover on special teams is one of the worst things that can happen to a team. It hands the other side a short field and kills your offense’s chance to build any momentum. There is a chance Jones ends up back on the practice squad, and if that happens, we will let you know. But this was a move that had to happen.

Think about how much a returner matters in a game like Sunday’s. Every time the Giants start a drive at their own 35 instead of their own 20, the offense has an easier day. And every turnover does the exact opposite.

So the replacement is a name some of you will remember. Xavier Gipson. He was with the Giants in the preseason and got cut when the roster went down to 53. He then landed on the Eagles’ practice squad. And now he is back on the Giants’ active roster, for his third stint with the team.

Let’s look at the full picture on Gipson, the good and the bad.

As a receiver, he has some experience. He has 29 career catches in 41 games for 274 yards and one touchdown. And he had a highlight play in the preseason this year, when he took a ball up the right sideline and scored. Harbaugh said this week that Gipson will likely do some things on offense too, so that receiving experience could come into play.

But the main reason he is here is the return game. And on kick returns, his numbers are really good. He has 51 career kick returns for 1,320 yards. That is a 25.9-yard average. No touchdowns, but no muffed kick returns either. That clean record on kickoffs is the part of his résumé I would hold on to the most. A returner with a long track record of not muffing kicks is a lot more valuable than one who gets you a few extra yards but puts the ball on the ground.

The punt return side is where it gets more complicated. Gipson has 74 career punt returns for 671 yards, a 9.1-yard average. But according to Pro Football Focus, he also has six muffed punts in his career. And on his career page, he has 12 total fumbles. So going from a returner who fumbled twice and got released to a returner with 12 career fumbles is a real risk. I am not going to pretend otherwise.

My opinion, not a report: I think the Giants may lean on Gipson more confidently on kickoffs than on punts, at least at first. On punts, the main job is simple. Catch it. Fair catch when you have to. Do not try to do too much. If he does that, he gives the Giants exactly what they need. He has some juice, he can turn on the burners, and if he holds on to the ball, he could give this offense a spark. Harbaugh said it himself. Maybe we can spark our return game a little bit. It’s up to him to come in here and make us right.

The second player the Giants released was Nico Reed. They signed him before the week two game against the Rams, when Paulson Adebo went to injured reserve. Reed did not play a single snap on defense, but he was a special teams contributor, with 33 special teams snaps. That is a real role, and it is why losing him matters a little. But the Giants replaced him with a player who does that job at a very high level.

And that is Tavierre Thomas. This is where the good news really kicks in.

Thomas is a longtime special teams ace who was on the Minnesota Vikings’ practice squad. Now he is on the Giants’ active roster. He has been in the NFL since 2018, so he is a veteran. He has 22 career starts on defense, but his bread and butter is special teams. He has played 2,310 career special teams snaps. That is a massive number. He led the Vikings with 19 special teams tackles in 2025.

And the grades back it up. His Pro Football Focus special teams grades have been above 80 again and again. Season after season: 86.5, 84.4, 87.0, 90.5, and 84.4. That is not a guy who had one good year. That is a guy who has been elite at this job for years. A reporter who covers the Vikings called him a great special teams player.

What I like most about Thomas is his range. He can help in kick coverage. He can help in punt coverage. He can play on the kick return team, the punt return team, and the field goal block unit. He can be a gunner and he can help cover kicks. The only thing he does not do is kick. A player like that makes the whole special teams unit better, because he can change roles from play to play depending on what the opponent shows.

And this matters right now, because the injury report showed Nick Jones did not practice with a knee injury. Jones has been one of the Giants’ gunners on punts, along with Elijah Campbell. Both of them have struggled with missed tackles and penalties this season, with special teams grades below 60, according to the reported analysis. ESPN’s Jordan Raanan suggested Thomas could step in as a gunner. Going from a sub-60 gunner to a guy who has graded in the mid-80s for five straight seasons is a huge upgrade at a spot most fans never think about.

So when you put dose number one together, the Giants moved on from a returner who could not hold the ball, brought back a returner with a clean record on kickoffs, and added one of the most consistent special teams players in the league. Harbaugh came up as a special teams coach. You knew he was not going to let this phase stay broken for long.

Dose number two: the front office is still hunting, and it is still backing its kicker.

Let’s start with the move the Giants did not make. They did not work out a kicker.

There was a lot of thought that they might bring in a kicker this week, because Dominic Zvada has left points on the field in recent games. He missed a 33-yarder in the 24-21 loss to Washington. He missed three field goals in the first Washington game, the 27-27 tie. All six of his misses this season have come from the right hash. And with a week and a half between games, you would think the Giants would at least bring someone in to compete.

They did hold a workout on Monday. Just not for a kicker. Harbaugh keeps saying he is confident in Zvada, and now the Giants’ actions are backing up the words. He said this week that Zvada was dead on in practice, hitting the middle pole numerous times, including from what he called the “amazing, scary right hash.” And he said the kickers were in the building working on their own on Monday, when the team was off.

I understand the thinking. A kicker needs confidence, and a coach who keeps showing faith in him can help him get it back. Zvada has a big leg, with six makes from 50-plus this season, and he went four for four in Philadelphia. But I also want to be fair. When kicks cost you chances to win, you have to keep watching. The Giants have made their choice, and Sunday will tell us if it was the right one. If it comes down to a kick, everybody will be holding their breath.

Now, the workout that has everybody asking questions. Running back.

The Giants worked out Antonio Gibson. You know the name. He spent his early years in Washington and played for the Patriots last season. He did not play much, or all that well, last season. But he has a lot of experience. He has played 83 career games, with 787 carries, a 4.2-yard average, almost 3,300 rushing yards, and 24 touchdowns. He was a really good back early in his career, and he is still only 28.

So why bring him in? There are a few possible reasons, and I want to be clear that these are theories, not reports.

One, Tyrone Tracy is on injured reserve and was lined up to come back later in this stretch. If his return is going to take longer than people thought, the Giants may want another experienced back.

Two, pass protection. I thought Devin Singletary looked rough as a pass protector over the last few weeks. And against a Jacksonville defense that gets pressure on about 37 and a half percent of dropbacks without blitzing much, picking up the rush is going to be critical. A veteran back who can block could have value.

Three, depth behind Cam Skattebo. Skattebo practiced fully this week despite a hamstring issue, and he is the engine of this offense. He just ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns against Washington, plus 44 receiving yards. But he plays a physical, violent style, and you want insurance behind a guy like that in December. Desrosiers looked good in his debut against Philadelphia, with three carries for 20 yards, but that is a small sample.

And think about how this connects to the Washington loss. On the third-down quarterback crack sweep late in that game, Skattebo was not on the field. A lot of fans were furious about that. Harbaugh said afterward the design was fine and the blocking needed to be better. But part of building a smarter short-yardage plan is having the right bodies available. A veteran back who can run between the tackles and pick up a blitz gives the coaching staff more options in those exact moments, especially if Skattebo needs a breather. Again, my opinion, not a report.

Right now, it does not sound like the Giants are going to sign Gibson. If that changes, we will let you know. But the workout itself is the good news. It tells me this front office is not sitting still, even after a loss and even with a short window. They are looking for ways to get better at every spot.

And there is one more roster note from Harbaugh’s press conference this week that connects here. Paulson Adebo, who went to injured reserve early in the season, was a healthy scratch last game. Harbaugh said Adebo had a really good game against Houston, but with everyone healthy again, he moved into more of a backup and special teams role. And Harbaugh said he told Adebo directly, in my mind, you’re a starter in this league, and you’re going to have to start again. That tells you the Giants now have real depth in the secondary. When a guy who played well as a starter is your backup, that is a sign of a healthy roster heading into the stretch run.

So when I put all four moves and the workout together, I see a team fixing the mistakes that hurt it. They moved on from a returner who could not hold the ball. They added a proven special teams ace with elite grades. They brought back a returner with a clean record on kickoffs. They kept faith in their kicker. And they kicked the tires on a veteran running back to make sure they have options.

The risk is clear. Gipson has 12 career fumbles and six muffed punts, and that could bring the same problem right back. The reward is a faster, more confident returner who can flip field position for the offense, with a coverage unit behind him that just got a lot better.

And if you want proof of how much special teams has mattered this season, just look back at the games. In the 27-27 tie with Washington, the Giants missed three field goals. Make one of those, and the Giants would be 6-3 right now instead of 5-3-1, and the season series with Washington would be split instead of Washington holding the edge. In Philadelphia, Jones muffed a punt, and the Giants survived it only because the defense was dominant, with seven sacks and five three-and-outs. And last Thursday against Washington, the Giants had a missed 33-yard field goal, a 12-yard punt from Jordan Stout, and a kickoff that went out of bounds, all in a game they lost by three. That is the pattern. This team has been giving away points and field position in the third phase, and its defense has had to bail it out over and over.

That is why I think these moves are bigger than they look. The Giants’ defense is good enough to keep almost any opponent under 24 points. The offense, with McCarthy, Skattebo, Likely and Nabers, is good enough to score 20-plus on most days. When special teams is merely average, that formula wins a lot of football games. When special teams is a disaster, it loses close ones. The goal here is not to turn special teams into a weapon overnight. It is to stop the bleeding.

So here is my checklist for special teams on Sunday. Zero turnovers on returns. At least one punt downed or fair caught inside the 10 with Thomas leading the coverage. No kickoffs out of bounds. And every makeable field goal goes through. Check those four boxes, and I like the Giants’ chances a lot against anybody, Jacksonville included.

Now let’s connect it to Jacksonville. The Jaguars are 7-2. They score close to 30 points a game. Trevor Lawrence is playing at an MVP level. Their defense allows the second-fewest points per game in the league, and they just added Jordan Davis and Jessie Bates III through trades. This game is going to be close. And in close games, special teams can swing everything. One muffed punt, one long return allowed, one missed kick, and the game flips. The Giants cannot afford another turnover on a return, not against a defense that already averages about 1.8 takeaways per game on its own. These moves are about making sure that does not happen.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. A couple of weeks ago, I said the return game was a bigger problem than people realized. The Giants agreed and made the move. I said I would ride with Zvada through Jacksonville, and the Giants are riding with him too. I picked the Giants to beat Washington, and that one was wrong. I own it.

Here is my prediction for Sunday, and hold me to it. Gipson catches every punt cleanly and breaks one kick return past the 35. Tavierre Thomas makes at least one tackle on coverage inside the 20. Zvada goes perfect. Skattebo carries the offense. And the Giants beat Jacksonville to move to 6-3-1 in front of a national audience. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A front office that is not sitting still. A coach who knows special teams better than almost anyone in the league. A defense ranked fifth in yards allowed. A running back playing with fire. And a two-game lead in the division. These moves are not flashy, and nobody is going to put Tavierre Thomas on a billboard in Times Square. But they show a team paying attention to the small things. And the small things are what win football games in November and December.
