John Harbaugh made a decision that almost no head coach in the NFL would make. When the Tennessee Titans told the Giants that Jeffery Simmons was not available, Harbaugh did not accept the answer. He went over the general manager’s head and called the Titans’ owner, Amy Adams Strunk, directly. And here is the part that makes this story wild. That is the same franchise that wanted to hire Harbaugh back in January. The Titans reportedly had a plane ready for his interview when the Giants landed him. So, nine months later, the coach Tennessee could not get is on the phone with its owner, trying to pry away its best defensive player. According to Jonathan Jones, he got the same answer. Ownership had empowered general manager Mike Borgonzi to make football decisions, and that was that. A few days later, Simmons was traded to Chicago for two first-round picks and more. And now, with the deadline on November 10th, Harbaugh and the Giants have a new target, and the door is open. Adam Schefter reported that the Colts are now open to trading Jonathan Taylor. And Jones reported that the Giants and Minnesota appear to be the favorites if Taylor is moved. Let’s get into it.

Before I go any further, I owe you a receipt, and I want to give it to you right at the top. In my last video, I told you the Giants walked away from Simmons when the price got too high. That is how I framed it, and based on the fuller reporting from Jonathan Jones, that was not quite right. The Giants did not get outbid in a back-and-forth. They were told no. Schoen first reached out to Borgonzi, and Borgonzi told him, like he told every other team that asked, that Simmons was not available. Then Harbaugh called the owner and got the same answer. It was not until Chicago general manager Ryan Poles came in with an offer Tennessee could not ignore that the answer changed. So the Giants did not walk away from a price. They never got the chance to see the price. I got that wrong, and I want you to have the accurate version.

And I actually think the accurate version is a better story. Because it tells you two things at once. First, it tells you how badly the Giants wanted Simmons. Head coaches do not usually call other teams’ owners. It is not against any rule, but it is rare, and it sends a very clear message. The Giants are not casually checking in on players. They are hunting. And second, it tells you something important about how the trade deadline really works.

Here is the lesson. Sellers almost always say a player is not available, right up until the moment someone makes an offer they cannot refuse. The Titans were not shopping Simmons. And then Chicago sent two first-round picks and a fourth, and got a fifth back, and suddenly he was available. Not available is not a permanent answer. It is an opening position. And the same thing just happened with Jonathan Taylor. A week ago, the message out of Indianapolis seemed to be that Taylor was not going anywhere. Now, Schefter reports the Colts are open to trading him, multiple teams have reached out, and talks are active and ongoing.

So why did the Colts change their minds? Look at what is happening in Indianapolis. On October 19th, the Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard after a 2 and 4 start and a loss to a Titans team that had not won a game. They have already traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. This is a franchise that is starting over. And a 27-year-old running back, as great as he is, does not always fit the timeline of a rebuild. By the time the Colts are ready to contend again, Taylor may be past his best years. So selling while his value is high is a logical decision. I do not blame them at all.

Now, here is the piece of information that changes how I think about Taylor. Last time, I told you the contract question mattered, and that I wanted to know how long he would be a Giant. Well, I looked into it. Back in August, the Colts and Taylor agreed on a two-year, 44 million dollar extension. That means he is not a rental. He is under contract for this season and the next two. So if the Giants trade for him, they are getting a player who can help in 2026 and in the window that opens next year, when Jaxson Dart, Brian Burns, and Tremaine Edmunds are all healthy again. That matters a lot. A pure rental should never cost premium picks. A player who helps you for three playoff pushes is a different conversation.

And there is another detail from Jones that I think is huge. He reported that the Giants and the Vikings are viewed as the favorites. But he also noted that Minnesota cannot fit Taylor under its salary cap right now. He did not mention any cap issue for the Giants. Think about what that means. If one of the two favorites cannot make the money work without major restructuring, the path opens wider for New York. Cap space is a weapon at the deadline, and right now, the Giants appear to have it.

But I do not want anyone to think this is a two-team race. There has also been chatter connecting the Green Bay Packers to Taylor, with their run game struggling and uncertainty in their backfield. So even if Minnesota’s cap is a problem, the Giants could still face real competition. And competition is exactly what drives a price up. That is why the Giants need to decide their limit before the bidding gets emotional.

Jones also reported something that tells you this front office is not done. He said the Giants are still looking for help at both running back and defensive tackle, and league sources expect them to make a move before the deadline, even after missing out on Simmons. So when people ask whether the Giants are going to sit quietly through November 10th, the answer from the people who cover this closely is no.

Now, let me remind you of the price we know about. Tom Pelissero reported that the Colts already turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick for Taylor. I have seen people speculate that the Giants made that offer. That is possible. But we do not know who made it, and I am going to treat it as a guess, because that is all it is.

So, why would the Giants want Taylor so badly? Start with the backfield. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve with a hamstring, and he will miss at least four games. Cam Skattebo has carried a heavy load, and he felt his own hamstring grab in overtime in Houston. Then think about what Taylor would do to this offense. Add him to a group that already has Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely, and you have three playmakers who can stand up against almost any team’s top three. Defenses would have to choose between loading the box to stop Taylor and keeping safeties deep to handle Nabers. That is a nightmare for any defensive coordinator.

And it would make life a lot easier for J.J. McCarthy. A quarterback who stepped in for an injured starter benefits more than anyone from an elite run game. Defenses have to respect play action. Second downs become manageable. Third and long becomes rare. McCarthy would not have to win every game with his arm. Taylor would let the Giants control the clock, protect leads, and take pressure off a young quarterback who has handled a difficult situation really well.

And think about the calendar. December and January football is cold and physical. The weather turns, passing gets harder, and the team that can run the ball usually controls the game. Taylor is exactly the kind of back who can wear a defense down with physical runs for three quarters, and then break one for 70 yards in the fourth. That is the kind of player who wins in the cold.

But now, let me give you the other side, because it is real. Running backs, even great ones, have shorter shelf lives than players at most other positions. And Taylor has dealt with injuries before. He had ankle problems a few years ago that cost him time. He has been productive and durable more recently, but running backs take a beating every week. Any team trading for him has to account for that risk. A premium pick for a player at a high-contact position is a real gamble.

And now let me give you the counterargument to my own counterargument, because I think this one is really good, and it has made me rethink part of my position. As fans, we tend to overrate draft picks. Let me show you what I mean. Look at the Giants’ second-round picks going back to 2013. Johnathan Hankins. Weston Richburg. Landon Collins, who was a really good pick, a first-team All-Pro in 2016. Sterling Shepard, also a good pick. Dalvin Tomlinson. Will Hernandez. Xavier McKinney. Azeez Ojulari. Wan’Dale Robinson. John Michael Schmitz. Tyler Nubin.

There are some solid players in that group. Some good starters. A couple of players who earned big contracts somewhere. But how many of them became the kind of player Jonathan Taylor is? A rushing champion. A player who can change a playoff game by himself. Maybe you can argue for Collins at his peak. But for most of that list, the answer is no. When you trade a second-round pick, the dream is that it turns into a star. The reality is that it almost never does.

So I want to adjust my position a little, and I want to be clear about why. Before, I was most worried about what the Giants would be giving up. After looking at that list, I am more comfortable with the idea of trading a second-round pick for a proven star. The franchise has not always drafted well in that range, and there is a real argument that trading a pick for a player you already know is great is the smarter bet.

But I still have a line. For Taylor alone, my ideal deal is a second-round pick plus a mid-round pick, maybe a third, or the projected compensatory fourth. That is more than the offer the Colts reportedly turned down, it shows real interest, and it does not cross into first-round territory. My absolute ceiling is still one first-round pick, and I am not adding anything to that. If the Colts want a first and a second, I hang up.

And here is why I think the Giants might not have to go that high. As the deadline gets closer, the Colts’ leverage could shrink. If no team is willing to go to a first, Indianapolis has to decide whether to take a strong day two package or keep a star running back on a rebuilding team, and pay him for two more years after this one. That decision often tilts toward the buyer in the final hours. Remember, Minnesota may not be able to make the money work. That could take one bidder out of the room.

Now, let’s go back to Harbaugh, because I think the phone call deserves more attention. That call shows you that he is not satisfied with leading the division. He sees a real opportunity this season, and he is willing to swing big. And that should not surprise anyone who watched his long run in Baltimore. He has always been known as an aggressive coach. Aggressive on fourth downs. Aggressive in big moments. Aggressive in pushing his organization to get better. Calling an owner directly is bold, but it is consistent with who he is. He does not wait for opportunities to come to him. He goes after them.

And think about the backstory one more time, because I cannot get over it. Back in January, Tennessee was one of the teams chasing Harbaugh. Reports at the time said the Titans had a plane ready for his interview when the Giants closed the deal. He chose New York. And now, in his first season, he is the one calling Nashville, asking the owner who wanted to hire him to hand over her best defensive player. You cannot write that. It also tells you Harbaugh is not shy about using every relationship he has. He knew the people in that building. He knew who made the final call. He tried the direct route. It did not work this time. But that kind of fearlessness is exactly why the Giants hired him.

And imagine if it had worked. Picture Simmons anchoring the interior of a Giants defense that already ranks in the top eight in yards allowed. With Dexter Lawrence gone, Simmons would have filled the biggest hole on this roster. That would have turned a good defense into a frightening one. It did not happen. But the fact that the Giants tried tells you how much they believe in this season.

And it also tells you something about the partnership. A general manager who is comfortable with his head coach calling another team’s owner is a general manager who trusts his coach completely. Schoen and Harbaugh are on the same page, and that makes the Giants a much more dangerous team at the deadline. The coach brings urgency. The general manager brings the discipline to know where the value is. When it works, you get trades like the one for McCarthy, a 2027 fifth-round pick for a starting quarterback who just won on the road in overtime with zero turnovers.

But here is my concern. Aggressiveness is great, but it has to be disciplined. The Giants have a lot going for them right now. A winning record. A top-eight defense by yardage. A strong salary cap position. Key players coming back next season. The worst thing they could do is give up too much of the future chasing a short-term boost. Simmons was worth chasing because he is a long-term cornerstone, not a short-term patch. Taylor is worth chasing because he is under contract through 2028. But anything that is purely a rental should not cost premium picks.

And there is a roster balance question too. If the Giants spend big on Taylor, what does it mean for Skattebo? He has been a huge part of this offense, and the last thing you want is a crowded backfield where nobody gets enough work. I would expect the Giants to use them as a tandem, with Taylor as the lead back and Skattebo as the physical change of pace. That could be a great pairing. Taylor wears you down with vision and speed. Skattebo punishes you with contact. But the coaches would have to manage it. Skattebo has earned his role with the way he runs, and keeping him involved matters for the locker room as much as for the offense.

And when Tracy is healthy again, probably around week 13 against San Francisco, the staff would suddenly have three backs who all deserve touches. That is a good problem to have. But it is still a problem.

And then there is defensive tackle. Jones said the Giants are still looking there too. Simmons is gone, but the need is still real. With Lawrence gone, the Giants need a big body in the middle to hold up against the run, especially against a team like Philadelphia that wants to run on third and short. Remember, San Francisco got DeForest Buckner from Indianapolis for a 2027 fourth-round pick. That is the kind of price that fits what Schoen has described. I would not be surprised if the Giants end up adding a veteran defensive tackle in a smaller deal, even if Taylor goes somewhere else. That is my opinion, not a report.

So, let me put all of this together. Harbaugh’s decision to call the Titans’ owner did not land Simmons. But it told the whole league that the Giants are serious buyers. The Colts are now open to trading Taylor, the Giants are one of the favorites, and their biggest competitor may not have the cap space. Taylor is under contract through 2028, which makes him more than a rental. And the Giants’ history with second-round picks makes a strong case that a proven star is worth more than a lottery ticket. If the price stays reasonable, I am in. If it climbs into first-round territory with more on top, I am out.

And here is the timeline again. The Giants play in Philadelphia on November 8th. The deadline is November 10th. Every day that passes makes it harder for a new player to learn the offense before the Eagles game. If a Taylor deal is going to happen in time for Philadelphia, it probably has to happen this week.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Colts move Taylor, and whether Minnesota can make the money work. Number two, whether the Packers become a serious bidder. Number three, whether the Giants add a defensive tackle, with or without Taylor. Number four, Skattebo’s hamstring. And number five, whether Harbaugh makes any more phone calls that end up in a Jonathan Jones report.

And here is a prediction you can hold me to. I think the Giants make at least one trade before November 10th. I am not going to tell you it will be Taylor, because I just owned a receipt and I do not want another one this week. But I believe this front office is going to add someone.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East. They have a coach who calls owners, a general manager who knows the value of a pick, and a roster that has already proven it can win close games. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
