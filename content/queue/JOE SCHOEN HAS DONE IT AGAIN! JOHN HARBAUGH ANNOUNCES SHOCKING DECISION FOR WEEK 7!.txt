Tomorrow in Houston, John Harbaugh’s shocking week seven decision becomes real. J.J. McCarthy, the quarterback who had been a Giant for less than a month, takes the first snap. And here is what most people are missing. This decision does not just change who throws the football. It changes the entire game plan. How the Giants protect, how they run the ball, how they use Malik Nabers, even how the defense has to play, because a young quarterback in his first start cannot afford to be playing from behind against C.J. Stroud. And when you go through this matchup piece by piece, you see something else. Almost every key battle on Sunday runs through a player Joe Schoen found in a place nobody was looking. A fifth-round trade at quarterback. A day-three rookie tackle. A fifth-round running back who became the best pass protector on the team. A practice squad edge rusher who just earned his roster spot. Folks, Schoen has done it again, and tomorrow we find out if it pays off. Today, I am breaking down every key matchup for Giants versus Texans, what McCarthy’s start changes, and my score prediction. First, let me remind you why this decision was shocking, because the timeline matters. On Monday, Harbaugh said the staff was working through the quarterback question and honing in on a decision. He said he admires and respects Jameis Winston. He said McCarthy just got here a couple of weeks ago, and the Giants were still building an offense he has a feel for. Almost everybody, me included, read that as one more start for Winston, then a full switch after the bye. Then ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that McCarthy would start in Houston. Harbaugh threw out the safe plan and went with what he saw against the Saints. Eleven of 21 for 243 yards, all in the fourth quarter, the third-most passing yards by a backup quarterback in a single quarter since 1978, and 17 unanswered points. That is the shock. Not that McCarthy got a chance, but that Harbaugh did not wait.

So, what does that change on Sunday? Let’s start with the offense, because it is going to look different.

With Jameis Winston, the Giants built a shotgun, tempo offense, with a lot of quick decisions and a quarterback who would rip the ball into tight windows. That offense produced big numbers, including back-to-back three-touchdown games. But it also produced turnovers, including that interception against the Saints when the offense went into turbo and a ball Winston thought only his receiver could reach ended up with a defensive back. With McCarthy, I expect the offense to lean into what he does best. More movement. Bootlegs and rollouts that get him outside the pocket. Read-option looks that make a defense account for his legs. And deep shots off play action, because McCarthy wants to attack downfield, and against the Saints he averaged close to 12 yards per attempt. Now, I want to be clear. That is my expectation, not something Harbaugh has announced. But when you have a quarterback with a big arm and real mobility, you build around it. And Harbaugh has seen this before in Baltimore, building an offense around a mobile young quarterback.

Now, the matchups. And the first one is the biggest. McCarthy against DeMeco Ryans.

Ryans is a defensive head coach. Before he took over in Houston, he was the defensive coordinator in San Francisco, and his defenses are built on speed, discipline, and pressure. He will have studied every snap of McCarthy’s fourth quarter against the Saints, and every snap of his time in Minnesota. And he is going to try to confuse him. Disguised coverages. Simulated pressures, where it looks like a blitz and then defenders drop back. Rotations after the snap. Looks McCarthy has not seen in a Giants uniform. That is the test for a quarterback in his first start for a new team. Not whether he can throw the deep ball. Whether he can recognize what is coming before the snap and get the ball out on time.

And add the crowd. NRG Stadium is going to be loud, with a Texans team that has won two straight and smells a chance to climb back into the race. On the road, communication gets harder. Silent counts, hand signals, checks at the line that have to be relayed over the noise. For a quarterback who has been in this offense for less than a month, that is one more layer of difficulty. It is exactly why the first few drives matter so much. If the Giants can quiet that building early with a clean drive or two, everything gets easier.

So, how do the Giants help him? First, give him easy answers early. Screens, quick throws, a couple of designed runs, plays where the read is simple and the ball comes out fast. Let him complete a few passes, feel the speed of the game, and settle in. Second, use the running backs as outlets. Tyrone Tracy Jr. was the best pass protector on the entire roster against the Saints, with an 80.8 pass-blocking grade, and he can catch, as he showed on that 17-yard touchdown out of the backfield. A back who can pick up a blitz and then leak out for a check-down is a young quarterback’s best friend. Third, use play action, even if the run game has struggled. Defenses still have to respect the fake, and it buys McCarthy an extra beat to read the field.

The second matchup. Malik Nabers against the Texans secondary. Nabers is on a heater. Three straight 100-yard games. Twelve catches for 165 yards against the Saints. The second-most receptions in NFL history through 25 career games, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. But Houston has one of the better secondaries the Giants will face this year, and they are not going to let him run free. Expect safety help shaded his way, and physical coverage at the line. So, the Giants have to get creative. Move Nabers around. Put him in motion. Line him up in the slot, in bunch formations, anywhere that makes it harder for the defense to jam him or double him. And if Houston commits two defenders to Nabers, someone else has to win. Isaiah Likely, who already has real chemistry with McCarthy after catching the two-point conversion and the 24-yard touchdown against the Saints. Charlie Jones, who made that 49-yard catch. Malachi Fields. And Darnell Mooney, if his neck allows him to play. If the secondary weapons win early, Nabers gets his chances later.

The third matchup. The Giants offensive line against Houston’s pass rush. This is the one that worries me most. Andrew Thomas has been playing through an injury, and his status has been a question all week. Jermaine Eluemunor did not play against the Saints. Jon Runyan Jr. has struggled in the run game. And Brian Hudson has moved ahead of John Michael Schmitz at center. That is a lot of moving parts in front of a quarterback making his first start. But here is where the Schoen pieces come in again. Rookie tackle J.C. Davis, a day-three pick, played the final 25 snaps against the Saints and did not allow a single pressure, with a 78.8 pass-blocking grade, second only to Thomas. If Thomas is limited, Davis is ready. And McCarthy’s mobility helps here too. A quarterback who can escape a collapsing pocket turns a sack into a scramble, and we saw that on his 28-yard run on the final drive against New Orleans.

The fourth matchup. The Giants pass rush against C.J. Stroud. And this might decide the game. Stroud is second in the NFL in passing yards with 1,714 through six weeks. He was the Offensive Rookie of the Year in 2023, and he reads defenses fast. If he gets time, he will pick this secondary apart. So, the rush has to get home. Kayvon Thibodeaux has the highest pass-rush win rate on the team over the last two weeks, even without a sack to show for it. Abdul Carter is a game-wrecker when he is healthy. And now Trace Ford, the edge rusher who was on the reserve/future list in January and earned three straight game-day elevations, gets his first game as a full member of the active roster. Dawuane Smoot adds veteran depth. And Dennard Wilson will bring pressure from everywhere. Arvell Reese on delayed blitzes. Andru Phillips off the slot, the way he got two sacks in Washington. The goal is simple. Speed up Stroud’s clock, and make him throw before his receivers are open.

The fifth matchup. The Giants secondary against Houston’s weapons. And this one has a storyline. Marvin Harrison Jr. just made his Texans debut after being traded from Arizona, with three catches for 34 yards, all of them for first downs. His last game before the trade was at MetLife, against these same Giants corners. And Tank Dell is coming back on Sunday after not playing since December 2024 because of a serious knee injury. DeMeco Ryans said he is not putting huge expectations on Dell. But a healthy Dell with his speed, next to Harrison Jr., gives Stroud options he has not had all season. The Giants have to answer with Joey Porter Jr., Deonte Banks, and Andru Phillips at corner, and Jevon Holland and Ar’Darius Washington at safety. Washington, by the way, had an 81.4 grade against the Saints, the second-highest on the defense. And keep an eye on the final injury report, because Colton Hood and Greg Newsome II were both dealing with concussions after the Saints game. If either one cannot go, the depth gets tested again.

The sixth matchup. The run defense. This is the one that cost the Giants against the Saints. New Orleans ran 38 times for 175 yards. And when the run defense fails, nothing else matters. The pass rush cannot pin its ears back, the safeties have to creep down, and Stroud gets play action against a defense that is guessing. D.J. Reader has been a monster in the middle, with seven tackles and seven stops against the Saints. When he is on the field, the Giants allow about 4.5 yards per carry. When he is off, it is 5.2. But he cannot do it alone. Since Dexter Lawrence left this building, the Giants have been searching for more help on the interior, and they worked out nose tackles this week. Shelby Harris and Darius Alexander have to step up. If the Giants can make Houston one-dimensional, the pass rush takes over.

And do not forget special teams. The fumble on the kickoff return before halftime against the Saints started a 17-point swing. On the road, against a team that has won two straight on close finishes, including a walk-off field goal in London, every hidden yard matters. Dominic Zvada answered his rough day in Washington with a clutch 41-yarder against the Saints. Jordan Stout has been flipping the field all season. If the Giants win the special teams battle and protect the ball in the return game, that alone could swing a close game.

And then there is the coaching battle. Harbaugh against Ryans. Harbaugh is a Super Bowl-winning head coach with nearly two decades of experience managing games like this one. He knows when to be aggressive on fourth down, when to take the points, and how to handle the clock at the end of a half. Ryans is one of the brightest defensive minds in the league, a former linebacker who coaches with energy and has his team playing hard. In a close game, the coach who makes the better adjustments at halftime usually wins. Against the Saints, the Giants did not stop the opening drive of the third quarter, and that turned a tie game into a deficit. That cannot happen again tomorrow.

Now, let’s bring it back to Joe Schoen, because when you look at that list of matchups, his fingerprints are everywhere. McCarthy at quarterback, a fifth-round trade. J.C. Davis at tackle if needed, a day-three pick. Tracy in the backfield, a fifth-round pick in 2024. Trace Ford on the edge, a practice squad player Schoen kept close. Nabers, the pick Schoen made over McCarthy in 2024. Carter, the third overall pick in 2025. Not every move has worked. The Darius Slayton contract did not. Najee Harris was released two months after signing. Jihad Ward lasted less than three weeks. But the pieces that matter tomorrow are mostly his good ones. That is what I mean when I say Schoen has done it again.

And there is one more irony in this game. Back in 2025, the trade Schoen made to get back into the first round for Jackson Dart was with the Houston Texans. Now the Giants face Houston, with Dart out for the rest of the regular season, and the quarterback in his place is the one Schoen passed on in 2024 and got for a fifth-round pick. This league is wild.

Now, the risks. I always give you both sides. This is McCarthy’s first start for a team he joined less than a month ago. He threw an interception on his first drive against the Saints. A defensive head coach is going to throw everything at him. The offensive line is banged up. The run game has been one of the weakest parts of this team all season. And the Texans are playing their best football of the year. If the Giants turn the ball over the way they did against New Orleans, four times, they will not win this game. That is the reality.

But here is the other reality. This team does not quit. Down 14 in the fourth quarter in Washington, they came back and walked out without a loss. Down 24 against the Saints, they came back to within one play. And Jameis Winston, a quarterback who has already won games for this team, is on the sideline if anything goes wrong. Harbaugh has a safety net that most teams do not have.

And I want to talk about Winston’s role tomorrow, because I think it is bigger than people realize. He is not just a backup standing on the sideline. He is the veteran who knows this offense better than anybody in that quarterback room. After every series, he can be the one sitting next to McCarthy on the bench, telling him what the safeties did, what coverage Houston rolled into, where the blitz came from. That is exactly what Winston did against the Saints after he was pulled. He said they were always communicating, play by play, drive by drive. A veteran who has been through every kind of game you can imagine, helping a young quarterback in his first start, is a real advantage. And if something goes wrong, he has already shown he can win games for this team.

And think about what this game means for McCarthy personally. In Minnesota, he was the tenth overall pick who missed his rookie season with a knee injury, watched Sam Darnold win 14 games in his place, then struggled and got hurt when he finally got his chance. By this fall, the Vikings moved him for a fifth-round pick. Tomorrow is his first start in a new city, for a team that believes in him, in a game that matters. A good performance does not just help the Giants. It rewrites the story of his career. And Giants fans know a thing or two about backup quarterbacks rewriting their stories. In 1990, Jeff Hostetler stepped in for an injured Phil Simms late in the season and ended up leading this franchise to a Super Bowl title. I am not saying McCarthy is Hostetler. I am saying nobody should be surprised when a quarterback with something to prove plays with an edge.

So, here is what I will be watching tomorrow. Number one, McCarthy’s first two drives. Does he look calm? Does the offense look built for him? Number two, the turnover column. Zero has to be the goal. Number three, the pass rush. Can Thibodeaux, Carter, and Ford make Stroud uncomfortable? And number four, the run defense in the first quarter. If the Giants stop the run early, they control this game.

And now, my prediction. I have gone back and forth on this all week, and I want to be honest about it. The Texans are hot, they are at home, and Stroud is a problem. But I think McCarthy’s energy, a week of preparation, and a Giants defense that has to be sick of hearing about 175 rushing yards add up to a close, physical game. My prediction is Giants 24, Texans 21. I think McCarthy throws two touchdowns, one of them to Nabers, the run defense holds up better than it did last week, and somebody on that defense makes the play that seals it late. That is my pick. And folks, think about what a win means. The Giants go into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East. The Eagles are without Jalen Hurts for the season. The Cowboys are 2 and 4. And after the bye, the Giants have two division games, at Philadelphia and at home against Washington. A win tomorrow, and this team takes control of the division with a new quarterback and real momentum. A loss, and it is three games without a win heading into the bye. That is how big Sunday is.

Harbaugh made the bold call. Schoen built the roster that made it possible. Now it is on the players. I believe in this team, and I have said it all season. This team can go all the way.
