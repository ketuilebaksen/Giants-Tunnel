Were the New York Giants robbed on Thursday night? One flag. Thirty-three yards. A pass interference call on Deonte Banks with the Giants leading 21-17 in the final two minutes, and one play later, Stefon Diggs was in the end zone and the Giants had lost 24-21. Fans all over social media are screaming rigged. So today, we are going to freeze the replay, look at that flag, and answer the question for real. Were the Giants robbed by the officials, or did they rob themselves long before that flag ever hit the ground? Stick with me, because the answer is going to make you angry either way.

What is up, Big Blue. Welcome back to the tunnel. I’m James, and this is New York Giants Tunnel, your home for everything Giants. If you are new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on the notifications, because it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now: was the Deonte Banks flag pass interference, yes or no? I want your verdict before you hear mine.

Let’s start with the play everybody is talking about.

Washington got the ball back trailing 21-17, at its own 29, with 1:46 left and timeouts in its pocket. Jayden Daniels dropped back and went deep for Treylon Burks. Banks was in coverage, there was contact, the ball fell incomplete, and the flag came out. Pass interference. Thirty-three yards. Just like that, Washington was in Giants territory.

Now, was it pass interference? Let me give you both sides, because there are two sides here.

The case for the Giants. Some people watching the broadcast did not think it was pass interference. The argument is that both players were fighting for position, Burks was doing his share of the hand-fighting, and Banks was a defensive back staying in the play and trying to make a play on the ball. If you are a Giants fan, you look at that and you say, that is football. That is two guys battling. Let them play.

The case for the officials. Banks was beaten, or close to it, and there was enough contact before the ball arrived that an official in that spot is going to call it nine times out of ten. And here is the part that matters. When a defensive back is in man coverage, and he feels the receiver getting on top of him, the natural reaction is to grab. One analyst said it perfectly. He hates man-to-man in too many situations for exactly this reason. If the corner gets beat, he panics, and the hands come out.

So here is my verdict, and I will label it clearly. My opinion, not a report: it was a borderline call, the kind that can go either way, and I understand why Giants fans are furious about it. But it was not a robbery. It was a judgment call on a play where Banks put himself in a position to get flagged. And I want to be fair to Banks, because I am not putting this whole loss on him. One play does not lose a football game. Not when you look at everything that happened before it.

And that is the real point of this video. Because the question that should make every Giants fan furious is not whether that was pass interference. The real question is this: how did Washington still have the ball with a chance to win the game in the first place?

Let me walk you through the fourth quarter, because it is unbelievable when you lay it all out.

At 8:13 of the fourth, Isaiah Likely took a McCarthy throw 66 yards to the house. Giants up 21-17. Likely finished with nine catches for 127 yards, a career high.

Now look at Washington’s first three possessions of the fourth quarter. Turnover on downs. Interception. Interception. Three straight possessions, and the Giants defense took the ball away every single time. The defense got the fourth-down stop. Then Jevon Holland picked off Daniels at the Giants’ 2-yard line, right at the goal line, with 5:27 left. Then Ar’Darius Washington jumped a fourth-down throw with 2:15 left and picked it off.

Think about that. Your defense takes the ball away three straight times in the fourth quarter of a division game, with a four-point lead. That is the kind of defense you can take into January. That is the kind of defense that wins you a division. And somehow, the Giants still lost.

Because here is what the offense did with those chances. New York went three-and-out after both interceptions. Both of them. You get the ball deep in your own end after Holland’s pick, and you cannot move the chains. You get the ball after the second pick with a little over two minutes left, needing one first down to basically salt the game away, and you cannot move the chains.

Let me break down both of those possessions, because the details are where the game was lost.

After the Holland interception, the Giants were backed up. Late in the fourth, they lined up for fourth-and-one from their own 11 with Jameis Winston in the game. And remember, the Winston sneak had already worked earlier in the game. It is something the Giants have executed well this season. But this time, John Harbaugh explained after the game that he was not seriously thinking about going for it there, deep in his own territory. The plan was to use the cadence and try to draw Washington offside. Winston was even yelling “Who let the dogs out” at the line, which Harbaugh said is part of the cadence. Washington did not jump. They played with good discipline. The play clock ran out, the Giants took the delay of game, and they punted.

Now, I get the idea there. You are not going to go for it on fourth down at your own 11 with a four-point lead. That would be reckless. Trying to steal a first down with a hard count is smart. But it shows you how the offense got there. They could not get the yards they needed on the first three downs.

And then the possession that really hurts. After the second interception, the Giants had third-and-three. Three yards to put real pressure on the clock. Convert that, and Washington is burning timeouts and maybe never gets the ball back with enough time. And here is the decision that has everyone talking. Cam Skattebo, who ran for 78 yards and two touchdowns in this game, the guy who was running hard all night, was off the field. The Giants called a quarterback crack sweep for J.J. McCarthy. The design is that the quarterback running gives you an extra blocker. Harbaugh said he thought they had a chance. But Washington’s rookie linebacker Sonny Styles came through and made the tackle for a loss. Harbaugh said after the game he would have to see the tape to see how Styles got through there. And because they lost two yards, Harbaugh said if they had been at four yards or less, he probably would have gone for it on fourth down. They were not. They punted.

So here is the honest truth about the robbery talk. The Giants had the game in their hands after the second interception. Third-and-three. Your best runner on the sideline. A quarterback sweep that got blown up. That is not the officials. That is a play call and an execution failure in the biggest moment of the game.

Now let’s go to the final drive, because there is more to it than the flag.

After the PI on Banks, Washington had third-and-four. Daniels hit Diggs on an inside-breaking route. Diggs broke a tackle and was gone. Thirty-two yards, touchdown, 57 seconds left. And the analysts on the broadcast pointed this out. In man-to-man, if you break one tackle, it is a wrap. There is no one else there. And the reason there was no one else there is because the Giants were in a zero blitz. Harbaugh confirmed it. The last two throws, he said, were against zero blitzes, and those were the routes that ended up getting run. He said, we get into a blitz and we get beat on an inside breaking route.

Diggs even did the Nestea plunge celebration, falling back into the end zone. The old-school fans know exactly what I am talking about. No flag on that one either. So if you are keeping score at home on the refs, that is two moments that Giants fans did not love.

And then the Giants’ last chance. Third-and-10, Styles on the rush again, and McCarthy looked deep for Malik Nabers. Rasul Douglas, new in town in a Commanders uniform, picked it off. Ballgame.

Now let me show you the part of this game that worries me the most, more than any flag. It is the zero blitz. Both sides of it.

On defense, Daniels had an incredible night against the blitz. According to the breakdown after the game, all three of his touchdown passes came against cover zero. He was three for three under center. He was six for six on play action. He was hitting explosives all over the place, including the touchdown to Antonio Williams, the rookie out of Clemson. Dan Quinn said back in March that once this offense got going, they would use play action to build explosives. We are starting to see that. And against the Giants’ blitz-heavy defense, Daniels looked comfortable, like he knew exactly where to go with the ball.

Now, I have been a big believer in this Giants blitz approach. Since Brian Burns went down in week three, the Giants have been one of the most blitz-heavy teams in the league, right behind the Vikings under Brian Flores. It turned this defense into a top-five unit heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. It got seven sacks in Philadelphia. But Thursday night showed the other side of it. A smart quarterback who sees the zero blitz coming, with a plan to beat it, can make you pay. And Daniels did, three times.

And then on offense, here is the stat that jumped off the screen. McCarthy against six or seven rushers on Thursday: two for six, 11 yards and an interception. Washington’s defensive coordinator comes from the Brian Flores branch of coaching, and that means you have to be ready for zero blitz. One of the analysts made the point that it did not look like the Giants had a plan for it. When you see a zero blitz coming, you need an answer ready. A sliding protection with the back on one side. A trips formation with a quick hitch or a smoke screen to one guy and two guys blocking. Something to get the ball out and hit the sideline. Instead, it looked like the Giants were just calling a play and hoping it hit. And the analyst’s take was that Washington figured out during the game that McCarthy was not seeing certain things, and they saved their best pressure for the last couple of minutes.

That ties right into what we talked about earlier this week. The film showed McCarthy locking onto his first read, and when that first read is covered, he struggles to get to the second option. Against a zero blitz, you do not have time to go through a long progression. You need a hot read. You need the ball out fast. That is the next big step for McCarthy, and the coaching staff has ten days to build that plan before Jacksonville.

And I want to be fair to McCarthy here. He threw for 293 yards, a career high, in the rain. He threw a 66-yard touchdown. Harbaugh said after the game he thought J.J. played very well. And McCarthy took ownership too. He said it absolutely sucks, and that no matter what, they have to find a way to get the win. He gave Washington credit for making more plays. He said this league is going to be tight games all the way to the wire, and they have to learn from this, keep progressing, and make sure it does not happen again in those critical situations. He said the boys battled, but they have to make more plays next time. That is the right answer from a young quarterback. No excuses. No blaming the refs.

Now let’s talk about the other thing that has been a problem in both Washington games, and that is the kicking game.

Go back to the first matchup, the 27-27 tie in week five. The Giants missed three field goals in that game. Three. On Thursday, Dominic Zvada missed a short one, a 33-yarder, early in the game. And how many points did the Giants lose by? Three. If that kick goes through, we are probably talking about overtime. And the kicking game issues did not stop there. The Giants had a kickoff go out of bounds. Jordan Stout had a 12-yard shank on a punt. And there was a loose ball late in the game that the Giants were fortunate to fall on, which could have been another turnover.

Harbaugh was asked about Zvada after the game and backed him all the way. He said they are not going to knee-jerk it with a rookie who has made a bunch of kicks. And I agree with that. Zvada went four for four in Philadelphia, including a 53-yarder, and he has six field goals of 50 or more yards this season. But let me say this plainly. If the Giants want to win this division and then win games in January, the kicking game has to be cleaner. Two games against Washington, a tie and a three-point loss, and the kicking game has been part of the story in both of them.

So let me put it all together and give you my final verdict on the robbery question.

Were the Giants robbed? My opinion, not a report: no. The Banks call was borderline, the kind of call that can drive you crazy, and I understand the frustration. But the Giants had at least five chances to put this game away and did not take them. A missed 33-yard field goal. A Nabers fumble deep in Washington territory in the third quarter. A three-and-out after the Holland interception. A three-and-out after the Ar’Darius Washington interception, with a quarterback sweep on third-and-three and Skattebo on the sideline. And a zero blitz call on the final drive that left Diggs one broken tackle away from the end zone. You take care of even one of those, and the Banks flag never matters.

The Giants did not get robbed by the officials. They let one get away. And there is a big difference, because a team that gets robbed has an excuse, and a team that lets one get away has a to-do list. The Giants have a to-do list.

Now here is the good part, and I want to end on this, because there is a lot of good to take from this game too.

One analyst called it a good loss. I know that sounds crazy, but hear me out. We are in good loss season in college football, and this was the NFL version. The Giants learned a lot about themselves. They learned the defense can take the ball away in big moments. They learned McCarthy can make big throws against a division rival. They learned Skattebo can carry the run game in the rain. And they learned exactly what they need to fix: the plan against the zero blitz, the late-game play calling, and the kicking game.

And the division is still wide open, but the Giants are still on top. The analysts’ view was that outside of Philadelphia, any of the other three teams could still win this thing, but the Giants are still the pick. The Giants defense is opportunistic and can make the big plays. Washington is starting to find itself. Dallas has the offense to score 30 on anybody. But the Giants are 5-3-1, alone in first place. Washington is 3-5-1. Dallas is 4-5. Philadelphia is 2-7. The Giants are two games up on Washington and a game and a half up on Dallas. Washington now has the edge head-to-head after the tie and this win, so the Giants do not want this race to get tight. But they still control their own path.

And now they get ten days off. They do not play again until Sunday, November 22, when they host Jacksonville at MetLife. Ten days to build a plan for the zero blitz. Ten days for McCarthy’s hip to keep healing. Ten days for Zvada to reset on the practice field. And later in the season, Tracy is on IR and lined up to come back around week 13 against San Francisco.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. I picked the Giants to win this game and move to 6-2-1. I was wrong. I said McCarthy would finish the game and play well, and he did. I said this defense would make Daniels pay, and it picked him off twice and got a fourth-down stop in the fourth quarter, but Daniels made them pay back on the plays that mattered most. So I will take the loss on my main pick and keep moving.

Here is my prediction for Jacksonville, and you can hold me to it. The Giants come out of this break with a real plan against the blitz. McCarthy gets the ball out fast on hot reads. Skattebo is on the field in the big short-yardage moments. Zvada makes his kicks. And the Giants beat Jacksonville to get to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I am going to say it again, because a three-point loss with three fourth-quarter takeaways does not change it. This team can go all the way. A defense that steals possessions. A young quarterback who threw for a career high. A running back who carried the offense in the rain. A coach who owns his mistakes. And a division lead with plenty of football left. The Giants were not robbed. They left a win on the field. And good teams go get it back.
