The Giants just made a roster move on the first day back from the bye, and the timing tells you everything. On Monday afternoon, they brought back a familiar face. Running back Eric Gray is back in New York, signed to the practice squad. And on that exact same day, John Harbaugh told reporters that Cam Skattebo, the Giants’ leading rusher in Houston, is day-to-day. I do not think that is a coincidence. And there is more. Ryan Dunleavy of the New York Post reported that the Colts’ asking price for Jonathan Taylor is high, and that the Giants will look at other offensive playmakers on the trade market. So, in one week, the Giants have lost Tyrone Tracy Jr. to injured reserve, added a rookie off another team’s practice squad, brought back Gray, and may be shifting their deadline focus away from the biggest name on the board. With Philadelphia on Sunday and Washington on Thursday night, that is two division games in five days, and the Giants cannot afford to be one injury away from scrambling. Today, I am breaking down every move, what it means, and why the running back room is the most important story of the week. Let’s get into it.

If Skattebo cannot go on Sunday, who do you trust to carry the ball in Philadelphia?

Let me start with the move that flew under the radar. On November 2nd, the Giants signed Eric Gray to the practice squad, and to make room, they released defensive lineman Josh Tupou from the practice squad. One out, one in. A straight swap.

Now, I know a practice squad signing does not get anybody out of their seat. But look at who Gray is. He was a fifth-round pick by the Giants back in 2023, out of Oklahoma. He played 34 games for this franchise. He was released at the end of August, spent some time on Cleveland’s practice squad, and was let go by the Browns in early October. So he is not a stranger. He knows the building, he knows a lot of the people, and he does not need a long introduction.

And that matters, because running back depth only matters at the exact moment you suddenly need it. Nobody thinks about the fourth or fifth running back until somebody goes down and you have a few days to figure out who is next. In that moment, you want somebody you already know. That is the value of Eric Gray. It is insurance, plain and simple. And the Giants chose that insurance over a defensive lineman on the practice squad. That tells you where the concern is right now. It is in the backfield.

And the reason for all of this is Tyrone Tracy Jr. He hurt his hamstring in the Houston game in the second quarter, and the Giants put him on injured reserve heading into the bye. That one stings, because Tracy was rolling. He scored three touchdowns over his last two games. He opened the scoring in Houston with a short touchdown run. And the week before, against New Orleans, he scored once on the ground and once through the air.

And the bigger picture on Tracy is more impressive than a lot of people realize. Since the Giants took him in the fifth round of the 2024 draft, he has more than 2,200 yards from scrimmage and 13 total touchdowns, according to the numbers shared this week. He is one of only three players in franchise history to top 1,000 scrimmage yards in each of his first two seasons. The other two are Odell Beckham Jr. and Saquon Barkley. That is serious company for a fifth-round pick. And he is a kick returner too, with a 55-yard return in the win over Arizona. So the Giants are not just losing a running back. They are losing a weapon on special teams.

But here is the most important piece of the Tracy news, and it came from Joe Schoen at his bye-week press conference. Schoen said the injury is not season-ending, and that it will not be something long-term. That changes how you read everything else. The Giants are not replacing Tracy forever. They are covering a stretch. Injured reserve means at least four games, so Tracy will miss Philadelphia, Washington, Jacksonville, and Indianapolis. If everything goes well, he can be back around week 13 against San Francisco.

So, who steps in? On October 27th, the Giants signed rookie running back Gregory Desrosiers to the active roster from the Los Angeles Chargers’ practice squad. And I owe you a small receipt here. When I first covered this move a couple of weeks ago, I did not name him, because I was not confident about the spelling and I did not want to get it wrong. Now we have it from the team. Gregory Desrosiers.

He is an undrafted rookie who has not appeared in an NFL game yet, so he is an unknown at this level. But the college resume is interesting. He played 48 college games across Louisville, UMass, and Memphis. In his time at Memphis, he ran 130 times for 791 yards. That is about 6.1 yards per carry, with 10 touchdowns. College numbers are not NFL numbers, but 6.1 a carry tells you why the Giants liked him enough to take him off another team’s practice squad. And remember what Schoen said about him at the bye. He said that is why the Giants were poaching a running back from the Chargers, and that he was excited to see what the rookie could do. To make room for him, the Giants released receiver Jason Brownlee from the practice squad.

And then there is Dean Lowry, the veteran defensive tackle the Giants added to the practice squad the same day. I covered Lowry a little in a previous video, but let me give you the full resume, because it is better than people think. He is 6 foot 6 and 296 pounds. He has played 132 career games with 85 starts. He was a fourth-round pick by Green Bay in 2016 and spent seven seasons with the Packers, where he missed only a handful of regular season games. He has 16 and a half career sacks. And he has playoff experience, including appearances in NFC Championship games.

But here is my fair take. Injuries got in the way at his last stops. He played for Minnesota in 2024, then he spent all of 2025 on injured reserve in Pittsburgh, and the Steelers released him at the final cut this summer. His last regular season game was December 21st, 2024, and, fun fact, that was against John Harbaugh’s Ravens. So there is a real question about what he has left. But for a practice squad spot, that is about as low risk as it gets. And with Dexter Lawrence gone and Jeffery Simmons in Chicago, the Giants could use any experienced body in the middle.

Now, let me give you the context for this whole running back room, because it was already under the microscope before Tracy got hurt. Go back to the loss to the Saints. All four running backs dressed for that game, and afterward, Harbaugh said somebody needed to take the bull by the horns. Two days later, on October 20th, the Giants released Najee Harris, along with outside linebacker Jihad Ward. That was a clear message. The Giants were done with a crowded backfield. They wanted the three young backs to grab the job.

And did they respond. Against Houston, Skattebo, who had zero carries the week before, ran for a career-high 124 yards, including a career-long 42-yard run in overtime. On the very next snap, Devin Singletary ran 30 yards for the game-winning touchdown with 7:25 left in overtime. According to the team, that is the longest walk-off touchdown run in overtime in Giants history. Tracy scored early. All three backs had a hand in that 27 to 21 win, which is exactly why losing one of them hurts so much.

So here is how I see the rotation now. Skattebo and Singletary have the hot hand, and they are going to carry most of the load. Behind them, you have Desrosiers, who has never played an NFL snap, and Gray on the practice squad, ready to be elevated if needed. And Patrick Ricard is at fullback, which helps in short yardage. I am not saying that is a bad situation. I am saying the top of the room matters a ton right now, because after those two, it is a lot of unknowns.

And let me give some credit to Devin Singletary here, because he might be the most important player in this whole story. Joe Schoen calls him Motor, and that nickname fits. He is a veteran who has been a lead back before, and he has been a pro about sharing the work all season. Then, when the Giants needed one play in overtime in Houston, he took it 30 yards to the house. If Skattebo is limited, Singletary is the guy who has to carry this offense on Sunday. He knows how to pass protect, he knows how to read the zone running game, and he will not put the ball on the ground. For a young quarterback like J.J. McCarthy, that kind of reliability is gold. I would not be shocked to see Singletary get 15 or more carries in Philadelphia. And if he does, I trust him.

And do not forget about Patrick Ricard. A lot of fans overlook the fullback, but Harbaugh had Ricard in Baltimore for years, and he knows exactly how to use him. When the backfield is thin, a fullback who can lead block on short yardage and goal line plays makes life a lot easier for whoever is carrying the ball. With Ricard in front of him, even a rookie like Desrosiers has a better chance to succeed if he gets thrown into the fire.

And that brings us to the news I would watch closest. On that same Monday, Harbaugh said Skattebo is day-to-day. Day-to-day is not ruled out. But it is not cleared either. It is that gray area where the medical staff is checking on him every single day. And remember, Skattebo said he felt his hamstring grab on that 42-yard run in Houston. Hamstrings are tricky for running backs. They affect burst and acceleration, which are exactly what make Skattebo dangerous.

So think about the worst case. If Skattebo cannot go on Sunday, and Tracy is on IR, the Giants are leaning on Singletary, with Desrosiers behind him and Gray possibly elevated from the practice squad. That is a very different room from the one that just won in Houston. I am not going to panic, because day-to-day is a long way from out. But with two division games in five days, every day of rest matters.

And here is something else to think about. Even if Skattebo plays on Sunday, the Giants have to turn around and play Washington on Thursday night. A running back with a sore hamstring playing a physical game in Philadelphia, and then playing again four days later? That is a real risk. I would not be surprised if the Giants manage Skattebo’s snaps carefully in Philadelphia, even if he is cleared. That is exactly why they wanted Gray in the building. A fresh body for the short week.

The other name to watch is Abdul Carter. He was absent from Monday’s practice because of an illness. An illness is not an injury, and that is the better situation. But Carter is one of the most important players on this defense. Brian Burns said he has been unblockable at times. Dennard Wilson has been stacking him on the same side with Kayvon Thibodeaux and Arvell Reese to create matchup problems. If he is limited on Sunday, the Giants lose a big piece of their pass rush against an Eagles offense that just gave up four sacks to Washington. I expect him to be fine by the weekend, but I will be watching the practice reports.

Now, let’s connect all of this to the trade deadline, because this is where it gets interesting. According to Schefter and Rapoport, the Giants are one of four teams that have shown interest in Jonathan Taylor, along with Minnesota, Denver, and Green Bay. ESPN reported that the Colts would want a package similar to what San Francisco gave Carolina for Christian McCaffrey in 2022, which included second, third, fourth, and fifth-round picks. One offer included a second-round pick plus more, and the Colts turned it down. No team has met their price yet.

And then Dunleavy reported that the asking price is high, and that the Giants will look at other offensive playmakers on the trade market. Let me be careful about what that means. It does not mean the Giants are out on Taylor. It means the Giants are not going to chase him at any price. And that lines up perfectly with everything we have heard from Schoen and Harbaugh. It has to make sense short term and long term. The Giants do not have draft capital to burn. They have to be smart shoppers.

And let me apply that to the McCaffrey comparison. That package was a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth. The Giants do not even have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks. So a McCaffrey-style deal would mean pushing into 2028 picks, or adding players. Harbaugh joked last week that 2028 is always easier to put on the credit card. But he also said any deal cannot be a one-and-done. Taylor is signed for two more years after this season, so he passes that test. The question is purely price.

And here is where the Eric Gray signing fits in. If the Giants were sure they were getting Taylor this week, would they bother bringing back a practice squad running back on Monday? Maybe. Depth is depth. But it also tells me the Giants are preparing to play Philadelphia with the backs they have. They are not waiting for a trade to save them. That is smart. You prepare for the worst case, and if a deal comes, it is a bonus.

So what do other offensive playmakers mean? It could be another running back. Names like Tony Pollard, Tyler Allgeier, and Brian Robinson Jr. have come up in fan and media conversations. It could be a receiver or a tight end. Remember, Theo Johnson missed the Houston game, and the Giants have relied heavily on Isaiah Likely and Malik Nabers. A veteran pass catcher who can give McCarthy another reliable target would fit Dunleavy’s description. I want to be clear, nobody has reported a specific target beyond Taylor. That is my read on what other playmakers could mean, not a report.

And let me give you my updated take. A few videos ago, I said my ceiling for Taylor alone was one first-round pick, and my ideal deal was a second plus a mid-round pick. The Colts are asking for something like a second, third, fourth, and fifth. That is a lot of picks for a team that is missing two of them. If the price does not come down, I would rather the Giants pivot. Get a cheaper veteran back to share the load with Skattebo and Singletary until Tracy returns, and use the savings to add a defensive tackle. That way, you address two needs instead of one.

But I also think the Colts’ price could drop as November 10th gets closer. If no team meets it, Indianapolis has to decide whether to keep a star running back on a rebuilding team, and pay him for two more years. That decision often tilts toward the buyer in the final hours. And the Giants have a game on Sunday that could change their urgency. If they win in Philadelphia and Skattebo comes out healthy, maybe they hold. If they lose, or Skattebo’s hamstring flares up, maybe they push. The deadline falls two days after the Eagles game for a reason. That game could decide what the Giants do.

Now, let me zoom out, because I think these roster moves tell you something bigger about this front office. Every move this month has had a clear purpose. Release Najee Harris to give the young backs the job. Poach Desrosiers to replace Tracy. Bring back Gray to protect against a Skattebo setback. Add Lowry to give the defensive line an experienced body. Activate Paulson Adebo to get a starting corner back on the field. None of these moves make headlines. But together, they show a team that is thinking a week ahead, not reacting a week behind.

And the standings make this even more important. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place. Dallas is 3 and 5 after losing at home to Arizona. Philadelphia is 2 and 6 after losing 27 to 10 to Washington, its sixth straight loss. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. The Giants have a three-loss cushion over Dallas and a four-loss cushion over Philadelphia. Two division wins in five days could make that cushion even bigger. That is why every roster spot matters right now.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, Skattebo’s practice participation on Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. Full participation by Friday would be a great sign. Number two, Carter’s return from illness. Number three, whether Gray gets elevated for Sunday, which would tell you how worried the Giants are about Skattebo. Number four, whether Desrosiers gets his first NFL snaps in Philadelphia. And number five, whether the Giants pivot from Taylor to another playmaker before November 10th.

And here is a prediction you can hold me to. I think Skattebo plays on Sunday, but on a managed workload, with Singletary getting more carries than he has all season. And I still think the Giants break the streak in Philadelphia.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, with a banged-up but resilient backfield, a front office that is planning ahead, and two division games that could put this race away. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
