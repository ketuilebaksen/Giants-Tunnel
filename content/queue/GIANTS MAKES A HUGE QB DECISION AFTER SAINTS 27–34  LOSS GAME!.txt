The scoreboard said 34 to 10. There were 11 minutes and 25 seconds left. And John Harbaugh made the biggest quarterback decision of his Giants tenure so far. He pulled Jameis Winston and sent in J.J. McCarthy, a quarterback who had been in this building for only a few weeks. What happened next is the reason the whole NFL is talking about the Giants this week, even after a loss. McCarthy threw for 243 yards, every single one of them in the fourth quarter. The Giants scored 17 unanswered points. They went from down 24 to one throw into the end zone away from tying the game, with a two-point try waiting behind it to win. The final was 34 to 27, Saints. But the number that matters most right now is 243, because it ties the most passing yards by a Giant in a single quarter since 1991. The only other Giant to do it was Eli Manning. Folks, Harbaugh’s decision at 34 to 10 may have changed the direction of this season, and today I am going to walk you through the full story of how this game got away, how McCarthy brought it back, and what this means for the quarterback job heading to Houston. Now, we have talked a lot this week about what Harbaugh said and what Winston said. Today, we finally have the full picture of the game itself, and you need to see how it got away from the Giants before McCarthy’s quarter makes complete sense.

And here is what stings. It looked so good early. The defense held New Orleans to three punts and a missed field goal on the Saints’ first four possessions. The Giants opened the scoring with a 17-yard catch and run, a touchdown that ended with a dive at the front right pylon with 5:14 left in the first quarter. Then they pushed the lead to 10 to nothing. Against a Saints team that came in on a three-game losing streak, that is exactly where you want to be.

Then, right before halftime, the whole game flipped. New Orleans hit a 58-yard field goal. And on the ensuing kickoff, Charlie Jones had the ball jarred loose, and the Saints recovered at the Giants’ 28-yard line. Tyler Shough scrambled for 25 yards on that drive and finished it with a two-yard touchdown pass on fourth and goal with one second left in the half. Ten points in the last 78 seconds, and a game the Giants had controlled for almost the entire half was tied at halftime. Folks, that sucks the air out of a stadium.

And it did not get better coming out of the break. The Saints got the ball to start the second half and went 79 yards in five plays, in less than three minutes. Alvin Kamara finished it off with a weaving 31-yard catch and run, and New Orleans had its first lead of the day, 17 to 10. That is the 17-point sequence Harbaugh was talking about in his press conference. Field goal, fumble, touchdown, touchdown. Seventeen points in a blink, and the momentum belonged to the Saints.

The run defense was a real problem. In the previous two weeks, the Saints ran 36 times for 105 yards. On Sunday, they ran 38 times for 175 yards, 4.6 yards a carry. Shough threw three touchdown passes, including an 18-yarder to Chris Olave that capped 34 straight Saints points, and he ran five times for 32 yards and a touchdown himself. That is exactly what Harbaugh meant when he said way too many quarterback scrambles. And the cornerback room took another hit. Colton Hood was already out with a concussion, and Greg Newsome II was ruled out with a concussion in the third quarter. So, the Giants were playing with their depth corners against a young quarterback who was feeling it.

But what really kept the Giants from answering was the quarterback play. This was Winston’s fourth straight start in place of Jackson Dart, and he finished 14 of 26 for 152 yards and a touchdown. On paper, that does not look like a disaster. But he threw two interceptions in the second half, and those two picks led to 10 Saints points. Add in the Charlie Jones fumble, and that is three Giants turnovers, all of them turned into points. It was not one catastrophic play. It was repeated giveaways at the worst possible times.

And I have to hold myself accountable here, because I said it on this channel the morning of the game. I told you the turnover column would be the scoreboard for Harbaugh’s ball security standard. By the time the game ended, the Giants had four turnovers. You cannot beat a team that is rolling when you keep handing it the ball. That is the lesson of this game, and it is a lesson this team has been hearing from its head coach for weeks.

So, it is 34 to 10, with 11:25 left. And John Harbaugh decides he has seen enough. Think about where Harbaugh is standing. The game is basically gone, and he turns to a quarterback the Giants acquired in a trade with the Vikings on September 28th. I do not think that was a panic move. I think it was a coach looking at a 24-point hole and saying, I need to see something different, and I need to see it now. Winston told us after the game that his quarterbacks coach told him before the switch. Harbaugh called it desperation time. But a coach does not make that move just to run out the clock. He makes it because he wants information. And boy, did he get some.

Now, here is the honest part. McCarthy’s first drive did not look like the start of anything special. He threw an interception, the Giants’ fourth turnover of the afternoon. If you were sitting in that stadium, you were thinking, here we go. Same story, different quarterback.

But his second drive was a completely different animal. It ended with a five-yard touchdown run with seven minutes left, and then McCarthy hit Isaiah Likely on the two-point conversion. 34 to 18. Suddenly, there was a little life in the building.

Then things got really interesting. McCarthy found Charlie Jones for a 49-yard completion. And I love that play for Charlie Jones, because he had the fumble earlier, and he came right back with a huge play. That is the deep pass Harbaugh called awesome in his press conference. That drive ended with a 24-yard touchdown to Isaiah Likely, the tight end’s fifth touchdown in six games as a Giant. With 5:06 left, the Giants had a chance to cut it to one possession, but the two-point try failed, and that hurt, because now they still needed more than one score. So, Dominic Zvada came out and kicked a 41-yard field goal with 2:04 left to make it 34 to 27. And folks, that kick matters too. After the three misses in the mud in Washington, the rookie hit when the Giants needed him.

That is 17 unanswered points in the final 11 minutes. And remember, the defense stepped up during that run too. It forced a turnover and three more punts, giving McCarthy the ball over and over again. Then came the final drive. McCarthy scrambled for 28 yards to keep it going, and the Giants got to fourth and one at the Saints’ 14-yard line with 12 seconds left. On the biggest play of the day, McCarthy looked for Isaiah Likely in the back of the end zone, and the pass fell incomplete. Shough came out and took a knee, and that was it.

Man, that was close. Down 24 at one point, and one play away from tying it, with Harbaugh telling us after the game that if they had scored, they were going for two to win it. Think about how wild that is.

And let me say something about that final play call, because I know some of you are going to question it. Fourth and one at the 14 with 12 seconds left and no time to waste. Some fans will ask why not take the easy first down and then attack the end zone. Here is the problem with that. With 12 seconds left, a short completion in the field of play eats the clock, and after the Washington game, we all know exactly how fast those seconds disappear. The only way to guarantee a real shot at the end zone was to take it right there. So the Giants went for the touchdown, they went to the player McCarthy trusted most, Isaiah Likely, and it did not connect. You can hate the result. But that is a decision with a clear plan behind it, and I would rather lose taking a real shot than lose because the clock ran out on a confused offense.

Now, let’s look at the numbers, because they jump off the page. McCarthy finished 11 of 21 for 243 yards, with one touchdown and one interception. All 243 of those yards came in the fourth quarter. That ties for the most passing yards by a Giant in a single quarter since 1991. The other was Eli Manning, in a 2012 game against Tampa Bay. And folks, if you remember that game, you remember how it ended. Eli threw for more than 500 yards that day, and the Giants came back to win a wild one. That is the company McCarthy joined in one quarter.

And here is another stat that tells you how strange this game was. Since 1950, this is the only time the Giants have had two quarterbacks throw for at least 150 yards in the same game. Winston had 152, McCarthy had 243. Two quarterbacks, one game, and a piece of franchise history nobody saw coming.

Now, I want to stay on Isaiah Likely for a minute, because his connection with McCarthy is worth watching. He caught the two-point conversion. He caught the 24-yard touchdown. And he was the guy McCarthy looked for on the biggest throw of the day, on fourth down at the end. When a quarterback who has only been here a few weeks keeps going back to the same tight end in the moments that matter, that tells you there is real chemistry building. It is early, but that can grow fast.

And then there is Malik Nabers, who is just doing things. Twelve catches, which ties his career high, for 165 yards, the third most of his career. That is his third straight game with 100 receiving yards. Before Sunday, there had been only six instances of a Giant doing that in the same season, and we are talking about names like Odell Beckham Jr., Victor Cruz, Mario Manningham, Homer Jones, and Del Shofner. And with 168 career receptions, Nabers passed Puka Nacua, who had 159, for the second-most receptions by a player in his first 25 career games in NFL history. The only player ahead of him is Odell Beckham Jr. with 176. So, whoever is throwing him the ball, Nabers is going to be a problem for every defense on the schedule.

And I want to give Charlie Jones his own moment here, because his day is a perfect picture of this team. He had the ball jarred loose on a kickoff return right before halftime, and that fumble started the Saints’ 17-point run. A lot of players would have disappeared after that. Instead, in the fourth quarter, he went up and made a 49-yard catch that kept the comeback alive. Now, there is a fair question for the coaches this week. Do you keep him returning kicks after a fumble that big? Some coaches would make a change to send a message. But remember how Harbaugh handled Zvada after the Washington game. He trusted the player, fixed the problem, and Zvada answered with a clutch 41-yarder on Sunday. I would not be surprised if Harbaugh gives Charlie Jones the same trust. A player who bounces back the way Jones did in the fourth quarter is a player you want to keep believing in.

But the running game, folks. That is still the hole in this offense. The Giants ran 20 times for 84 yards. Najee Harris got the start at running back, his first start since January 11th, 2025. The Giants dressed four running backs, six different Giants had a rushing attempt, and Cam Skattebo was not one of them. That is the rotation Harbaugh defended in his press conference, when he said somebody needs to step up and be the back, and when one of them does, that is your answer. Right now, nobody has. And as we talked about earlier this week, that matters even more if McCarthy plays, because a mobile quarterback’s read-option game only works if the defense has to respect the running back too.

So now, let’s talk about the real question. The decision. Harbaugh’s in-game decision at 34 to 10 is done, and it worked about as well as it possibly could have without a win. Now comes the bigger one. Who starts in Houston?

Here is the case for McCarthy, and after seeing the full numbers, it is stronger than I thought. He came in cold, he threw an interception on his first drive, and he did not fold. He answered with three straight scoring drives, a 49-yard shot, a 24-yard touchdown, a 28-yard scramble on the final drive, and a throw into the end zone to tie it. His mobility showed up in a real way. And after the game, McCarthy said there was something special about this group from the moment he walked in, that Harbaugh builds confidence through work, not just talk, and he talked about how this team responded when Brian Burns and Jackson Dart went down. That is a quarterback who already sounds like he belongs in this locker room.

And here is the case for Winston, because it has not disappeared. He went unbeaten in his first three starts, two wins and a tie. He threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games. He led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback in Washington. And let’s be fair about McCarthy’s quarter. When a defense is up 24 in the fourth quarter, it plays softer. It protects against the big play and lets you move. Harbaugh himself said desperation time is different than normal football. One quarter does not settle anything, and McCarthy has never faced a full game plan built specifically for him as a Giant.

So, what do I think? In my last two videos, I told you my read was that Winston would likely stay the starter while McCarthy’s role grew. I want to be honest with you. After seeing the full numbers, my read is shifting. Not because one quarter settles it, it does not. But because the specific problem in this game was turnovers, and Winston’s two second-half interceptions turned directly into 10 points. That is the exact thing Harbaugh has called the most important thing with this offense. A coach who says ball security is his top priority, watching his starter give it away twice, and then watching the backup put up 243 yards in a quarter? I think McCarthy now has a very real chance to start in Houston. I am labeling that clearly as my opinion, not a report. Harbaugh said the process starts right now, and I expect him to watch every snap of tape before he says a word.

And whatever he decides, here is what gives me confidence. Remember how Winston handled it at the podium. He praised McCarthy, said he was proud of him, and said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. So, if Harbaugh goes with McCarthy, Winston is not going to become a distraction. And if Harbaugh sticks with Winston, McCarthy just proved he is ready to step in at any moment. Giants fans have seen what a ready backup can do. In 1990, Jeff Hostetler came off the bench when Phil Simms got hurt and took this franchise all the way to a Super Bowl title. I am not saying this is that. I am saying a quarterback room with two guys who can win games is a strength, not a problem.

Let’s look at the big picture. The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, still on top of the NFC East. Next is a road trip to Houston, against a team Harbaugh says is getting healthy, with a strong secondary. Then comes the bye, and then the Eagles. If the Giants clean up the turnovers, find a running back who takes the job, and get the quarterback decision right, this team has everything else. A receiver chasing Odell Beckham Jr.’s records. A tight end who keeps finding the end zone. A defense that held the Saints to three punts and a missed field goal to start the game, and forced a turnover and three more punts during the comeback.
