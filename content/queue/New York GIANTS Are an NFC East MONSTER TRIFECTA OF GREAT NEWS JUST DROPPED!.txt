The New York Giants are one win away from turning the NFC East into a one-team race, and the math is brutal for everybody else. Here is the trifecta. Number one, the standings. A win tonight against Washington puts the Commanders four games behind the Giants with eight games left, and it gives the Giants the head-to-head edge after the 27 to 27 tie in week five. Number two, the defense. The Giants have allowed only one opponent all season to throw for more than 200 yards, and they just turned a Hail Mary into a sack by sending six rushers. Number three, the coaching. On a short week, at home, a national panel was asked who they trust more, John Harbaugh or Dan Quinn, and they backed Harbaugh. But here is the twist. The same blitz that won the game in Philadelphia could lose the game tonight, because Jayden Daniels is not Joe Milton. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with the standings, because this is what makes tonight so big. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 4 and 5. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 7. The Giants are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. And they are unbeaten in the division, with a win over Dallas, a tie with Washington, and a win in Philadelphia.

Now, here is the math for tonight. If the Giants win, they go to 6 and 2 and 1. Washington drops to 2 and 6 and 1. That is a four-game gap with eight games left. And because the first game was a tie, a win tonight gives the Giants the head-to-head edge over Washington for the rest of the season. The Giants would also be 3 and 0 and 1 in the division, which matters for tiebreakers against everybody.

But if the Giants lose, it gets uncomfortable. They would drop to 5 and 3 and 1. Washington would climb to 3 and 5 and 1. The gap shrinks to two games, and Washington would own the head-to-head edge. Dallas would be lurking just a game and a half back. And suddenly, the conversation changes from how big is the lead to can the Giants hold on.

And think about what a win would mean against Dallas too. The Cowboys almost lost to the Colts last weekend, a team that just sold off Quenton Nelson, DeForest Buckner, Keenan Allen, and Josh Downs. Dallas found a way to survive, but it was not pretty. If the Giants win tonight, they would be two and a half games ahead of Dallas in the standings, with a head-to-head win over the Cowboys already in the bank from week one. One analyst called this a prime opportunity to create separation from Dallas, and he is right. The Giants have not won the NFC East since 2011. A win tonight would put them in a position where they control their own destiny for the rest of the season, with room for a bad week or two.

And the schedule after tonight helps too. A Thursday night game means extra rest before the next one. The Giants get time to heal up McCarthy’s hip, Skattebo’s hamstring, and Abdul Carter’s knee and back. And Tyrone Tracy Jr. is getting closer to coming back from injured reserve around week 13. A win tonight, followed by that extra rest, could set the Giants up perfectly for the stretch run.

John Harbaugh explained this to his team this week. He said that when you play a team for the second time, the first result shapes what the second game means. If you won the first one, the second one either puts you a game ahead or makes you even. If you lost it, the second one either makes you even or puts you a game behind. But the Giants and Washington tied. So Harbaugh said this one is for all the marbles. He called it the tiebreaker. He said it is like going into the second half of a game tied. Win it, and you are one up on that team for the rest of the season.

And one of the players reportedly called it a two-and-oh week, because of what it means in the division. Harbaugh always preaches one and oh, every week. But even he admitted this one carries a little more weight.

Now, the second part of the trifecta. The defense. The numbers are wild. The Eagles threw for 169 yards against the Giants. The Texans threw for 153. And that same Texans team just threw for around 350 yards the following week against someone else. Through nine weeks, only one team has gone over 200 passing yards against the Giants.

A member of the Giants’ secondary was asked why, and he said it starts with the mindset of the group, and how close they are. They want to succeed for each other. And then he gave credit to the pass rush. He said he has seen Abdul Carter get to the quarterback in about two seconds. When you have rushers like that, the defensive backs can play great complementary football. They can sit on routes, because the quarterback is running out of time before the receiver even makes his break.

And that leads to the play everyone is talking about. The final snap in Philadelphia. The Eagles needed a Hail Mary. And Dennard Wilson sent six rushers. One analyst could not believe it. He said, they wanted to throw a Hail Mary, and the Giants said, hell no, we’re blitzing you. The defense sacked Joe Milton before the receivers could even get to the end zone. Game over.

And the analysts explained why it worked. On a Hail Mary, every receiver is running deep. Nobody is breaking off a hot route. So if you send six rushers, the quarterback cannot wait for his receivers to get downfield. And if he throws early, nobody is there. One analyst asked why more defensive coordinators do not do that. Just bring the house. The simple things are genius.

But here is the part I want to slow down on, because it matters tonight. That call worked against Joe Milton. Milton is a big-armed quarterback who can run, but he is not Jayden Daniels. Daniels is one of the most dangerous quarterbacks in the league outside of the pocket. If the Giants send six at Daniels and he slips through just once, the defensive backs are suddenly chasing receivers with their backs to a quarterback who can run for 20 yards in a blink. That is how a brilliant call on Sunday can become a disaster on Thursday.

So what should Dennard Wilson do? This is where Arvell Reese becomes the key. One analyst pointed out earlier this week that the only Giants player as fast as Daniels is Reese. So Wilson has to decide. Use Reese as a pass rusher, where he just had three sacks in Philadelphia? Or use him as a spy, sitting in the middle of the field, ready to chase Daniels if he takes off? On the last play in Philadelphia, the Giants sent the house. Against Daniels, I think you need Reese watching him on the key downs.

My guess is we see a mix. Reese rushing on obvious passing downs early in the game, when Daniels is more likely to stay in the pocket. And Reese spying on third and medium and late in the game, when Daniels is most likely to take off. And the key is disguise. If Daniels cannot tell before the snap whether Reese is coming or waiting, that hesitation alone can win the down. That is my read, not a report.

And think about how the Giants handled Joe Milton’s legs in Philadelphia. Milton ran for 64 yards and led the Eagles in rushing. That is a lot for a quarterback who is not known as a runner the way Daniels is. A few times, the Giants’ edge rushers got too far upfield, and Milton escaped. Against Daniels, that cannot happen. The edge rushers have to keep contain. Kayvon Thibodeaux has been doing a lot of the dirty work all season, and one analyst said he controls the line of scrimmage with his hands. That kind of disciplined rush is exactly what you need against a quarterback like Daniels. Win your gap, stay in your lane, and collapse the pocket from the outside in.

And here is something interesting the panel brought up about Daniels. One analyst said he does not always understand what Daniels does at the end of games. With a couple of plays left, he does not throw the ball into the end zone. The analyst asked whether Daniels is coached that way, or whether he is doing it on his own. Another analyst said if Daniels is doing it himself, that is worse, because it means the instincts are not there. And a third pinned it on Dan Quinn and the coaching staff.

Now, I want to be fair to Daniels. He was the Offensive Rookie of the Year in 2024, and he led Washington to the NFC Championship Game that season. He has made huge plays at the end of games before. But this season has been different. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1. And if late-game decisions have become a weakness, the Giants’ defense needs to be ready to take advantage. Make Daniels hold the ball. Make him decide. And when he hesitates, get to him.

Now, the third part of the trifecta. The coaching. The panel was asked, on a short week, who do you trust more, Harbaugh or Quinn? And they picked Harbaugh. One analyst said that based on what he has seen over the last few weeks, he trusts the Giants’ defense the most. He said the Giants at home is a prime opportunity to create separation from Dallas. Another analyst also picked the Giants, and pointed out that J.J. McCarthy’s teams are 5 and 1 against the spread in his last six starts, going back to Minnesota.

And Harbaugh has explained his whole approach this week in a way that shows why players trust him. When he was asked about the trade deadline, and whether standing pat sent the wrong message, he flipped it around. He said you could just as well make the case that you show belief in your players by keeping all the guys you have. He said the Giants love their guys and feel like they have everything they need to accomplish everything they want. Now, he said, they just have to go do it. That is a coach putting his faith in his locker room. And tonight, that locker room gets a chance to prove him right.

And I understand why the panel trusts Harbaugh. Look at how the Giants have won this year. Close games. Ugly games. Road games. Games with injuries at quarterback. One analyst said something I loved. He said this was another game where, if any of the old Giants coaches were in charge, the Giants would have lost. He named Joe Judge, Pat Shurmur, and Brian Daboll. And he said the difference now is a head coach who knows what he is doing.

Dan Quinn is a good coach too. He took Washington to the NFC Championship Game in his first season. But this year, his team is 2 and 5 and 1. They just lost 20 to 13 to the Rams. And they are banged up. Their center, Lucas Patrick, did not practice early in the week because of a concussion. Receiver Treylon Burks was limited with a foot injury. And defensive tackle Daron Payne is out for the season with a broken foot. Daniels was a full participant with a left elbow issue, so he will play. But Washington is not the same team that went to the NFC Championship.

Now, let’s talk about the Giants’ quarterback, because one analyst on that panel sparked a debate. He said J.J. McCarthy was not playing well in Philadelphia before he got hurt. He said McCarthy was struggling, and it did not look like the same quarterback who beat the Texans. And another analyst said that when Jameis Winston came in, he was ready to stick with Winston, at least for that game. Winston hit Malachi Fields for 16 yards on third and 15, converted a fourth and one himself, and found Isaiah Likely in the end zone at the end of a 14-play, 76-yard drive.

But the analyst also said McCarthy needs to pick it up, and that this game is a perfect opportunity for him to do that. And I agree. McCarthy practiced fully on Tuesday after his hip pointer. He has not thrown an interception in his two Giants starts. And with Cam Skattebo back at full practice, the running game should be a lot better than it was in Philadelphia, where the Giants ran for just 80 yards on 31 carries without him. A better running game means fewer third and longs, fewer sacks, and more play action for McCarthy.

And one more thing on McCarthy. One analyst asked, where was the Malik Nabers who went for 100 yards in four straight weeks? Nabers had five catches for 43 yards in Philadelphia, and he dropped a pass that could have been a big play. But remember, Nabers was just named NFC Offensive Player of the Month, the first Giants receiver ever to win that award. One quiet game does not change that. I expect the Giants to get Nabers involved early tonight.

And think about the weapons McCarthy has if the protection holds up. Isaiah Likely just broke his career high for catches in a season, with nine games still to play. He caught the touchdown in Philadelphia. Darnell Mooney led the Giants with 54 receiving yards against the Eagles. Rookie Malachi Fields made the biggest catch of the game on that third and 15. Theo Johnson has been back at full practice after his neck injury. And Jalin Hyatt was just signed to the active roster, giving the Giants another deep threat. That is a lot of options. The problem in Philadelphia was not the receivers. It was time. McCarthy was sacked five times before halftime. If the line gives him a clean pocket tonight, he has people to throw to.

And there is a lesson from the Houston game here. In Houston, the Giants ran for 188 yards and four touchdowns. McCarthy only had to throw 28 times, and he hit two big shots down the field, including a 42-yarder to Nabers and a 47-yarder to Fields. When the running game works, the deep passing game opens up. In Philadelphia, without Skattebo, the run game disappeared, and so did the deep shots. With Skattebo back tonight, I expect the Giants to look a lot more like the Houston version of this offense.

And let me add one more point from that panel that I think is important. One analyst said he has a fear about this team, because he wants to love them so much. He said the Giants cannot keep playing to the level of their opponent. With two and a half minutes left in the third quarter in Philadelphia, the Giants were losing 8 to 6 to a 2 and 6 team. That could have been a disastrous loss. He is right. The Giants need to be sharper tonight. Seven penalties in the first half in Philadelphia. A muffed punt. A too-many-men penalty on an extra point that gave the Eagles a two-point try. Those mistakes cannot happen against Daniels.

And the special teams have to be better too. Charlie Jones muffed a punt in Philadelphia that gave the Eagles the ball at the Giants’ 47. The only reason it did not cost the Giants was that Arvell Reese forced a fumble on the very next possession. Jones was a full participant this week with his hand, so he should be ready. But in a close division game, a mistake like that can decide everything. On the bright side, Dominic Zvada went four for four on field goals in Philadelphia, including a 53-yarder, and punter Jordan Stout averaged over 52 yards a punt with five inside the 20. If the special teams play like that again, the Giants will win the field position battle.

So here are my three keys for tonight.

Key number one. Run the ball with Skattebo. He is back, and he is the spark. Washington is thin on the interior of its defensive line without Payne. Attack it.

Key number two. Contain Daniels. Do not let him escape the pocket. Use Reese wisely, rusher or spy, and disguise it. And on the last snap, if it comes to that, think twice before sending six.

Key number three. Clean football. No penalties on special teams. No turnovers. Protect McCarthy. The Giants win when they play clean, and they almost lose when they do not.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, Skattebo’s workload. Number two, how Dennard Wilson uses Reese against Daniels. Number three, McCarthy’s first few drives, because a fast start ends the quarterback debate. Number four, Abdul Carter, who was limited this week with knee and back issues. And number five, the penalty count.

And here is my prediction. The Giants win and move to 6 and 2 and 1. They take the head-to-head edge over Washington. The pass defense keeps Daniels under 200 passing yards. And the NFC East race starts to feel like it is over. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1, first place, unbeaten in the division, with a defense that has smothered almost every passing game it has faced and a coach the national panel trusts more than the man on the other sideline. A win tonight, and the rest of the division is staring up at a monster. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
