Good news number one: the Colts have been gutted.

Nelson has been one of the best guards in football for years. Buckner has been the heart of their defensive line. Allen was a veteran target. He has completed less than 51 percent of his passes in his career, and he has thrown more interceptions than touchdowns. The Colts went out and gave Jones a big contract because Richardson had not been good enough. And now, after all of that, Richardson is the guy again. And with Richardson under center, the host expects the offense to look a little different from what head coach and play-caller Shane Steichen has been running. Richardson likes to throw the ball downfield. He is a runner. In the red zone, the Colts will use his legs, so you have to bring him down. That is the danger.

So what is the plan? The host said you want a defender in his face making him think in the pocket. Get after him. Make him move his feet. Make him go through his reads. If a quarterback who completes barely half his passes has to read the whole field under pressure, that is a recipe for turnovers.

And here is the big piece. The Colts' offense, even before this change, was one of the worst in the NFL. According to the preview, with Jones at quarterback, the Colts were 26th in points per game, 30th in yards per game, 30th in passing yards per game, and 31st in yards per play. Good news number two: the Colts' defense is a perfect matchup for an offense that needs a confidence boost.

The host said the Giants have not looked sharp on offense the last couple of weeks. We talked about it in my last video. McCarthy completed 47 percent of his passes against Jacksonville. Isaiah Likely had only three targets because the tight ends had to stay in and block. Cam Skattebo fumbled. So this offense needs a game where things go right.

And the Colts' defense might be exactly the right opponent. Look at the numbers from the preview. The Colts rank 28th in points allowed per game and 25th in yards allowed per game. They give up 5.9 yards per play. Their run defense is the worst in the NFL on an expected points per play basis.

Let me explain that stat, because it matters. Expected points measures how much a play changes a team's chance to score, based on down, distance and field position. A defense near the bottom of that list is giving up plays that add a lot to the offense's scoring chances. It is a better measure than raw yards, because raw yards can get inflated or deflated by the game situation. So when the host says the Colts have the worst run defense in the league by that measure, that is a big deal.

Even the pass defense, which ranks 13th in passing yards allowed, is 18th in pass expected points per play. The host said that 13th ranking is a little misleading, because a lot of teams get ahead of the Colts early and then just run the ball. So they do not have to throw.

And here is the part that should make every Giants fan smile. The Colts do not blitz much. They are at one of the lowest blitz rates in the league. And they are last in the NFL in total pressures and last in pressure percentage. Remember what happened against Jacksonville? The Giants had to keep their tight ends in to block because the Jaguars' pass rush was so good. That took Likely out of the game. Against a defense that cannot generate pressure, Likely should be free to run routes again. That is McCarthy's safety net coming back.

Now, the host did add a warning. The Giants have not looked great against the blitz the last couple of weeks. On the opening drive against Jacksonville, the blitz rattled them a little. The blitz had some success against the Eagles, and it got to McCarthy against Washington. So the host expects the Colts to blitz more than usual on Sunday. Any defensive coordinator who watches the tape will see it. But even if they do, can they get home? They are last in pressure for a reason. The Giants still need a plan for it, but this is the kind of defense McCarthy can pick apart if he gets the ball out on time.
