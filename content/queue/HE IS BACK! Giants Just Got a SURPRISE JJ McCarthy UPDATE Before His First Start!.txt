He is back. Paulson Adebo, the cornerback the Giants lost to injured reserve back in September, has been designated to return and was on the practice field this week. Andrew Thomas is back at practice too. But the biggest surprise of the week came from offensive coordinator Matt Nagy, and it is all about J.J. McCarthy. Nagy revealed how much of the playbook McCarthy has actually had access to since he arrived. Against Washington, it was around 50 percent of the offense. Against the Saints, it grew to around 90 percent. And now, heading into his first start in Houston, Nagy says that number is 100 percent. Folks, that means the quarterback who threw for 243 yards in a single quarter against New Orleans did it without the full offense. Tomorrow, for the first time, he has all of it. Today, I am going to break down what that really means, how McCarthy’s legs change what the Giants can call, the injury updates on both sides of the ball, and why defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson is fired up about effort. Now that McCarthy has the full playbook, what is the first play you want to see Nagy call in Houston?

Let’s start with the McCarthy update, because I think it tells us a lot about how the coaching staff has been handling him. When McCarthy arrived from Minnesota on September 28th, he was learning a brand-new system on the fly. So, the coaches gave him a slice of the offense, the plays he knew best and could run cleanly. That was the package we saw against Washington, about half the offense. Then, by the Saints game, he was up to about 90 percent. And that is the version of McCarthy who came in down 34 to 10 and threw for 243 yards in the fourth quarter, the third-most passing yards by a backup quarterback in a single quarter since 1978. Now, with a full week as the starter, Nagy says it is 100 percent.

Why does that matter? Because it gives the coaches room to build a real game plan around him. They do not have to spend the whole week working around restrictions. They can call the plays that best attack Houston’s defense, not just the plays McCarthy happens to know. And for a defensive head coach like DeMeco Ryans, that is a headache. Last week, Houston could study McCarthy’s tape and assume he was working with a limited menu. This week, that menu just got a lot bigger.

But I want to be clear about what this does not mean. Having a play available does not mean a quarterback will execute it perfectly. McCarthy still has to recognize pressure before the snap. He still has to know where his answers are when the blitz comes. He still has to get the ball out when the defense gives him an opening. Houston is not going to care how much he studied this week. They are going to test whether he can use that information quickly, in a loud stadium, against disguised looks. That is the next step for him, and it is why I am excited without expecting everything to suddenly look easy.

And here is where Nagy’s history matters. Before he came to New York, Nagy spent years in Kansas City, coaching Patrick Mahomes as both a quarterbacks coach and an offensive coordinator. In between, he was the head coach in Chicago, where he built an offense for a young quarterback in Mitchell Trubisky that helped win a division title in 2018. He has done this before. He knows how to take a young quarterback, figure out what he does best, and build the plan around it. Going from 50 to 90 to 100 percent in a few weeks tells you Nagy believes McCarthy is absorbing it.

Now, the bigger change might be how McCarthy’s legs affect the defense. With read options and bootlegs, a defender cannot just charge straight at the running back. He has to respect the possibility that McCarthy keeps the ball, or rolls outside to throw. That little hesitation can matter a lot. It can create space for the runner. It can change the angle of a block. It can give a receiver one more moment to get open. Against the Saints, the Giants ran 20 times for 84 yards. Anything that makes a defender pause for half a second helps that run game.

And that is where the difference between McCarthy and Jameis Winston really shows up. This is not me saying Winston cannot play. He went unbeaten in his first three starts and threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games. It is about what each quarterback allows the coaches to call. Winston is more comfortable working from the pocket. McCarthy gives them another option with his legs. Knowing every assignment does not make every play a good fit for every quarterback. Now the Giants can call the plays that fit McCarthy.

My hope is that they use that difference thoughtfully. Moving the quarterback can help, but asking him to run constantly creates a different set of problems, starting with more hits on a quarterback who missed his entire rookie season with a knee injury. And before anybody turns this into a promise about the running game, the blocking still has to hold up. A defender hesitating will not save a play if somebody comes through untouched. The backs still have to read their blocks, and McCarthy has to make the right decision about keeping or handing off. What really changes is the number of things a defense has to prepare for. That gives the Giants a chance to create better situations. They still have to finish the plays.

And John Harbaugh knows this playbook story better than anybody. In 2018 in Baltimore, the Ravens used their bye week to rebuild their entire offense around a young, mobile quarterback in Lamar Jackson. They came out of that bye with a completely different attack, and they won the division. The Giants are doing something similar, except they did not wait for the bye. They are doing it in one week, on the road. That is bold. And if it works, imagine what this offense looks like after two more weeks of work during the bye.

And there is a lesson from that 2018 run too. In Lamar Jackson’s first start, the Ravens leaned on his legs heavily, and he ran the ball 27 times. It worked, they won the game, but it also showed how many hits a young quarterback can take if you build the whole plan around his running. Over time, Harbaugh and his staff found the balance, and Lamar became an MVP. I expect the Giants to learn from that. Use McCarthy’s mobility as a weapon, not as the whole offense. A few designed runs, a few bootlegs, and a lot of smart decisions about when to slide and get down.

And think about it from Houston’s side. All week, the Texans had to prepare for two different quarterbacks. Winston’s tape shows a pocket passer who will throw into tight windows. McCarthy’s tape shows a mobile quarterback who wants to push the ball downfield. Once the report came out that McCarthy is starting, Houston could focus its plan. But the McCarthy tape it has is limited, one quarter against the Saints with about 90 percent of the playbook, and a handful of snaps in Washington with about half of it. With the full offense now available, the Giants can show Houston things that are not on any film. That is a real advantage for a first start.

Now, the other half of the title. He is back. Paulson Adebo. The Giants put him on injured reserve back on September 18th, and this week they designated him to return, and he practiced on a limited basis. That is real progress for a secondary that has been hit hard. But I want to be careful here too. Opening his practice window is not the same as activating him for a game. Once a player is designated to return, the team has a window to see how he holds up in practice before deciding whether to put him back on the active roster. So, I want to see him healthy enough to contribute before treating his return as a solution. Getting a player back matters most when he can actually perform the job you are asking him to do.

And remember who Adebo is. The Giants signed him in free agency in 2025 to be a starting cornerback, a long, physical player who can match up with bigger receivers on the outside. That is exactly the kind of corner you want against a receiver like Marvin Harrison Jr. Before he went down, he was part of the plan. Losing him early in the season forced the Giants to lean on depth corners and young players, and they survived it. Getting a starting-caliber corner back for the second half of the season is like making a trade without giving anything up.

And the timing could not be better, because look at the rest of the secondary. Greg Newsome II stayed out of practice while dealing with his concussion. Jevon Holland returned on a limited basis, but he is still working through the concussion protocol. Colton Hood was already dealing with a concussion after the Saints game. Those are separate situations, and practice progress does not guarantee clearance. But against C.J. Stroud, who is second in the NFL in passing yards, with Marvin Harrison Jr. just arriving in Houston and Tank Dell coming back from his knee injury, the Giants need every healthy defensive back they can find. If Adebo is not ready for this game, his return after the bye could be a big boost for the stretch against the Eagles and Washington.

And there is another defensive injury that worries me. Tremaine Edmunds missed another practice with a shoulder injury, so his availability is uncertain. If he cannot go, that puts even more on Arvell Reese in the middle of the defense. Reese has been incredible as a rookie, leading the league in solo tackles at one point and making plays all over the field. But a defense needs continuity in the middle, and until the Giants know who is available, it is hard to know how much of it they will have.

Now, the offensive line, because protecting McCarthy in his first start has to be at the top of the list. Andrew Thomas, Jermaine Eluemunor, and Marcus Mbow all returned to practice on Thursday as limited participants, after missing Wednesday. That is encouraging. But limited practice does not mean they are cleared to play, and it does not tell us their official game status. Right now, the useful update is that they are back doing some work. If Thomas and Mbow are ready, they could be important pieces of the plan, while Eluemunor continues working through his calf injury. That is a possibility, not a confirmed lineup.

And here is the thing. I would not rush any of them just because the quarterback situation changed. You need linemen who can move well enough to do their jobs for a full game. Starting somebody who cannot hold up could leave the offense scrambling before it ever finds a rhythm. That is why rookie tackle J.C. Davis matters so much. He played the final 25 snaps against the Saints and did not allow a single pressure. If Thomas is limited, the Giants have a rookie who already proved he can handle a hostile moment.

Now, the defense, and this is the part of the week I want every Giants fan to hear. Dennard Wilson made his frustration clear after the Saints game, especially about players failing to run to the ball. And I understand why it bothered him. Missed tackles happen, even to good players. But when the next defender stops chasing, the original mistake gets bigger.

The Alvin Kamara touchdown is the example that sticks with me. The Saints opened the second half with that weaving 31-yard catch and run, the play that gave New Orleans its first lead of the day. The Giants had chances to limit that play. But missed tackles and poor pursuit let Kamara keep going. When a teammate misses, everybody else has to respond immediately. You cannot assume somebody farther downfield is going to handle it. That is how a catch that should be manageable becomes a touchdown. I do not need every defender making a highlight play. I need them running hard enough to give the next guy some help.

And here is why this matters so much this week. The Saints ran for 175 yards. Kamara made defenders miss. Tyler Shough scrambled all afternoon. Against Houston, with Stroud, Harrison Jr., Tank Dell, and a run game that will test the middle, effort and pursuit are not optional. Now, what matters is whether Wilson gets a response. A strong message sounds good during the week. The next game tells you if anybody listened. I want to see defenders closing space, staying involved, and finishing when they arrive. And if the same effort problems continue, playing time needs to become part of the conversation. You cannot ask the offense to adjust to a new quarterback while accepting defensive mistakes that come from players taking themselves out of the play.

And this staff has already shown it will make changes. Cam Skattebo did not get an offensive touch against the Saints after his ball security problems. The Giants moved on from Najee Harris. They released Jihad Ward and gave his spot to Trace Ford. They benched a quarterback who was unbeaten in his first three starts. Those are different situations, and I am not saying they all happened for the same reason. But they show that this group is not settled, and that nobody’s job is safe if the standard is not met. The important thing is that every decision has a clear purpose. Changing names only helps if the replacement gives the team something it was missing.

And do not forget Jameis Winston in all of this. He went from starter to backup in a week, and how he handles that matters to this locker room. Everything he said after the Saints game tells me he will be ready. He praised McCarthy, said he was proud of him, and said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. Now he becomes the veteran voice on the sideline, helping a young quarterback with the full playbook for the first time. And if anything goes wrong, the Giants have a quarterback who has already won games this season ready to go.

Now, the backfield. With Harris gone, Skattebo has a chance to rebuild trust. But I would not hand him his old workload automatically. Holding onto the football is the starting point. After that, I want to see patience behind the blocking and a clear understanding of his assignment. Running hard is valuable, but it does not help if you are hitting the wrong hole or forcing a play before the blocks develop. One idea I have seen floated is five to ten touches as a reasonable starting point for Skattebo, depending on how the game goes, and I agree with that. Earn a manageable role and build from there. Tyrone Tracy Jr. could get more work after scoring two touchdowns and grading as the team’s best pass protector against the Saints. And Devin Singletary can stay involved, especially on third down. That gives the coaches a way to evaluate what they have, who protects the ball, who handles the details, who keeps the offense on schedule, before deciding an outside addition is necessary.

And yes, the trade chatter is out there. Jonathan Taylor would get anybody’s attention, with the Colts in the middle of a front office shake-up. But interest and availability are two very different things. I am not saying the Colts are offering him, or that the Giants are negotiating for him. If the price involved a valuable draft pick, I would have serious questions, because this team has bigger needs on the offensive line. Tony Pollard is another name some people have mentioned. A player can be useful and still be the wrong move if the cost does not make sense. Fix the blocking first. Then worry about the running back.

So, here is where it all lands. McCarthy goes into his first start with 100 percent of the playbook, a week of first-team reps, and an offensive coordinator who has built offenses around young quarterbacks before. The offensive line is getting bodies back at practice. Paulson Adebo is working his way back. But the secondary is still banged up, Tremaine Edmunds is uncertain, and the defense has to answer Dennard Wilson’s challenge.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, the final injury report. Who is officially in, and who is out, especially Thomas, Edmunds, Holland, and Newsome? Number two, McCarthy’s first few drives. Does the game plan look built for him, with movement, bootlegs, and quick answers? Number three, defensive pursuit. If a Texan breaks a tackle, are there three more blue jerseys arriving? And number four, the turnover column, because that is still the number that decides this game.

The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, in first place in the NFC East, with the Eagles missing Jalen Hurts and the Cowboys at 2 and 4. A win in Houston sends them into the bye on top of the division, with a young quarterback who now has the full playbook and two more weeks to grow. I still believe what I have been saying all season. This team can go all the way.
