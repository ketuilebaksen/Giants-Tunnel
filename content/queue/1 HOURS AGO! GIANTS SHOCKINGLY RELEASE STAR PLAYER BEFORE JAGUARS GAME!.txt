The New York Giants just made a move nobody saw coming this week. Right before a huge showdown with a 7-2 Jacksonville team, the Giants released Charlie Jones, the man they trusted with the ball on punts and kickoffs. In his place, they signed Xavier Gipson off the Eagles’ practice squad. And that was not the only move. They also brought in veteran defensive back Tavierre Thomas from the Vikings’ practice squad. Two moves, both aimed at the same problem, special teams. And there are still big questions about the kicker. Let’s break all of it down, and then I want to talk about what the Vikings might be thinking right now about J.J. McCarthy.

First, the context. The Giants lost 24-21 to Washington last Thursday, a game they had multiple chances to put away. They are 5-3-1, still alone in first in the NFC East, two games ahead of both Washington and Dallas. And now Jacksonville comes to MetLife on Sunday at 7-2, fresh off putting 40 points on Tennessee. The front office clearly is not standing still after that loss. And when you look at these moves, the timing tells you a lot.

Move number one: Charlie Jones is out, Xavier Gipson is in.

Let me be clear about something before we start. Charlie Jones was not a star in the traditional sense. But he had one of the most important jobs on this team. Every punt. Every kickoff. The first touch of so many drives. And that is why this move matters.

Here is why the Giants did it. Every time Jones got under a punt or a kickoff, I got nervous. And I think a lot of you did too. I wanted to trust him, but I always wondered if he was going to secure the football. And those concerns were not imaginary. He lost a fumble on a kickoff against New Orleans. He lost another one on a punt against Philadelphia. In a season where so many Giants games have been decided by one score, you simply cannot keep handing the other team free possessions. That muff in Philadelphia could have flipped that whole game. The Giants survived it. They might not survive the next one.

So now it is Xavier Gipson. And there is some familiarity here. Gipson was with the Giants during the preseason, so he knows the building, he knows the coaches, he knows the system. He is a smaller guy, 5-foot-9, and he has real experience returning both punts and kickoffs. Across his career, he has 671 punt return yards. In 2024, he had 33 punt returns for 266 yards. Last season, between the Jets and the Eagles, he added 86 yards on eight punt returns and 309 yards on 12 kickoff returns. That is real experience.

But I am not going to sit here and tell you this is an automatic upgrade, because there is a number that worries me. Gipson has fumbled 12 times in his NFL career, and five of those were lost to the other team. That is the exact problem the Giants are trying to fix. So this is a bet on a guy with experience and familiarity, not a guaranteed fix. He also has not appeared in a game this season, which makes his debut against Jacksonville even more interesting.

My opinion, not a report: I like the thinking. When you have a returner you cannot trust, you have to make a change, even if the replacement is not perfect. A fresh set of hands and a fresh mindset can matter. But Gipson has to earn trust on Sunday. If he catches everything cleanly and gives the offense a few extra yards of field position, this move is a win. If he puts one on the ground, the Giants are right back where they started.

And by the way, this also answers a question we were asking last week. A lot of fans wanted the Giants to sign Hollywood Brown, and I said the deciding factor was whether he could return kicks. Instead, the Giants went straight to a guy whose main value is the return game. That tells you exactly what the coaching staff was most worried about.

Move number two: Tavierre Thomas joins the secondary.

On the defensive side, the Giants signed Tavierre Thomas from the Minnesota practice squad and made a corresponding roster move to open up the spot. Thomas has been around the league for nine seasons. He has played for Cleveland, Houston, Tampa Bay and Minnesota. He has slot corner experience, which adds depth to the secondary. But the part I really like is his other role. Thomas has spent a lot of his career on special teams. He knows how to cover kicks. He knows how to play on the punt unit. He is a core special teams type of player.

So when you put these two moves together, I do not think the timing is random. One guy can return kicks. The other guy can help cover them. To me, this is the front office and John Harbaugh responding directly to the mistakes that have made winnable games harder. Muffed returns. Bad coverage. Field position lost. And against a Jacksonville team that just scored 40 on Tennessee, you cannot give away short fields. If you do, you are asking your defense to rescue you all afternoon.

And remember, Harbaugh came up as a special teams coach. This is his world. When he sees a problem in the kicking game, he is going to attack it. These two moves are him attacking it.

Let me explain why this matters so much, because special teams can feel like an afterthought until it costs you a game. Think about how the Giants play. This is a team built on defense, a defense that was fifth in the league in total defense heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game. It is a team that wins close, low-scoring games. In those games, every possession and every yard of field position is magnified. A clean punt return that starts you at the 40 instead of the 25 is the difference between a field goal try and a punt. A muffed return that gives the opponent the ball at your 20 is basically a gift of three or seven points. And when you lose by three, like the Giants did against Washington, those hidden yards add up fast.

So here is what I am watching from Gipson on Sunday. Number one, does he catch everything? I do not care if he breaks a long one. I care that the ball is secure every single time. Number two, does he make smart decisions about when to fair catch and when to let the ball bounce? A lot of returner mistakes come from trying to do too much. Number three, does he give the offense a few extra yards when the return is there? If he checks those three boxes, the Giants have fixed one of their biggest leaks.

And on coverage, Tavierre Thomas is the kind of veteran who brings something that does not show up in the box score. Nine seasons in the league means he has seen every kind of return scheme. He knows how to stay in his lane, how to break down in space, how to avoid the block in the back that kills a drive before it starts. Special teams coaches love guys like that, and Harbaugh is a special teams coach at heart. Do not be surprised if Thomas is out there on most of the kicking units right away.

There is also a message in these moves for the rest of the roster. Charlie Jones was a guy the Giants liked, and they still moved on after two lost fumbles. That tells every player in that locker room that ball security is not optional. It is the standard. And after a game where the Giants lost a fumble deep in Washington territory, I think that message was intended for more than just the return man.

Now, the biggest special teams question is still sitting there. The kicker.

Let’s look at the full numbers on Dominic Zvada, because I want to be fair to him. Through nine games, he is 17 for 23 on field goals. He made his first nine attempts of the season. Then he went 8 for 14 across his next five games. From 50 yards and beyond, he is 6 for 9. So three of his misses came from shorter distances. He has made all 16 of his extra points. And here is the detail that stands out: all six of his misses have come from the right hash. Harbaugh confirmed there is a plant-foot technique issue showing up on that side.

Against Washington, he missed a 33-yarder in a game the Giants lost by three. And I want to be careful here, because football is not that simple. Plenty of other mistakes contributed to that loss. But those are three points you expect your kicker to deliver. And it goes beyond the points themselves. When you do not fully trust your kicker, every fourth-down decision near field-goal range gets complicated. A big leg is exciting when you are lining up from 55. It does not help much if you cannot depend on a kick from 33.

So do the Giants make a change before Jacksonville? Let’s look at the options people are talking about.

Ben Sauls is the first name. He kicked well for the Giants last year. He does not have Zvada’s proven long-range power, but there is a real argument for valuing consistency. Sauls was released with an injury settlement before this season, and he has reportedly been getting looks from other teams.

Graham Gano is another familiar name. But his injury history makes that tough. The Giants released him earlier this year after a failed physical related to his neck, so I would not assume he is ready to come back.

And Justin Tucker is on the Chiefs’ practice squad. Personally, I would not want the Giants to go there. There are a lot of questions around him, and I do not see a clear reason to take on that uncertainty in the middle of a division race.

On the other side, there is the case for patience. Harbaugh has backed his young kicker publicly, and he has made it clear he does not want to panic. He said they are not going to knee-jerk it with a rookie who has made a bunch of kicks. And I understand that, especially with a kicker who has shown serious leg strength and has six makes from 50-plus.

Here is where I land. My opinion, not a report: patience is fine, but patience has to come with results. The Giants just made two special teams moves this week. If the kicker was not on the list, that tells me Harbaugh is giving Zvada at least one more game to show the right-hash fix is working. I think that is reasonable. But if he misses a makeable kick in a close game against Jacksonville, I think the next special teams move is at kicker.

Now, while special teams have everybody talking, I want to switch gears to something I find fascinating. J.J. McCarthy, and what the Minnesota Vikings might be thinking right now.

There is a former NFL scout who argued this week that Kevin O’Connell was a driving force behind Minnesota moving on from McCarthy. That is his opinion, not a confirmed fact, and I want to be clear about that. But the bigger picture is worth talking about.

The Vikings drafted McCarthy in the top ten. He missed his entire rookie season with injury. In his second year, he started 10 games and went 6-4. That is not perfect, but it is not nothing for a young quarterback still learning the league. And then Minnesota traded him away and accepted a fifth-round pick in return. Later, the Vikings brought in Kyler Murray, and then they sent a third-round pick to the Raiders to bring back Kirk Cousins.

Look at that sequence for a second. You move a young quarterback you drafted in the top ten for a fifth-round pick. Then you spend a third-round pick to go get a veteran, because you still need dependable play at the position. Yes, injuries played a part in all of that. But it tells you Minnesota was not comfortable with its quarterback situation, and it makes you wonder if they gave up on McCarthy too soon.

Because look at what he did on Thursday. Against Washington, McCarthy completed 23 of 36 passes for a career-high 293 yards, with one touchdown and one interception. Was he flawless? Absolutely not. The final interception hurt. The struggles against the blitz are real. But I saw throws in that game that made me believe there is more to develop here. His arm gave the Giants chances, even under pressure. And Harbaugh said after watching the tape that he feels more strongly than ever that McCarthy is capable of winning games.

So I would not be surprised if Minnesota looks back at that trade one day and wonders if it made the decision too early. My opinion, not a report.

Now, here is where it gets interesting for the Giants. This is still Jackson Dart’s team long-term, in my view. Dart was the guy the offense was built around, and he can create plays that I do not want the Giants to give up on. But I refuse to ignore what McCarthy is doing. Having two young quarterbacks with real ability is a good problem to have, as long as the Giants handle it the right way.

If McCarthy keeps improving, next offseason could bring real choices. Maybe there is an open competition when Dart is healthy. Maybe another team sees McCarthy’s progress and calls the Giants with a real offer. Remember, the Vikings let him go for a fifth. If he finishes this season strong, his value could be a lot higher than that. Neither of those things is guaranteed, so nobody should rush it. Right now, the job is simple. Help the quarterback who is playing and keep this team winning.

And speaking of winning, let’s talk about the path.

At 5-3-1, the Giants are still leading the NFC East. The Washington loss hurt, but I still see a clear path to nine wins, and possibly more. There are games against Indianapolis, Cleveland, Dallas and Philadelphia that I believe are winnable. Win those four, and you are at nine wins. And depending on what happens around the division, that could be enough. Dallas is 4-6 after getting hammered by San Francisco. Washington is 3-5-1, but holds the head-to-head edge. Philadelphia is 2-7.

But I will not pretend the road is easy. Jacksonville is a serious test. San Francisco worries me too. And Seattle and Detroit are tough road trips. That is why Sunday matters so much. Beating a 7-2 Jaguars team at home would send a much stronger message than any amount of talk about what this team might do later.

And for that to happen, I need more from some important players.

Cam Skattebo gave the Giants 78 rushing yards and two touchdowns against Washington. I want him to keep bringing that same energy while protecting the football.

Malik Nabers is the other one. The costly fumble in that game was his, deep in Washington territory. And he could not come down with that difficult final ball on the last drive. Not every tough catch is going to be completed. But when your team needs somebody to come down with the ball, your best playmakers have to give you their best. I think Nabers knows that. And I think he has a big response game coming.

The same goes for McCarthy and the offense as a whole. Moving the ball is great, but you have to finish drives. If the defense gives you another opportunity, you cannot hand it right back with a quick three-and-out. Washington punished those mistakes, and Jacksonville could punish them even faster. The Giants do not need a perfect afternoon on Sunday. But they absolutely need a cleaner one.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Last week I said the Giants needed a reliable returner, and that the return game was a bigger issue than people realized. The front office agreed and made the move. I also said I would ride with Zvada through Jacksonville, and it looks like the Giants are doing the same. And I picked the Giants to beat Washington. That one was wrong. So we keep moving.

Here is my prediction for Sunday, and hold me to it. Gipson catches every punt cleanly and gives the Giants at least one big return. Tavierre Thomas makes a tackle on kick coverage. Zvada goes perfect on the day. McCarthy plays a clean game, and Skattebo carries the offense. And the Giants knock off Jacksonville and move to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. The front office is not sitting still. The coach is attacking the problems that cost them in Washington. The quarterback is growing. The defense is top five in the league. And the division lead is still two games. When I look at these two additions, I am cautiously encouraged. Not because they fix everything, but because they show a team that knows exactly what went wrong and is doing something about it.
