John Harbaugh’s quarterback decision for Houston is taking shape, and if you read his words carefully, it might not be the answer half of this fanbase was hoping for. At his Monday press conference, the very first question was about the quarterback, and Harbaugh said the staff is working through it and honing in on a decision. But listen to how he talked about each man. On Jameis Winston, he said he admires and respects him, that Winston has helped this team win games and has done what he had to do. On J.J. McCarthy, he pointed out that McCarthy just got here a couple of weeks ago, and that the Giants are still building an offense he has a feel for. Folks, that is a coach telling you which way he is leaning. My read, and I am labeling it clearly as my read, is that Jameis Winston is in line to start against the Texans, with McCarthy’s role continuing to grow, even after McCarthy threw for 243 yards in a single quarter last Sunday. That is a huge call, because it means Harbaugh is choosing a full week of preparation and a veteran who knows the system over the hottest fourth quarter any Giants quarterback has had in years. And it comes in the same week the Giants reshaped their roster with a series of moves that tell you a lot about how this staff thinks. Today, I am going to break down the quarterback decision, every Tuesday roster move, and a Texans team that looks a lot more dangerous than its record. Let’s get into it.

If Harbaugh goes with Winston in Houston, do you agree, or do you want to see McCarthy start?

Let me start with why this decision is so hard, because you cannot understand it without remembering what happened against the Saints. The Giants were down 34 to 10 with 11:25 left when J.J. McCarthy replaced Jameis Winston. He went on to throw for 243 yards, every one of them in the fourth quarter. That ties for the most passing yards by a Giant in a single quarter since at least 1991, right alongside Eli Manning, who also had 243 in a fourth quarter against Tampa Bay in 2012. And since 1950, that was the only time the Giants ever had two quarterbacks throw for at least 150 yards in the same game. Folks, that is not a backup quarterback quietly finishing a blowout. That is a quarterback who walked into a 24-point hole and nearly dug his team out of it.

So why would Harbaugh lean toward Winston? Let’s go through it, because his words give us the answer.

First, the system. Harbaugh said McCarthy just got here a couple of weeks ago, and the Giants are still building an offense that he has a feel for. That is the key phrase. When a quarterback comes in during desperation time, the offense is simple. Spread it out, throw it deep, go fast. The defense is playing soft, protecting a big lead. But starting a game is a completely different job. You have to know every protection, every audible, every check at the line against every look a defense can show you. You have to handle a full game plan built specifically to stop you. And McCarthy, through no fault of his own, has had only a few weeks to learn all of that. Harbaugh is telling you that the Giants are building that offense for McCarthy, but it is not finished yet.

Second, the trust. Harbaugh said he admires and respects Winston, and that Winston has helped this team win games. Remember, this is the quarterback who took over after Jackson Dart went down and went unbeaten in his first three starts, two wins and a tie. He threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games, the first Giant to do that since Eli in 2016. He led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback in Washington. And here is a detail a lot of people forget. The Giants signed Winston to a two-year extension back on September 7th, before the season even got rolling. That is not a franchise treating him like a stopgap. That is a commitment.

Third, the way Winston handled Sunday. After he was pulled, he did not complain, he did not campaign, he praised McCarthy, and he said the decision belongs to his head coach. He took full responsibility for the plays he left out there. When a veteran handles adversity like that, it earns him credit in the building. And I think that matters to Harbaugh.

But I am not going to pretend the case for McCarthy has gone away, because it has not. Against the Saints, McCarthy averaged close to 12 yards per attempt, while Winston averaged just under six. McCarthy attacked downfield, he showed his arm, and he scrambled for 28 yards on the final drive. And the reason Winston was pulled in the first place was turnovers. Two second-half interceptions that turned directly into 10 Saints points, on a team whose head coach has called ball security the most important thing with his offense. If Winston starts in Houston and the turnovers continue, the pressure to make a change is going to be enormous.

And think about the price tag on McCarthy. The Giants got him from Minnesota on September 28th for a 2027 fifth-round pick. That is not a huge investment, and that matters, because it means the Giants are not under pressure to play him just to justify a trade. They can bring him along the right way. Build the offense around his strengths. Grow his package week by week. And then, when he is ready to run a full game plan, give him the keys. That is the patient approach, and I think it is the approach Harbaugh has been signaling all week.

And remember McCarthy’s story, because it explains why patience makes sense. He was a top-ten pick by the Vikings in 2024, a national champion at Michigan, and then he missed his entire rookie season with a knee injury. His path in Minnesota never went the way anybody planned, and by this fall, the Vikings were willing to move him for a late-round pick. That is a quarterback who has been through a lot of disruption in a short career. The last thing the Giants want is to throw him into a full starting role before the offense is built for him, have it go badly, and set him back again. If they bring him along the right way, they may end up with a quarterback who can help them for a long time, not just for one quarter.

So, if my read is right, what does the plan look like in Houston? I expect Winston to start and run the base offense. I expect McCarthy to get a real package, more than the handful of snaps he got in Washington. Some designed runs, some read-option looks, some shot plays where his arm can stretch the defense. And I expect Harbaugh to have a much shorter leash than he did early in the season. If the turnovers show up again, I would not be surprised to see McCarthy earlier than the fourth quarter. That is the balance. Respect the veteran, develop the young quarterback, and protect the football above everything.

And if it is Winston, here is what he has to do in Houston. First, keep it simple early. Against the Saints, his first interception came when the offense went into turbo, the up-tempo mode, and a ball he thought only his receiver could reach ended up with a defensive back. In a hostile road stadium, the Giants do not need hero throws in the first quarter. They need completions, first downs, and a quarterback who gets comfortable. Second, lean on Malik Nabers. Nabers just had his third straight 100-yard game, 12 catches for 165 yards against the Saints, and he is chasing Odell Beckham Jr. in the record books. When in doubt, give your best player a chance. Third, stay ahead of the chains. Winston said it himself after the Saints game. He wants to stay positive on first and second down. If the Giants can do that, the playbook opens up, the turnovers go down, and the pressure on the quarterback decision eases. If Winston plays a clean, efficient game in Houston, this whole controversy cools off. If he does not, the door opens wide for McCarthy.

Now, let me give you the other side one more time, because some of you are going to be furious about this. Some fans will say, how can you sit a guy who just threw for 243 yards in a quarter? You ride the hot hand. And that is a fair point. Quarterbacks get confidence from playing, and McCarthy’s confidence has never been higher. But here is how I would answer it. A hot quarter in desperation time is not the same as being ready to start against a playoff-caliber quarterback on the road. Harbaugh has been through this exact decision before, in Baltimore with Joe Flacco and Lamar Jackson, and he did not make that switch after one quarter. He made it when the young quarterback was ready, and when the situation demanded it. I trust him to do the same here.

Now, the roster moves, because the Giants made a series of changes on Tuesday that I did not fully expect.

Let’s start with Trace Ford, because this channel has been banging the drum for him for weeks. On Tuesday, October 20th, the Giants signed Ford to the active roster off the practice squad. He is number 48, 6 foot 2, 252 pounds, out of Oklahoma. He has been in this organization’s plans for a while. He was on the reserve/future list back in January. He was waived on August 30th, then went right back onto the practice squad on August 31st. And since then, he has been a game-day regular. The Giants elevated him on October 3rd, again on October 10th, and again on October 17th. Three weeks in a row. You do not keep calling a guy up unless the coaches like what they see. Now, he has a real roster spot, and he has earned it.

And look at who went out the door to make that happen. The Giants released Najee Harris, who signed as a free agent on August 18th, only about two months ago. A veteran running back cut six games into the season tells you exactly how the Giants feel about that position right now. And they released Jihad Ward, the outside linebacker they signed on October 1st from Minnesota’s practice squad. Under three weeks with the team. When you set that right next to the Trace Ford move, the picture is clear. The Giants took the player who has been in their building, getting elevated week after week, and decided he deserves the spot more than a short-term pickup. I like that. Rewarding the guy who has been putting in the work is a fair way to run a roster, and it sends a message to everybody on the practice squad.

And you cannot talk about that outside linebacker spot without remembering what else has happened there. Brian Burns went on injured reserve on September 29th. Chauncey Golston went on injured reserve on October 10th. On October 13th, the Giants brought in Dawuane Smoot as a practice squad veteran. That room has been reshuffled for weeks. I would not call it panic. I would call it a team searching for the right group, and Trace Ford is the latest answer.

The last piece from Tuesday was wide receiver Jason Brownlee, who was added to the practice squad. And receiver has been another position in constant motion. Odell Beckham Jr. was let go on September 29th. Braxton Berrios went on injured reserve on October 7th. And with Darnell Mooney leaving the Saints game with a neck injury, the Giants need bodies behind Malik Nabers, Malachi Fields, and Charlie Jones. Brownlee is simply the next name in line. And look at the rest of the last couple of weeks. Malik Harrison was signed to the active roster on October 13th. Josh Gesky joined the practice squad on October 14th. That is a lot of movement for a team sitting at 3 and 2 and 1. To me, that tells you the Giants are not sitting around hoping things fix themselves. They are tinkering every single week, and I respect that.

Now, the opponent. And folks, do not let the record fool you. The Houston Texans are 2 and 4 on paper, but they look a lot better than that right now. They lost their first four games, and then they won back-to-back. Their latest win was a 34 to 31 thriller over the Jaguars at Wembley Stadium in London, with Ka’imi Fairbairn kicking a 49-yard field goal as time expired. That is a team that has found its belief.

And C.J. Stroud is putting up numbers. He is second in the NFL in passing yards with 1,714 through six weeks, behind only Matthew Stafford at 1,794, and the Giants already saw Stafford back in week two. So, this is the second top passer in the league that this secondary will face this season. And remember who Stroud is. He was the Offensive Rookie of the Year in 2023, when he led the Texans to a division title as a rookie. He is not a young quarterback you can confuse with one disguise. He reads defenses fast, he throws with anticipation, and when he gets into a rhythm, he can pick apart a secondary in a hurry. And with Colton Hood and Greg Newsome II both dealing with concussions after the Saints game, the Giants’ cornerback depth is going to be tested again.

And here is a storyline Giants fans will love. The Texans are adding weapons, and one of them is Marvin Harrison Jr. He made his Texans debut after being traded from the Cardinals, and he had three catches for 34 yards, every one of them for a first down. And get this. His most recent game before that trade was at MetLife Stadium, against the Giants. So, the Giants are going to see him again this Sunday, in a different uniform. And yes, this channel once floated Harrison Jr. as a name the Giants could think about. Now he is lining up against them.

On top of that, DeMeco Ryans confirmed that Tank Dell is coming back on Sunday. Dell has 10 touchdowns in 25 career games, but he has not played since December 21st, 2024, because of a serious knee injury. That is a long road back, and credit to him for making it. Ryans said he is not putting huge expectations on him, he just wants Dell to play the offense the right way. But a healthy Tank Dell, with his speed, next to a new weapon like Harrison Jr., gives Stroud more options than he has had all season.

So, what does that mean for the Giants? It means the pass rush has to get home. Kayvon Thibodeaux has the highest pass-rush win rate on the team over the last two weeks. Abdul Carter is a weapon when healthy. Now Trace Ford gets his chance on the active roster. And Dennard Wilson’s blitz packages have to speed up Stroud’s clock, because if he gets time to throw to those receivers, this secondary is going to have a long day. At the same time, the run defense has to be fixed after giving up 175 yards to the Saints. If Houston can run the ball and then throw it over the top, that is the nightmare scenario.

And this is why the quarterback decision matters so much. In a game where Stroud can put up points, the Giants cannot afford to give away possessions. Four turnovers against the Saints turned a 10-point lead into a 24-point deficit. Against Stroud, the same mistakes would be fatal. Whoever is under center has to protect the football, and the offense has to sustain drives to keep Stroud on the sideline. That is the real test of Harbaugh’s decision. Not the stat line. The turnover column.

And before Sunday, there is one more thing I will be watching, and that is the practice reports. Who takes the first-team reps on Wednesday and Thursday tells you a lot. If Winston is getting the bulk of them, the decision is made, whether Harbaugh announces it or not. If the reps are split closer to even, that tells you McCarthy’s package is going to be a real part of the game plan. And keep an eye on Darnell Mooney’s neck, Andrew Thomas’s status, and the two cornerbacks with concussions. Every one of those names matters against a passing attack like Houston’s.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. Number one, the quarterback. Does Winston start, and how early do we see McCarthy? Number two, the turnovers. Zero has to be the goal. Number three, the pass rush against Stroud, including Trace Ford’s first game as an official member of the active roster. And number four, the running game. With Najee Harris gone, how do Cam Skattebo, Tyrone Tracy Jr., and Devin Singletary split the work?

The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, still in first place in the NFC East. The Eagles just lost Jalen Hurts. The Cowboys are 2 and 4. A win in Houston sends this team into the bye week at 4 and 2 and 1, on top of the division, with two weeks to get healthy. A loss, and suddenly this team has gone three games without a win. That is how big Sunday is. And I believe this team is ready for it. They have fought back two weeks in a row. Now it is time to finish. This team can go all the way.
