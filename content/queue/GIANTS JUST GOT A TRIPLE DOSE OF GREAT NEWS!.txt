Triple dose of good news for the Giants, and the first one just made a cornerback very rich. Greg Newsome, the former first-round pick who came to New York on a one-year prove-it deal this offseason, just signed a three-year, 46 million dollar extension, with 32 million dollars guaranteed. Halfway through his first season as a Giant, he proved it. That is dose number one. Dose number two, the rest of the NFC East fell further behind while the Giants sat on the couch during their bye. And dose number three, the defensive tackle market just opened up. Albert Breer says Patriots defensive tackle Christian Barmore is very clearly available, for a price that sounds like a fourth-round pick. And that matters, because the Giants were reportedly interested in Washington’s Daron Payne before he broke his foot. So the Giants are paying their own, sitting in first place, and shopping for help up front with the deadline five days away. Let’s get into it.

Was 46 million dollars the right price to keep Greg Newsome?

Let me start with Newsome, because this deal tells you a lot about where this franchise is right now. According to his agent, Erik Burkhardt, the extension is three years and 46 million dollars, with 32 million guaranteed. Newsome is 26 years old. He was a first-round pick by the Cleveland Browns in 2021, out of Northwestern. The Browns traded him to Jacksonville last October. And this past offseason, the Giants signed him to a one-year deal.

Think about how rare this is. One-year deals are usually exactly what they sound like. A player bets on himself, plays out the season, and then hits free agency to find out what the market will pay. Teams almost never tear up a prove-it deal halfway through the year and give the player a long-term contract with that much guaranteed money. For the Giants to do it now, in early November, tells you they saw enough. And they did not want to risk him walking in March.

And the numbers back it up. Through seven games, Newsome has 17 tackles, six passes defended, and an interception. But numbers do not tell the whole story for a cornerback. The best corners often have quiet stat sheets, because quarterbacks stop throwing at them. And remember what the national broadcast pointed out a couple of weeks ago. The Giants are allowing the third fewest yards per completion in the league, and they have the fourth most pass breakups. Newsome is a big part of that.

And here is why this connects to something I told you a couple of weeks ago. At his bye-week press conference, Joe Schoen talked about a choice the Giants had to make before the deadline. He said they would look at the whole roster, the free agents and the trade candidates, and decide whether to approach some of their own players about extensions during the bye, or whether to spend draft resources and financial resources on somebody outside the building. That was the choice. And now we know at least part of the answer. The Giants took care of one of their own first.

At the time, the reporters were asking about Deonte Banks and possible extension talks. I spent a lot of time on Banks in that video. And it turns out the first extension went to the other cornerback, the one who was only supposed to be here for a year. I did not see that coming. But I love it. When a team is winning, the players notice who gets rewarded. Newsome came in on a one-year deal, played like a starter, and got paid. Every player in that locker room just saw that. That is how you build a culture where people want to stay.

And think about Newsome’s journey to get here. A first-round pick in Cleveland. Traded to Jacksonville in the middle of last season. Then a one-year deal in New York, where nobody promised him anything. That is three teams in about a year. A lot of players would have taken that personally and let it affect their play. Newsome used it as fuel. He came into a new building, with a new coaching staff that gave everybody a blank slate, and he earned his money the hard way. That is the kind of story Harbaugh loves.

And look at what the secondary looks like now. Newsome on one side. Deonte Banks, who won his job out of training camp and has turned his career around, on the other. Paulson Adebo, who came back from injured reserve and started in Houston. Tyler Nubin at safety. That is a young, deep group that is only getting better. And with Newsome locked up through 2029, the Giants do not have to worry about rebuilding the cornerback room next spring. They can spend that energy and money somewhere else.

And there is one more layer to this. Paying Newsome now does not stop the Giants from adding at the deadline. Remember, the Giants are in a strong cap position. Newsome’s extension locks in the secondary for the next few years, and it lets the front office focus its trade energy on the two spots everyone keeps pointing at. Running back and defensive tackle.

Now, dose number two. I covered this in detail a couple of videos ago, so I will keep it short. While the Giants were on their bye, Dallas lost at home in overtime to Arizona, 33 to 30. And on Sunday night, Philadelphia lost 27 to 10 to Washington, its sixth straight loss. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1 and in first place. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. The Giants have two losses. Nobody else in the division has fewer than four.

And this is why every insider keeps saying the same thing. When people talk about which teams will be aggressive buyers at the deadline, the Giants are one of the first names. I cannot tell you how strange that feels. For years, the Giants were the team waiting for something to happen, the team selling at the deadline or doing nothing at all. Now they are the team people are watching. That is what a three-loss lead in your division does.

But I want to keep a warning in your head, because it matters. Every rumor at the deadline sounds like a great idea. And not every big deadline trade works. In recent years, we have seen contenders give up real picks for players who got hurt or did not fit. A first-place record does not guarantee that a trade will work. And the Giants are doing this without Brian Burns, without Tremaine Edmunds, and without Jaxson Dart. Schoen said that does not change much about how they approach the deadline. But it does mean any move has to fill a real hole, not just make a headline.

So, let’s get to dose number three, the defensive tackle market. And it starts with a deal that almost happened. According to Ian Rapoport, the Giants were interested in Washington defensive tackle Daron Payne. Washington was making him available. And then, on the Friday before the Commanders played the Eagles, Payne suffered a Jones fracture in his foot in practice. It was season-ending, and he went on injured reserve. Rapoport said we will never know whether a trade would have happened.

Now, I want to be careful here, because I have seen some people say the Giants were about to finalize a deal for Payne. That is not what was reported. What was reported is that the Giants were interested and Washington was willing to listen. We do not know how close it got. But it tells you that the Giants’ interest in defensive tackle is real. First Jeffery Simmons, where Harbaugh called the Titans’ owner. Then Payne, before the injury. When a team chases two different defensive tackles in two weeks, that is not noise. That is a plan.

And there is another interesting wrinkle here. Payne was in the final year of his contract. He is a former 13th overall pick out of Alabama, and he will be a free agent next year. So even though the trade did not happen, the Giants could circle back in March. But that does not help them this season. They need a big body in the middle now, with Dexter Lawrence gone and Dean Lowry still on the practice squad.

And here is something that matters for the Giants on Thursday night. Payne’s injury hurts Washington too. When he went down, Washington was already without Javon Kinlaw, and Johnny Newton was dealing with a pec injury. That left the Commanders very thin on the interior. They still beat Philadelphia on Sunday night, so I am not going to pretend they are helpless. But when the Giants host Washington on a short week, they will be facing a defensive line that just lost its most experienced interior player. If the Giants can run the ball the way they did in Houston, that could be a big edge.

So, who is left? That is where Christian Barmore comes in. Albert Breer said on NBC Sports Boston that the Patriots have listened to offers for Barmore after he fell behind Milton Williams and Cory Durden in their rotation. Breer said, quote, this is a player that very clearly is available. And his understanding is that the price would be about a fourth-round pick.

Let me give you the whole picture on Barmore, the good and the bad. The good. He was a second-round pick by New England in 2021. In 2024, he signed a four-year, 84 million dollar extension. And even in a reduced role this year, he has been productive. He has 3 and a half sacks, second on the team, and 14 quarterback pressures. He is a disruptive interior pass rusher when he is on the field.

The bad. Right after that 2024 extension, he was diagnosed with blood clots and missed nearly all of that season. This year, his snap share has dropped from about 65 percent last season to under 44 percent. And his cap hit is 17.1 million dollars, the third largest on the Patriots. If New England trades him, it clears almost 8 million dollars this season and about 41 million across 2027 and 2028. That is why the Patriots are open to it. And that is the money the Giants would be taking on.

So, does Barmore make sense for the Giants? I think he could, but with conditions. First, the price. A fourth-round pick is a fair price for a player with his talent. But here is the problem. The Giants do not have their own 2027 fourth-round pick. It went in the deal to move up for Malachi Fields. And the projected compensatory fourth cannot be traded until the league actually awards it, which happens in the spring. So a fourth-round deal would probably mean a 2028 fourth, or a different combination of picks. That is doable, but it is not as simple as it sounds.

Second, the money. Barmore’s contract is big, and it runs through 2028. Harbaugh said any deal has to fit the Giants’ financial profile and price tag, and it cannot be a one-and-done. Barmore passes the one-and-done test, because he is under contract for two more years after this one. But the Giants would have to believe he is worth that money in 2027 and 2028, not just for the next ten games. They just committed 46 million dollars to Newsome. They have to make sure the numbers work.

And third, the fit. With Lawrence gone, the Giants need a big body who can hold up against the run, especially on third and short. Barmore is more of a pass rusher than a pure run stuffer. But here is what excites me. Picture Barmore pushing the pocket from the inside while Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux, and Arvell Reese come off the edge. Dennard Wilson has been stacking those three on the same side of the formation to create problems. Add an interior rusher who can collapse the middle, and quarterbacks would have nowhere to step up. That could be scary.

So here is my take, and this is my opinion, not a report. If the Giants can get Barmore for a 2028 fourth or a similar mid-round package, I would do it. The risk is the contract and his health history. But the upside is a disruptive interior player on a defense that has been missing one since Lawrence left. And unlike Jonathan Taylor, he would not cost a premium pick.

And let me compare the options the Giants have been linked to, because they are very different players. Simmons was the dream, an All-Pro, but Chicago paid two first-round picks. Payne was the experienced veteran on an expiring deal, but his season is over. Grover Stewart came up in Art Stapleton’s trade idea as part of a bigger Taylor package, and he is more of a pure run stuffer. And Barmore is the interior pass rusher with upside, health questions, and a big contract. If the Giants want a run stopper, Stewart fits better. If they want someone who can collapse the pocket next to their edge rushers, Barmore fits better. My guess is they would take either one at the right price.

And speaking of Taylor, the situation has not changed much since my last video. The Giants have been in contact with the Colts. Ryan Dunleavy reported that the asking price is high, and that the Giants will look at other offensive playmakers. Taylor himself has said he has not asked for a trade and told the Colts’ owner he loves the organization. My prediction from last time stands. I do not think Taylor will be a Giant by November 10th. But I would love to be wrong.

I have also seen people float bigger names, like Maxx Crosby. Let me be clear. Nobody credible has reported that the Giants are pursuing Crosby. That is a fan idea, not a report. And with the Giants’ draft picks, I have a hard time seeing how a true blockbuster for an elite player would work. Right now, the Giants have their first, second, and third-round picks in 2027, plus two sixths and a projected compensatory fourth. That is not a lot of ammunition for a superstar.

So when people ask, if it is not Taylor, who is the blockbuster? My answer is, there might not be one. And that might be okay. The Giants might end up making two smaller moves instead of one big one. A defensive tackle like Barmore, and maybe a veteran running back or a pass catcher to help until Tracy returns. That is not as exciting as a blockbuster. But it might be smarter.

And I keep coming back to how this team got here. Not through blockbusters. Through smart moves. A fifth-round pick for J.J. McCarthy. A one-year deal for Greg Newsome that just turned into a long-term starter. Picking up players off other teams’ practice squads. Bringing back Eric Gray when the backfield got thin. This front office has been at its best when it finds value. I want them to stay that way at the deadline.

Now, let’s not forget the game that matters most this week. Philadelphia, on Sunday. The Eagles are 2 and 6, they have lost six in a row, and they are playing with Tanner McKee at quarterback. The Giants have lost 13 straight in Philadelphia, counting the playoffs. Harbaugh said they do not care about that streak. Good. Cam Skattebo is day-to-day with his hamstring, and Abdul Carter missed Monday’s practice with an illness. Those are the two names I am watching on the practice reports. And then the Giants come home to face Washington on Thursday night. Two division games in five days.

And here is the connection to the deadline. The deadline is Tuesday, two days after the Eagles game. If the Giants win in Philadelphia, they will be 5 and 2 and 1, with an even bigger cushion. That might make them more willing to add a piece like Barmore for the stretch run. If they lose, they might get more careful. Either way, the next five days will shape the rest of this season.

And for Newsome personally, Sunday is a great chance to show the Giants made the right call. The Eagles’ passing game has struggled with Tanner McKee at quarterback, and Washington sacked him four times on Sunday night. If the Giants’ pass rush gets home, McKee will be forced into quick throws, and that is where a cornerback like Newsome can jump a route and change a game. A big play from him in Philadelphia, a week after getting paid, would be the perfect way to start the next chapter.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether the Giants make a move for Barmore or another defensive tackle before Tuesday. Number two, whether the Colts lower their price on Taylor. Number three, whether there is another extension coming, maybe for Deonte Banks. Number four, Skattebo and Carter on the practice reports. And number five, how Newsome plays in Philadelphia, now that he has been paid.

And here is a prediction you can hold me to. I think the Giants make a defensive tackle trade before November 10th. I do not know if it will be Barmore. But I think they add a body in the middle. If I am wrong, I will own the receipt.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, first place in the NFC East, paying their own players, and shopping for help up front. That is a triple dose of good news. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
