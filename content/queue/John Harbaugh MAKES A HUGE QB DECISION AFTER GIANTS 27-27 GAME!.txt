John Harbaugh has made his decision. After a 27 to 27 tie in Washington, after sixty minutes and a full overtime period that ended with nobody winning, the Giants head coach walked to the podium and settled the biggest question in this building. Jameis Winston is still the starting quarterback for Sunday’s home game against the New Orleans Saints. But that is only half of it, folks. Because for the first time this season, J.J. McCarthy is getting a real role. Not a package that sits in the drawer. Not a backup who never leaves the sideline. A planned set of snaps, built for him, designed to put the former top-ten pick on the field in a Giants uniform at MetLife Stadium. Two quarterbacks, one game plan, and a head coach who just told the entire league exactly how he plans to win this division. Today I am going to break down why Harbaugh made this call, why Winston keeps the job, why McCarthy’s legs could be the key to fixing a stalled run game, the risks that come with it, and why Jameis Winston of all people might be the perfect quarterback to make a two-quarterback plan work. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the situation Harbaugh was facing, because the context matters. The Giants are 3 and 1 and 1 after that tie in Washington. They are still alone in first place in the NFC East. But a tie leaves a strange taste in your mouth. The Giants scored 27 points on the road, against a division rival with Jayden Daniels back in a brace, and they had a full overtime period to finish the job. They did not. And when a team leaves a win on the table, the questions always find their way to the quarterback. Every Giants fan has been asking the same thing for two weeks now. When does J.J. McCarthy play?

And the signs had been building all along. Remember our three signals checklist on this channel? Signal one, McCarthy named the backup. Check. Harbaugh said it was on the table early last week, and the published depth chart made it official, with Jake Haener moved to the active roster as the third quarterback. Signal two, real progress in practice. Check. Matt Nagy gave McCarthy an A-plus for how fast he is learning the offense, with Brian Callahan, the former Titans head coach, working with him every day as the passing game coordinator and quarterbacks coach. Signal three, a special package of plays. We learned that the Giants had one ready against Arizona, but Harbaugh said they never got close to using it. So the package was built. The question was always when, not if. And now, folks, we have our answer. Signal three is about to go live.

Now let’s be clear about what this decision is and what it is not. It is not a benching. Jameis Winston is still the starter. He runs the offense, he takes the vast majority of the snaps, and he leads this team into a game against the franchise where he spent years of his career. And honestly, I think that is the right call, and here is why. Winston has earned it. The Giants are 2 and 0 and 1 in the games he has started since Jackson Dart went down. Over the last two weeks, the offense has scored 63 points, 36 against Arizona and 27 in Washington, after weeks of living on field goals. The offense finally found the end zone with him at quarterback. His chemistry with Malik Nabers is growing, and the deep ball is back in this offense. And remember what he told a reporter when he was asked about being a backup. I am not the backup here anymore. You do not take the job away from a man who has kept this team in first place.

But here is why the McCarthy role makes sense too, and it comes down to the biggest problem on this offense. The running game. Against Arizona, Cam Skattebo and Najee Harris combined for 88 yards on 27 carries. Harbaugh himself admitted the run game has to get better. In week one against Dallas, this team ran for 165 yards. Against Tennessee in the nor’easter, 139. Against Arizona, the ground game stalled, and that cannot become a trend. Defenses are crashing hard on the running back because they do not have to worry about anything else, and when a quarterback is not a threat to keep the ball, every defender on the field gets a free run at the man with the handoff.

And that is where J.J. McCarthy changes the math. Winston can move a little, but defenses do not respect him as a runner. McCarthy is a different story. He ran for 181 yards and four touchdowns last season in Minnesota. He played in a Michigan offense under Jim Harbaugh, John’s brother, that leaned heavily on the run game and on quarterback movement. When McCarthy is on the field, the defensive end cannot just crash on Skattebo. He has to respect the keeper. Zone read, the end commits to the running back, McCarthy pulls it, and there is open grass on the edge. Do it once, and the next time that defensive end hesitates for half a second. And that half a second is a crease for Skattebo. Folks, that is the whole idea. A handful of McCarthy snaps can make every Winston snap better, because defenses have to prepare for both.

And think about how much harder this makes life for opposing coordinators. Every week, they game plan for Jameis Winston. A pocket passer who loves the deep ball, who will push the ball downfield to Nabers and attack the middle with Isaiah Likely and Theo Johnson. Now they also have to install a plan for a mobile quarterback with a completely different skill set. Different run fits. Different pass rush lanes. Different coverage responsibilities. That is practice time they do not have. That is meeting time they have to split. And that is exactly the kind of advantage Harbaugh loves to create, making the other team prepare for more than they can handle.

Now, I know what some of you are thinking. Two-quarterback systems do not work. You cannot get a quarterback into rhythm if you keep pulling him off the field. And that is a fair concern. The biggest risk here is Winston’s rhythm. He is a quarterback who gets hot. When he is in the flow of a game, the deep shots come, the confidence builds, and the offense rolls. If the coaches pull him for a series at the wrong moment, it could cool him off. So the key will be how the package is used. A few planned snaps. Specific situations. Maybe early-down runs, maybe in the red zone, maybe in short yardage. Not entire drives that break up the flow of the game. Remember, Phil Simms told us a couple of weeks ago that he did not expect McCarthy to get a huge part of any game, maybe a run or something, but not ten plays at a time. I think that is exactly what we will see. A targeted weapon, not a platoon.

And here is the part of this story I absolutely love, folks. If there is one quarterback in the NFL who knows how to handle this kind of setup, it is Jameis Winston. Go back to 2020 in New Orleans. Winston was in the Saints quarterback room with Drew Brees and Taysom Hill. And Sean Payton was famous for using Taysom Hill in special packages, bringing him in for designed runs, trick plays, and short-yardage situations, while Brees ran the offense. Winston watched that up close. He saw how a starter can thrive while a second quarterback with a different skill set gets a handful of snaps. He saw how it stressed defenses. And now, against the Saints of all teams, he gets to be on the other side of that idea, as the starter with a dynamic second quarterback behind him. You cannot make this stuff up. The man knows the system, he knows the locker room dynamics, and he has the personality to embrace it instead of resenting it.

And think about Winston’s whole approach this season. After the Arizona game, he said everything he does functions from gratitude, that every time he steps on the field is a privilege, because there are only 32 starting quarterbacks in the world. That is not a man who is going to sulk because the young guy gets a few snaps. That is a veteran who wants to win, and who understands that whatever helps this team win helps him too. I fully expect Jameis to be the loudest guy on the sideline cheering when McCarthy breaks a run.

Now let’s talk about what this means for McCarthy, because this is a big moment for him. He has been in the building for barely two weeks. He has learned the terminology, the protections, the progressions. He has earned an A-plus in the classroom. But the real test is the field. Learning a play on a whiteboard and running it with a defense flying at you are two different things. A package gives him a controlled way to get live game experience, at home, against a Saints team that has shown real flaws, without throwing him into the fire with the whole offense on his shoulders. That is smart development. And it is exactly what you want for a former top-ten pick who lost his entire rookie season to a knee injury and is still building his game. Every snap he takes now makes him more ready for whatever comes later.

And let’s be honest about what later could mean. Jameis Winston controls his own timetable as the starter, and that has not changed. If he keeps moving the offense, scoring points, and protecting the football, he keeps the job. But if the turnovers pile up, the coaching staff now has a quarterback who has actually taken live snaps in this offense, in a real game, ready to step in. That is insurance. And there is still the January door. Harbaugh ruled Jackson Dart out for the regular season, not for the playoffs. Whatever happens with McCarthy and Winston, the Giants are building a quarterback room that can survive anything.

And folks, think about the message this sends to the entire locker room, because that might be the most important part of the decision. Harbaugh is telling his team two things at once. First, loyalty is earned on the field. Winston kept this team in first place, so Winston keeps the job. That matters to every veteran in that building, because it shows them that performance is rewarded. Second, nobody gets comfortable. The young quarterback is coming, he is learning fast, and he is going to get his chances. That matters to every young player in that building, because it shows them that the coaching staff will give opportunity to the guys who earn it in practice. That is how you build a competitive culture. Not by making dramatic changes after a tie, but by keeping the pressure on and the standard high every single week.

And let’s talk about the tie itself for a second, because I think it played a role in the timing. The Giants had a full overtime period in Washington to win that game, and they could not find the one play that would have done it. Overtime is about short fields, third downs, and possessions where every single snap matters. And when you have a quarterback in your building who can turn a broken play into a first down with his legs, you start thinking about those situations differently. A third and three. A fourth and one. A red zone snap when the defense is selling out against the pass. Those are exactly the moments where a package for a mobile quarterback can steal a game. I would not be surprised at all if the staff looked at that overtime and thought, we have a weapon we are not using. And now they are going to use it.

Now, what about the Saints game itself? Here is what I will be watching. Number one, how many snaps McCarthy actually gets and when. Is it a series in the first half? Is it red zone only? Is it short yardage? The usage will tell us how much trust the staff has in him. Number two, how Winston responds. Does he stay in rhythm? Does he play with the same confidence? Remember our Winston benchmarks. The final box is touchdowns and no turnovers in the same game. If he checks that box against his old team, this quarterback conversation goes quiet for a long time. Number three, the run game. If the McCarthy package is doing its job, we should see more room for Skattebo, Harris, and Tyrone Tracy Jr., even on the snaps when Winston is in the game. That is the real measure of success. Not McCarthy’s stat line, but how much better the whole offense looks.

And let’s not forget the defense, because this decision is happening on a team that wins with defense first. Arvell Reese, who leads the entire NFL in solo tackles through four weeks, is becoming the weapon this defense builds around. Abdul Carter is winning off the edge and needs to start finishing with sacks. Jadeveon Clowney is setting the other side. Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne are collapsing the middle. Jevon Holland, Deonte Banks, Greg Newsome II, and Joey Porter Jr. are patrolling the secondary. And the special teams, with Dominic Zvada still perfect and Jordan Stout flipping the field, remain the best in football. The quarterback decision is about giving that defense more help. More sustained drives, fewer turnovers, more points, and more chances to play with a lead and let the pass rush hunt.

Now, the injuries, because they matter for this game too. Malik Nabers dealt with knee soreness last week, and the Giants have to be careful with a receiver still working back from a torn ACL. Andrew Thomas battled a groin injury. Cam Skattebo has been limited with a shoulder. Isaiah Likely has been managing knee and groin issues. Chauncey Golston’s neck injury could keep him out for a while, Jason Pinnock has been in the concussion protocol, and Braxton Berrios is on injured reserve. That is a lot of bodies to manage. And in a way, that makes the McCarthy package even more valuable. When your skill players are banged up, a quarterback who can create yards with his legs gives you another way to move the ball without leaning too hard on anyone.

And the opponent makes this storyline even better. Winston spent four seasons in New Orleans, from 2020 through 2023, first behind Drew Brees, then as a starter, then as the veteran presence in that quarterback room. He knows that building, he knows that organization, and he knows a lot of the people on that sideline. And it was in that very city, years earlier in his Tampa days, where he gave one of the most famous pregame speeches of his career. Now he gets to face the Saints at MetLife, as the starter of a first-place team, with a two-quarterback plan that looks a lot like the one he watched up close in New Orleans. If you are writing a script for Jameis Winston’s season, folks, this game is a chapter you would circle in red. And for the defense, a home game against a Saints team that has shown real flaws is the perfect chance to get back on the winning track.

So let’s step back and look at the big picture. The Giants are 3 and 1 and 1, alone in first place in the NFC East, coming home to MetLife, where they have not lost all season. Dallas is 2 and 3 and stumbling, Washington is 1 and 3 and 1, and Philadelphia cannot pass the Giants this week no matter what happened in London. Next comes the Saints, then a road trip to Houston, and then the week eight bye. Win the next two, and the Giants go into the break at 5 and 1 and 1, with a first-place cushion, a quarterback room with two real options, and the January door still open for Jackson Dart. That is a dream scenario for a team that everybody wrote off a month ago.

And here is my verdict on Harbaugh’s decision. I love it. It is bold, but it is not reckless. It keeps the starter who has earned the job. It develops the young quarterback the front office traded for. It attacks the biggest weakness on the offense. It gives opposing defenses one more problem to solve. And it fits everything we know about John Harbaugh, a coach who has won a Super Bowl, who has built offenses around mobile quarterbacks before, and who never stops looking for an edge. This is not a coach panicking after a tie. This is a coach getting more aggressive after a tie. And that is exactly the mindset of a team that plans on playing in January.

I have said it for weeks on this channel, and I will keep saying it. This team can go all the way this season. The defense is real. The culture is real. And now the offense has a new wrinkle that could take it to another level. If this two-quarterback plan works, the rest of the NFC is going to have a lot more to worry about than just Jameis Winston.
