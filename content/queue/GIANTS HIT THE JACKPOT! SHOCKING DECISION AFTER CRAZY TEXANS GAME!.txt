The Giants just hit the jackpot in Houston. Final score, Giants 27, Texans 21. J.J. McCarthy, the quarterback the Giants got for a fifth-round pick less than a month ago, won his first start as a Giant, on the road, against a pass rush led by the NFL’s sack leader, Will Anderson Jr. The Giants extended their winning streak over Houston to six straight. They head into the bye week at 4 and 2 and 1, alone in first place in the NFC East. And after a win like that, John Harbaugh’s next decision is not complicated anymore. J.J. McCarthy is the Giants’ starting quarterback coming out of the bye. Not a one-week audition. Not a package. The guy. Folks, a month ago, if you had told me the Giants would be sitting in first place with McCarthy as the starter, I would have laughed. Today, I am going to break down what this win means, what it means for the Texans, why the quarterback decision makes sense, and the one thing that still worries me heading into the second half of the season. Let’s get into it.

First, receipts, because that is how this channel works, good or bad. My prediction all week was Giants 24, Texans 21. The final was Giants 27, Texans 21. I had the winner right, and I had the Texans’ number right on the nose. I was off by a field goal on the Giants. I will take that one. And a week ago, I also owned a mistake, because I predicted Jameis Winston would start this game, and Harbaugh went with McCarthy instead. Harbaugh’s call was the right one, and the scoreboard just proved it.

Now, let’s talk about why this win matters so much, starting with the history. Coming into Sunday, the Giants led the all-time regular-season series with Houston 5 to 1, with five straight wins going back to 2006. Now it is six straight. And that is the Giants’ longest winning streak against a team outside the division since they beat the Rams seven straight times from 2002 to 2016. That is a streak most people did not even know existed before this week. Now it is a real piece of history.

Second, the standings. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Coming into the weekend, the Eagles and the Cowboys were both 2 and 4, and Washington was 1 and 4 and 1. Nobody in this division has been able to separate, and the Giants are the team sitting on top. They go into the bye with a cushion, a new quarterback, and real momentum. That is the jackpot.

And third, the quarterback. Let me be careful here, because I do not want to throw numbers at you before I have gone through the full box score. But here is what we know for sure. McCarthy started on the road, in a loud building, against a defensive head coach in DeMeco Ryans, with Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter coming off the edges, and the Giants put up 27 points and won the game. That is what you ask for from a quarterback in his first start. Win the game. I will go through the film and the individual performances in detail during the bye week, because a lot of players deserve credit for this one, on both sides of the ball. But the headline is simple. The quarterback Harbaugh bet on delivered a road win.

And if that sounds familiar, it should. Back in 2018 in Baltimore, Harbaugh handed the ball to a young, mobile quarterback in Lamar Jackson in the middle of the season. Jackson won his first start, and the Ravens went 6 and 1 with him as the starter the rest of the way, winning the division. I am not saying McCarthy is Lamar Jackson. Nobody is. But the pattern is the same. Harbaugh trusted his eyes, made the change before the outside world thought he should, and the young quarterback won right away.

Now, a lot of you asked me to talk about the Texans, so let’s do it properly, because I think people are underrating what the Giants just beat.

Houston came into this game hot. After starting 0 and 4, the Texans had won two straight, including a 34 to 31 thriller over Jacksonville in London on a walk-off field goal by Ka’imi Fairbairn. C.J. Stroud was second in the NFL in passing yards with 1,714 through six weeks, coming off a 312-yard, three-touchdown game. Will Anderson Jr. led the entire league with 7.5 sacks. Danielle Hunter came in third in the NFL in sacks since 2015. They had just added Marvin Harrison Jr. in a big trade with Arizona, and Tank Dell was returning after not playing since December 2024 because of a serious knee injury. On paper, that is a team that was supposed to be rolling.

And the Giants held them to 21 points. Think about that. The week before, Houston scored 34. This week, against a Giants defense that had just given up 175 rushing yards to the Saints and was missing bodies in the secondary, the Texans managed 21. That is a defense that answered Dennard Wilson’s challenge. All week, Wilson was frustrated about players not running to the ball, and Harbaugh told the media it is nobody’s fault, it is everybody’s responsibility. Well, the response showed up on Sunday.

So, what does this loss mean for Houston? It drops the Texans to 2 and 5, and it snaps their two-game winning streak right when they thought they had turned the corner. For DeMeco Ryans, that is a tough one, because this was a home game against a team starting a quarterback making his first start for his new team. Those are the games a contender is supposed to win. And for the Harrison Jr. trade, it is an early reminder that adding a big name in the middle of the season does not instantly change your results. Remember what I said before the game. Two trades were colliding in Houston. The Texans made the loud one. The Giants made the quiet one, a fifth-round pick for McCarthy. On Sunday, the quiet trade won.

And here is a little bonus for the college football fans. Stroud played at Ohio State. McCarthy played at Michigan. Back in 2022, McCarthy’s Michigan went into Columbus and beat Stroud’s Ohio State 45 to 23. Four years later, McCarthy beat Stroud again, this time in the NFL, in Stroud’s own building. I promise you, Michigan fans in the Giants fanbase are enjoying that one a little extra today.

Now, I want to be fair to the Texans, because they are not a bad team. Stroud is still one of the best young quarterbacks in the league. Anderson and Hunter are still two of the most dangerous pass rushers in football. Nico Collins, Jaylin Noel, Harrison Jr., and a healthy Tank Dell give them real weapons. At 2 and 5, they have dug themselves a hole, but I would not be shocked to see them climb back into the AFC South race. Which makes this win even better for the Giants. They beat a good team on the road, not a broken one.

And for Houston, the questions now get louder. They have to figure out how to turn all that talent into consistent results. A pass rush led by the league’s sack leader only matters if it shows up in the fourth quarter of close games. A quarterback who is second in the league in passing yards only matters if the offense finishes drives. And a midseason trade for a star receiver only pays off if he becomes part of the offense quickly. Those are real problems for DeMeco Ryans to solve. For the Giants, they are somebody else’s problems now.

Now, let’s go back to how we got here, because I think the Saints game looks different after Sunday. A week ago, people were split. Some were ready to crown McCarthy after that fourth quarter. Others were waving it off as garbage time. The fair reading was always somewhere in the middle. Yes, a team trailing by 24 throws on every down, and a team protecting a big lead plays softer. But it is hard to move the ball at all when the opponent knows you have to pass, and it is harder still when you are a quarterback coming off the bench cold. Both things were true. McCarthy showed real arm talent, mobility, and poise, in a situation that flatters quarterbacks.

What Sunday did was answer the biggest question. Can he do it in a normal game, when the defense plays straight and knows he is the starter, with a full week to prepare for him? And the answer, at least for one week, is yes. He won. That does not make him a finished product. But it moves the conversation from, is this real, to, how good can this get?

And there was something about McCarthy’s arrival that I think is underrated. When Jackson Dart went down, the Giants lost more than a quarterback. They lost a piece of their offensive identity. The designed quarterback runs and run-pass option looks that were built for a mobile quarterback largely disappeared. When McCarthy came in against the Saints, those looks started to come back. And with Matt Nagy opening 100 percent of the playbook for him this week, the offense finally had all its tools again. A defense that has to account for a quarterback who can run has to play with more caution on every snap. That helps the running backs, and it opens space for the receivers.

Speaking of receivers, Malik Nabers came into this game on an absolute tear. Twelve catches for 165 yards against the Saints, his third straight 100-yard game, and the second-most receptions in NFL history through 25 career games, behind only Odell Beckham Jr. Isaiah Likely added nearly 100 yards and a touchdown against New Orleans, and he had already built real chemistry with McCarthy. Those two are the heart of this passing game, and Houston knew it. The challenge going forward is making sure the offense does not become too dependent on just those two, because the Eagles will try to take them away too.

So now, the decision. And let me explain why this is a shocking one, even if it feels obvious after a win. Think about where this quarterback room was just seven weeks ago. On September 7th, the Giants gave Jameis Winston a two-year extension. Jackson Dart was the future. McCarthy was in Minnesota. Then Dart went down for the rest of the regular season, Winston took over and went unbeaten in his first three starts, and the Giants traded for McCarthy on September 28th as an insurance policy. In less than a month, McCarthy has gone from a fifth-round afterthought to the starting quarterback of a first-place team. That is one of the fastest quarterback rises you will ever see in this league.

So, the decision. After the bye, the Giants go to Philadelphia, then come home to face Washington. Two division games, back to back, with first place on the line. And Harbaugh’s decision, after this win, is that McCarthy is the starter. I think it is the right call, and here is why.

First, Harbaugh has told us all season what drives his decisions. What gives the team the best chance to win this game. He said it himself on Thursday, and he even joked that if Tom Brady came out of retirement, he would not play him in the first week, because it would not be in the best interest of the team. McCarthy just gave the Giants a road win over a team with one of the best pass rushes in football. That is the best evidence any coach could ask for.

Second, the bye week. McCarthy now gets two full weeks of first-team reps before the Eagles game. Remember, Nagy said McCarthy had about half the playbook against Washington, about 90 percent against the Saints, and all of it this week. Imagine what the offense looks like after two more weeks of building it around him. That is a huge advantage heading into the biggest stretch of the season.

Third, the locker room. Jameis Winston has handled this with total class. After he was pulled against the Saints, he praised McCarthy, said he was proud of him, and said the decision belongs to Harbaugh. A veteran who handles a demotion like a professional is still incredibly valuable. Winston went unbeaten in his first three starts this season. He threw three touchdowns in back-to-back games. He led a 14-point fourth-quarter comeback in Washington. If anything happens to McCarthy, the Giants have a backup who has already won games for them. That is a luxury.

But I want to give you the risk too, because there always is one. McCarthy has now started one game for this team. He has a history of injuries, including a knee injury that cost him his entire rookie season in Minnesota. Harbaugh wants a physical team, and a mobile quarterback fits that, but only if the staff protects him. Every extra hit is a chance for another injury, and this team has already lost one quarterback for the year. I expect the staff to design his runs carefully and to coach him to slide or throw the ball away when a play breaks down. The legs are a tool, not a crutch.

Now, the bye week itself, because the timing could not be better. This team has been beaten up. Paulson Adebo just came back from injured reserve. Andrew Thomas has been working through a groin issue. Jermaine Eluemunor has been dealing with a calf. Greg Newsome II, Colton Hood, and Jevon Holland have all been dealing with concussions. Tremaine Edmunds has had a shoulder injury. Brian Burns and Chauncey Golston are on injured reserve. Two weeks off gives a lot of those players a real chance to heal. And it gives the coaching staff a natural moment to evaluate everything, the offense with a full-time McCarthy, the running back rotation between Tyrone Tracy Jr., Cam Skattebo, and Devin Singletary, and a defense that still needs to fix its explosive plays.

And I want to be honest about my own hesitation, because the temptation after a win like this is to believe everything is fixed. It is not. One week ago, this team gave up 34 straight points to the Saints. It turned the ball over four times. It gave up 175 yards on the ground. Harbaugh himself said on Thursday that if this team has been anything, it has been inconsistent. One win in Houston does not erase that. The defense still has to tackle. The line still has to protect. The running game still has to produce. If I were running this team, I would treat Sunday as proof of what this team can be, not proof that the work is done.

But here is what I keep coming back to. This team does not quit. Down 14 in the fourth quarter in Washington, it came back and walked out without a loss. Down 24 against the Saints, it came back to within one throw of tying the game. And on Sunday, in a hostile building, against a team that had won two straight, it finished the job. That is a culture. You cannot fake it, and you cannot buy it in a trade. Teams that play hard for 60 minutes steal games they are not supposed to win, and in a division this tight, that might be the difference.

And think about the road ahead. After the bye, the Giants go to Philadelphia to face an Eagles team that is expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the rest of the season. I hope Hurts makes a full recovery, because his health matters more than any game. But from a football standpoint, that is a huge opportunity. Then the Giants come home to face Washington, the team they tied in that crazy game earlier this season. Win those two, and the Giants could have a real cushion in the NFC East by the middle of November. Lose them, and everything this team built over the first seven weeks could slip away fast. That is how thin the margins are in this division, and that is why the bye week matters so much. Every day of rest, every rep McCarthy gets with Nabers and Likely, and every fix to the run defense counts.

So, here is what I will be watching during the bye. Number one, the injury updates, especially Thomas, Holland, and the cornerbacks. Number two, how the coaches talk about McCarthy, and whether the offense expands even further. Number three, the running back room, and whether one back finally takes the job Harbaugh has been begging someone to take. And number four, the rest of the NFC East, because every Eagles, Cowboys, and Commanders loss makes this Giants lead a little more comfortable.

Joe Schoen traded a fifth-round pick for a quarterback the Vikings did not want. John Harbaugh had the guts to start him in a road game against the league’s sack leader. And J.J. McCarthy won. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, in first place, heading into the bye with all the momentum in the world. I have been saying it since the beginning of this season, through the injuries, the tie, the loss to the Saints, and the quarterback change. And I am saying it louder today. This team can go all the way.
