Joe Schoen has done it again. Just when you thought the most aggressive front office in football was finally going to sit still before Washington, the Giants have signed Jadeveon Clowney. Yes, that Jadeveon Clowney. The former number one overall pick. The veteran edge rusher who had 9.5 sacks for John Harbaugh in Baltimore back in 2023. And he is walking into a Giants locker room that has been desperate for a proven pass rusher ever since Brian Burns went down. Folks, remember last week when the Giants suddenly cleared about eleven and a half million dollars in cap space with that Chauncey Golston restructure, and everybody was asking why? Now we know at least part of the answer. Schoen did not wait for the trade deadline. He did not wait for the bye week. He found the piece this defense was missing and he went and got it, just days before a division road game. Today I am going to break down exactly what Clowney brings, why the Harbaugh connection makes this a perfect fit, how he changes life for Abdul Carter, who just posted the highest pass rush grade in the league without a single sack, and why this signing lands at the exact moment this defense is starting to look like a nightmare for the rest of the NFC. And then we are going to talk about the play that might define this entire season, Deonte Banks going from bust to hero. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the signing, because the timing tells you everything about how this front office thinks. Count the moves with me, folks. Daron Payne before week two. Kirk Cousins, J.J. McCarthy, and Mac Jones in the quarterback frenzy. Joey Porter Jr. for a first-round pick. Jihad Ward off Minnesota’s practice squad. And now Jadeveon Clowney. Every time this team shows a weakness, Joe Schoen answers it within days. And the weakness on this defense has been obvious since the Brian Burns injury. One team sack through three games. Pressure without finishes. Abdul Carter doing everything except getting home. The Giants needed a veteran edge who knows how to turn pressure into production, and they found one with deep roots in this coaching staff.

Because here is what makes this signing so smart. Clowney is not a stranger to John Harbaugh. In 2023, he played for Harbaugh in Baltimore and had one of the best seasons of his career, 9.5 sacks on one of the best defenses in the league. And who else was on that Ravens staff in 2023? Dennard Wilson, the man now running this Giants defense. Folks, that means Clowney does not need to learn a new language. He knows the terminology, he knows the expectations, and he knows exactly what Harbaugh demands. We saw the same thing with Patrick Ricard and with Jihad Ward. When Harbaugh brings in someone he trusts, that player plugs in immediately. This is not a project. This is a plug-and-play veteran.

And now think about what Clowney does for this rotation, because this is where it gets exciting. Clowney has always been one of the best run-setting edge defenders in football. He is strong at the point of attack, he sets the edge, he forces runs back inside where Dexter Lawrence and Daron Payne are waiting. That means on early downs, the Giants can play him on the edge and keep the run defense stout. And on passing downs, he brings veteran savvy, power, and a motor that does not quit. Put him on one side and Abdul Carter on the other, and suddenly offenses cannot slide their protection toward Carter anymore. Carter, the man with the 90.1 pass rush grade, gets more one-on-one matchups. Kayvon Thibodeaux gets fresher legs in a rotation. Jihad Ward can slide inside on obvious passing downs. Folks, one signing just made every edge rusher on this roster better.

And the timing could not be better, because Jayden Daniels is expected back for Washington on Sunday, wearing a brace on his left elbow. A quarterback coming off an elbow injury, protecting himself, facing a defensive front with Lawrence, Payne, Carter, and now Clowney? That is not a comfortable Sunday. And Clowney has faced mobile quarterbacks his whole career. He knows how to rush with discipline, keep contain, and not let the quarterback escape outside. That is exactly the skill set you need against a player like Daniels.

Now, let me be honest, because this channel always is. Clowney is a veteran, and he is not the explosive athlete he was when he came out of South Carolina. Nobody should expect him to put up Brian Burns numbers. Whether he is active on Sunday or eases in over the next week or two is up to the coaching staff, and we will find out more as the week goes on. But that is not why you sign him. You sign him for the edge setting, the veteran presence, the timely pressures, and the experience of a player who has been in the biggest games this league has to offer. On a young defense full of rising players like Carter, Reese, and Banks, a veteran voice who has done it under this exact coaching staff is worth more than the stat sheet. Joe Schoen keeps surprising people, and he keeps being right.

And here is what makes this signing land even harder. It comes at a moment when this defense is already starting to take off. So let’s talk about where it all turned. Let’s start with Deonte Banks, because his story is the heart of this whole thing. You have to remember where he was. Banks was the Giants’ first-round pick in 2023, a long, physical corner with all the tools, and for a while, it just did not happen for him. The penalties. The coverage busts. The confidence going in the wrong direction. By this past offseason, the word around the league was that the organization was seriously considering whether he should even be on this football team. Folks, that is where he was. A former first-round pick fighting for his roster spot, carrying the bust label everywhere he went.

And then the new staff arrived. John Harbaugh. Dennard Wilson. A new voice, a new scheme, a new standard. And slowly, Banks started to rebuild. Then came the calf injury that cost him two games. And then came Arizona. He comes back, he gets flagged for pass interference in the final minute, giving the Cardinals first and goal from the four with 22 seconds left. Most young players spiral in that moment. Banks did not. On the very same drive, he broke up a pass. And then on fourth and goal, with everything on the line, he read Jacoby Brissett, jumped the throw, and took it 98 yards to the house. Four passes defensed in that game, the most of any player in the league that week. The second-longest touchdown with no time left on the clock since the 1970 merger. NFC Defensive Player of the Week, his first weekly award ever. Folks, that is not just redemption. That is a career being reborn on national highlight reels.

And here is what I love most. One analyst made the point that if the Giants lose that game, the whole conversation is different. They are 2 and 2. People are talking about Jameis Winston’s interceptions. People are talking about whether the McCarthy era should start now. The season feels fragile. Instead, one play from a player people had written off flipped the entire narrative. That is what a turning point looks like. And that is why I believe we will look back on that pick six as the moment the 2026 Giants became real.

Now let’s zoom out, because the play was not an accident. It came out of an identity this team has built from the ground up, and one analyst described it perfectly. He compared it to the old days, when Bill Parcells was running this franchise. A tough defense, a power running game, a coach who demands everything from you, and all of a sudden, the thing starts to snowball. And folks, think about the roster decisions that built this identity, the decisions people laughed at. Let’s get a 300-pound fullback in here. That is Patrick Ricard, Pancake Pat, leading the way for the power run game. Let’s invest in the offensive line when everybody else is chasing flashy skill players. Let’s go get a tight end weapon like Isaiah Likely. Let’s lean on Malik Nabers on the outside. And let’s play a little power football with speed on defense. Nobody in the modern NFL builds like that anymore. Everybody is chasing spread offenses and track meets. The Giants went the other way, and now, even with their starting quarterback hurt, they are in first place in the division. That is pretty cool, folks.

And folks, the Parcells comparison runs even deeper than the analysts mentioned, because there is one Parcells season that should give every Giants fan chills right now. Go back to 1990. The Giants were rolling behind a punishing defense and a ball-control offense. Then in December, Phil Simms, the starting quarterback, broke his foot. The season was supposed to be over. Instead, backup Jeff Hostetler stepped in, the defense carried the load, the running game ground out the clock, and the Giants went all the way, beating Buffalo in Super Bowl XXV. A backup quarterback, a dominant defense, a tough running game, and a head coach who demanded everything. Does that formula sound familiar, folks? Because it is almost exactly the blueprint John Harbaugh is running right now. And on the very same afternoon Banks made his play, the franchise honored the 40th anniversary of the 1986 championship team, another Parcells team built on defense and toughness. History has a funny way of rhyming in this building.

And think about what Banks’ turnaround means going forward, because the timing is perfect. The Giants just traded a first-round pick for Joey Porter Jr., a six-foot-three shutdown corner who allowed a 73.6 passer rating last season. Greg Newsome II just got his first interception as a Giant. Jevon Holland is the reigning NFC Defensive Player of the Month. And now Banks, the guy who looked like the weak link, is the reigning NFC Defensive Player of the Week. Folks, a month ago people were calling this secondary the weakness of the team. Now it might be the deepest unit on the roster. When the player you were worried about becomes a playmaker, the whole room gets stronger.

And the foundation of all of it was the offseason. One analyst said that when you talk to anybody who was there, coaches, front office people, players, they all say the same thing. We were working out there, it was smoking hot, and we got no breaks. And the coach did not feel sorry for us at all. Folks, think about how that sounds in October. In July, it sounds like a complaint. In October, at 3 and 1, it sounds like the reason. That is the Harbaugh way. Old-school football. Hard work. Intensity. Do your job. And the players are not complaining about it now, because they can see the results on the scoreboard. When the hard work turns into wins, the hard work becomes the culture.

And that culture created something you cannot coach directly, a belief. The analysts talked about it this week, the why not us mentality spreading around the league. The Raiders nearly beat Kansas City and are asking, why not us. The Falcons looked like the worst team in football in the first two weeks, and now they are rolling and asking, why not us. In Carolina, they are chanting MVP for Bryce Young. Every surprise team in this league is feeding on that belief. But here is the difference with the Giants, folks. The Giants are not just believing. They are proving it in the hardest possible circumstances. Losing your franchise quarterback, losing your best pass rusher, and still sitting alone in first place. Why not us? For the Giants, that is not a question anymore. It is a statement.

Now let’s talk about one of the most exciting developments on this defense, because I think Abdul Carter is about to take off. Against Arizona, Carter posted a 90.1 pass rush grade from PFF, the highest pass rush grade of any player in the league on Sunday. And here is the wild part, he did it without a single sack. How? Six quarterback pressures. Three quarterback hits. A pass rush win rate of 32.4 percent, one of the best marks in the entire league. And multiple pressures that came in under two and a half seconds. Folks, those are fast pressures. That means he is winning off the snap, beating tackles before the quarterback can even finish his drop. That is what elite pass rushers do.

And here is the pattern I want you to notice. Carter’s two best games this season came against Dallas and against Arizona, the two games where the Giants played with a lead. And that is not a coincidence. One analyst said it perfectly. He always thought that if the Giants could play with a lead, if they could turn a game into a shootout and unleash their pass rushers on a team trying to catch up, this group could develop into a frightening defense to play. That is exactly what we saw. When the opponent has to throw, Carter gets to pin his ears back and go. When the offense scores, the pass rush gets to attack. And now that the offense has found the end zone again, the Giants are starting to find that formula. Get a lead, unleash the rush, and let the secondary make plays on the back end. That is how you win the NFC East.

And the sacks are coming, folks, especially now that Clowney is here to draw some attention away from him. We said it before. Pressures come first, sacks follow. A player who is winning at a 32 percent rate and getting to the quarterback in under two and a half seconds is going to start finishing those plays. And when that happens, Abdul Carter becomes a household name. Lightning quick, the analysts called him. The best of the remaining pass rushers with Brian Burns out. And the Giants have built a smart rotation around him. When teams try to run at Carter, the Giants can bring in Kayvon Thibodeaux, who is a little better at stuffing the run. That kind of flexibility lets each player do what he does best.

And that brings me to the run defense, because this might be the most underrated improvement on the entire team. Last year, folks, the Giants were embarrassing against the run. Teams just lined up and ran right through them. This year, it is a different story. One analyst said he does not know exactly why, but they are able to play the run better, and he credited Dennard Wilson, John Harbaugh, and the hard work of the offseason. I will add a few more names. Chauncey Golston, who the analysts called a big stud in the middle. Daron Payne, who arrived before week two and changed the interior completely. Arvell Reese flying sideline to sideline, leading the entire NFL in solo tackles. When you can stop the run, you force teams to throw. When teams throw, Carter and the pass rush get to eat. When the pass rush eats, the secondary gets turnovers. It all connects, folks. It is a chain reaction, and it starts with not being embarrassed on first down anymore.

Now let’s talk about the quarterback, because Jameis Winston is the most entertaining story in the NFL right now, for better and for worse. John Harbaugh called the Arizona game a big step forward. For the first time since week one, the Giants got into the end zone with an offensive touchdown, and it was the first time they had done it with Winston as the quarterback. That is huge. And when the players talked after the game, they kept bringing up the same thing, the timing and the chemistry with Winston are developing, now that he is getting all the starter reps in practice. And you could see it on the field. The touchdown passes to Malik Nabers and Theo Johnson were right on the button, perfectly placed and perfectly timed. Nabers said in the locker room that his chemistry and timing with Jameis are coming along really nicely, and he had his biggest game since coming back from the knee injury.

And then there were the big plays, the true Jameis Winston experience. The third touchdown was a scramble drill to rookie Malachi Fields, classic Jameis, extending the play, keeping his eyes downfield, and finding the open man. If you look at the big play chart from that game, it is full of his deep shots. Winston deep left, 40 yards. Winston deep right, 38 yards. That is a quarterback who is not afraid to attack, and that is exactly what this offense needed after weeks of dink and dunk and field goals.

But of course, folks, there is the other side. The oh no plays. Two interceptions, and honestly, they were pretty unsightly. It was feast or famine, a roller coaster ride. One reporter described it perfectly, it is a thrill, but there could be some motion sickness along the way with Winston back there. And that leads to the big question everybody is asking. How long is this tenable? The Giants are scoring points and winning games with Winston, but how long can they live with the interceptions? Can they contain them? Winston says he feels like he is getting better and better, that he is heating up. And I believe him. But the turnovers are the one thing that could blow this Cinderella run apart if they start piling up in the wrong game. That is the explosive problem hanging over all of this.

Folks, remember our three signals checklist for McCarthy? The first was being named the backup. The third was a special package of plays. Well, the package existed. It was ready. Harbaugh just said they never got close to using it. And now Harbaugh has said it is on the table that McCarthy will be the number two quarterback this week in Washington. So what does that tell you? The Giants are accelerating his preparation. They are trying to get him up to speed as fast as they possibly can. They are high on J.J. McCarthy, and we are probably going to see him on the field sooner rather than later.

But here is the key point, and every reporter around this team agrees on it. Jameis Winston is largely in control of that timetable. As long as he is moving the offense, scoring points, and winning games, he holds off McCarthy. It is a week-to-week question. If the interceptions keep coming and the wins stop, the door opens. If Winston keeps producing and the Giants keep winning, McCarthy keeps learning from the sideline. Folks, I actually love this setup. Think about how rare it is for a team to have a gunslinging veteran who is winning and a former top-ten pick with a ready-made package, both in uniform on the same Sunday. It is healthy competition.
