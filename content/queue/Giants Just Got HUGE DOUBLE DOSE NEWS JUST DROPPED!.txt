Two pieces of news just landed that could reshape the entire NFC East, and the Giants are sitting right in the middle of it. First, the Philadelphia Eagles are expected to be without Jalen Hurts for the rest of the season. Hurts is dealing with a frightening medical situation involving blood clots, and while his camp says they expect a full recovery with no long-term effects, the Eagles now have to play the rest of this year without their starting quarterback. Second, the Dallas Cowboys have fallen to 2 and 4, with an offense that keeps stalling and a defense that cannot get off the field. Put those two things together, and the Giants, at 3 and 2 and 1, suddenly have a real opening to take control of this division. But here is the catch. Opportunity does not win games. And after that loss to the Saints, the Giants have their own problems to fix before they can take advantage of anything. So today, I am going to break down both pieces of news, what they mean for the race, and the two situations inside this building that will decide whether the Giants actually cash in. Let me start with Philadelphia, and I want to start the right way. Before we talk about a single thing on the football field, this is about a man’s health. Blood clots are serious, and nobody should celebrate a player dealing with something like this, no matter what jersey he wears. I hope Jalen Hurts recovers fully and gets back on the field, and the most encouraging part of this whole story is that update from his camp. They expect a full recovery, without long-term effects. That is what matters first.

There have also been questions about how this unfolded, including how he was cleared to play in the Eagles’ game in London, and about his hospital visit and everything that followed. I understand why people are asking. But I am not a doctor, and I do not have enough medical information to say anybody made the wrong call. So I am going to stick to what we actually know. Hurts is expected to miss the rest of the season, his camp expects a full recovery, and the Eagles now have to support their quarterback through that recovery while figuring out how to keep their season together.

Now, from a football standpoint, here is what it means. Losing your starting quarterback forces you to rethink everything. What your offense can do, how you call plays, how you win close games. Philadelphia came into this season expecting to contend, and this is much bigger than an ordinary losing streak. Does it put more pressure on Nick Sirianni? Absolutely. Does it mean his job is suddenly in danger, or that the Eagles are about to tear everything down? I would not go that far. But look how quickly their challenge has changed.

And Giants fans, think about the schedule. After Houston, the Giants get their bye week. And right after the bye, it is a massive divisional showdown with Philadelphia. That game, which looked like the toughest test of the first half of this season, now looks very different. The Eagles will still have a talented roster, a strong secondary, and a coaching staff that knows how to win. But they will be playing without the quarterback who has led them for years.

And think about what that means for Dennard Wilson’s defense. All season, this channel has talked about Wilson’s blitz-heavy approach, the pressure packages that let a nickel corner like Andru Phillips record two sacks in Washington. Blitzing a veteran franchise quarterback is risky, because he has seen every look and knows exactly where the hot read is. Blitzing a quarterback who has to step into a new role in the middle of a season is a different story. Every disguise, every delayed blitz from Arvell Reese, every look the backup has not seen on film becomes a chance to speed up his clock and force a mistake. And the bye week gives Wilson two full weeks to build that plan. Now, I want to be clear, that only matters if the Giants fix their own run defense first. After giving up 175 rushing yards to the Saints, no blitz package is going to matter if the Eagles can just hand the ball off and move the chains. But if the Giants can make Philadelphia one-dimensional, that matchup tilts in New York’s favor.

But I want to be careful here, because history gives us a very clear warning. Go back to 2017. The Eagles lost their starting quarterback, Carson Wentz, to a torn ACL in December. Everybody wrote them off. And Nick Foles came off the bench and led that team all the way to a Super Bowl title, beating Tom Brady and the Patriots. Same franchise. Same situation. So no, losing a quarterback does not automatically mean the Eagles collapse. A well-built roster can survive it. That is why I keep saying this creates an opportunity for the Giants, not a guarantee. It does not make New York better than Philadelphia. The Giants still have to prove that on the field.

Now, the second piece of news, and it is the Dallas Cowboys. Dallas is 2 and 4, and the offense has struggled badly, especially against the Buccaneers and the Packers. Those are tough defenses, and they can make life hard on anybody. But Dallas has not found enough answers when things break down. And on the other side, its defense has not done enough to get opposing offenses off the field. When your offense stalls and your defense cannot get stops, that is a bad combination for any team trying to climb the standings.

And then there is Washington, still trying to shake off that wild tie against the Giants and the questions about those final 55 seconds. Put all three rivals together, and you see why this matters. The Giants are 3 and 2 and 1, and they are on top of the NFC East. The Eagles are dealing with the loss of their quarterback. The Cowboys are 2 and 4. Washington has not found its footing since that tie. Folks, the door to this division is wide open.

But I want to stay realistic, because I know this fanbase, and I know some of you are already booking playoff tickets. The Giants cannot sit back and wait for Philadelphia to stumble and Dallas to keep losing. They have to handle their own business. And losing to the Saints at home, after missing earlier chances in that Washington game, is exactly why I am not getting carried away. The division is there to compete for. But New York has to play better football, starting this week in Houston.

And remember, we have seen this kind of division before. Back in 2020, Washington won the NFC East with a 7 and 9 record, because every team in the division was struggling. The Giants finished one game behind them that year. One game. That is how fine the margins are in a weak division. Every single game matters, every tie matters, and every mistake against a team like the Saints can come back to haunt you in December. So when people tell you the Saints loss does not matter because the division is weak, I disagree. In a weak division, it matters more, because every team is going to be fighting over the same handful of wins.

So, what is standing in the Giants’ way? Two things, and the first one is the running game. Harbaugh called it not even close to good enough after the Saints game. The Giants ran 20 times for 84 yards. They dressed four running backs, and six different Giants had a rushing attempt. Cam Skattebo was not one of them.

And the Skattebo situation has become one of the biggest storylines of the week. Harbaugh pushed back hard on the idea that Skattebo had been benched. He pointed to the way the game unfolded, and to the use of different backs in passing situations once the Giants fell behind. But Skattebo himself connected his reduced role to his mistakes the week before, including that overtime fumble in Washington. Folks, that tells me this is about more than counting carries. The coaches need backs they can trust. And earning that trust takes more than running hard.

For Skattebo, ball security has to come first. That is not up for debate after everything Harbaugh has said about fumbles this season. But I am also looking at the other things that never show up on a highlight reel. Does he hit the right lane? Does he make good decisions when the play breaks down? And does he protect the quarterback in pass protection? A back can bring all the energy in the world, and Skattebo brings a lot of it, but if he misses an opening or a blitz pickup, the offense pays for it. I want to see him make the routine plays consistently, so the coaches have fewer reasons to take him off the field. And I like that he owned it. A young player who says, I know why my role changed, and I am going to earn it back, is a player who is going to be fine.

But I do not want to put all of this on the backs. The offensive line has struggled, and injuries at tackle have made a difficult situation worse. Rookie Francis Mauigoa needs to be part of the improvement up front, along with the rest of that group. When the blocking is not there, a running back has very little to work with. But when an opening does appear, somebody has to find it and get through it. The line and the backs have to help each other.

And there was one bright spot on that line against the Saints that I do not want you to miss. Rookie tackle J.C. Davis. When Andrew Thomas came out of the game, Davis stepped in for the final 25 offensive snaps and did not allow a single pressure. For a rookie thrown into that situation, in a comeback where the defense knew the Giants had to throw, that is impressive. Now, I am not ready to hand him a starting job after one appearance. But with Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor both dealing with injuries this season, the Giants need dependable depth at tackle, and Davis just gave them a reason to trust him. And if McCarthy ends up playing more, a tackle who can hold up in pass protection while the quarterback extends plays is worth even more.

Now, there were also some positives in the running back room. The back who scored the Giants’ first touchdown on a 17-yard catch and run also punched in the five-yard touchdown run that started the comeback. He only had six carries for 18 yards, which is not impressive on its own, but two touchdowns are exactly the kind of contribution this offense needs. Six carries do not prove anybody is the answer to everything wrong with the rushing attack. But Harbaugh has made it clear that he wants someone to take control of the job, and nobody has done it consistently yet. Whoever produces in Houston could make that decision a lot easier.

And if nobody steps up, I think the Giants at least have to consider whether help is available elsewhere. Now, I want to be very clear. These are possibilities to think about, not a confirmed shopping list, and I have no indication that the Giants are working on any deal. But if I am looking around the league, names like Tony Pollard, Tyler Allgeier, and Emanuel Wilson would be worth a phone call, depending on health, price, and whether their teams would even talk. And here is a name that will make some of you laugh after Sunday. Alvin Kamara. He just torched the Giants with that weaving 31-yard catch and run to open the second half, and what he offers as both a runner and a receiver would interest me. And the wildest idea some fans have floated is Saquon Barkley. But a deal between division rivals is very hard to picture, and I have zero indication that move is even possible. Let’s keep that one in the dream column.

Now, the second thing standing in the Giants’ way, and it connects directly to the run game. The quarterback position. With Jackson Dart out for the rest of the regular season following his knee injury, John Harbaugh has to decide between Jameis Winston and J.J. McCarthy. And there is new information on McCarthy that I think is important.

What stood out against the Saints was how willing McCarthy was to attack downfield. He was not just taking the easy underneath throws. He was giving his receivers chances farther down the field, and that changes how an offense looks. When a quarterback can threaten those areas, defenders have more ground to cover, and less room to cheat toward the line of scrimmage. And folks, that helps the running game too. A safety who has to respect the deep ball cannot creep into the box to stop the run.

And his arm strength is real. McCarthy reportedly hit 61 miles per hour on a throwing velocity test at the combine, just behind Joe Milton III. That gives us a reason to talk about his arm alongside strong-armed quarterbacks from other draft classes, including names like Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes. Now, I want to be careful. Those measurements did not all come from the exact same test, and it definitely does not make McCarthy as good as those quarterbacks. It just means the arm talent is there. Now he has to turn that talent into consistent quarterback play.

And look at the numbers against the Saints. McCarthy averaged close to 12 yards per pass attempt. Winston averaged just under six. McCarthy was pushing the ball deeper and producing bigger gains. But I have to account for the situation, just like I did in the last video. New Orleans was protecting a big lead, and that changes how a defense plays. One quarter does not tell us everything about a quarterback. I liked what he showed, but I want to see him handle a full game, with the mistakes and adjustments that come with it.

And his legs matter too. A quarterback who can keep the ball and run gives defenders one more decision to make, and that can create space for the running backs, especially when the offense mixes in option plays. Remember, McCarthy scrambled for 28 yards on that final drive against the Saints. But mobility will not fix everything. If the blocking breaks down or a back misses the opening, those problems are still there. McCarthy can give this offense another tool, but the other ten players still have jobs to do.

So, where do I land? McCarthy showed enough against the Saints to deserve a serious look. I am not saying one late rally should decide the position for the rest of the year. I am saying the Giants need to find out whether that arm and that mobility can help them win now. And this division news makes that question even more urgent. If the NFC East is wide open, the Giants cannot afford to spend the next month figuring out their quarterback. They need to know who gives them the best chance to win this division, and they need to know soon.

And here is how all of this ties together. The Eagles losing Hurts and the Cowboys sitting at 2 and 4 open the door. But the Giants’ run game and their quarterback decision will decide whether they walk through it. If the Giants fix the turnovers, find a back who takes the job, and get the quarterback call right, they have everything else they need. A receiver in Malik Nabers who just put together his third straight 100-yard game and is chasing Odell Beckham Jr. in the record book. A tight end in Isaiah Likely who has five touchdowns in six games. A rookie kicker who answered his rough day in Washington with a clutch kick against the Saints. And a head coach who has already shown he is not afraid to make the hard call.

So, here is what I will be watching this week. Number one, the quarterback announcement for Houston. Number two, the running back reps. Does Skattebo get his role back, or does somebody else grab the job? Number three, Andrew Thomas’s status, and whether J.C. Davis is ready if Thomas cannot go. And number four, the rest of the NFC East. How do the Eagles respond in their first game knowing Hurts is out for the year? And can Dallas stop the bleeding?

Here is my bottom line. The double dose of news is real. The Eagles and Cowboys are both in trouble, and the Giants are in first place. But I have watched this franchise long enough to know that opportunity can slip away as fast as it shows up. This is the moment for the Giants to go take it. Win in Houston, go into the bye at 4 and 2 and 1, and come out of it ready to face an Eagles team without its quarterback, with a chance to put real distance between themselves and the rest of the division. I have said it all season, and the news this week only makes me believe it more. This team can go all the way.
