The Giants have a serious quarterback decision to make, and they have less than four days to make it. J.J. McCarthy took a big hit in Philadelphia and came out with a hip injury. He was listed as questionable to return. Jameis Winston came in and threw the only Giants touchdown of the day. And then, on the very next possession, McCarthy came back and finished the game. Now, with Washington coming to MetLife on Thursday night, the Giants have to decide who starts. Do they go back to McCarthy, the quarterback they traded for and who insisted on playing through pain? Or do they go with Winston, the veteran who has started four games this season and just delivered again? This quarterback room has been a revolving door all year. Jaxson Dart is out for the season. Winston started four games. McCarthy has now started two. And on Sunday, both of them played in the same game. Today, I am going to break down exactly what happened, what the numbers say, and who I think should start on Thursday. Let’s get into it.

Let me set the scene, because the win itself deserves a lot of credit. The Giants came out of the bye knowing they had a chance to extend their lead in the NFC East, and they did exactly that with a 19 to 11 win in Philadelphia. The Giants had not won there since 2013. That streak is over. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1 in John Harbaugh’s first season. The Eagles fell to 2 and 7 with their seventh straight loss. A road win like that is exactly when you would expect everything to feel settled. But the quarterback position refuses to let that happen.

Here is how it unfolded. With 12:02 left in the third quarter, McCarthy ran for three yards and took a big hit from Eagles linebacker Zack Baun. It was a heavy collision for both players. Baun actually hurt his back on the play. And McCarthy came out of it with a hip injury, and he was listed as questionable to return.

Before the injury, McCarthy had completed 9 of 13 passes for 101 yards. But he had also been sacked five times. That is a lot of punishment for a quarterback making only his second start for the Giants. And when he went down, a lot of Giants fans had that sinking feeling. We have seen this before. In week two, Jaxson Dart went down on his seventh snap against the Rams, and he has not played since.

But this time, the next man up was no stranger. Jameis Winston came in. And this is not new for him. He started four games earlier this season while Dart was out, and he went 2 and 1 and 1. But coming in cold in the middle of a game is a different challenge. And Winston handled it really well. He completed 6 of 8 passes for 71 yards, and he ran twice for 11 yards. Harbaugh called his deep sideline throw to Malachi Fields the throw of the game. And then Winston threw the Giants’ only touchdown of the afternoon, a five-yard pass to Isaiah Likely, to put the Giants ahead 13 to 8 with 2:25 left in the third quarter.

I think that matters more than people realize. When your starting quarterback goes down in a hostile stadium, the easy thing is for the offense to fall apart. Instead, Winston led the drive that separated the Giants in this game. That tells you how ready he is.

And then came the part that surprised a lot of people. McCarthy came back on the Giants’ very next possession. He returned to the game even after being listed as questionable. And Harbaugh revealed after the game that the coaches talked about leaving Winston in, especially after he had just led the touchdown drive. But the trainers and the doctor said McCarthy was good, and McCarthy insisted on playing. He finished 12 of 19 for 127 yards, with no interceptions. He also ran four times for 22 yards, including a first down on the left sideline where he was clearly limping. Harbaugh said, you’ve got to be kidding me. He was in pain, and he still ran fast for that first down.

Those are not huge numbers. But think about what McCarthy was dealing with. He was hurt. He had been sacked five times. And he went back in and finished the game without a turnover. That tells you a lot about how he sees this job.

But it also raises the real question. Coming back from a hip injury in the moment, with adrenaline flowing, is one thing. Getting ready to play again on a short week, against a division rival, is a completely different story. Harbaugh said it himself after the game. Now McCarthy is going to have to fight to get ready to play on Thursday.

So, let’s talk about the decision. Here is the case for McCarthy. He is the starter. The Giants traded a 2027 fifth-round pick to get him on September 28th, and they believe he is part of their plan. He has not thrown an interception in his two starts as a Giant. Harbaugh said before the Eagles game that McCarthy was pretty much caught up on the playbook, and McCarthy said he understands the intent behind every play now. And the way he fought through the injury earned him a lot of respect in that locker room. One reporter said the players and the coaches love him for gutting it out.

And here is the case for Winston. He is healthy. He knows this offense. He has started four games this year. He just threw the go-ahead touchdown in the biggest game of the season. And on a short week, with only a couple of practices before Thursday, a healthy veteran might be the safer choice than a starter who is managing a hip injury. The Giants have a two-game lead in the standings. They do not need to risk turning a hip injury into a long-term problem.

So here is my take, and this is my opinion, not a report. If McCarthy practices and the medical staff clears him without limitations, he should start. That has been the plan since September, and he has earned it. But if he is limited all week, I would start Winston and not think twice. A healthy Winston on a short week is better than a hobbled McCarthy who cannot move in the pocket. And Washington’s defense just sacked Tanner McKee four times a couple of weeks ago. A quarterback who cannot escape is going to take a lot of hits.

And let me add one more layer to this decision. There is the long-term picture too. Jaxson Dart is coming back next year. The Giants traded for McCarthy to stabilize this season, but they also want to know if he can be part of the future. Every start McCarthy makes is an evaluation. The coaches want to see how he handles a short week, how he handles a defense that has studied his tape, and how he handles pressure in a big game. Those are things they cannot learn if he is on the sideline. So there is a reason to play him if he is healthy, beyond just this one game. But there is also a reason to protect him. If the hip turns into a long-term problem, the Giants lose that evaluation completely. That is the balance Harbaugh and the medical staff have to find this week.

And think about how different this is from the start of the season. In week two, when Dart went down, the Giants had no plan. Winston was thrown in, and the Giants had to scramble. Now, the Giants have two quarterbacks who have both won games this season. Winston has wins over Tennessee and Arizona, a tie with Washington, and now the go-ahead touchdown in Philadelphia. McCarthy has wins over Houston and Philadelphia. That is depth. That is a luxury most teams do not have.

Now, let’s talk about the people around the quarterback, because they matter just as much. Isaiah Likely had a huge day. He caught five passes for 53 yards and the touchdown. And in the process, he broke his personal record for catches in a season, with nine games still to play. His previous career high was 42, back in 2024 with the Ravens. Harbaugh had Likely in Baltimore, and now he is a go-to target in New York.

Malik Nabers had five catches for 43 yards. It was a quiet day for him, and it was frustrating too. He dropped a pass that could have been a big play, and he threw his helmet onto the bench late in the second quarter. But remember, Nabers was just named the NFC Offensive Player of the Month. He is the first Giants wide receiver ever to win that award. One quiet game in a hostile stadium does not change how good he has been.

Darnell Mooney led the team with 54 receiving yards on three catches. Rookie Malachi Fields added three catches for 40 yards, including that big sideline catch from Winston. And Jalin Hyatt was targeted once on a deep ball early.

And here is a receipt for me. Before the game, Harbaugh hinted that roster moves were coming, and he said there was a reason the Giants had opened a roster spot. I guessed it would be a running back, probably Eric Gray, being signed to the active roster. I was wrong. The Giants signed Jalin Hyatt to the active roster, and they elevated Phil Mafah from the practice squad. So I got that one wrong. I will own it.

Now, the running game, because this is the biggest problem the quarterbacks are dealing with. With Cam Skattebo out with his hamstring, Devin Singletary got his first start since week two of last season. And he ran 21 times for 31 yards. That is a tough day at the office. The Giants had just 16 rushing yards on 10 carries in the first half. They finished with 80 yards on 31 attempts. That is 2.6 yards per carry. And when the ground game cannot do its job, everything lands on the quarterback’s shoulders. Third and long, over and over. That is how McCarthy got sacked five times before halftime.

And one more receipt on this. Before the game, I predicted Singletary would have a big day. He did not. That one is on me.

But there was one bright spot in the backfield. Rookie Gregory Desrosiers, the back the Giants signed off the Chargers’ practice squad, had three carries for 20 yards in his NFL debut. That is almost seven yards per carry. It is a small sample, but it is a good sign. If Skattebo cannot go on Thursday, I would like to see Desrosiers get more chances.

And the running back situation could get better soon. Skattebo has been dealing with that hamstring since he felt it grab on his 42-yard run in overtime in Houston. If he can go on Thursday, the whole offense changes. He ran for 124 yards in Houston, and he makes defenses respect the run, which opens up play action for the quarterback. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve and can come back around week 13 against San Francisco. And the Giants have Eric Gray and Phil Mafah for depth. So the backfield is thin right now, but help is on the way. That matters for the quarterback decision too. A healthy running game takes pressure off whoever is under center.

Now, let’s talk about why the quarterbacks were able to survive all of this. The defense. And nobody did more than Arvell Reese. Early in the second quarter, Reese came off the edge untouched, sacked Joe Milton, and forced a fumble. Micah McFadden, who has been his partner at inside linebacker since Tremaine Edmunds went on injured reserve, recovered it. That erased a Charlie Jones muffed punt.

And Reese was not done. The rookie, the fifth overall pick in this year’s draft, finished with 12 tackles, 10 of them solo, plus three sacks and that forced fumble. His nine tackles in the first half were the most by a Giants rookie in a single half since at least 1994. And he is only the second rookie in franchise history with three sacks in a game. The other one was B.J. Hill in 2018.

The whole defense played like that. It forced five three-and-outs, and the Giants came into the game leading the NFL in that category. The Eagles converted only 5 of 18 third downs. And the Giants had a season-high seven sacks, including one from Darius Alexander on the final play to seal it.

The only Eagles touchdown came from Saquon Barkley, a three-yard run late in the first quarter. And then the Giants made a mistake that could have haunted them. They were flagged for too many men on the field on the extra point, and the Eagles went for two from the one-yard line. Joe Milton ran it in, for an 8 to 6 lead. That is the kind of sloppy mistake the Giants cannot keep making.

And it was not the only one. The Giants had seven penalties before halftime. Deonte Banks was flagged for a 30-yard pass interference. Charlie Jones muffed a punt. In a closer game, against a better team, those mistakes can cost you. Harbaugh preached discipline all week before this game, and the Giants still made too many errors in the first half. They cleaned it up after halftime, and that is a good sign. But on a short week against Washington, they need to be clean from the start.

And the defense’s three-and-outs matter for the quarterback decision too. When the defense gets off the field quickly, the offense gets more chances, and the quarterback does not have to force anything. Whether it is McCarthy or Winston on Thursday, if the defense keeps forcing punts like it did in Philadelphia, the quarterback just has to protect the football and let the field position work.

And then there is the kicking game, which quietly saved the day. Dominic Zvada had missed five of his previous nine field goal attempts over three games. On Sunday, he went four for four, including a 53-yarder. He now has six field goals of 50 yards or more this season. And here is a crazy stat. All other rookie kickers in Giants history combined have five. That is a franchise record that keeps growing.

And do not forget the punter. Jordan Stout came into the game averaging a league-best 51.6 yards per punt. He averaged 52.3 on six punts on Sunday, with five of them downed inside the 20. In a game decided by eight points, that field position mattered a lot. When the offense is struggling to move the ball, a punter who keeps pinning the other team deep is like an extra defender. Every drive the Eagles started inside their own 20 was a drive where Joe Milton had to go 80 yards against a defense that was sacking him seven times. That is how you win ugly.

And here is a perspective I think is important. The Eagles have their own quarterback mess. They have used three different starters this season. Jalen Hurts, then Tanner McKee, then Joe Milton. Milton threw for 169 yards on 18 of 34. He actually led the Eagles in rushing with 64 yards. So the Giants are not the only team dealing with chaos at quarterback. But they are the ones who walked out with the win. That tells you how important it is to have a real answer at quarterback, even when the situation is messy. And the Giants have two answers.

Now, let’s look ahead to Thursday. Washington comes to MetLife for the second division game in five days. The first meeting ended in a 27 to 27 tie in week five. Washington is 2 and 5 and 1 after a 20 to 13 loss to the Rams. The Commanders will be desperate. And there is no time to catch your breath on a short week.

And that is why McCarthy’s hip matters so much. The Giants need a quarterback who can stand in the pocket and make throws, because the running game has not been there without Skattebo. If Skattebo comes back, that changes everything. If he does not, the quarterback has to carry more of the load. And a quarterback with a sore hip, behind a line that allowed five sacks in a half, is a real concern.

Here is what I will be watching this week. Number one, whether McCarthy practices on Monday and Tuesday, and at what level. Number two, whether Skattebo is back for Thursday. Number three, whether the Giants’ offensive line can protect better than it did in Philadelphia. Number four, whether Desrosiers gets more carries. And number five, whether the Giants clean up the penalties, especially on special teams.

And here is my prediction for Thursday’s starter. I think McCarthy starts. He insisted on going back in on Sunday. He wants this job. And I think the medical staff will clear him. But I also think Winston will be ready, and if McCarthy gets hurt again, the Giants will not miss a beat. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1, first in the NFC East, with a quarterback room that keeps getting tested and keeps finding a way. A tough starter, a reliable backup, a rookie linebacker playing like a superstar, and a kicker who just went four for four. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
