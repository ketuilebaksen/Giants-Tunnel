John Harbaugh just stepped to the podium before the Jacksonville game and laid out exactly what he wants from J.J. McCarthy in his fourth start. Confident. Aggressive. Smart. Complete passes. Handle all the blitz checks. And then he said the line that matters most: he has been playing winning football, and I am really happy with the way he is playing. That is the McCarthy announcement. But that was not all. Harbaugh also revealed that Dominic Zvada was dead on in practice from what he called the “amazing, scary right hash.” He explained what Xavier Gipson is really here to do. He went to bat for Abdul Carter and called him a game wrecker. And he promised an aggressive game plan against a 7-2 Jaguars team. Let’s go through all of it.

If you are new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on the notifications, because it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now: what is the one thing McCarthy has to do better against Jacksonville? Handle the blitz, take more shots, or protect the football? Tell me in the comments.

Let’s start with the quarterback, because that is the headline.

A reporter asked Harbaugh what the next step is in McCarthy’s progression, heading into his fourth start with the team. And Harbaugh paused, kind of sighed, and then gave the list. He said, I want him to play as good as he can possibly play. I want him to be confident. I want him to be aggressive. I want him to be smart. I want him to complete passes. I want him to handle all the blitz checks. I want him to play the best football he can play.

And then he said something I really like. He said, I’m not sitting there like a scout saying, oh, he can work on his plant foot or his follow-through. The coaches will look at that stuff. But all you really focus on is, let’s go play the best game we can possibly play. Let’s play a winning football game. And then: he’s been playing winning football. I’m really happy with the way he’s playing.

He finished with this. There are going to be some plays that he will have a chance to make, and he will make most of them. He won’t make them all. But I hope he makes the ones that make a difference in the game. And he said that is probably the biggest thing for any quarterback.

Let me tell you why I think this answer is so important.

First, notice the word “aggressive” right next to the word “smart.” That is the balance we have been talking about on this channel all week. Gunslinger and game manager, at the right times. Harbaugh wants both. He wants McCarthy to take his shots, but take the right ones.

Second, and this is the big one, look at the phrase “handle all the blitz checks.” That is not a random thing to say. That is the exact weakness that showed up against Washington. Against six or seven rushers, McCarthy was two for six for 11 yards and an interception. Washington’s defensive staff comes from the Brian Flores tree, and they sent everything at him in the final minutes. Harbaugh did not name the problem directly, but he did not have to. Handling the blitz checks means getting the protection right before the snap, knowing where the hot read is, and getting the ball out before the pressure gets home. That is the homework for this week, and the head coach just told you it is at the top of the list.

And third, the line about making the plays that make a difference. That is the closer conversation. McCarthy made a huge play against Washington, the 66-yard touchdown to Isaiah Likely to take a 21-17 lead. He threw for a career-high 293 yards. But the final drive ended with an interception. Harbaugh is saying, you are not going to be perfect, but in the moments that decide the game, I need you to be the guy. That is the next step from good young quarterback to winning quarterback.

Then there was the aggressive question. A reporter brought up Harbaugh saying he still trusts McCarthy, and the idea of letting it loose in big situations. And he asked, against an offense like Jacksonville’s that can put up points, do the Giants have to be more aggressive this week?

Harbaugh said, we’ll figure that out. Whatever we’ve got to do to win the game, we’ll be doing it. And then he went on one of those classic Harbaugh riffs that I love. He said, watch every game around the league. You see a play and you think, what are they doing? I promise you, they are doing everything they possibly can to win the game. Nobody calls a play thinking, this play is kind of stupid, but let’s call it anyway. That’s all the hours of work that go into it. And then he said, the other team is doing everything they can to make it look like a stupid play. That’s the beauty of the sport. It’s chaos out there.

And then he finished with this. We just have to find a way to put a great game plan together, an aggressive game plan. We believe it’s always aggressive. I hope it looks aggressive. I hope you’re singing the praises about how this aggressive game plan won the game for the Giants.

My take: that is a coach who heard the criticism about playing scared against Washington, and is answering it without being defensive. He is basically saying, we are going to be aggressive, and I hope you are praising us for it on Monday. Combine that with last week, when he said he was kicking himself for not going for it on that fourth-and-one deep in his own end, and I think we are going to see a more aggressive Giants team on Sunday. My opinion, not a report.

Now, Zvada. This is the best news of the week.

A reporter asked how much a kicker can really work on a plant-foot technique issue week to week, compared to the offseason. And Harbaugh said, that’s probably the thing you can work on the most for that position, probably more than any other position. Then he said, he had a great day today. He was dead on. He hit the middle pole, the camera pole, numerous times, punting or kicking field goals. And then the line that made everybody laugh: from the right hash, you know, from that amazing, scary right hash, he just nailed it.

He also said the kickers were in the building on Monday working on it on their own, on a day when the team did not even have anything scheduled. The team came back Tuesday, but the kickers were already in there Monday.

I love this for a few reasons. One, Harbaugh is a special teams coach at heart, and he is not just defending Zvada with words anymore. He is telling you what he is seeing on the practice field. Two, the fact that the kickers came in on their own on an off day tells you Zvada is taking this seriously. He knows all six of his misses this season have come from the right hash, and he is attacking the problem. And three, Harbaugh is right about kicking. Of all the positions on the field, technique for a kicker is the most repeatable thing to work on. You can kick a hundred balls from the right hash in a week. You cannot do that with a quarterback reading a defense.

Now, practice is practice. Games are games. Zvada is 17 for 23 on field goals, with six makes from 50-plus, and all 16 extra points. But the miss from 33 against Washington is what everybody remembers. He has to carry the practice work into the game. But if you are looking for a sign that the fix is working, the head coach just gave it to you.

Next, Xavier Gipson.

A reporter asked why the Giants brought him in. And Harbaugh said, we saw him in training camp and in the offseason. Liked him. Thought he did really well. The return element is obviously a big part of it. Maybe we can spark our return game a little bit. That’s kind of the main thought. And then he added something new. He said Gipson will do some things on offense too, most likely. And then: it’s up to him to come in here and make us right. I’m excited to see him out there, and I know he’s excited to get his shot.

So there it is. The main job is the return game, after Charlie Jones lost two fumbles on returns this season, one against New Orleans and one in Philadelphia. But Gipson might also get a few offensive snaps, maybe on gadget plays or jet motion, things that use his speed. And I love the phrase “make us right.” That is Harbaugh saying, we made a decision to bring you in, now prove that decision was correct.

Now, the Abdul Carter conversation, because this was the longest answer of the press conference and I think it was the most passionate.

A reporter brought up the debate around Carter. The raw sack numbers are not great, but the deeper metrics, pressures and quick pressures, are good. So does Harbaugh need more from him?

And Harbaugh pushed back hard. He said, no. I believe the sacks will come. We want sacks, sacks are a big deal, but so is everything else. And then he explained how he evaluates players. He said it starts with the tape. He does not personally go through every metric every week. He looks at team stats, but he is grading himself, he is grading every single player, and every coach is grading his position all the time. And then he said, he’s grading out really well on my sheet, I can tell you that, and on the coach’s sheet.

Then he said, he’s a disruptive guy. I promise you they’re accounting for him. The sacks are just going to come. And then this: you can have a bunch of sacks and really not be playing that good, too. We’ve seen that before. He called Carter a disruptive player, a game changer, a game wrecker. And he said, I think there’s going to be a lot of game wrecks in his future.

A reporter followed up and asked if Harbaugh worries that a pass rusher without sacks might start selling out to chase them. And Harbaugh said, if I thought that, I would talk to him about it, but I don’t, because I know where he’s at. He said they talk all the time, at practice, every time they see each other, and Carter is not even in that place. He doesn’t care. He just wants to go play the best football he possibly can. Harbaugh said Carter is not going to get dragged into that temptation. He’s going to play winning football, and he knows the sacks are coming. And when they come, everybody will be happy. Maybe he’ll knock the ball out, pick it up and run it in for a touchdown. That could happen.

And when someone asked why the pressures have not turned into more sacks, Harbaugh said there is no one answer. He said if you get bogged down in that question, you are not doing your job coaching the team or playing the next play. He compared it to a path into the jungle. Where is it going to lead you? What are you even trying to accomplish by taking yourself there? He said every pass rush is broken down to a tee in the film room, they study all of it, and then you leave it there and go play the next play.

My take on this. I think Harbaugh is right, and here is why it matters for Sunday. Against Washington, the Giants only got two quarterback hits on Jayden Daniels. The pass rush was the missing piece. Some of that was Daniels getting the ball out fast and Washington living in third-and-short. But a pass rusher who is disrupting plays, even without sacks, is changing the game. When offenses slide protection to Carter, someone else like Arvell Reese gets a one-on-one. That is how Reese got three sacks in Philadelphia. Carter does not need to lead the team in sacks for this pass rush to work. But I do think a big sack game is coming for him. My opinion, not a report: Carter gets home at least once against Jacksonville.

Now, Jacksonville. Harbaugh was asked what has helped the Jaguars get to the next level. And his answer was pure Harbaugh. He said, I could care less about that analysis of why they are where they are. All you do is look at the team they are right now. They’re good. What makes them good? They play good defense, offense and special teams. They’ve got a really good quarterback. They’ve got talented players on offense. Their offensive line is big and strong. We could go on for a while. And then he asked, what’s their record? And he quoted Bill Parcells. You are what your record says you are. The Jaguars are really good. The record says so.

That is respect. And it is also a reminder of what this game is. Jacksonville is 7-2, coming off a 40-23 win over Tennessee, with a plus-125 point differential. The Giants have not beaten a team with a winning record yet this season. This is the test.

He also talked about the players Jacksonville added on defense through trades. He said it does not change much structurally about how they run their defense. The good thing, he said, is that the Giants have already played against both of those players this year, so the offensive line has a sense of them. They are good players. They help Jacksonville’s defense. But he said, it doesn’t matter, we’re going to play the guys in the Jaguars uniforms.

And a couple of other notes from the presser that I found interesting.

Harbaugh had a rare Sunday off with the extended break, and he said he watched the Jaguars game while doing his Jaguars work, and even saw a couple of things on TV that he put in his report. Then he sat on the couch and watched the entire Dallas and San Francisco game. He called it a wild game, a great game, and said both teams are offensive juggernauts. And of course, the result was great for the Giants. Dallas lost and fell to 4-6, two games behind the Giants.

He also cleared up a leftover question from Thursday. The Giants have been a defer team all season, but against Washington they chose to receive the opening kickoff. Why? Harbaugh said one of their defensive players got dinged on his shoulder in warmups and was being looked at, and they were not going to have him for the first series. So they took the ball first to buy some time, the offense put some plays together, and the player was able to get back out there. That is a smart, small decision that shows how much goes into game day that we never see.

And he talked about roster decisions too. When a reporter asked about a veteran who was a healthy scratch last game, Harbaugh said the player had a really good game against Houston, but with everyone healthy again, he moved into more of a backup and special teams role. And Harbaugh said he told him directly, in my mind, you’re a starter in this league, and you’re going to have to start again. When asked about rotating the swing tackle the last couple of weeks, Harbaugh said it was partly health and partly that both players are doing well, and that it is almost a weekly conversation about which one to go with. That tells you this roster has real depth now, which you need in December.

So let me sum up what we learned.

One, Harbaugh wants McCarthy to be confident, aggressive and smart, to complete passes, and to handle all the blitz checks. And he is really happy with how McCarthy is playing.

Two, Zvada was dead on in practice, including from the right hash, and the kickers were in the building on their own on an off day.

Three, Gipson is here to spark the return game and might get some offensive snaps too.

Four, Harbaugh believes Abdul Carter is a game wrecker and that the sacks are coming.

Five, the Giants plan to bring an aggressive game plan against a Jacksonville team Harbaugh respects a lot.

The standings are still on the Giants’ side. They are 5-3-1 and alone in first in the NFC East. Dallas is 4-6. Washington is 3-5-1, with the head-to-head edge. Philadelphia is 2-7. And the Giants have had a long break to get healthy and fix things.

Here is my prediction for Sunday, and you can hold me to it. McCarthy handles the blitz checks, gets the ball out fast, and throws for two touchdowns. Zvada makes a kick from the right hash that matters. Gipson gives the Giants a spark on a return. Carter gets his sack. And the Giants beat Jacksonville to move to 6-3-1, their first win of the season over a team with a winning record. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

A young quarterback who is playing winning football. A kicker who is fixing his technique. A pass rusher his coach calls a game wrecker. And an aggressive game plan for a statement game. You are what your record says you are. Right now, the Giants are a first-place team. On Sunday, they get to prove it against a heavyweight.
