The Giants just got three big updates on the eve of Philadelphia, and one of them changes the entire game plan. Update number one, Cam Skattebo is officially doubtful for Sunday after not practicing fully all week with his hamstring. That means Devin Singletary is about to carry this offense in the biggest game of the season. Update number two, Malik Nabers is a full go. He practiced fully on Friday with no injury designation after that scary knee scare on Thursday. And update number three is on the other sideline. The Eagles ruled out DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, and two defensive backs. So the Giants walk into Philadelphia without their hottest running back, but with their best receiver, against a team starting a new quarterback with its top receiver sitting out. And on the trade front, Albert Breer just called the Giants a serious suitor for Jonathan Taylor. Let’s get into it.

If you are new here, hit that subscribe button, it costs you nothing and it helps this channel more than you know. And drop a comment right now. With Skattebo doubtful, how many yards does Devin Singletary run for on Sunday?

Let me start with Skattebo, because I owe you a receipt. A couple of videos ago, I predicted Skattebo would play on Sunday with a managed workload. He was limited on Friday, after not practicing on Wednesday or Thursday, and the Giants listed him as doubtful. Doubtful usually means a player is not going to play. So I was probably wrong. And I am not upset about being wrong on this one. Two division games in five days, with a hamstring that grabbed on him in overtime in Houston. The smart move is to give it time.

Think about it from the Giants’ point of view. Skattebo is the emotional engine of this offense. He ran for 124 yards in Houston, his first 100-yard game, with a touchdown and that 42-yard run in overtime that set up the win. But hamstrings are tricky. If you push a running back too soon, a small strain can turn into a big one, and suddenly you lose him for a month instead of a week. With Washington coming on Thursday night, sitting him on Sunday could be the best way to have him back sooner. That is my read, not a report.

So, what does the backfield look like without him? It starts with Devin Singletary. Harbaugh calls him Motor, and he is the veteran of this group. He scored the 30-yard walk-off touchdown in Houston. He knows how to pass protect, he knows the zone running game, and he protects the football. Behind him is Gregory Desrosiers, the undrafted rookie the Giants took off the Chargers’ practice squad. He is on the active roster and can dress. And then there are Eric Gray and Phil Mafah on the practice squad. If Skattebo is out, I would expect the Giants to elevate one of them for Sunday, probably Gray, since he has played 34 games for this franchise and knows the building.

And let me give Singletary his due, because a lot of fans forget what he has done in this league. He was a third-round pick by Buffalo in 2019, and he was a lead back for the Bills before he went to Houston and then came to New York. He is not a flashy runner. He does not have Skattebo’s violence or Tracy’s burst. But he is patient, he finds the cutback lane, and he rarely makes the big mistake. In a hostile stadium, against a desperate team, that kind of steady running is exactly what a young quarterback needs. The Giants do not need Singletary to be a hero. They need him to keep the offense on schedule. Second and five instead of second and ten. That is how you win on the road.

And if Desrosiers gets his first NFL carries, this could be a fun moment to watch. At Memphis, he averaged about 6.1 yards per carry. Harbaugh called him a very talented young guy this week. If the Giants get a lead in the second half, I would not be surprised to see him get a few series to give Singletary a breather, especially with another game on Thursday night.

And do not forget Patrick Ricard at fullback. When the backfield is thin, a fullback who can lead block on short yardage and goal line plays matters a lot. Harbaugh had Ricard for years in Baltimore. He knows how to use him.

Now, update number two, and this one is a huge relief. Malik Nabers was a full participant on Friday and has no injury designation. On Thursday, he did not practice because of a knee issue, and a lot of us got nervous, me included, because of the torn ACL he suffered last season. But Nabers said on Thursday that he was good for Sunday, and Friday backed that up.

And here is another receipt. In my last video, I said I would not be shocked if the Giants limited Nabers in Philadelphia, or even held him out, to protect him for the long run. That does not look like it is happening. He is a full go. I am happy to be wrong about that one too.

And Nabers matters even more now that Skattebo is likely out. Think about it. If defenses do not have to fear Skattebo running between the tackles, they might load up on Nabers. But if the Giants can run the ball with Singletary, the Eagles have to keep a safety down in the box, and that leaves Nabers in more one-on-one situations. And Harbaugh said this week that J.J. McCarthy is pretty much caught up on the playbook. A fully prepared McCarthy with a fully healthy Nabers is a dangerous combination.

And the rest of the Giants’ report is very clean. Andrew Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor, both tackles who were limited earlier in the week, were full participants on Friday and have no designation. Abdul Carter, who missed time with an illness, was full again. Darius Alexander, Theo Johnson, Micah McFadden, Malik Harrison, Colton Hood, Trace Ford, DJ Reader, and Marcus Mbow were all full. The only other name with a designation is Charlie Jones, the Giants’ returner, who is questionable with a hand injury. So outside of Skattebo, the Giants are about as healthy as they have been in a while.

That is a big deal for the offensive line, especially. Earlier in the week, I was worried about both tackles playing at less than 100 percent. Now it looks like Thomas and Eluemunor are good to go. And with Singletary carrying the load, the Giants need that line to open holes.

Now, update number three, and this is on the Eagles’ side. Philadelphia ruled out wide receiver DeVonta Smith and wide receiver Dontayvion Wicks. They also ruled out linebacker Jihaad Campbell, defensive back Michael Carter, cornerback Jonathan Jones, and receiver and returner Britain Covey. That is a lot of talent missing. And Tanner McKee, the quarterback they just benched, is listed as questionable with a chest injury.

Think about what that means for Joe Milton. He is making his first start for the Eagles. And he will be doing it without DeVonta Smith, one of the best receivers in the league, and without Wicks. That is a huge problem for a young quarterback with a big arm. Who is he going to throw to? It probably means more targets for Dallas Goedert at tight end, and more passes to Saquon Barkley out of the backfield.

And Barkley is the name to watch. He was limited on Friday after missing practice earlier in the week with hamstring and back issues, but he does not have a game status, which suggests he is expected to play. Harbaugh said this week that Barkley is an elusive back who can take the ball from the A gap to the edge to the backside edge, and that he is still a threat catching the ball out of the backfield. With Smith and Wicks out, I think the Eagles will lean heavily on Barkley, both on handoffs and on checkdowns. The Giants’ linebackers have to be ready for that.

And the Eagles’ defense is missing pieces too. Without Jonathan Jones and Michael Carter in the secondary, and without Jihaad Campbell at linebacker, Philadelphia is thin in coverage. That is exactly where Nabers, Malachi Fields, Isaiah Likely, and Theo Johnson can find openings. If McCarthy gets time in the pocket, there should be chances downfield.

So here is how I see the matchup. The Giants have their best receiver. The Eagles do not have theirs. The Giants have a healthy offensive line. The Eagles have a first-time starter at quarterback behind theirs. The Giants are missing their hottest running back. The Eagles’ best weapon is their running back. On paper, that favors the Giants. But I have to remind you of one thing. The Giants have lost 13 straight games in Philadelphia, counting the playoffs, and they have not won there since 2013. On paper does not win games in Philadelphia.

And the history in that building is ugly for the Giants. The last time they won in Philadelphia was October of 2013. Since then, every trip has ended in a loss, including the playoff game in January of 2023, when the Eagles beat the Giants 38 to 7 in the divisional round. Most of the players on this roster were not here for those games. The quarterback arrived in September. The head coach arrived in January. And Harbaugh has made it very clear. He said he does not care about the streak, and this week he said he did not have anything to say about it. He refuses to give that history any power.

And remember, Harbaugh knows that stadium better than most visiting coaches. He spent nearly a decade on the Eagles’ staff, most of it under Andy Reid, before he got the Baltimore job. He knows how loud it gets, how that crowd reacts to a slow start, and how quickly it can turn on its own team when things go wrong. That is why the start of this game matters so much. If the Giants score first and the defense gets a couple of early stops, a 2 and 6 team with a first-time starter could start to feel the pressure from its own fans.

Now, let’s get to the trade update. Albert Breer reported that the Giants are a serious suitor for Jonathan Taylor. But he also said the Giants are not currently offering a package the Colts are looking for. That lines up with everything we have heard. Jordan Raanan reported that the Giants made an offer. Jeremy Fowler reported that executives value Taylor at around a second-round pick plus another pick. And the Colts are reportedly holding out for something closer to a first-round pick, or the McCaffrey-style package. The Giants are in the race. They are just not at the Colts’ number.

And there is a new layer to this that I think a lot of people are missing. According to reporting gathered by Pro Football Rumors, drawing on Dan Duggan of The Athletic, Raanan, and Pat Leonard of the Daily News, the Giants are not looking for rentals. They want players who are signed beyond this season. That fits Taylor, who is signed through 2028. It does not fit a 33-year-old like Grover Stewart as well, unless his contract runs past this year. And Harbaugh has said he would prefer to use 2028 picks for any deadline addition.

And here is the part I found really interesting. Duggan identified three Giants as possible trade candidates because of limited playing time. Center John Michael Schmitz, who is a pending free agent. Safety Tyler Nubin. And defensive tackle Darius Alexander. Both Nubin and Alexander are on their rookie contracts. And Joe Schoen has said the Giants could even try to add picks at the deadline.

Let me give you a small correction on myself here. In a recent video, I listed Tyler Nubin as part of the Giants’ young secondary core. And he is a talented young player. But according to Duggan, his playing time has been limited, and that is why his name is coming up as a trade candidate. I want you to have the full picture.

So think about what that means. The Giants might not just be buyers. They might be buyers and sellers at the same time. Trade a young player who is not getting snaps, maybe add a pick, and use that pick to help get a deal done for someone like Taylor. Remember, Darius Alexander was part of Art Stapleton’s trade idea, where the Giants would send Alexander and picks to Indianapolis for Taylor and Stewart. It was just an idea, not a report. But Duggan’s reporting makes that kind of structure a little more realistic. The Giants have players who could interest a rebuilding team.

And this is where a team’s depth becomes a trade chip. Alexander was limited earlier this week with a foot injury, but he was full on Friday. Nubin and Schmitz are healthy. If a player is not playing much, he is worth more to the Giants as a piece in a trade than as a backup on the sideline. That is smart roster management. That is my read, not a report.

And here is the big picture on the deadline. Everyone seems to be waiting on the Colts. Big Blue View put it simply. The Giants, and everyone else, are waiting on Indianapolis. The Colts have offers from several teams. Schefter thinks Taylor will be moved, maybe before the Eagles game, maybe right before Tuesday’s deadline. Graziano thinks the Giants could be the team. And I am still on the other side of that. I still do not think Taylor ends up a Giant, because I think the Colts’ price stays too high. But Breer calling the Giants a serious suitor is another sign that I might be wrong. If I am, I will own it.

Now, let me give you the game plan I want to see on Sunday, with all of this in mind.

On offense, run the ball with Singletary early. Get him into a rhythm. The Eagles’ defense is missing pieces, and the Giants’ line is healthy. If Singletary can get 15 to 20 carries and keep the Giants on schedule, McCarthy will have manageable third downs and play-action chances. Then take shots to Nabers when the Eagles bring a safety down.

On defense, contain Joe Milton. He can run. Last season, in relief for Dallas against the Giants, he ran four times for 43 yards. The edge rushers have to keep him in the pocket. And with Smith and Wicks out, I want the linebackers and safeties watching Barkley and Goedert. Make Milton beat you with his arm, to receivers he has barely played with.

And on special teams, watch Charlie Jones. If he cannot go with that hand injury, the Giants need someone reliable to field punts and kicks. In a game like this, a muffed punt could change everything.

And the standings make this game huge. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. A win would make the Giants 5 and 2 and 1, unbeaten in three division games, and five losses ahead of Philadelphia in the loss column. And then Washington comes to MetLife on Thursday night. Harbaugh called these the two most important games of the season. He was right.

And here is how Sunday connects to Tuesday. If the Giants win in Philadelphia, they go into the deadline at 5 and 2 and 1, with a massive lead and a healthy Nabers. That might make them comfortable walking away from the Colts’ price on Taylor, especially if Singletary has a big game. Or it might push them to go all in, because the division would be right there for the taking. If the Giants lose, the decision gets harder. A loss with Skattebo out could make the running back need feel more urgent. The Eagles game will not just decide the standings. It could decide what Joe Schoen does on Tuesday.

And then there is Thursday. Washington just beat Philadelphia 27 to 10 behind a clean game from Jayden Daniels. But the Commanders lost Daron Payne for the season with a broken foot, and they were already thin on the interior of their defensive line. On a short week, the team that is healthier usually has the edge. If the Giants come out of Philadelphia without new injuries, they will be in great shape for Thursday night. That is another reason why resting Skattebo on Sunday could pay off.

But remember what Harbaugh said this week about complacency. The Giants are fighting for their lives too. The word favorite is meaningless with ten games left. One and oh, every week. The opportunity is not the division. The opportunity is this game, and inside this game, the next play.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, whether Skattebo is officially inactive, and whether the Giants elevate Eric Gray. Number two, how many carries Singletary gets. Number three, how the Giants’ pass rush handles Milton’s legs. Number four, how the Eagles use Barkley without Smith and Wicks. And number five, any trade news before kickoff.

And here is my prediction. I am sticking with it, and I am feeling even better about it now. The Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. Singletary has a big day, McCarthy throws his first touchdown pass as a Giant, and the pass rush forces Milton into a big mistake. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, with a healthy Nabers, a healthy offensive line, a backfield led by a veteran they trust, and a chance to end 13 years of pain in Philadelphia. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
