Two surprising signings, one huge win. A week ago, the Giants were coming off a heartbreaking 24-21 loss to Washington, and the front office made two moves that barely made a headline. They signed veteran defensive back Tavierre Thomas off the Vikings' practice squad, and they brought back wide receiver and returner Xavier Gipson for his third stint with the team. Fans asked, why these guys? Why now? Well, on Sunday, the Giants beat the 7-2 Jacksonville Jaguars 23-18, and the area those two signings were supposed to fix, special teams, did not lose them the game. Today, I am going to break down both signings, the other roster moves nobody is talking about, how it all played out against Jacksonville, and what it means going into Indianapolis.

Let me take you back to where this started, because it explains why every move this week mattered.

Thursday, November 12. The Giants lost 24-21 to Washington at MetLife Stadium. It was their first division loss of the season. Isaiah Likely caught a 66-yard touchdown from J.J. McCarthy, and McCarthy threw for a career-high 293 yards. But Stefon Diggs scored the go-ahead touchdown from 32 yards out with 57 seconds left, and that was the game. John Harbaugh called it a missed opportunity, and he said the loss did not change the team's goals. I liked that. A loss like that can send a team spiraling, and the way Harbaugh talked told me he was not going to let that happen.

And then the schedule got harder. Jacksonville was coming to town at 7-2. The Jaguars had just beaten Tennessee 40-23, with Trevor Lawrence throwing for 352 yards and three touchdowns in that game. They had scored 30 or more points in four straight games. And the NFL flexed the game to 4:25 on CBS, the national window.

So the Giants had a choice. Panic, or make smart, small moves. They made smart, small moves.

Signing number one: Xavier Gipson.

On Monday, the Giants signed Gipson from the Philadelphia Eagles' practice squad to the active roster. He is 5-foot-9, 189 pounds, and he came out of Stephen F. Austin. And this was his third stint with the Giants. He was with them in the preseason, got cut when the roster went down to 53, and landed in Philadelphia. When a team keeps going back to the same player, it usually means they trust what he does.

And his résumé is all about returns. He has 74 career punt returns for 671 yards and a touchdown. He has 51 career kickoff returns for about 1,320 yards, a 25.9-yard average, with no muffed kickoffs. He is a returner first. That is a role that quietly decides a lot of games, because field position adds up.

Now, there was a real concern with Gipson too. He has 12 career fumbles, and Pro Football Focus has him with six muffed punts. And he replaced Charlie Jones, who had lost two fumbles on returns this season, one on a kickoff against New Orleans and one on a punt in Philadelphia. So the Giants were swapping one ball-security question for another. Harbaugh said it plainly when he was asked about Gipson. He said they liked him in camp, that maybe he can spark the return game, and that it's up to him to come in and make us right.

So how did Gipson do on Sunday? He returned kicks and he handled a punt, and most importantly, there were no muffs. After a season of watching the ball hit the turf on returns, a clean day was exactly what this team needed. It was not a highlight day, and I am not going to pretend it was. But in a five-point game against a 7-2 team, not giving away a possession is a win.

Signing number two: Tavierre Thomas.

Thomas came from the Minnesota Vikings' practice squad. He has played 116 regular-season games in his career, for Cleveland, Houston, Tampa Bay and Minnesota, with two career interceptions and 22 career starts on defense. But his real job is special teams. He has played more than 2,300 career special teams snaps. He led the Vikings with 19 special teams tackles in 2025. And his Pro Football Focus special teams grades have been above 80 again and again, with seasons of 86.5, 84.4, 87.0, 90.5 and 84.4.

The idea was that Thomas could help as a gunner on punts, where the Giants had struggled with missed tackles and penalties, and give the secondary a veteran depth piece. And here is why that depth mattered right away. Against Jacksonville, Greg Newsome II left early with an ankle injury. Cornerback Nic Jones, who had a knee injury and missed practice during the week, was inactive. Safety Jason Pinnock was inactive too. So the Giants needed every healthy body in that secondary. And the guys who stepped up delivered. Paulson Adebo came in for Newsome and got his first interception of the season. Deonte Banks picked off Lawrence in the first quarter. Dru Phillips broke up a fourth-and-one pass at the Giants' 11.

Now, I want to be honest with you about the coverage units. Ameer Abdullah opened the game with a 47-yard kickoff return, the longest the Giants have allowed all season, and an illegal formation penalty on Elijah Campbell helped Jacksonville get a field goal. So one week with Thomas did not fix everything. But the rest of the day, the special teams were solid. Jordan Stout boomed four punts averaging more than 60 yards gross, with one downed inside the 20. And Dominic Zvada made field goals of 53 and 54 yards, both from the right hash, the side where all six of his misses had come before Sunday. He did miss a 32-yarder, also from the right hash, but those two long kicks gave him eight field goals of 50-plus yards this season, tying Graham Gano's franchise record from 2022.

And here is the bigger point about field position. Before Sunday, the Giants' special teams had been giving away free possessions. Two lost fumbles on returns. A 12-yard punt against Washington. A kickoff out of bounds. Missed short field goals. In a game decided by five points, even one of those mistakes could have flipped the result. On Sunday, the Giants avoided almost all of them. That is not luck. That is the result of a coaching staff that identified the problem and went and got the right people to fix it.

So were these two signings the reason the Giants won? No. The defense was the reason the Giants won. But these signings were part of a special teams unit that did not hand Jacksonville the game, and after the Washington loss, that is exactly what they were brought in to do.

And these two signings tell you something about how this front office operates. Think back to the trade deadline. The Giants put a real offer on the table for Jonathan Taylor, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth, and when the Colts did not take it, the offer was pulled. They stood pat. Harbaugh said it was about showing belief in your guys by keeping all the guys you got, and that there was no short-term fix worth an exorbitant cost. They also did not have their 2027 fourth or fifth-round picks to work with, after the trades for Malachi Fields and J.J. McCarthy. So instead of a splash, the Giants have been fixing problems with smart, cheap, targeted moves. A returner who knows the building. A special teams veteran with years of elite grades. Bring the waived guys back to the practice squad so you do not lose them. That is a front office that knows exactly where its weaknesses are and does not overpay to patch them.

Now, the other roster moves nobody is talking about.

When the Giants signed Gipson and Thomas, they waived Charlie Jones and defensive back Nico Reed to make room. Reed had been signed before the week two Rams game when Paulson Adebo went on injured reserve, and he played 33 special teams snaps without taking a defensive snap. But here is the interesting part. On Wednesday, the Giants brought both Jones and Reed back on the practice squad. So the two players they waived on Monday are still in the building, just in a different role. I like that, because it keeps depth around without using active roster spots. If Gipson struggles, or if there is an injury, the Giants have a familiar face ready.

There were a couple of other moves too. On Tuesday, the Giants released running back Eric Gray from the practice squad. Gray was a fifth-round pick in 2023, and he played 34 games for the Giants. He had rejoined the team on November 2 after a stint with Cleveland. The team did not give a reason for the move, and I will not guess. And on Wednesday, the Giants released offensive lineman Gino Vandermark from the practice squad to make room for Jones and Reed.

And remember, the Giants also brought in veteran running back Antonio Gibson for a workout during the week. They did not sign him. But between Gray's release and the Gibson workout, it is clear the front office is keeping a close eye on the running back room, especially with Tyrone Tracy on injured reserve and Cam Skattebo dealing with fumble issues. Skattebo fumbled again on Sunday, his fifth of the season, which one report says leads the league. My opinion, not a report: I would not be surprised to see another move at running back at some point.

Now let's talk about the injury report, because this was a big piece of good news before the game.

On Friday, the Giants had no players with a game designation. None. That is a clean report heading into a tough opponent, and you almost never see that after a hard loss. Linebacker Malik Harrison was limited with a groin injury on Wednesday, and Pinnock was limited with a hip injury, but both were upgraded to full participants on Thursday. Banks with a shoulder injury, long snapper Ben Mann with an illness, and Skattebo with a hamstring injury were all full participants. Pinnock and Nic Jones did end up inactive on Sunday, but the core of the roster was healthy.

Jacksonville was not as lucky. Their center, Robert Hainsey, was ruled out with a knee injury, and defensive end B.J. Green II was out with a forearm injury. Defensive tackle Matt Dickerson and defensive end Danny Striggow were questionable, and both ended up inactive. Losing your center matters a lot, because he is the one who helps set the protection for the whole line. And the Giants took advantage. Abdul Carter had two sacks, a career high, and four quarterback hits. DJ Reader added a sack. The Giants hit Trevor Lawrence 11 times. I am not saying the Hainsey injury was the only reason, but it sure did not hurt.

And there was more good news for the Giants off the field this week, in the form of awards.

Arvell Reese was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week after his three-sack, 12-tackle game in Philadelphia. He was also named Pepsi NFL Rookie of the Week for the second time this season. And he became the third Giants rookie linebacker to win NFC Defensive Player of the Week, after Devon Kennard in 2014 and Kayvon Thibodeaux in 2022. That is great company. Jevon Holland was named NFC Defensive Player of the Month for September. And Malik Nabers was named NFC Offensive Player of the Month for October. So this team has real playmakers on both sides of the ball. And Nabers showed it again on Sunday, with seven catches and an eight-yard touchdown, his third of the season.

So let's go back to Sunday and put the whole picture together.

The Giants beat Jacksonville 23-18 as six-point underdogs at home. They moved to 6-3-1 and stayed on top of the NFC East. Jacksonville fell to 7-3, and its three-game winning streak ended. And that streak of four straight games with 30 or more points? It ended too. The Giants held the Jaguars to 18. The all-time series between these teams was tied at four wins apiece coming in, with the Giants winning the last meeting in Jacksonville in 2022. Now the Giants lead it.

The defense was the star. Lawrence threw for 301 yards on 47 attempts, but he completed only 25 passes, threw two interceptions, and the Giants added a third pick on a two-point try, when Thibodeaux intercepted it after Brian Thomas Jr.'s touchdown to keep the lead at eight. Dru Phillips' fourth-down breakup ended Jacksonville's streak of 14 straight red zone drives with points.

The offense did enough. McCarthy went 18 of 38 for 173 yards, a touchdown and no interceptions, and he ran for 38 yards on four carries. Jameis Winston punched in a one-yard sneak for the Giants' first lead. Isaiah Likely became the first Giants tight end with 600-plus receiving yards in the first 10 games of a season since Mark Bavaro in 1987. And rookie Malachi Fields made a huge 19-yard catch for a first down with under three minutes left to help close it out.

But the offense also stalled late, with four straight possessions without a first down while Jacksonville cut the lead from 14 to five. That is the same closing problem we saw against Washington. This time, the Giants survived. They have to fix it before it costs them again.

Let me also talk about where Gipson could go from here, because I think his role might grow. Harbaugh said when they signed him that Gipson will most likely do some things on offense too, not just returns. He has 29 career catches in 41 games, and he had a long touchdown up the right sideline in the preseason. With his speed, I could see the Giants using him on jet motion, a quick screen, or a reverse, the kind of gadget play that makes a defense hesitate for a split second. In an offense that has struggled to close games, one or two of those plays can be enough to move the chains in the four-minute drill. My opinion, not a report.

And the secondary depth question is going to be a big one going into Indianapolis. If Newsome's ankle keeps him out, Adebo looks like the next man up, and he just showed he can make plays. Harbaugh told him this week that he is a starter in this league, and he backed that up with an interception. Behind him, Thomas gives you a veteran who has played the slot and knows how to tackle in space. That is exactly the kind of insurance you want in late November, when every team is banged up.

And think about what Indianapolis brings. A new starting quarterback in Anthony Richardson, who has a huge arm and can run, but has struggled with consistency. A team that just lost 26-3. A road game that some people will call a trap game, sitting right between a statement win over Jacksonville and a home game against San Francisco. Those are the games where special teams can make the difference. A muffed punt or a long return allowed can give a struggling team life. A clean special teams game keeps them down. So the Gipson and Thomas signings matter just as much this week as they did last week. And there is a storyline here too. These are the same Colts who turned down the Giants' Jonathan Taylor offer at the deadline. Taylor will be on the other sideline on Sunday. The Giants kept their picks. Now they get to show they did not need him.

Now the receipts, because I always own them. Last week, I said these special teams moves were not flashy but could matter in a close game. The game was close, and special teams did not cost the Giants. I said Gipson's first job was to catch everything cleanly, and he did. I said I would ride with Zvada through Jacksonville, and he made two huge kicks from the right hash, even if he missed a short one. And I predicted the Giants would win 23-20. The final was 23-18. I will take that.

Here is my prediction for Indianapolis, and you can hold me to it. The Colts are 3-8, they just lost 26-3 to Houston, and they waived Daniel Jones, with Anthony Richardson set to start. The Giants go on the road and win. Gipson breaks at least one return past midfield. Thomas makes a tackle on punt coverage. Skattebo holds on to the football. And the Giants move to 7-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I will say it one more time. This team can go all the way. A front office that made smart moves instead of panicking. A coach who knows how to fix special teams. A defense full of award winners. A star receiver who was the best offensive player in the conference in October. And a first-place record after beating a 7-2 team. The two surprising signings will not get billboards in Times Square. But depth wins in November and December. And the Giants just showed they have it.
