The Giants are facing a quarterback decision before Jacksonville, and it is not the one you think. Nobody is benching J.J. McCarthy. John Harbaugh just said he feels more strongly than ever that McCarthy can win games. The real decision is about what kind of quarterback McCarthy is going to be from here. One side of this fan base is begging the Giants to let him sling it deep, the way he did against Houston and nearly did against New Orleans. The other side, and reportedly that includes his own star receiver, is saying the Giants should stop hunting home run balls and play smarter. So which one is it? The gunslinger or the game manager? That is the shocking QB decision, and today we are going to settle it.

And drop a comment right now: do you want McCarthy throwing deep more against Jacksonville, or do you want him playing it smarter and shorter? Gunslinger or game manager. Pick one.

Before we get to the quarterback, I want to start with a fan question that has been going around this week, because it sets the table for everything else. The point was this. Everybody knows how big a Giants fan I am, but we need to take a step back and remember how bad this team was for the last ten years. Let’s not get lost in the wins that came from a weak schedule, and let’s remember we still have a ways to go.

And I think that is a fair point, but I also think multiple things can be true at the same time. Yes, the Giants are overachieving compared to where people had them in the summer. Yes, they have been overcoming adversity, including losing Brian Burns for the season back in week three. But here is where I land. This team is past the moral victory stage. When John Harbaugh was hired, the feeling around this team changed. You went from a fan base that woke up on Sundays hoping to win to a fan base that now expects to win. And the Giants have earned that expectation. There is enough talent. They are well coached enough. You were up in the fourth quarter against Washington. You should have won that football game.

Is the season over? No. Are the Giants going to miss the playoffs? I do not think so. Do the Giants still control their own destiny in the NFC East? Yes. They are 5-3-1, alone in first, two games ahead of Washington and a game and a half ahead of Dallas going into the weekend. And the NFL is the king of worst-to-first stories. This team has enough to win this division.

Okay. Now, the quarterback decision.

Let’s start with what happened on Thursday, because the debate comes straight out of that game. In the first half against Washington, the Giants’ offense was very horizontal. A lot of screens. A lot of quick, sideways stuff, trying to get the short passing game going. Washington’s defense clearly wanted to keep everything in front of them. And in the second half, McCarthy started to heat up. He took what the defense gave him, kept the offense on schedule, and then hit Isaiah Likely for the 66-yard touchdown. He finished with 293 yards, a career high.

Then the end. Three chances in the fourth quarter to win it with a couple of first downs. Three-and-out, three-and-out, and then the final interception on a deep ball to Malik Nabers with Washington blitzing everybody.

So now you have two camps.

Camp number one: let him throw it deep.

The argument from a lot of fans is simple. McCarthy has a big arm. When the Giants let him push the ball down the field, good things happen. He beat Houston that way. He nearly beat the Saints that way. He threw the 66-yarder to Likely on Thursday. So why not do more of it? Throw it up to Malachi Fields and let him go get it once or twice a game. Get Darnell Mooney one-on-one on a deep route, because he has the speed to separate. Take more shots to Nabers, the best receiver on the team. Make the defense respect the deep ball, and everything underneath opens up.

And there is a real case here. When a defense like Washington’s wants to keep everything in front of them, the only way to punish that is to go over the top every now and then. If you never take the shot, the safeties creep up, the linebackers sit on the short routes, and the screens get blown up.

Camp number two: play smarter and stop hunting home runs.

This is where it gets spicy. According to one Giants show this week, Nabers said on Friday that the Giants should not be looking for home run balls, and that they should have a better plan and play smarter. And the host of that show took it as Nabers basically calling out McCarthy in the media, since the final interception was a deep ball intended for Nabers himself.

Now, I want to be careful here, because I have not seen the full context of every word Nabers said, and I am not going to put words in his mouth. But if that is the message, I actually understand the football side of it. Go back to the stat we talked about earlier this week. Against six or seven rushers on Thursday, McCarthy was two for six for 11 yards and an interception. When Washington sent pressure, the deep ball was not the answer. The answer was a hot read, a quick throw, a smart plan to beat the blitz. Harbaugh admitted after watching the tape that a good pass might have been the better choice than three straight runs to close it out. That does not mean a bomb. That means a smart, high-percentage throw to move the chains.

But I also want to be fair about the criticism Nabers is getting. The host of that show went hard at him. He said Nabers needs to be a better teammate, needs to hold himself to the same standard he holds everyone else to, and pointed to the body language against the Eagles, the dropped touchdown in Philly, and the fumble in Washington territory on Thursday. His line was basically, catch the football and hold on to it.

That is harsh. And I am not going to pile on. We already heard Nabers say he is tired of people calling him a bad teammate, and that he has never been on a team where anyone called him that. What I will say is this. Nabers had a rough Thursday. Five catches for 44 yards and a lost fumble. If he wants the offense to play smarter, the best way to lead that is to be the most reliable player on the field. And I think he will be. My opinion, not a report: Nabers has a big game against Jacksonville.

So here is the decision, and this is where I want to land the plane.

My opinion, not a report: the answer is not gunslinger or game manager. The answer is both, at the right times. And I think this is exactly what Harbaugh was hinting at this week.

Think about what McCarthy has shown us. Early in his starts, he was the safest quarterback in the league. Of the 31 quarterbacks with multiple starts since week seven, he was the only one without a turnover. That is the game manager. But he was also the only one without a passing or rushing touchdown. That is the problem with only being the game manager. Then on Thursday, he threw his first touchdown pass as a Giant on a big shot, the 66-yarder, and he threw his first real turnover on a big shot, the final interception. That is the gunslinger, with the reward and the risk.

The ideal McCarthy is somewhere in the middle. He takes the deep shot when the defense gives it to him, like when Likely or Fields has a one-on-one or Mooney gets a step. But when the defense brings the house, he gets the ball out quick to the hot read and lives to fight another down. When the defense sits back and keeps everything in front, he takes the profit underneath, moves the chains, and waits for the safety to cheat up. And then he hits him over the top.

That is what the best young quarterbacks learn to do. And it is a skill that only comes with reps. Harbaugh said this week that they are still in the process of growing McCarthy’s confidence. He said McCarthy has earned that right. And he said he wants to close games out on offense. Putting all of that together, I think the decision is clear. The Giants are going to trust McCarthy more, not less. And they are going to give him a cleaner plan against the blitz.

So what does that look like on the field against Jacksonville? Let me give you my three keys for McCarthy, and these are my own keys, not anything the team has said.

Key number one: win the first fifteen minutes with rhythm. The first half against Washington was too sideways. Screens are fine, but when the defense is sitting on them, you have to mix in intermediate throws, the 12 to 18 yard routes over the middle where Likely has been so good. Likely had nine catches for 127 yards on Thursday. He is the bridge between the short game and the deep game. Feed him early and the whole offense gets into a rhythm.

Key number two: have the blitz answer ready before the blitz comes. That means the protection and the hot read are set before the snap. If Jacksonville shows pressure, McCarthy should already know where the ball is going. Skattebo out of the backfield is a great answer here. He had 44 receiving yards on Thursday. A quick throw to him in the flat against a blitz can turn into a big gain, because the defenders who would normally be there are rushing the quarterback.

Key number three: take one shot a quarter, and only when it is there. Not every drive. Not forced. But when Fields or Mooney has a one-on-one and the safety is cheating, let it fly. One completion of 40 yards changes how a defense plays you for the rest of the day. That is the balance between the gunslinger and the game manager.

And let me say a word about the backup, because some people will bring up Jameis Winston every time McCarthy throws an interception. Winston has been great in his role. He saved the day in Philadelphia with a 14-play, 76-yard touchdown drive. He is in the short-yardage sneak packages, and he brought the “Who let the dogs out” cadence on Thursday. But Harbaugh has made it clear. McCarthy is the starter, and he has earned it. Having Winston behind him is a luxury, not a controversy. That is how a healthy quarterback room is supposed to work.

Now let’s talk about the other big question from the mailbag this week, because it ties into the quarterback, too. The schedule.

Look at what is coming. Jacksonville at home this Sunday, November 22. Then a stretch that includes Indianapolis, San Francisco coming cross country to MetLife, and Seattle, and then Cleveland after that. One fan asked whether the Giants can win at least one of those next four games. And the host said if the Giants lose four straight there, he would be really disappointed. He said you can beat Jacksonville, they are not unbeatable. You should beat Indianapolis. You can beat San Francisco when they fly across the country to New Jersey. And why can’t the Giants beat Seattle? He said he is expecting a 2-2 split over the next four, and then a win against the Browns.

Here is my take. I think 2-2 is the floor, not the ceiling. My opinion, not a report: this defense was fifth in the league in total defense heading into week 10, allowing 301.4 yards per game, after being one of the worst defenses in football last season. Arvell Reese is making history. Tracy is on IR and is lined up to come back later in this stretch. The Giants are coming off ten days of rest. If McCarthy takes the next step against the blitz, I think this team can do better than 2-2. But you have to start with Jacksonville. Win that one, and the whole conversation changes.

And this is why the quarterback decision matters so much right now. Against good teams, in close games, the quarterback is the one who closes. Thursday night, the offense had three chances to close and did not. If McCarthy closes even one of those, the Giants are 6-2-1 and everybody is talking about the best team in the NFC East. So the next four games are not just about the record. They are about McCarthy proving he can be the closer.

Now, the pizza pie of blame. One host on a Giants show put it this way, and I love the image. If you are cutting up a pizza of blame for the Washington loss, the defense gets one slice. Everybody else, the offense, special teams and coaching, can split up the rest. The defense won the game at least three times, with a fourth-down stop, the Holland interception and the Ar’Darius Washington interception. The offense had a 66-yard touchdown that should have been the winner. Good teams find a way to win. Bad teams find a way to lose. And on Thursday, the Giants found a way to lose.

I agree with that, mostly. I would give the defense one slice, too, mainly for the final drive, when the zero blitz got beat and Diggs broke a tackle. But the rest of that pie belongs to the offense in the four-minute drill, the special teams, and the late-game coaching calls. And Harbaugh has owned his part of that. He said he could have thrown the ball. He said he is kicking himself for not going for it on fourth-and-one deep in his own end. That is the kind of honesty you want from your head coach.

And while we are on special teams, there was another big question in the mailbag. Why did the Giants get rid of the kicker from last year? A fan said the old kicker never missed, and asked why they moved on. The answer from the host was that Harbaugh believed Dominic Zvada was better. The previous kicker, Ben Sauls, is not on a roster right now, and the host said he trusts Harbaugh’s evaluation of kickers. And he made the point that Harbaugh probably looks at Zvada as a guy who could be his kicker for the next five, ten, twelve years.

But the host also said, you have got to make your kicks. You cannot miss 33-yarders. You cannot be so erratic that your coach does not trust you on long field goals. Keep the ball in bounds on kickoffs. Kick it out of the back of the end zone when you are supposed to. He said Zvada was money at Michigan in 2024, but not as good in 2025, and that Zvada has had games where he has been great for the Giants, but that the kicking game cost the team chances in both Washington games this year. The host said he has one foot on needing a new kicker and one foot on riding with Zvada.

Here is where I land on Zvada. Harbaugh told us this week that there is a specific plant-foot technique issue, and that all of the misses have been showing up on the right hash. That is a fixable problem. And Harbaugh is a special teams coach at heart. I am riding with Zvada through Jacksonville. If the right-hash problem shows up again in a close game, then we have a real conversation. My opinion, not a report.

Now let me bring it back to the quarterback one more time, because this is the decision that matters the most.

Receipts, because I always own them. I told you before the Washington game that McCarthy would finish the game and play well. He did, with a career high, and his coach backed him. I also told you the Giants would win that game. Wrong. And earlier this week, I said McCarthy’s biggest weakness was getting off his first read under pressure. Thursday showed that is still the thing he has to fix. So I was right about the problem, and the problem showed up at the worst time.

Here is my prediction for Jacksonville, and you can hold me to it. McCarthy throws for at least one touchdown. He takes a couple of deep shots early, to Fields or Mooney, to keep the safeties honest. When Jacksonville blitzes, he gets the ball out fast. And in the fourth quarter, if the Giants have a lead, he is the one who closes it with a smart throw on third down. The Giants win and get to 6-3-1. If I am wrong, I will be right here owning it.

And I am going to say it again, because I believe it. This team can go all the way. They have a coach who has changed the expectations in this building. They have a defense that is top five in the league. They have a running back in Cam Skattebo playing with fire. They have a deep group of pass catchers in Likely, Nabers, Mooney and Fields. And they have a young quarterback whose coach trusts him more than ever. The decision is made. McCarthy is the guy. Now it is about what kind of guy he becomes. And I think he becomes the kind of quarterback who wins in December.
