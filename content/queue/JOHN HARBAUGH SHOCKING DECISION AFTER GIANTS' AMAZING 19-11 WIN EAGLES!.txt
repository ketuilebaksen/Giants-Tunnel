John Harbaugh just made his position on the trade deadline very clear, and it might not be what some Giants fans wanted to hear. After the 19 to 11 win in Philadelphia, a reporter asked about a report that the Giants had taken their offer for Jonathan Taylor off the table. And Harbaugh did not deny it. He said, I’m not saying that report’s not true. He said the Giants are not ruling anything out, but he does not see anything particularly imminent right now. And then he drew a hard line. We’re not just going to go for a short-term fix at an exorbitant cost. We’re not going to do that. That’s not where we’re at. Folks, that is the head coach of a first-place team, with the deadline less than 48 hours away, telling the whole league the Giants will not overpay. And that was not even the only big decision Harbaugh talked about. He revealed that the Giants thought about leaving Jameis Winston in at quarterback for the final drive, and J.J. McCarthy insisted on going back in. Today, I am going to break down everything Harbaugh said after the biggest Giants win in Philadelphia in 13 years. Let’s get into it.

Let me start with how Harbaugh opened his press conference, because it was emotional. He said it had been about 19 years since he was coaching in Philadelphia, and coming back in on the bus, seeing the Linc, coming down Interstate 95, in a Giants bus, was really something. He said he did not think it was going to be quite that, but it was all that. It was a moment. This is Giants and Eagles in Philadelphia. This is a big rivalry.

Remember, Harbaugh spent nearly a decade on the Eagles’ staff before he got the Baltimore job. He knows that building. He knows those fans. And all week, when reporters asked him about it, he brushed it off and said it was just kind of cool. But after the game, you could hear it in his voice. This one meant something to him.

And then he gave credit to both sides. He said the Eagles played a really good, really tough football game. And his guys met the challenge. They never flinched. They never blinked. And then he said something I think sums up this whole season. They played winning football. Not perfect football, but winning football, and found a way to win the game. All three phases, starting with the defense.

He talked about special teams too. He admitted they had some issues early, and we know what those were. Charlie Jones fumbled a punt and gave Philadelphia the ball at the Giants’ 47. But Harbaugh said the coverage units got it together, and the kickoff and punt coverage were great after that.

And then Harbaugh talked about faith. He said the team chaplain talked to the players about heart, and guarding your heart. He said the team gave God the glory at the end. And he said something very honest about himself. Sometimes you have to get out of yourself, believe in your God, and believe in your men. And he said that in the second half, when he figured that out, he was better, and the team seemed to be better too. That is a coach admitting he had to reset during the game. I respect that a lot.

Now, let’s get to the quarterback situation, because this was one of the most interesting parts of the press conference. A reporter pointed out that the last time the Giants had a quarterback get hurt during a game, everything felt jumbled the rest of the way. This time, it did not. Harbaugh said that was a great point, and one he had not thought about. He said maybe it is because Jameis Winston has played so much this season. Winston started four games while Jaxson Dart was out, and he went 2 and 1 and 1.

And then Harbaugh singled out what he called the throw of the game. After a sack put the Giants in a long-yardage situation, Winston threw a deep sideline route to Malachi Fields. Harbaugh said Winston threw a dime right on the sideline, and Fields ran a great route. He said it was impossible to cover, about as well executed as you can get, and it got that drive started. And then the Giants kept grinding against a tough defense and finished the drive in the end zone, with Winston’s five-yard touchdown to Isaiah Likely. Not a field goal. A touchdown. Harbaugh said you have to give Winston and the guys around him a lot of credit.

And think about what that means for this team. Winston has now been the starter, the backup, and the closer at different points this season. He keeps answering every time the Giants need him. That is a rare thing in this league. A lot of teams would kill for a backup quarterback like that.

Now, McCarthy. A reporter asked what McCarthy showed by going back into the game, and pointed out that he ran for a first down basically on one leg. And Harbaugh said, you’ve got to be kidding me. He said McCarthy was in pain. But he ran fast for that first down. Harbaugh joked that there was probably a little adrenaline flowing through his veins. And then he revealed the decision. He said the Giants were not pushing McCarthy back out there. McCarthy was able to play, but he insisted on playing, even at the end. And the coaches actually thought about putting Winston in for the last drive. But McCarthy insisted on being out there.

And later, when another reporter asked directly if he thought about leaving Winston in, especially after Winston had just led the Giants down the field for the touchdown, Harbaugh said yes. They had that conversation, and it was definitely part of the conversation. But the trainers and the doctor said McCarthy was good, and McCarthy said he was ready to go. So they put him back out there.

That is a fascinating decision. Think about it. Winston had the hot hand. He had just thrown the go-ahead touchdown. A lot of coaches would have ridden him to the finish. But Harbaugh trusted the medical staff, and he trusted his starter. And McCarthy closed out the game without a turnover. He finished 12 of 19 for 127 yards with no interceptions. In his two starts as a Giant, he has not thrown a single interception.

And Harbaugh was asked what that means to the locker room. He said it means a lot. It demonstrates the heart they were talking about. He said every player has the opportunity to lead and show that at some point. And when it is the quarterback, fighting through an injury and going back in, that is big. And then he added the reality check. Now McCarthy is going to have to fight to get ready to play on Thursday. The Giants host Washington on a short week, and the hip is going to be the story to watch.

Let me give you my take on that, and this is my opinion, not a report. If McCarthy is not close to 100 percent by Thursday, I would not hesitate to start Winston. The Giants have a two-game lead in the standings. They do not need to risk turning a hip injury into a long-term problem. And Winston just showed again that he can win games. But if McCarthy practices and the medical staff clears him, he should start. That has been the plan since September, and he has earned it.

Now, let’s talk about the star of the game, Arvell Reese. A reporter asked if Reese got a game ball, and Harbaugh said he did. He said Reese could have gotten two or three for what he did. He said he wanted to give him the grand slam. He had a bunch of tackles, tackles for loss, sacks, pressures. Harbaugh said there were about six things he did statistically that were amazing. Reese finished with three sacks, and he forced a fumble that Micah McFadden recovered.

And then Harbaugh told a great story. A reporter asked about seeing Reese become a game-changing player. And Harbaugh said that is what the Giants hoped for when Reese fell to them in the draft. He said he and Joe Schoen were giddy. They were beside themselves. And then he talked about the person. He said Reese is a great young man. Humble, very tough, works really hard, very coachable, wants to do things the right way, and is a great leader in his own quiet way. He said he could not say enough about him. But he also said Reese is still young, and he has a lot in front of him.

That is the perfect way to talk about a rookie. Praise him, but keep him grounded. And remember what Schoen said during the bye. The kid just turned 21 a couple of months ago. The sky is the limit. After Sunday, you can see why both of them are so excited.

And then a reporter asked the question that made me laugh. Does it say something about a team when it can win even when everything is not going well? Harbaugh said yes. And then he said, have there been games where everything’s been going well for us this year? I’m trying to think. I don’t know if we’ve had any of those. He said he would like to have one or two of those, and see how the team handles winning when everything is going its way.

And he is right. Think about this season. A tie in Washington. An overtime win in Houston. A grind in Philadelphia. Close games, every week. The Giants are 5 and 2 and 1, and they have not had one easy win. Harbaugh said this is what the NFL is. It’s tough. It’s crazy town. It’s hard. Every team is really good. He said the Eagles were fighting for their lives, and you could see it in the way they played. And the Giants still overcame what they had to overcome and won.

And Harbaugh also explained what winning football looked like on Sunday. He said the team played hard, rough, and physical, but also really smart. He said the Giants did not give up big plays, and they did not turn the ball over. They punted the ball, they tackled, and they kept everything moving forward for the most part. That is the formula. It is not flashy. But in a game that came down to eight points, it was enough. And look at the numbers. The Giants have now scored 182 points and allowed 172 this season. A plus-10 point differential, for a team that is 5 and 2 and 1. This is a team that lives in close games, and keeps finding ways to win them.

And one more thing on this. When Harbaugh was asked what it is about this team that lets it find ways to win, he said they just have it. They have what it takes. He said he does not know how to describe it other than that. He said he thinks they have had it all along, and they found it in these games. And then he said something I love. Sometimes it is one thing to have it, and another thing to find it. This team has found it.

Now, the trade deadline, and the part of the press conference that is going to get the most attention. A reporter said the Giants have about 48 hours to put the finishing touches on this team, and asked what the game showed him. Harbaugh said there is nobody on the team the Giants want to trade. They like all of their guys. And then he said there has to be somebody out there that you really want to trade for, that makes sense. Short term and long term. It has to make sense short term and long term.

And then he said the line I quoted at the start. We’re not just going to go for a short-term fix at an exorbitant cost. We’re not going to do that. That’s not where we’re at. He said that when teams do that sort of thing, it usually does not work out. You end up paying a hefty price and you do not get the return on it. And he said he never thinks a team is one step away or one player away. To him, it is always a total momentum kind of thing. But if the right opportunity comes, and there is a deal that makes sense, the Giants will do it.

And he praised the front office. He said Joe Schoen does a great job, and he mentioned the people working with him, including Chris Rossetti. He said they are making a lot of calls. We’re calling everybody, he said.

Then came the Taylor question. A reporter said there was a report that the Giants had made an offer for Taylor and then taken it off the table. Is the door closed? Harbaugh said the Giants are open to any kind of situation. But things do change, day to day. One day you might do something, and the next day something happens that changes your positioning. And then he said, I’m not saying that report’s not true. He said those conversations are taking place. He said he cannot talk about specific players who are on other teams. But he said the Giants are not ruling anything out. And then the key line. I don’t see anything particularly imminent right now either. So, we’ll see what happens.

So let me put that in plain English. The Giants made an offer for Taylor. According to the report the reporter mentioned, they pulled it back. Harbaugh did not deny it. And he said nothing is imminent. That sounds like a team that has decided the Colts’ price is too high. Remember, the Colts reportedly wanted a first-round pick or something like the McCaffrey package. Executives around the league valued Taylor at about a second-round pick plus another pick. The Giants were not willing to close that gap. And Harbaugh just told you why. No short-term fix at an exorbitant cost.

And here is my receipt, and this time it might be a good one. For the last few weeks, I have been predicting that Taylor would not end up a Giant, because I thought the Colts’ price would stay too high for this front office. ESPN’s insiders were leaning the other way. Dan Graziano said he would not be surprised if the Giants got him. Now it looks like the Giants are walking away. The deadline is not here yet, and things can change, as Harbaugh said. So I am not taking a victory lap. But it is trending in that direction.

And I think it is the right decision. The Giants just won in Philadelphia with 16 rushing yards in the first half and without Cam Skattebo. Tyrone Tracy Jr. will be back around week 13. Skattebo could be back as soon as Thursday. The running back room is thin, but it is not broken. Paying a premium for Taylor would have been a luxury. And as one analyst said this week, a 20 million dollar running back is only worth it if you feel like you are going to the Super Bowl.

But I still think the Giants add someone. Harbaugh said they are calling everybody. Jordan Raanan said he thought the Giants were more likely to add a defensive tackle than a running back. With Dexter Lawrence gone, the interior of the defensive line is still the biggest need. I predicted a defensive tackle trade before the deadline. I am sticking with it, but I will admit Harbaugh’s tone makes it less certain. If nothing happens, I will own that receipt too.

And finally, Dominic Zvada. A reporter asked about Harbaugh’s confidence in his kicker, and Harbaugh said thanks for asking, he should have given Zvada a game ball. But then he joked that Zvada is supposed to make those kicks, right? That is what he does, and he did it. Zvada went four for four, from 53, 49, 38, and 49 yards. And Harbaugh said those were not easy kicks. He said the wind was blowing from the corner behind the right shoulder back across to the other corner. Those were legit kicks. And he agreed that a young kicker needs to prove himself, not just to the coaches, but to the players in that locker room. After missing two field goals in Houston, Zvada proved it.

I questioned whether the Giants should bring in competition for Zvada after Houston. That is another receipt I will happily own. He answered it in the toughest conditions in the toughest building. And in a game the Giants won by eight points, his four field goals were the difference.

He gave Reese the first game ball and called him humble, tough, coachable, and a quiet leader. He said Zvada should have gotten a game ball too. And on the trade deadline, he made it clear. No short-term fix at an exorbitant cost. Nothing imminent. But they are calling everybody.
