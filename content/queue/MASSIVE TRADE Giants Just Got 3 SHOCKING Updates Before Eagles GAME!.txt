The Giants just made a real offer for Jonathan Taylor, and that is only the first of three big updates before Philadelphia. According to ESPN’s Jordan Raanan, the Giants have put an offer on the table for the Colts’ star running back. The Colts reportedly want a first-round pick, but might accept a second-round pick plus more, and the Giants have not closed that gap yet. Update number two, Deonte Banks and the Giants could not agree on a contract extension, even after Greg Newsome got paid. And update number three is the one that worries me most. Malik Nabers did not practice on Thursday because of a knee issue, and Cam Skattebo missed practice again with his hamstring. So the Giants head into a division game in Philadelphia with their top receiver and their hottest running back both on the injury report, a trade offer sitting in Indianapolis, and a cornerback who just said no to their money. And they are facing a brand new quarterback in Joe Milton. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the trade offer, because it is the headline. Raanan reported that the Giants have made an offer for Taylor. That is the first time we have heard from a reliable insider that the Giants actually put something on the table, instead of just checking in. But Raanan added two important details. First, the Colts reportedly want a first-round pick, though they might accept a second-rounder plus more. And second, the Giants have not closed that gap, and Raanan is not sure they want to pay it.

And separately, remember that Tom Pelissero reported the Colts turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick, from a team he did not identify. I want to be careful here. Those are two separate pieces of information. We do not know if the rejected offer was the Giants’ offer. It could be. It could be another team. What we do know is that a second plus something was not enough, and the Giants are still somewhere short of what Indianapolis wants.

And then Raanan said something that I think is even more important. He believes the Giants are more likely to trade for a defensive tackle than a running back. Remember, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that Tampa Bay’s Vita Vea is on the Giants’ radar, but the Buccaneers are not expected to trade him. And Fowler said Colts defensive tackle Grover Stewart is a more realistic option. There have also been reports that the Giants had interest in DeForest Buckner before Indianapolis sent him to San Francisco. And we already know about Jeffery Simmons, where Harbaugh called the Titans’ owner, and Daron Payne, before he broke his foot.

So here is the pattern. Simmons is in Chicago. Buckner is in San Francisco. Payne is on injured reserve. Vea probably is not available. That leaves Stewart as the name that keeps coming up, along with Christian Barmore in New England. And I said in a recent video that I think the Giants make a defensive tackle trade before November 10th. Raanan’s read makes me feel better about that prediction.

And think about this. Stewart and Taylor are on the same team. If the Giants are already talking to Indianapolis about Taylor, the Colts’ front office is already on the phone. That makes a combined deal at least possible. Art Stapleton floated that kind of idea a couple of weeks ago, with Taylor and Stewart coming to New York. I want to be clear, that was his idea, not a report. But if the Giants want both a running back and a defensive tackle, one phone call to Indianapolis could cover both.

So what would I offer? My price has not changed much. For Taylor alone, I would go up to a second-round pick plus a mid-round pick. A 2027 second and a 2028 third would be my ceiling. The Giants are missing their 2027 fourth and fifth-round picks, so every pick matters. And if the Colts want something like the McCaffrey package, a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth, then somebody else can win that bidding. If the Giants can get Stewart included, I would think about adding a little more. That is my opinion, not a report.

Now, update number two. Deonte Banks. According to reports this week, the Giants tried to extend Banks, and the two sides could not reach a deal. Bobby Skinner of Giants Nation reported that the Giants offered Banks a deal with the same structure as Greg Newsome’s, at a slightly lower average. Newsome’s deal, remember, is three years and up to 46 million dollars, with 32 million guaranteed. Banks turned it down.

And here is my receipt. Last week, when Newsome got paid, I said another extension might be coming, maybe for Banks. I was half right. The Giants did try. But Banks said no. And I understand why his agent would push back. Once a teammate at the same position signs, there is an actual number on the table. If you are Banks, the first question you ask is, why am I worth less than Newsome?

And the Giants better have a good football answer to that question. Because Banks has played really well. Look at what he did in Houston. Nico Collins, one of the best receivers in the league, drew four targets with Banks in coverage, according to the coverage numbers shared after the game. Collins caught one of them for four yards. Think about that. Houston tried to feed its best receiver against Banks four times and got almost nothing. That is the kind of game that shows you how much Banks has turned his career around.

And remember what Joe Schoen said about Banks during the bye. He had a great rookie year, struggled for a couple of years, and then the new staff gave everyone a blank slate. Banks won the job coming out of camp. And Schoen said he never came to his office asking to be traded and never complained. He just put his head down and worked. So this is not a player who is being difficult. This is a player who believes he has earned a certain number.

So what would I do? I would keep talking. A three-year deal still makes sense for both sides. Banks is young enough that a three-year contract gives him security and still leaves him another big negotiation before he turns 30. For the Giants, it means paying a corner who has proven he can cover the other team’s best receiver. I would be comfortable going a little higher than the first offer, if the guarantees and the structure make sense. That is my opinion, not a report. The worst outcome would be heading into the offseason with no deal and having to think about the franchise tag.

And let me remind you of one more thing, because there is a cautionary tale in Giants history. Stevie Brown intercepted eight passes for the Giants in 2012. Then he tore his ACL in the 2013 preseason and missed the entire year. One injury changed everything about his career. That is why both sides have a reason to get a deal done. Banks wants security. The Giants want to lock up a player before the price goes up. There is a deal there somewhere. It is about finding the middle. Maybe the guaranteed money matters more to Banks than the headline number. Maybe that is where the Giants can give a little and get him signed.

But here is the good news. Banks turning down the offer does not mean anything bad for this season. Harbaugh said this week that the Giants have no policy against negotiating during the season. If it becomes a distraction, they stop. If it is not a distraction, they keep going. And Banks has shown all year that he is a pro. I expect him to play just as hard in Philadelphia as he did in Houston.

Now, update number three, and this is the one I am most worried about. The injury report. Let me go through it. On Wednesday, Cam Skattebo did not practice with his hamstring. He also missed Thursday. Abdul Carter missed Wednesday with an illness, but he was back as a full participant on Thursday. That is great news. Carter is going to play.

But then on Thursday, a new name showed up. Malik Nabers did not practice because of a knee issue. That is a big one. Nabers is the Giants’ best receiver, and Matt Nagy has been building more and more of the passing game around him. We do not know how serious it is. It could be rest. It could be maintenance. It could be something more. I am not going to guess. But when a player is added to the injury report on Thursday with a knee, you pay attention.

And there is a reason Giants fans get nervous any time Nabers and the word knee show up in the same sentence. He tore his ACL early last season, and this year has been about getting him back to the player he was as a rookie, when he caught 109 passes for more than 1,200 yards. The report does not say which knee, and it does not say how serious it is, so I am not going to connect dots that nobody has connected. But you can understand why the staff would be careful. With a game on Sunday and another on Thursday night, I would not be shocked if the Giants limit him in Philadelphia, or even hold him out, to protect him for the long run. A first-place team with ten games left does not need to take a risk with its best receiver in November. That is my read, not a report. Friday’s injury report will tell us a lot.

And the offensive line is banged up too. Left tackle Andrew Thomas was limited on Wednesday and Thursday with a groin injury. Right tackle Jermaine Eluemunor was limited both days with a calf injury. Darius Alexander was limited on Thursday with a foot injury. Receiver Charlie Jones was limited both days with a hand issue. On the positive side, Theo Johnson, Marcus Mbow, DJ Reader, Micah McFadden, Trace Ford, Malik Harrison, and Colton Hood were all full participants on Thursday.

So let me think about what this means. If Thomas and Eluemunor both play at less than 100 percent, the Giants’ offensive line is going to have to work hard against a Philadelphia front that still has talent. And with a short turnaround to Washington on Thursday night, the Giants have to be careful. They cannot afford to lose both tackles for multiple games.

Now, who gets the ball if Skattebo cannot go? I would start with Devin Singletary. Harbaugh calls him Motor, and he scored the 30-yard walk-off touchdown in Houston. Behind him is Gregory Desrosiers, the rookie the Giants signed off the Chargers’ practice squad. He is on the active roster, so he can dress as it stands. Eric Gray and Phil Mafah are on the practice squad, so getting one of them into the game would take an elevation. That is an important detail. If Skattebo is out, I would expect at least one of those two to be elevated for Sunday.

And even if Skattebo dresses, I would watch how the Giants use him. It is one thing to take a handoff between the tackles. It is another to hit the sideline with room to run and trust that hamstring to open up. Remember, Skattebo said he felt his hamstring grab on that 42-yard run in overtime, and he went down on purpose because the Giants were already in field goal range. With the Washington game four days later, I would not be surprised if the Giants hold him out on Sunday to have him ready for Thursday. That is my read, not a report.

And if Nabers cannot go, the passing game shifts. McCarthy would lean more on Isaiah Likely, Malachi Fields, who had the 47-yard catch in Houston, and Theo Johnson. Harbaugh said this week that McCarthy is pretty much caught up on the playbook. That matters even more if he has to play without his top target. The good news is that the Giants have shown they can win without lighting it up through the air. They beat Houston without a passing touchdown.

Now, let’s talk about Joe Milton, because the Giants’ defensive plan just changed. Nick Sirianni benched Tanner McKee after three straight losses. And Milton is getting his first regular season start for the Eagles. A lot of people want to know what to expect from him, so let me give you the two numbers that matter.

The first one is from the final week of the 2024 season. Milton came off the bench for New England against Buffalo, and he completed 22 of 29 passes for 241 yards, with a touchdown pass and a rushing touchdown. That is the version of Milton the Eagles are hoping for. A big arm, a clean pocket, and receivers getting downfield.

The second one is from last season, when he came in for Dallas against the Giants after Dak Prescott was pulled. According to the box score, Milton went 7 of 13 for 73 yards with an interception through the air. But he also ran four times for 43 yards. And that is the number that should scare the Giants. Milton can hurt you with his legs.

So here is what I want from the Giants’ pass rush on Sunday. Do not take the bait. When Milton gives the edge rushers a lane inside, they cannot all crash down and let him escape outside. They have to keep him in the pocket. Make him throw the ball. I would even live with a short completion. If he checks it down five yards short of the first down marker, somebody still has to beat a tackler. What I do not want is a third and eight where the Giants cover everybody, and then Milton runs past the sticks because both edge rushers ended up behind him.

And think about how strange this is for Philadelphia. Jalen Hurts is expected to miss the rest of the season with a serious medical issue. McKee started three games and lost them all. Now it is Milton, their third starting quarterback this year. Meanwhile, the Giants walk in with J.J. McCarthy starting for a first-place team. If you had that matchup circled before the season, you knew something nobody else did.

And the Eagles are banged up too. On Wednesday, Saquon Barkley did not practice with hamstring and back issues. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks did not practice. Linebacker Jihaad Campbell and cornerback Jonathan Jones did not practice. Kicker Jake Elliott was limited with an ankle. Edge rusher Jonathan Greenard was limited. Those are a lot of important players. If Barkley and Smith are limited on Sunday, the Eagles’ offense gets even thinner around a first-time starter.

But I am not going to let anybody get comfortable. Harbaugh said this week that the Giants are fighting for their lives, and that the word favorite is meaningless with ten games left. The Eagles are 2 and 6, they have lost six in a row, and they are at home. A desperate team is a dangerous team. And the Giants have lost 13 straight games in Philadelphia, counting the playoffs. The Giants have to make Milton earn every drive, especially early. A couple of three-and-outs to start the game could take that crowd out of it. But a quarterback scrambling for first downs gives that crowd something to get loud about.

And the standings make this game even bigger. The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1. Dallas is 3 and 5. Washington is 2 and 4 and 1. Philadelphia is 2 and 6. A win on Sunday would make the Giants 5 and 2 and 1, and it would put the Eagles five losses behind them in the loss column. And then Washington comes to MetLife Stadium on Thursday night. Two division games in five days.

So let me put the three updates together. The Giants made an offer for Taylor, but the gap is real, and they may pivot to a defensive tackle. Banks turned down an extension, but there is still a deal to be made. And the injury report is the biggest concern, with Nabers and Skattebo both missing Thursday’s practice and both tackles limited. That is a lot going on for a team that has a division game on Sunday and another on Thursday.

Here is what I will be watching. Number one, Friday’s injury report and game status designations, especially for Nabers and Skattebo. Number two, whether the Giants elevate Eric Gray or Phil Mafah from the practice squad. Number three, whether Andrew Thomas and Jermaine Eluemunor play the whole game. Number four, how the pass rush handles Milton’s legs. And number five, whether the Giants close the gap on Taylor or pivot to Grover Stewart before Tuesday.

And here is my prediction, and I am sticking with it. The Giants break the streak in Philadelphia. It might not be pretty, especially if Nabers and Skattebo are limited. But I think the defense makes Milton uncomfortable, and I think Singletary has a big day. You can hold me to it.

The Giants are 4 and 2 and 1, banged up but in first place, with a front office that is actively shopping and a head coach who will not let anybody relax. I have said it all season, and I am saying it again. This team can go all the way.
